# The First Of The Month Was Painted

He went to work on the eighth at seven and was twenty minutes late, which was the ordinary condition of his life, and on the second floor at about half past seven a man he had cleaned beside for three years put a cloth down and said his name.

"Lode," he said. "Second floor. I do your sink on Thursdays."

"I know you do my sink."

"Halb came through here at six with the ledger and he told the whole landing." Lode wiped his hands on his apron. "Not the account. He was doing the monthly posting and he had your house on a line. He said you lodged a plate at the Exchange yesterday and that it is against a thirty-year account and that it is in a public return."

He did not say it unkindly. That was the part Lin Hao had not been ready for. Lode said it the way a man says a thing about somebody's brother.

"How many heard it."

"Half the landing, and the other half will have it by lunch." Lode picked the cloth up again. "I did not ask you about it. I want that on the record from me and not from anybody on this floor, because in about a week one of the men down at the gate is going to ask you about it and he is going to think he is being interesting."

He went back to the sinks.

He got half an hour off from Dewey, who ran the second floor and owed him a free morning in April and had never mentioned it, and Dewey took the half hour and made a joke about it and then looked at the door to see whether anybody had heard the joke.

## Cordwain

The district office in Cordwain held its rating hearings on the second floor and Beatrix Halm's had been set for ten in the morning on the eighth of June, and it was the fifth of a series and the fourth postponement, and it was about her right ear.

He got there at five to ten and she was on the second step with her hat in her hands.

"It was granted in March," she said. "March, by a letter with a form number on it, and I have had it in a coat since March. It was the fourth of five and they have moved it four times because I work on the line and a signal operator cannot attend a hearing on a working morning, which is true, and which I have said in writing, and which they have continued to find inconvenient."

"What changed this morning."

"A woman at the counter on the bottom went through my instrument and it took her about ninety seconds." Beatrix Halm turned the letter round so he could read it. "There is a line on the back in the Office's type and it says the supporting instrument is encumbered. I asked her what encumbered meant. She said a standing that is answerable for something else.

"She said it the way you say a word in another language."

"What does it mean in yours."

"It means your plate," said Beatrix Halm. "It means that since yesterday afternoon there is a document in a chained book at the Exchange that says a man who cleans six floors at the Fourth Branch has put his standing behind six women and a broker's account, and as of the hour I walked in there, a rating claim supported by the standing of that household cannot be heard, because a called standing cannot be drawn against."

They went up the steps.

"I want you to be very careful with your face," she said. "I am not going to do this in front of a clerk. I am going to do it at the bottom of the steps like a person."

"It was your hearing."

"It was my hearing and I had it in a coat since March and I put the household's standing on it in April because it was the only instrument I had and because you said the standing of a household can be produced at a counter." She stopped on the step below him.

"And I want you to be careful with your face for about four seconds and then I want you to stop, because there is nothing to be careful with. It is a cost. You paid it. That is what costs are and I have signed the notebook and I know whose signature is under the last page."

"I did not know the standing was carrying your hearing."

"You did not know because the standing has not been told what it is standing on since the nineteenth of May," said Beatrix Halm, "and that is not a complaint about you, it is the subject of the whole month, and I have been part of it for five days and I have watched five other women do the same arithmetic badly and get angry at each other about it.

"So: one hearing, postponed to the first of July, which is the next valuation. It is twenty-three days.

"I have had four postponements and I am going to have five, and the fifth is going to be the last one. After the first of July it is either accepted or refused, and it is not going to be refused. It is going to be heard by a panel, and I am going to stand in front of them and be asked what a signal operator who cannot hear in one ear is doing in a cab."

She put her hat on.

"Now you are going to tell me you are sorry, and I would like you to do it in about four words, because I have been standing on this step since half past nine and I have not got much left."

"I am sorry," said Lin Hao.

"Good," said Beatrix Halm. "Four words. Now go and look at your board, because it is a Thursday and it is half past ten, and I have been on a tram for four years and I know exactly when those men come."

## The Board

The board at the side of the wall is not the wall.

That is the first thing the city never tells you. The wall is where the ranks go, in tiles, and a hundred and forty thousand people a week read it and most of them are reading for one name. The board at the side is eleven feet long and it is painted once a month on the first, before the figures go up, and it carries the standing households: number, standing, date of certification.

There were two hundred and something lines on it. It had been a hundred and eighty in the spring.

The number was on the board in tile, and it was four days up from the bottom, and it was in a paint that was a slightly different white from the paint above it, because the crew that does the monthly pass is not the crew that does the Thursday pass, and the Office buys one shade for one and another for the other.

And a person's name next to it, typed small, at the head line, because a board that carries a number without a name is a board that carries a number, and this one carries both.

"That is the schedule's date and not the certificate's," said the white-bearded man. He was on the crate with a trowel and he had not started, because it was ten past ten and the Thursday pass is nine o'clock and there is nothing on this week's list. "The board is painted off the schedules, and the schedule of that household is the fourth of last month.

"Your certificate is the twenty-seventh of this month. This board does not know that and will not until the first of next month, and on the first of next month it will paint the same four words."

"It has been up seven days."

"It went up on the first at about a quarter to ten in the morning." He said it without weight, which was worse than anything else could have been. "There is a man in a plaster hat who does the first. He is not one of us and he does not know the wall and he paints from the book, which is correct, and he painted yours out of the book on the first of this month while you were somewhere else in this city with your coat off."

He put the trowel down.

"You did not see it go up."

"I was on a landing at eleven with a woman copying a roll."

"Then it has been on that board for seven days," said the white-bearded man, "and it is not going to come down, because it is not a mistake. It is the correct entry and it was correct on the first and it will be correct on the fifteenth and on the first of next month, and if it comes down it leaves a ghost, and a ghost on that board is a hole, and I have seen a hole on that board once and we do not talk about it."

He picked the trowel up again.

"There is a name next to it as well," he said. "I am not going to ask you whether you would rather it had your number or your name, because you have worked that out already, and you worked it out on a landing at ten at night in May, and you told me about it on the pavement while I was sitting on a kerb for an hour I am not going to explain to anybody."

He went back to the crate.

## After

He came up the sixty-one steps at about ten past eleven with nothing to say, which is a thing that has happened to him perhaps four times in five years.

Nadia Ferrous was on the landing with her notebook — her notebook, the sixth one, the one she keeps because she is the household's conscience and writes things down about the household out of plain habit, unasked and uncommissioned — and she looked at him and shut it.

"You have had a morning."

"I have had a morning."

"Do you want the whole of it or the version for a kitchen?"

"The version for a kitchen," said Lin Hao.

Nadia Ferrous opened it again and said, "Sun Meiling has been up on that wall with a chair since about ten past nine and she has been looking at the seventh row in the daylight, which she has not done since the twenty-ninth of May, because for ten days the only light that ever touched it was the stair window at about seven in the evening, and she put it there on purpose.

"It is there in the morning now," said Nadia Ferrous. "It has been there since the beginning of June because the sun comes round the back of that building in about the first week of the month and hits that landing at about a quarter past ten, and she has known that since the second of June and she has not said so and she has been going up there in the mornings to check.

"She is not checking that her name is still there," said Nadia Ferrous. "She is checking whether anybody in this house is going to have to hold a lamp for it."
