# Eleven Dockets

The tenth was the last day of the demand and by a quarter past ten in the morning the account had a standing against it and the standing was a plate at 8,138 and the head of it had put it there himself, on the seventh, in a chained book, in a broker's hand.

It took four minutes at a desk on the ground floor of the Exchange. A clerk wrote one word in one column of a sheet and turned it round and read it back, and the word was SECURED, and neither of them said anything about it, and the count printed itself on the sheet in three places and the building read none of them.

Then he came home up sixty-one steps at half past eleven with a coat over his arm that was not his.

## Twenty-One Days

The rest of the morning was arithmetic and it was the first arithmetic in four volumes that had a date on the good side of it.

The account was secured from the seventh. The demand was met on the tenth, in four minutes, by a clerk writing one word. The examination stood, and the examination is entered against a broker of record, and a broker of record with a live account and no problem is a broker of record whose book goes to the next sitting in four years without a question asked about item one.

And Beatrix Halm's hearing came back on the first of July, in writing, at nine in the morning of the first, in a room on the second floor of a district office in Cordwain, because a rating claim supported by an encumbered instrument becomes a rating claim with no instrument at all on the day the valuation clears, and she would be standing in front of a panel being asked what a signal operator who cannot hear in one ear is doing in a cab.

She did not celebrate, and the household let her alone, and that was correct.

"And from the seventh until the first," said Runa Sato, "nothing in this house draws on anything. Every permission, every correction, every instrument any of us has bought in the last month with the standing of this household behind it, is unpayable for three weeks and three days.

"That is twenty-one days out of this morning and three days behind us, and I have been counting since the seventh and I intend to go on counting in this kitchen out loud until the first, so that the count does not have to live in one head."

"Three weeks and three days," said Sun Meiling, "and it is the first time in five years that there has been a date on the good side of anything in this kitchen, and it is on the good side because a man put a plate into a chained book, and I am not going to be the one who says out loud what that is, and I would like somebody else to notice it eventually and say something."

## The Coat

Petra Ilyin had gone back to the Vant at nine in the morning and had gone on her own, which was the first time in eleven years that going back had cost her nothing at all, and she had left without telling the household, and had told Sun Meiling where the key was under the third stair.

The coat was on the peg in a room eleven feet by seven with a bolted table with nothing on it and four pegs on a rail and a high window with a grille in it and a door standing open.

"Eight days," she said, coming in with it over her arm. "I have been in about four hundred rooms since the second and I have not had to think about any of them, and on the second I went into one and my hand went out for the frame out of eleven years' habit and there was no frame there, and I laughed in a corridor on my own, which I am told is not what officers do."

She put the coat on the back of a chair and did not put it on.

"There was something in the pocket."

She put the bundle on the kitchen table and it took her two hands and it made a sound like a small pack of cards being set down.

There were a hundred and thirty-two of them. They were dockets about nine inches by five, thin, folded twice, and they had been folded twice for eleven years by somebody who is careful with paper.

One a month. The first was dated in the spring of the eleventh year back and the last was dated the first of this month, and there was one for every month in between, in order, with no gaps, and each one carried the account and the premises and the word PRODUCE and a space at the foot about two inches deep for a mark.

Each one had a mark in it.

Small, in ink, at the foot, two letters and a date, in a hand that everybody at that table could read from across a kitchen because it had been writing entries in that book for thirty years.

"Heart," said Nadia Ferrous, after a while. "Is that a heart."

"It is my mark," said Marguerite Vale. "It is how I have signed production on that account for eleven years, once a month, on the second, in the afternoon. A hundred and thirty-two marks in a hundred and thirty-two margins, four seconds each, and I have never once left a gap in one of them."

## What She Did Not Say

The kitchen was quiet for about a minute.

"You received them," said Sun Meiling.

"I received them."

"Once a month for eleven years."

"Once a month for eleven years, on the second, at the Exchange. Every one of them was produced against on the fourth and I entered the production myself, in the margin, in my own hand, a hundred and thirty-two times, and I did not once ask who was producing it. The answer was that a broker receives production from an officer, and an officer's business is the officer's own."

"You knew."

"I knew there was an account and a person and a monthly docket and I knew that the person had not been produced to this desk in eleven years because I had never once seen her come up sixty-one steps and I had been in this building four times in eleven years, and I did not ask because I did not want the answer and I have had that answer since the second of June and I have not said it in a room until now."

She put both hands flat on the table.

"I want to be exact about it, because there is a version of this in which I am a woman who trapped a girl in a room for eleven years and I would like that version to be on the table next to the true one, and I want it said out loud by somebody who is not me so that it is in the air.

"I was never asked whether I wanted to release that oath. I swore it and I let it run and I marked a hundred and thirty-two of them in a margin in ink. She could not leave a room. I could have gone and got her. Eleven years, and four times in this building, and not once, and not because of anything she did, and because every single month a piece of paper came up a desk and I marked it and put it in a drawer and asked nothing."

"She did not know either," said Beatrix Halm, from the middle of the table, in a voice the table had not heard from her before. "That is what this kitchen keeps getting round to, and it took you five months and a docket with a coat in it. She did not know she was held.

"She has told us the name of the person who did it and it is a person in a room in 2015 who has been dead since 2011, and the reason she remembers the address instead of the face is that the oath was never on her. It was on me. I never gave it. I just did not open the envelope."

Petra Ilyin did not answer that, because there was nothing to answer, and she sat at a kitchen table for the first time in her life with eleven months of her own life on it and let it be true.

## Which List

"You have been in this kitchen three times," said Nadia Ferrous, "and you have been in this house three times in eleven years and there is a woman at the back with the door open who has not been out of a building in eleven years, and I would like to ask you the question I ask everybody, and it is the only question I have ever asked anybody in this room and I have never once liked it."

"Ask it."

"Which list are you on," said Nadia Ferrous. "Because there are three and I keep all three and they do not agree. Seven women in this house. Six of them keep the notebook. Six of them keep an address at Ferrier Street and the answer has been six since the twenty-second of March. And there is a woman in the room at the back who is on none of the three and sleeps in the building anyway."

"None of them," said Petra Ilyin.

"Right," said Nadia Ferrous, and got out her notebook, and wrote it down, and showed it round, which she does. "So there are two of you now. You are a discharged officer of a guild that did not know you were in this building until the fourth, and she is a chaplain who has not left a room in eleven years, and between the two of you that is about twenty-two years of a life spent on the wrong side of every form in this city.

"And I would like it noted that neither of you has ever once complained about it to me, and that I have been the only person in this house who has ever asked either of you the question."

## The Twelfth

Then she squared the hundred and thirty-two up into a pile and put her hand flat on it, and said, "That is every docket this account has issued since the eleventh year back, and I have been an officer since I was twenty-two, and there is one a month because a monthly account is docketed monthly, and there is one more in that coat and I have not put it on the table."

She took her hand off the pile.

"I took it out at about nine o'clock this morning before I put the coat on, and I have had it in my hand for two hours, and it is dated the first of July, and today is the tenth of June."

The room looked at the sheet of paper in her hand and the sheet of paper in her hand was a docket with the account on it and the premises on it and PRODUCE in the middle of it and a space at the foot about two inches deep.

"Three weeks early," said Runa Sato. "Dockets go out on the first."

"Dockets go out on the first," said Petra Ilyin, "and that one is dated the first of July and it was in a coat pocket at nine o'clock this morning, in this building, on the first of June, and it has been coming to me once a month for eleven years and this is the first time one of them has ever arrived before its own date.

"Somebody entered it early. Somebody in that building knows what is going to happen on the first of July, has written it out, and has put it in a coat pocket on the first, nine days ago, and it is addressed to Ferrier Street and it is for a household of six with a head whose standing is lodged and answerable."

She put it down on top of the bundle.

"Seventeen days to the certificate," said Lin Hao. "Three weeks and three days is what his plate bought on the seventh, that window runs to the first of July, and somebody inside that building has already written the paper for the day it runs out."
