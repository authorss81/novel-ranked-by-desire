# What The Division Did Not Tell

Sun Meiling had the sheet face up on the table at eight in the morning on the thirteenth with a pencil in her hand and two pages of her own copying pinned under a saucer, and she had been up since five and she had eaten nothing.

"You did not read the foot of it," she said.

"No."

"I did not read the foot of it either and I have not slept, and I have read the foot of it four times this morning." She put a finger under a line at the bottom of the second page. "Every correction that alters an indexed schedule is countersigned before it goes out. That is not in this document.

It is in the standing orders of the office and it has been in them for longer than either of us, and it is the reason a correction is a correction and not a letter, and it is the reason it can be produced out of a file by anybody holding the file."

She turned the sheet round so the room could see the bottom of the second page, and there was a line and a date and a name in a small upright hand, and Nadia Ferrous read it out loud before anybody else got to it.

"Wren Kallo. Counter-forensics. Thirteenth."

"Countersigned this morning," said Sun Meiling. "Which means it went through her bench at about eight and it came out of her division at about eight, and there is no requisition number on it anywhere, because there is no requisition. This did not go out against a man. It went out against the office's own arithmetic, and a thing that goes out against the office's own arithmetic does not need a person to send it and does not get a person to answer it."

"That is what a correction is," said Ines Duarte, from the stair, "and it is why a correction is more dangerous than a notice of production and I have been in this building for four years and I did not think of it until about ten minutes ago."

## Two Instruments

She came down and sat down, which she had not done on the twelfth.

"There are two instruments on that table and they are from the same office and they are four days apart and they are about two entirely different things, and I would like the household to hold them apart, because if they are held together they look like a campaign and they are not a campaign. They are an index doing its job twice.

"The first is the notice of production on the ninth, which carries my name in the third line, and it is about the schedule of the fourth and about the auditor of record, and it is a defect.

"The second is this, and it is not about the schedule at all. The schedule has been filed since the fourth of May. This is about the premises, and the premises answerable under a return, and the return was made on the third of this month at ten past seven in the evening by a district officer standing in a doorway."

"Who," said Nadia Ferrous.

"Not a question you get to ask me," said Ines Duarte, "and I am not going to pretend I do not know the answer.

I know the answer because the district officer's name is on the return and the return is not a document this house has ever been given a copy of, and because there is one district officer in this city who has written a line and left it, and I have known that since the third of this month and I have not said it because saying it would have been saying more than I was asked."

"Two days after she met us," said Petra Ilyin, from the back, "on the third. And the return went into the district bag and the district bag goes into the Office's index and the index is constituted from the returned lines and not from the totals, and a total is arithmetic and a returned line is evidence."

"Yes," said Ines Duarte.

"So it is not a mistake," said Beatrix Halm. "That is the thing I want to be clear about before anybody in this room starts hunting for whoever made it. Somebody made it on purpose, and a person who wanted a person named would have named her, and it is still blank, and there is nothing on that sheet with her name on it, and I want that said at this table before the room starts."

## What The Blank Is

The room at the back had been left alone all morning, and at about half past ten it was not.

The door had been open every day for a year, and it was open, and Agnete Lindqvist was standing in it with her hand on the frame in the ordinary way, in the daytime, and she had heard all of it.

"May I say the thing and go back," she said.

"Say the thing."

"There is one instrument in this city that says a person is here and has been left out," said Agnete Lindqvist. "It is the empty line.

An officer writes a total and strikes nothing out, and a form that is not full is read as a person who was not entered, and that has been true in this city for as long as there have been forms and my Church has taught it as the only freedom there is, because every other freedom in this city is a thing you have to give somebody your name for."

She put her other hand on the frame.

"Put my name on that line and the line closes and the refusal ends. That is all it does. It does not open a door and it does not unlock anything. It ends the only arrangement I have been living inside since the year I stopped being a clerk."

"Then we do not do it," said Sun Meiling.

"You are going to have to do it or you are going to answer the fourth page with a figure that is one short, and the fourth page goes into the record of the head of this house in his own name, and then there are two false things about a woman who has done nothing at all for eleven years, and the second one will be in a Bureau's file and the first one will be on the wall of a district office."

"Who writes the line," said Lin Hao.

"The officer. It is the officer's return." She said it the way she said a thing she had checked twice. "I am not able to write my own line on a premises return. There is no provision for it.

I have checked, and I checked it in May, and I have been checking it once a month since May, and it is the one thing in eleven years I have been permitted to go and look at, because the Church keeps the rules of every instrument in this city and it is the only reason I have been of any use at all."

## The Third Thing

The room was very still.

"So that is the whole of it," said Agnete Lindqvist. "There is a form that asks the head to fill it in, and there is a form that asks the head to explain it, and there is a form that asks the head to strike a line out, and all three of them go to his plate, and every one of them is signed by him in his own name.

"And there is a fourth thing, which is the reason I am standing in this doorway at half past ten in the morning instead of where I was, and I will say it and then I am going back.

"The thing I want is not my name. The thing I want is that if a name is ever written on that line, it is not written by the Office and it is not written by him and it is not written by any of you.

And that is not possible, and I have known it was not possible since May, and I have been sitting behind a door for eleven years waiting for somebody to give me an impossible thing, and I would like it noted that it was given to me this morning by a woman who was crying about a form."

"I was not crying," said Sun Meiling.

"You were crying about a form," said Agnete Lindqvist, "and it is the first time in eleven years that anybody in this house has been angry on my account about a piece of paper, and I am going back to my room now, and I want the door left where it is, and I want whoever writes on that line to do it knowing that I know exactly what a line is."

She went back and the door stayed open.

## What The Lead Wants

He was standing by the window at the far end of the kitchen, and he had said almost nothing for two and a half hours, and he had one thing and he had been holding it since about ten past eight.

"The counter-signature," he said. "Wren Kallo countersigned it at eight this morning and she was not asked to. That is a standing instruction of the office and it means somebody in this city whose entire trade is looking at the shape of an edit put her name on a correction about this household, and she has done it, and she did not tell me, and she has never once told me anything in this city without telling somebody else as well."

"That is her arrangement and it has been hers for four years," said Sun Meiling.

"It is her arrangement, and I have never once minded it, and I have also never once thought about what it would be like to have somebody in this city see a correction with this address on it and have to put a name on the bottom of it." He had both hands flat on the table and did not move them. "I have spent a month teaching this household that a document is the dangerous thing.

I have spent a month being glad that the record is only a pencil on a landing. And a woman I have not seen in a year countersigned a form about that pencil this morning at eight o'clock and I did not know until twenty to nine."

"Write to her."

"I am not writing to her," he said. "There is no list she is on, at no address, in a city where a thing you cannot post does not exist. If I want to speak to her before Thursday I have to go to the counter where she works and stand in a queue with forty other people and ask for her by name, and she will be told a man from Ferrier Street asked for her by name, and she will not be able to keep that either."

"Then what do you want."

"I want to know what she saw," he said, "before she decides what to do about it, and I want to know it before the fourth page goes, because on the day the fourth page goes to Cobb Street in his own name with six on it, and a false sworn answer is a thing an office can only act on once it has the paper, and she countersigns the paper."
