# The Complete Version

The notice was carried up the sixty-one steps just after eleven on the ninth, in an envelope with a form number on it, and the name on the envelope was a name at Ferrier Street and not a name at Lantern Row, which is how he knew before he had it open that it was about the household and not about him.

Ines Duarte came in behind it at eleven twenty because she had come up the steps with the man who had brought it and had told him, on the bottom stair, in the voice she uses at a counter, that if she was going to be handed a document about her own premises it was going to be handed to her in front of the people it concerned.

She read it standing up, in the flat voice of a clerk reading to a man who has not read it, and it took her about a minute and a half, and not one person in that kitchen sat down.

"Office of the audit division of this city. To the head of a standing household, premises Ferrier Street. You are required to produce the record of that household as it stands in the index of this office, for the purposes of a certification to be granted on the twenty-seventh of June.

"The auditor of record upon the schedule raised against those premises on the twenty-first of May is named in the third line of this notice. The officer holding that schedule may not certify a household of which she is a member."

She turned it round.

"Third line," she said.

Sun Meiling took it. She read the first line and then she read the third line and then she read the third line a second time, which is a thing she does, and which by now is the most frightening thing about her.

"Ines."

"I am not going to say it. I am going to let the room do it, because I have been in eleven hundred rooms of this office and there are two people in Ostara who could do this arithmetic and you are one of them and I would like to see whether you arrive at it the way I have arrived at it."

## The Arithmetic

Runa Sato had it in about a minute and a half.

"The certificate is dated the twenty-seventh and the schedule goes up on the twenty-sixth, and there are two working days between them and in those two the certificate has to be issued and filed and carried to an address in Ferrier Street.

"And the schedule cannot be certified by the auditor of record because the auditor of record is a member of the household the schedule is about. So on the twenty-sixth of June the schedule does not go up. And on the twenty-seventh there is no certificate."

"That is right," said Ines Duarte.

"Then somebody at the top of that building has to do something about it before the twenty-sixth."

"Somebody at the top of that building is going to do something about it before the twenty-sixth," said Ines Duarte, "and what they are going to do is remove the auditor of record from the schedule, because that is the only instrument in this city that deals with a conflict of that shape, and it works, and it takes a line and a date and an initial, and I have watched it used twice in four years."

"And it is your line."

"It is my line and it is the only line on that schedule that can be taken out without somebody's standing going with it." She said it with absolutely no weight. "Every other one of you is on that page because a man with a plate answered for you in his own hand on the thirtieth of May.

"There is no way to take any of you off that page. There is one instrument in the office for removing a person from a certified schedule and it is for an officer with a conflict, and I am the only person on that page with a conflict, and I have been the only person on that page with a conflict since the sixth of May and I knew it and I put myself on it anyway."

She put the notice on the table face down.

"I am not discussing it," she said. "I said in a corridor in April that you were not to ask me about this and I have not discussed it for four years and I am not discussing it on a morning when I have handed a piece of paper into a kitchen and let the room work out what it costs."

"You handed it in," said Sun Meiling.

"I handed it in at half past six this morning before it was due, which is a thing I have done once in eleven years and which I did because I was not prepared to have it arrive on a landing while Beatrix Halm was reading a tram timetable at the bottom of it."

## The Complete Version

"There is one more thing," said Sun Meiling, "and I want to say it slowly because it is the worst thing on that table and it is not in the notice.

"Look at the top of it. Look at what is printed above the address."

Ines Duarte turned it over.

There was a line above the address that the whole room had read straight past, because it was in the Office's type and it was the shape of a line that is printed on about four hundred thousand pieces of paper in this city.

"This office indexes every schedule a member of the division appears upon," said Sun Meiling. "It has indexed that schedule since the twenty-first of May. It holds the head's line, and six members, and standing, and instrument, and the name of the auditor of record.

"And it indexes this household. The same premises. The same six names. The same head. Two entries in one index about one set of six women, and they were made by the same office on the same day and they do not know about each other, and the rule that keeps them apart is a rule that says an officer is not told about her own division's index sheets.

"So this notice has arrived at a household because an office made a complete version of it, and Cobb Street has never been inside this house and is never going to be told what is in it, and the complete version is the only one in this city.

"Ours is a pencil board on a landing and a notebook under a table and a book with no title on a shelf on Lantern Row. That one is in their type and it is in a file and it is the only one with a date on every line of it."

She looked up.

"That is the safest thing about this household and it is the first time anybody has said so in four years, and I would like it noted that the safety comes from a form, because the office does not know there are seven women in this kitchen and it is not going to ask, and not one person in that building is going to ask, and that is the whole of what I have left to say about it this morning."

The room was very quiet, and Ines Duarte stood at the end of the table with both hands flat on it, and did not say one word, and she was not going to be asked.

## After

She picked the notice up off the table at about half past twelve and squared it against her palm.

"I want to say one procedural thing and then I am going to leave and go back to a building where four hundred people are waiting for me to not be interesting, and I would like it taken as procedural, because it is."

"Say it."

"This extract is not a copy of our file," said Ines Duarte. "It is an extract from their index of our file, and it is the only complete version of this record that exists, and it carries a standing lodged at a guild against a broker's account with a date on the foot of it that was written in a chained book three days ago.

"And if it goes back down to Cobb Street, it goes back with that foot on it, and the index will be reconciled against the chained book on the next posting, and the head's plate will become a line in a division's file on the same page as a member's name, and that is the end of the only thing in this house that has been secret by accident for five years."

"You want it to stay on the table," said Nadia Ferrous.

"I want it to stay on the table," said Ines Duarte, "and I want the copy in the notebook to be made, and I want the household to understand that the two documents will then differ, and I want the difference written down, because I am the only person alive who can read both of them properly and one day there will be a third copy and somebody will have to know which of the two is the true one."

Nadia Ferrous wrote it down. She wrote it down on the page, and then she turned the notebook round and put it on the table face up in front of Ines Duarte, which she had never once done, and Ines Duarte looked at it for about four seconds and did not touch it.

"And one more," she said. "The last one, and it is not procedural.

"I am going to be asking to be removed from that schedule. Not today and not in a fit of anything. In a room with a door on it, on an afternoon, in writing, with a reason attached, in the way it is done."

The room did not make a sound.

"You may not ask me about it," she said, "and I am asking you now for the whole of it, for the next seventeen days, because the certificate is dated the twenty-seventh and the schedule goes up on the twenty-sixth and there are two working days in that and I would like to be in a room with people I did not put in this house when I do it."

"Anything you want," said Lin Hao.

"Be told," said Ines Duarte. "That is the whole of it and it is the first standing instruction I have given in this house in four years. If anything comes to that address with a form number on it, whoever opens it tells me before anybody discusses it, and I do not want an answer from this room tonight and I would like it written down in a book that is not mine."
