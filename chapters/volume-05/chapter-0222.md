# Seven Where It Says Six

The sheet came up the sixty-one steps at about ten in the morning on the twelfth in an envelope with a form number on it, and Nadia Ferrous opened it on the landing because Nadia Ferrous opened everything, and she read the first line and then went back down sixty-one steps at a run and caught the tram.

She did not read the second line.

"I have not read the second line," she said, when she came in at about half past two.

"I have read the first line and I have put it face down on the table and I have gone and got her, and the instruction was to tell her first, and I have done that, and I would like somebody in this kitchen to understand that it is now half past two in the afternoon and I have been sitting here for four hours with a piece of paper I am not allowed to talk about."

"You did exactly what was asked," said Sun Meiling.

"I did exactly what was asked and I would like it noticed that exactly what was asked took four hours off the middle of the week," said Nadia Ferrous. "That is the price of the instruction. It is not free and it is not going to be free every time."

The table had the envelope face down on it and the rest of the household arranged around it at a distance that was not natural, and two hundred and something people had come through the market below Tessa's hatch that morning and not one of them knew there was a piece of paper on a table six floors above it with a number in the corner.

## Four Hours

Runa Sato had cleaned the envelope off and put it back four times.

"Three days and about twenty hours," she said. "That is what is left of it if the Office says four days from the date on it, and the date on it is the twelfth, and we are on the twelfth, and the certificate is in fifteen days and forty-five of the sixty are gone."

"Read it to me."

"Not until she has read it," said Runa Sato. "You gave her the instruction and the instruction is not a courtesy. If we read it before she gets here we will have an opinion about it and she will walk into a room with an opinion about it in it, and a woman like that can tell the difference from the door."

"So we sit here for four hours with an opinion growing."

"We sit here for four hours with an opinion not growing," said Runa Sato, "which is a different discipline and I have been doing it since the seventh of June about a plate."

Sun Meiling turned the envelope over at about a quarter to five and looked at the postmark and put it back.

"It came into the district bag this morning with the district's own deliveries," she said. "Not through a counter. It was carried up with a bundle, which means it came off a van that works the Ferrier Street end, and it was put in with the post by a woman who has a route and does not know what she was carrying. That is not a thing the audit division does when it is angry with a house.

That is a thing it does at the end of a week when it has found an arithmetic that does not agree with itself."

## What The Office Says

Ines Duarte came in at twenty past five with her coat still on and her bag over her arm and she did not take her coat off, which she had never done in that kitchen in four years, and she put her hand flat on the envelope and turned it over and read it standing up in about a minute and ten seconds.

She read it twice. The second time was slower.

"Seven," she said.

"Seven what."

"The index of this office is constituted from the returned lines of the district premises returns, and the district premises return for those premises was made on the third of this month by an officer of this district, and it carries seven returned lines, and six of them have names on them and one of them has nothing on it, and the total in the foot of the column says six."

She put one finger on the middle of the sheet.

"This office does not use the total. It never has. A total is an officer's arithmetic and the arithmetic is the officer's, and the returned lines are the evidence, and a returned line that is empty cannot be struck out of the index by anybody who has not got a name to strike it out with."

Nadia Ferrous said, in a voice with nothing in it at all, "She left it blank."

"An officer of the district left the seventh line blank on the third of this month at about ten past seven in the evening," said Ines Duarte, "and wrote six in the foot of the column, which is what the officer believed, and the office has read the seven lines and counted seven, because the office is not unkind. The office is worse than unkind. The office is exact."

There was a woman asleep behind a door on that floor, and the Office had found her by counting a blank.

## The Instruction

"You are going to tell us what happens if it is not answered," said Lin Hao.

"If it is answered in four days it stays in the index as it is answered and the reconciliation is done and the line stays in the index as an empty line in a file that the office holds a copy of, and in every office that has asked the office for one, and no clerk in any of them ever turns to that page." She squared the sheet against her palm. "If it is not answered in four days it is answered for him.

The head of a household is required to give the figure of the persons of reading age keeping an address at his premises and the Office will enter its own figure against his name, and it does that in a single line, and it does not ask anybody anything, because the Office does not have an instrument for asking."

"What is the instrument for asking."

"There is a form on the fourth page which asks the head to reconcile, and the form on the fifth page which asks the head to explain a discrepancy, and both of them go to the head's plate, and both of them are answered in the head's own name and go into the file that his plate is standing in at a broker's counter." She put the sheet down.

"And there is a third thing this city can do with a line on a return, which is to strike it through, and striking a line through on a premises return is a question asked in the only place in this city where a question gets asked for free."

"So the answer is to answer it," said Sun Meiling.

"The answer is to answer it in four days," said Ines Duarte, "and it is the twelfth and I have been in this room for six minutes and I am going to ask this room one thing and I would like it answered out loud and I would like it answered by the person it belongs to and I would like it answered before I sit down.

"Is there a seventh person keeping an address at those premises."

Nothing in the room answered her, and in that silence he understood that the rest of them had known there was an answer to that question four days ago and had not asked it, and that he was the one who had been given the answer on the third by a district officer standing in a doorway at ten past seven in the evening, and that the reason she had not been entered was that she had never once been asked whether she wanted to be.

He looked at the door at the back of the room, which was open, and the room behind it was dark.

"Yes," he said. "There is a seventh person keeping an address at those premises."

"Say the name," said Sun Meiling.

He said it.

## After

Ines Duarte did not look at the back of the room and she did not look at him, and she picked the sheet up off the table and squared it and said, in the flat voice she uses at a counter:

"You will answer it yourself, and you will answer it in your own name, and the form is the fourth page and the fourth page takes a figure and nothing else. It does not take a reason. It does not take a name. It takes a figure, and the figure goes into the record of the head of that household, and the head of that household is the figure's only owner.

"I am going to say the other thing once and then I am going to go down to Lantern Row and I am not going to discuss it.

"The schedule of the fourth cannot be certified by its own auditor of record, and that is on the ninth's notice, and it is why the schedule does not go up on the twenty-sixth and there is no certificate on the twenty-seventh. That is a defect and it is about me.

"This one is about you. And it goes into the same schedule, because the schedule carries your answer and I carry the schedule." She had her coat on and her bag on her arm. "I am not asking you anything and you are not going to tell me anything, and I would like the household to note that I have said the second of those two things out loud and have not asked for it."

She went out. She was gone in about ninety seconds.

He stood at the end of the table with the fourth page in front of him and read it twice, because it was the same page the other instrument had been on, and the difference between the two was that this one only wanted a number.

"I have spent five years not putting a number where a number is asked for," he said. "Every office I have ever been inside has had a place where my plate goes and I have never once been the one who wrote it. That was the whole plan and it worked and it cost me the file."

"It is still the whole plan," said Sun Meiling. "It has just acquired a fourth page."

"The fourth page wants a figure and I am going to write six on it in two nights' time after work and I am going to do it at this table with the whole house watching, and I want that said out loud tonight while there are still six of us and a door open."

"Say what is going to be on it," said Runa Sato, "and then everybody in this room goes to bed, and not one person in this house has an opinion about it before the instrument has gone."

The kitchen was quiet for a while, and then Nadia Ferrous took her notebook out and shut it again, which was the first time in four years she had done that at that table.

"Six," she said. "We answer six. Write it down, all of you. We answer six, in his own name, on the fourth page, and we do it in four days, and then we go to bed.

And I want it written down that I have written the word six in this notebook on the twelfth of June and that I did not put the name next to it, and that the name is in the room, and that a Bureau instrument is going to be answered from this table inside four days, and that it is going to be about the woman behind that door, and that not one woman at this table is going to be able to forget it."

She wrote it down. And she turned the notebook round and put it on the table face up, which she had done twice in a month and never before that.
