# Six Persons Of Reading Age

He wrote it on the kitchen table at about nine at night on the fourteenth with the whole house in the room, because he had said out loud on the twelfth that he was going to do that and there is not one instrument in this city that lets a man go back on a thing he said in front of a room full of them.

The fourth page took a figure.

There was a box at the top of it for the premises and a box at the side for the household number and then there was a line ruled across the page with the words PERSONS OF READING AGE KEEPING AN ADDRESS, and under that line there was one ruled space, and in the space he wrote SIX.

He wrote it in his own hand and he did it quickly, which he noticed himself doing.

"Read it out," said Runa Sato. "It is your instrument and somebody in this room has to hear the whole of it, including the small print, and I am the person in this room who reads instruments."

"Instrument evidencing the return made on the third of this month by a district officer of this district, in answer to a correction raised by the audit division on the date of that instrument. Head of the household. Premises Ferrier Street. Number as at the head line. Persons of reading age keeping an address at those premises: six. Signed."

"And under it?"

"There is nothing under it."

"There is always something under it," said Sun Meiling, "and it is the part that is small. Go on."

He read the small part. It said that the answer is given by the head of the household in his own name and enters the record of the premises at the address and is answerable against the head's standing, and it said the word *answerable* four times in nine lines, and he had not noticed the word the first time he read it and he noticed it now because Runa Sato had made him read it aloud.

"Answerable against the head's standing," said Runa Sato. "Say the next part."

"There is no next part."

"There is, because I have been reading the standing orders of four divisions for four years and I have never once had to ask twice." She put both hands on the table. "Answerable means answerable.

It means that if this figure is wrong, the head answers for it out of his standing, and if the head's standing is lodged against an account, then the account is answerable for it, and if the account is in a chained book, then the person holding the book can produce this page at a valuation and call your standing against it.

"Your lie is not a Bureau document. It is a Bureau document with a guild tail on it, and in about four years it belongs to a woman called Rask."

Nothing was said for a while, and that was correct, because there was nothing to say to that except no.

## The Number

"Forty-seven of the sixty are gone," said Runa Sato. "Thirteen to a certificate. And the plate this is answered against is lodged in a chained book until the first of July and cannot be withdrawn before it, so if you want to know what it costs, the price of this page is that for eleven days it cannot be taken out of the book, and after the first of July it can."

"How many days."

"From tomorrow it is sixteen," said Runa Sato. "And it will be eleven on the twentieth. It is the same run as the window and it gets shorter every day and there is not one thing in this house that has a different length."

## Ines

She had been in the room the whole time with her coat off, which she had not done on the twelfth, and she did not come and read it.

He wanted her not to. That was the whole of what he had wanted for two days and he had not said it to a living soul, and he wanted it so badly that when she got up at about a quarter past nine and came round the table and put her hand flat on the page he felt something go out of him that had been holding his shoulders up.

She read it in about four seconds. She did not read the small print twice and she did not read it at all the second time, which meant she had read it once and it was enough, which meant she had seen the figure and the signature and the standing line and had put the three of them together in four seconds, and he knew exactly what that cost her and exactly how long it had taken.

"Is it six," said Ines Duarte.

"It is what I am writing," he said.

"Then it is six," she said.

She put the page down, squared it against the palm of her hand the way she squared every sheet she had ever handled in her life, and went up the stairs, and did not say one other word in that kitchen for the whole of the rest of the night.

## What It Is

"There was a woman behind a door on this floor," said Sun Meiling, "who has spent eleven years outside every form in this city, and tonight one person in this house went inside one on purpose to keep her there, and the instrument is a good one and it is in his own name, and it is going to Cobb Street, and Cobb Street keeps it."

"Say the rest of it."

"That is the whole of it and I am not going to improve on it." She had her arms folded and she was not looking at him.

"Eight days ago I made a wall and a file agree with each other so that neither of them was a lie, and it was the best four days of my life since the twenty-ninth of last month, and tonight I sat at this table and watched six of us agree to a thing that is not true and put it in a book.

I did not have a vote. I want that on the record. I did not have a vote and I would have voted against it and I would still rather have done it than had that blank read out in a division's type."

"That is the whole of the argument," said Nadia Ferrous, "and it was made in nine seconds by the person in this house who has the best claim to make it, and I am going to write it down and I am going to write down that you did not have a vote, because in about four years somebody is going to read that page and find out how many women were in this room and how many of them knew."

"Some of us knew and all of us were in the room," said Petra Ilyin, "and I would like that written down instead of a number, because a number is the one thing in this house a person can choose, and I would like it written down that I chose it.""

## The Coat Pocket

She had been at the table for the whole of it, and she had not eaten, and she had the bundle with her, because she had carried it down from a peg on the landing where it lives under a coat that is not hers.

"Can I say one thing and it is about the first of July and it is about this page and not about the page."

"Say it."

"I have a docket in that bundle dated the first of July," said Petra Ilyin, "and it came out of a coat pocket on the tenth, and there is a space at the foot of it two inches deep where the holder marks production, and there is nothing in the space, and that is the first time in eleven years that one of those has come to me with the mark not on it.

"A broker marks production against an account when she produces it. That is the whole of the mark. If it is not marked at its date, it is not produced, and an account that is not produced at a valuation goes on the dead list, and when an account goes on the dead list every security standing against it comes off with it."

She put her hand flat on the bundle.

"So on the first of July there is a valuation, and there is a docket, and there is a plate in a chained book that has a false sworn answer answered against it, and any one of those three things on its own is ordinary and all three of them on the same morning in the same building is a thing somebody has written out and put in a coat pocket three weeks early.

"And I have not been asked what the account is for and I am not going to ask, and I want it said in this room tonight that the reason not one woman in this house has asked me is that the two women who could have asked each other did not ask each other for eleven years, and that is the same sentence twice, and it is the only thing in this house tonight that I am actually frightened of."

She picked the bundle up, and then she stopped with her foot on the first step.

"One more thing and then I will go up," she said. "You gave that page to me because I am not on a roll. I want to say what that costs, because not one other person in this room is going to say it and I have been an officer for eleven years and this is the exact shape of it.

"A document that is answered by a person who is on nothing is a document with no owner inside it. That is good, and it is the best thing about it, and it also means that if the answer is wrong there is not one officer in the city who can be disciplined for it except the man who signed it, and he signed it in his own name, and he is not a man anybody in this room thinks is stupid.

"So when that page gets read in four years by a woman who has never met any of us, she will not find a household and she will find a head, and a head who answers for six in his own writing." Petra Ilyin looked at him. "You have made yourself the only possible criminal in a document about this household. I do not know whether that was in the plan. It was in the room when I came down."

"He gives it to me," she said. "The fourth page. I will carry it to Cobb Street on the sixteenth, because I have to go past there anyway to buy a wire, and because the woman who carries a page like this one should be somebody who is not on a roll of any kind in this city, and I am that woman, and there is not a single box on any form in this town that says so."

He gave it to her.

She went up the stairs with it, and the kitchen was empty, and at the bottom of the stair the door at the back of the house was still standing open, and behind it there was a light on, and from where he stood in the dark of the kitchen he could hear a woman who had not left a building in eleven years turning the pages of a book.
