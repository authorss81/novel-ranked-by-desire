# Eight Thousand One Hundred And Thirty-Eight

The notebook was on the kitchen table at a quarter to eight in the morning with a line on it in Beatrix Halm's hand.

07:58. Off the north line. Satchel. Slow.

He read it standing up and then he read it again because there was only one line on it and there had been nine every day for six days.

"One."

"One," said Beatrix Halm. "Six days of nine and then one, and I have been sitting on a tram since six waiting for the ninth and what came was one, and I would give you the rest of my hearing for a reason and I have not got one."

The oven came on at eight. Sun Meiling was on the landing with the copy of the copy and the district copy of Marit Kolb's card where she keeps the things she intends to be cited from.

Then Runa Sato, from the bottom of the stairs, said, "There is a woman coming up."

## The Landing

She came up eleven steps and stopped at the twelfth, because she had been told the household's arrangement, and that was the last thing about her that was like Marit Kolb.

"Ortwin Halb," she said. "Discipline, twelve years, and I have a summons and it is not a demand and I want the difference said out loud at the top of these stairs before anybody decides I am the other one. A demand is dated. A summons is a person in a hallway."

"Say what it is for."

"It is for an account that came off the dead list on the fourth at five o'clock and has no security against it, and I am required to give the broker of record notice that the account is unsecured, and the broker of record is upstairs in a coat, and that is the whole of it, and I have been in three houses this week doing this and in two of them the broker was pleased to see me."

Marguerite Vale came down to the twelfth step and read the summons standing up and gave it back.

"You are not to take a copy of anything," she said.

"I am not to take a copy of anything."

"That was not in it."

"It was not in it," said Ortwin Halb, "and it is in mine, and I have written the second one because I have got four years of this behind me and I would rather produce a summons with a line on it that is not in the standard than produce one that is."

She said it without heat, and then she went up the stairs anyway, and the household had not asked her to.

At the top she went straight to the wall and put her hand flat on the sixth name.

"How many," she said.

"Six names and a head," said Sun Meiling from the kitchen door.

"Then I need six names." Ortwin Halb had a card out and it was not a district card, and it was smaller. "I am entitled to the persons answerable under an unsecured account and the only record of them in this building is on that wall, and I have been up sixty-one steps to look at it and I am going to write it down."

She took the pencil off the shelf by the rail.

It was a two-inch stub and it was not on the wall and it was not hers. Sun Meiling had put it there on the twenty-ninth of last month at about ten o'clock at night.

"Don't," said Sun Meiling.

"This is a pencil."

"That is my pencil and that is my name and you are going to take my name off that wall in your own hand and put it in a guild's book, and I have spent eight days making sure that wall and a file agree with each other so that neither of them is a lie, and you are going to make them disagree by one line in a card the size of my palm."

Ortwin Halb stopped with her hand an inch off the shelf.

"Ma'am, I can do this on the second of July. I am permitted to do this on the second of July. If I do it now it is early, and an early copy is worth more to the office than a copy taken late, and I will not pretend that I do not know that."

Runa Sato came up four steps and put a hand on the rail between them, and did not touch anybody, and her whole weight went onto the rail so that it made a sound in the wall.

Ortwin Halb looked at the arm on the rail and then at the woman it belonged to.

"If you touch me on this landing," she said, in exactly the same voice, "I will write down that a member of a certified household obstructed an officer serving an instrument, and it will be true, and it will be the second line of the summoning note, and your head signed the declaration that makes you one. That is not a threat. It is what I have got instead of a weapon and I have been told I am allowed to use it."

"I am not going to touch you," said Runa Sato, "and I would like it written down that I came up four steps."

Sun Meiling said, "Give me the card."

"I cannot give you the card."

"You can give me the card," said Sun Meiling, "and you will not, because you have twelve years and you have never given one away, and that is correct and I would not want it otherwise.

"And I am going to say the thing out loud now and I would like the man from the discipline side to write it down, because you are here with a pencil and this is the only thing in this house that is worth writing down.

"Take my name. All six of them, off the wall, in ink, in your book, on the seventh of June. It is going to be the same six names you have got in a card you copied two days ago, and there is no reason on earth for you to put my name back in pencil afterwards.

"Write it. I have been in pencil for eight days because I was frightened of being permanent, and being permanent on a wall is not the same thing as being permanent in a book, and I would like to find out which one I was actually afraid of."

Ortwin Halb wrote six names on the card on the landing of the house at Ferrier Street at nine in the morning on the seventh of June and put the pencil back on the shelf, and the lead of it was flat, and Sun Meiling looked at it when she came past.

## What It Costs

Ortwin Halb went down at ten past nine and Marguerite Vale went up to get her coat.

"Four days early, counted from the fourth," she said. "The demand is dated the tenth and I have had it in my coat since half past nine this morning.

"And what came up sixty-one steps was not the demand. It was a woman. Somebody in that building has decided that a broker who lets an entry come off the dead list does not get the grace of a month. It was not the clerk and it was not the woman with the card, and I would like to know whose hand it is, and I am not going to be told until I can afford to be."

"How much of your own standing can answer for it."

"None. The examination is on the six. I cannot secure an account with the thing that is already securing it."

"Then the ten days does not matter," said Lin Hao, "because there is only one answer on the sixth and it is on the seventh."

"It is on the seventh at four o'clock," said Marguerite Vale. "The desk that lodges a standing is open until four and it is the desk in the Exchange and I have used it twice in thirty years and both times I have regretted it inside a week.

"And you are going to ask me for it and I am going to say yes, and I am going to do it in the room with everybody in it, and I want it said out loud that I know exactly what I am putting in my own book this afternoon and that I am doing it on purpose with my own hand."

She took her coat off the back of the chair and did not put it on.

"Eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight," she said. "Say it out loud in the kitchen before you take it out of your coat, because a figure in a room is a figure and a figure in a pocket is a thing you did on your own, and I have watched a man in this house do that once already and I have read what it cost."

## The Kitchen

He said it with his hand in his coat pocket and then he took his hand out of his coat pocket and put it on the table, which was the part he had promised himself he would do.

"Eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight," he said, and did not look at the window. "A floor of eight thousand one hundred and forty-one. A tolerance of three.

"It has not moved a grain in five years. Nothing has been spent out of it and nothing can be, because there is nothing behind it. Before four this afternoon I am going to write it into a chained book at a desk on the ground floor of the Exchange, lodge it against an account that has been dead for thirty years, and put six of you behind it.

"That is the whole of the arithmetic and there is a subtraction on it. If I do not, they take the standing out of her on the tenth, and the examination goes with it, and the certificate issues on the twenty-seventh to a household with a certificate on it and nothing under it.

"Forty of the sixty are gone," he said. "Twenty are left."

The kitchen said nothing for about four seconds, and then three people said things at once, and all three of them were wrong, and he let them finish, because they had all been wrong in front of him for four years and they were going to be wrong now and it was not his job to stop them.

Sun Meiling said, "You decided this before the seventh."

"I decided it at ten past nine this morning standing at the bottom of my own stairs," he said, "and that is four minutes ago, and you are going to have to decide what to do with that."

## The Desk

The desk that lodges a standing was on the ground floor of the Exchange and it was the only desk in that building with a chain on it that was not a book, and the clerk behind it was a young man of about thirty who had his name on a plate and had not been in the guild's service long and who read the form twice before he filled in the top of it.

"This is not the usual thing," he said. "Securities are lodged by the broker. This is a standing lodged by a private person against a broker's account, and it is the second form, and it is the one that takes a head."

"Read me the second line."

The clerk read it, because that is what a clerk does.

"A standing lodged under this head is security against the account named and is answerable for the account named. It is released with the book and not by the person who lodged it, and it may be called by the holder of the book at any valuation, and the person who lodged it has no right of withdrawal between the lodging and the next valuation."

"How long is a valuation."

"A month, unless the book changes hands."

"Then it does not come back," said Lin Hao.

"It does not come back," said the clerk, "and I am obliged to ask you whether you have been told that by a person whose trade it is to know."

"I have been told it by a rank-two broker of this guild at twenty past two this afternoon and she is standing behind me and she has not corrected you."

Marguerite Vale said, "He has not."

The clerk filled it in. ACCOUNT: entered against the broker of record, thirty years, unsecured since the fourth. STANDING LODGED: a figure. HEAD TO WHOM THE STANDING ANSWERS: he said the name of the premises and the number on the slip, because the form wanted a household and a household had one, and the clerk wrote both.

"Date," said the clerk.

He looked at the clock on the wall of the ground floor of the Exchange, which was four minutes slow and had been four minutes slow since before he started cleaning floors in that district.

"Seventh of this month," he said, "and it is twenty to four."

He signed it and the clerk initialled it and the clerk turned the sheet round and read back the whole of it, the way a woman at a counter on the thirtieth of last month had read a declaration back to him across a counter, and Lin Hao stood on the wrong side of a counter for the second time in eight days and had not thought about it once until that afternoon.

## After

The copy went into the chained book in Marguerite Vale's own hand and the chain came round her wrist while she wrote it, which she had never mentioned that she did, and he did not ask, and she saw him not asking and said, "Everyone does it," and went back up.

They came out into the street at about ten past four.

"Say the sentence," said Lin Hao.

"I am not going to say the sentence."

"Marguerite."

"The sentence is that a man with a plate at 8,138 has pledged the whole of it, on an unsecured account, in a chained book, with a woman in this city who has never met him and who will be holding that book in four years' time." She was walking fast and she was not looking at him.

"And that the certificate which arrives in this household on the twenty-seventh of June will issue, if it issues, upon a standing that a stranger can call at any valuation without asking either of us.

"That is the sentence. You have had it since a quarter past two this afternoon and you did not need me to say it and I would rather keep the one thing I am good at, which is arithmetic, and lose the right to be dramatic about it before I am forty."

They walked about half a block.

"It is in the quarterly return as well," she said. "Not today. Today it is in the real book.

"At the next lodgement, which is the last working day of next month, because this month's went in on the twenty-fourth and a quarterly return is not amended, it is added to. A man who cleans washrooms, entered as the security on a thirty-year account, in my dictation, in a public filing. That return has a line for the standing and a line for the value and it has nothing at all for the reason."

"Write one in anyway," said Lin Hao.

"I am not going to write one in," said Marguerite Vale. "I will write what is true and it will look like a man with nothing behind a woman with a rank, and that is exactly what it is, and if you need the word *because* on that page then you have picked the wrong broker and I would rather tell you now than at the next sitting."
