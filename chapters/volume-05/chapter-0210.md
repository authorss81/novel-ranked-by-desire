# Six Names And No Spaces

He asked for four o'clock and got half past two, because the last working day of a month is not a day anybody in that building works to a clock.

The counter on the second floor of the Reading was the same counter and the queue at three o'clock had seven people in it, most of them with the look of a person who has been putting this off since the first of the month and has now run out of month.

He filled it in standing up, against the end of the counter, with a pen off a string.

Six. He wrote the figure in the box the figure went in and he wrote it in the ordinary way, and he did not write it the way a man writes a thing he has argued about.

Then six lines, numbered, one to six, and beside each of them three things, and he wrote all eighteen without stopping once.

One. Ines Duarte. Rank two. Instrument: the head's assumption of members, the nineteenth of this month, and the certification schedule of the fourth, page two.

Office held: the audit division of this city, in which she is a rank-two auditor. He did not have to be told to write that. She wrote it on a card in a district clerk's hand on the twenty-third, and the card is in an intake in Cobb Street, and on the twenty-fifth she read the whole of it out loud on her own floor in the flat voice of a clerk and he was in the room and did not have to hold any of it.

Two and three and four were short. Two lines of post and a band each and a service file or a correction or a schedule, and Tessa Okonkwo's instrument said a clinic in the Vestry that has never been funded and did not say why.

Five. Marguerite Vale. Rank two. Instrument: the Vant's quarterly return, lodged at the Exchange on the twenty-fourth of this month, item one, entered by hand against the broker of record. Office held: rank two of the Vant of Brokers, and the examination of this household is entered against her, and on the death, resignation or replacement of the broker of record the examination passes to the holder.

Six.

He wrote it and it took about four seconds and it was the only one of the eighteen things on that sheet that he had said out loud in a kitchen with a lamp on, in front of the room, two nights before.

Six. Sun Meiling. No standing entered. Instrument: the certification schedule of the fourth of May, page two, and the head's assumption of the nineteenth of this month. No office, no standing and no entitlement in any body corporate, guild, exchange or office of this city.

Then the number of persons of reading age keeping an address at the premises, and he wrote six, because it has been six since the twenty-second of March and it has never once been wrong.

Then the head's name in his own hand, and the date, and the hour, which was two forty-one in the afternoon, and then his signature at the bottom, which took him longer than any other line on the page and which Doyle would have read back to him and did not.

A woman at the counter read the whole of it back to him anyway. She was not Doyle.

"Nine words to check," she said, and went through them, and when she came to the bottom she said, "That is a head's answer for six members and there is one of them with nothing at all in the middle and one of them with a guild on it, and I am not supposed to have opinions."

"Then do not have one."

"I have a professional question," she said. "Line six has no standing entered. It is permitted. It goes in with a tick beside it, and the tick means the head has answered for a member whose standing the office does not hold, and there is one tick on every form of this kind in the city that carries it and I have never seen two."

She put the tick.

"You will get a slip," she said. "Not the form. The slip has the number on it and the number is the household's, and from this afternoon this household has a number in this building that is not yours."

He had been waiting thirty days for that and he had thought it would feel like the nineteenth of May.

It felt smaller than that and colder.

She wrote it out on a slip about two inches by one and gave it to him across the counter with the same hand she read it back with.

The small print at the foot was the only part of that sheet he had not needed.

WHERE THE HEAD'S CERTIFICATE DOES NOT AGREE WITH THE SCHEDULE OF RECORD, THE HEAD SHALL STATE THE FIGURE HE CERTIFIES AND THE REASON, AND THE SCHEDULE OF RECORD STANDS.

Six agrees with a schedule of the fourth of May, and so there was no reason column and he wrote nothing in it, and there is no line on that sheet anywhere that says a man had come within one name of putting his hand on a woman's own account.

In about eleven years a woman in that building will read a head's declaration that agrees with a certificate and will have no way at all of knowing how close the two figures came to not agreeing.

34-6-118, and after it a date, the thirtieth of this month, and a time, and the word LUMPED.

He stood in the street with it and read it three times, because a household's number is not a man's number and a man who reads it twice in a street will start imagining what it is for.

A number is how a thing is found. A number is not a name, and a name is findable, and a number is findable in a way that a name is not, because a name requires somebody to know that it is the right name and a number requires nothing at all.

It is the fastest way into a building in this city that anybody has ever thought of, and he has just gone and picked one up for himself in a queue.

He came up the sixty-one steps at about twenty past three.

Sun Meiling was at the kitchen table with the lamp on and the form was gone off it, and she did not get up when he came in.

"You said you would come up here and tell me it is in," she said.

He put the slip on the table face up.

She read it. She did not have his gift and she read it the way a person reads a thing they intend to be cited from, once, slowly, without going back, because that is a thing she has learned off somebody for five months.

"Thirty-four of six one one eight," she said. "And the thirtieth. And it is not your name on it."

"It is not my name on it."

"That is what I wanted and I am not going to thank you for it," said Sun Meiling, "because it is not a gift, it is the receipt, and a receipt is not a favour."

Then she went up.

He followed her up about two minutes later because he wanted to see it in the dark, and she was standing at the bottom of the last six steps with her back to the stair rail, which is where she has stood since the fifteenth of April.

The light came across the stair window and stopped about two inches above the seventh row, where it had stopped every evening since the thirtieth of April, and her name was in the two inches of dark under it in pencil, in her hand, with the twenty-ninth of this month under it and three lines under that.

He did not read it. He had read it three times the night before and it said the same thing it had said then, and a man who goes up sixty-one steps to look at a sentence he has got by heart is a man who has come up here to be seen.

Above the rows, in a heavier line, THE HEAD OF THE HOUSEHOLD, and under it a name in a man's hand dated the twelfth of this month.

Six names. One head. No spaces anywhere on that wall for the first time since the second of this month.

"You cannot read that from the bottom of the stairs," he said.

"No," said Sun Meiling. "You cannot, and neither can a district clerk, and in four days a woman with a card is going to stand six feet off that wall at about ten past seven in the evening and she is going to have to ask somebody to hold a lamp, or she is going to have to come closer, and either way she is going to read it.

"And that is going to be the first time in nineteen years that she has had to ask. She will write it on the card. She will write the hour and the person asked and the number of names and the number of spaces, and the number of spaces will be nil, and there will not be one column on that card for the reason, and she will want to know the reason and I am going to tell her nothing at all.

"That is what I have bought and it is not a house and it is not protection and it is not a row any more. It is a line of pencil in the dark and a woman who has to come closer."

She put her hand flat on the wall four inches below it.

"Thirty-three of the sixty are gone," she said. "Twenty-seven are left. Twenty-seven days to a certificate with six members on page two of it, and one of them is me, and I did not have a say, and I asked for the row, and those are both of those things at once and they are going to stay both of those things at once."

Then she said, "Do not come up here again tonight. Come up tomorrow night when the light is the same and it has been the same every night since the thirtieth of April, and I will want it to be an ordinary evening."

She went down.

He went to work at seven and did the second floor and the third and the fourth and the fifth and the sixth, and he was twenty minutes late, and nothing in this city was ever going to write down the hour he arrived.

In an office two floors above that building, on the last working day of the month, a woman who had put his premises on a sheet on the twenty-first read a slip with a number on it and a tick beside the sixth line and then, because the rule she worked under was that an officer does not certify a household she is a member of, opened a second book.

It had a name in it. She read it twice, and the second time she read it the way a person reads a document they intend to be cited from, and then she wrote, in the third column, four words that had never been written in that building about a person who worked in it.

SHE IS ONE OF OURS.
