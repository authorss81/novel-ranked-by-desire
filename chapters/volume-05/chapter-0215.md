# Eleven Years Of It

She came up the sixty-one steps at about half past eight in the morning on the fifth and she had not been able to walk up them without stopping on the fourth, which is what happens to a body for eleven years and which is now finished, and she stood in the kitchen doorway and said the household's own figure back to them before anybody had put anything on the table.

"Thirty-eight of the sixty are gone," she said. "Twenty-two are left. Twenty-two days to a certificate, and a certificate you have not got, and the rest of it is going to be said in this kitchen now, because there are seven women in this room and one of them has been in this building for longer than any of you has."

There were seven women in the kitchen. Six of them kept a notebook. The seventh had not kept one since the twenty-ninth of last month and had been written into a guild's month book in ink on the sixth.

Marguerite Vale came in behind her with her coat still on and did not sit down.

"Say the account," said Lin Hao.

"I have said it to one person and I am going to say it to eight and then I am never going to say it again while I am alive." She put one hand flat on the table. "It was entered against the broker of record in the year the Veil broke. Thirty years.

"It has never been discharged and it has never been written off. Nineteen years of it were carried on money that stopped coming, and in the eleventh year I secured it against a person instead, because I could not pay it and could not write it off and the guild had stopped carrying it.

"The person was Petra Ilyin. She came up here eleven years ago to collect it and I made her swear to hold, and she held, and I let her."

The room went through about four different kinds of quiet in about nine seconds.

"Eleven years," said Runa Sato.

"Eleven years."

"In this building."

"In this building. Four times, that I know of. She has not been on the roll and she has not been in the notebook and she has not been on any list in this house at any time, because a person inside an obligation cannot also be a person inside a household, and I made that decision eleven years ago in a room with not another person in it to consult."

"You had an officer of the Vant living in the house," said Ines Duarte, "for eleven years, and none of us knew, and the head of the household certified six members on the thirtieth of last month."

"Yes," said Marguerite Vale.

Nadia Ferrous put her hand up, actually put it up, the way she had at a counter in Cordwain four years ago.

"Where does this sit," said Nadia Ferrous. "On the load order. Bottom to top. Because Runa did it on the twenty-sixth and it went book, examination, broker, broker's rank two, head, six members, and this thing is not on any of those rungs and I would like to know what I have been carrying."

"It sits under all of it," said Marguerite Vale, "and it sits in me, and it is not on the chain at all. The chain is a book and an examination and a book that another woman buys. That is a thirty-year entry and it is in my own name and I have never been able to discharge it and there is no rung in this house for it."

## The Tenth

"It came off the dead list on the fourth at about five o'clock in the afternoon," she said, "and I released the oath myself, in the real book, with the pen chained to it, and the security against a live account is nothing, and a live unsecured account in the senior broker's own name is the one thing in this guild that does not sit still and wait for the sitting.

"They will require security. That is not a threat, it is a form, and the form has a date on it and the date is the tenth, because a monthly call is on the first and a demand on an account that has come off the dead list is due before the next one."

"Then give them something," said Sun Meiling.

"There is nothing in this house to give them." She said it evenly. "The examination is entered against me and it is on six of you. I cannot secure an account with the examination that is already securing it. That is the whole of the trap and it was built by me on the twenty-fourth of last month and I did not see it until about ten o'clock on the second."

"You could give them the book," said Beatrix Halm.

"I have four books," said Marguerite Vale. "One of them is the one they are about to take off me in four years."

She turned round.

"There is one standing in this house and it is not mine and it is not any of yours. It is a plate at eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight with a tolerance of three, it has not moved a grain in five years, and it is the head of a household of six by an assumption signed on a counter on the nineteenth of last month."

Nothing in that kitchen said anything.

"They will ask for it," said Marguerite Vale. "Not today. Today they will do what they always do, which is nothing, and write nothing, and wait, and on the tenth there will be a demand with a date on it and it will want an instrument, and the instrument will have to be a standing, because there is no instrument in Ostara for anything else.

"And I am not going to ask any of you for a figure," she said, "and I want that said out loud in this kitchen while there are seven women in it, because I have already taken one thing off a person in this building that had been hers for eleven years and I did not have to be asked, and I am not doing it twice."

## After

Petra Ilyin took the cup Nadia Ferrous put in front of her and did not drink from it.

"I want to say one thing and then I will go and be a person somewhere," she said. "I have been an officer of that guild for eleven years and I did not know I was in this building's house.

"I was on a landing four times in eleven years and I came up sixty-one steps and I went down them, and the whole time I thought I was coming to collect from a woman in a building and not from a household with six members on a roll and a certificate arriving in twenty-two days.

"If anybody in this room has been waiting for me to be a woman who has been wronged, I would like to get that out of the air before it settles. I was not wronged. I was owed a discharge and I did not ask for it because an officer does not ask, and that is the same mistake six of you have been making for a month in the other direction."

She put the cup down without having drunk from it.

"The account is yours," she said, to Marguerite Vale, "and it has been yours for thirty years and I have been the reason you could stop looking at it. As of yesterday evening that has stopped and I am not sorry and I am not grateful either, and I want it observed that I have not asked you once what the account is for."

Marguerite Vale looked at her for a long moment and then said, "No. You have not."

"I have not," said Petra Ilyin, "and I am going to keep not asking, and you are going to have to decide on your own whether that is a kindness or a debt, and I have decided it is neither, it is an ordinary week and I am not going to be dramatic about it."

Runa Sato pushed her chair back.

"Right," she said. "Ten days is not ten days, it is three, and it is the seventh tomorrow and we are going to be asked for a standing. I am the one that carries things in this house and I have been told so by six women in a kitchen and I have never once been able to do anything about it, and I would like to say the thing out loud before anybody else does.

"His plate is the standing. There is no second answer and there is no clever answer, and if we do not lodge it they will take it out of the broker on the tenth and they will take it out of the broker by removing her from four books and the examination will go with her and the certificate will issue on the twenty-seventh to a household with a certificate and no standing under it."

"And if we do lodge it," said Tessa Okonkwo, from the back, where she had been standing for about four minutes, "then a man who has never in his life put anything at risk for anybody in this house puts the only thing he has at risk for six of them, and the margin on it is three, and I would like everybody to look at the figure three before we decide anything else this week."

"Three," said Runa Sato. "It has been three since the seventh of April and it has been three since the fourth of May and it is going to be three on the twenty-seventh of June."

The kitchen went quiet, and everybody in it was doing the same sum, and Sun Meiling came down four steps off the landing and put herself in the middle of it.

"No," she said. "Not like that."

"Sun—"

"I said no to something in this room eight days ago and I was right about it, and it has cost me the only thing I owned, and I am not standing here watching the last of it go up a ladder in a different building." She had the copy of the copy under her arm again.

"The certificate is dated the twenty-seventh. The schedule goes up on the twenty-sixth because the Office does not sit on the twenty-seventh of a month. If his plate is against a guild's book on the seventh, then on the twenty-seventh of June a certificate issues on a standing that a stranger can call.

"That is the sentence I want written down and I want it written where I can reach it. We have spent a month moving numbers between documents to make this household safe. We are about to move the only number in it into a book that a woman we have never met is going to buy."

She looked at him.

"You are going to do it anyway," she said, "because you are going to decide that it is cheaper than letting it be taken out of Marguerite, and that is a real argument and I have no answer to it. I am telling you that I know the shape of it and that I am not going to be angry in the third person about it this time."
