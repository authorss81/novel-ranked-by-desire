# The Roll Comes Down

The envelope came up the sixty-one steps at about eleven on the nineteenth of June with a guild's mark on it and it sat on the kitchen table face down under a salt dish until twenty past two in the afternoon, because everybody in this house had walked past it all morning and not one of them had turned it over, and the reason for that was that the last three things to arrive at that table had each cost somebody in this room something.

Sun Meiling turned it over.

"That is a valuation sheet," she said. "Assessment side. That is Hanne Voog's desk and Hanne Voog came up these steps on the first of this month and copied six names off a wall into a guild's book and said the figure out loud on a landing because she is frightened of saying numbers in rooms."

She read it standing up. It took her about half a minute and she read it twice, and the second time was slower, and then she put it on the table and did not sit down.

"They have valued it early," she said. "That is not allowed and they have done it anyway, and I would like everybody to understand what that means before anybody gets angry, because it means the assessment side cannot carry a security into a valuation unpriced, and unpriced is exactly what it would have been if not one person in this house had answered the sheet they sent on the first with a description."

## The Description

Marguerite Vale read it in about eleven seconds and put it down and said, "It is correct and I am going to sign it and I am going to do it now, in this kitchen, in front of all of you, because in about four years the person who has to defend this description will not be me."

"Which part are you not going to defend."

"The reason," said Marguerite Vale. "It does not ask for one. It asks for what the standing secures, and the standing secures a household, and a household is six named persons, and I am going to write their names on a guild's sheet in my own hand and hand it down at four this afternoon, and the whole of it will be about ninety seconds and it is exactly what I priced at a window in four minutes on the sixteenth of last month."

She wrote it standing at the end of the table, in ink, with the dates at the top in the ordinary way, and she did not look up while she did it.

Hanne Voog's sheet said that a standing lodged on the seventh of June under the second form at the Exchange against an unsecured account of thirty years is secured at the figure of the head, and the figure of the head is eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight, and the yield is a word said four times a year by the holder of the book, and the persons answerable are as follows.

Six names.

"Read them out," said Sun Meiling.

"No."

"They are on this table in your handwriting and they were on a landing in your handwriting on the first and you said them out loud on the stair because you were frightened," said Sun Meiling. "Read them out."

Marguerite Vale read six names out loud in a kitchen, in order, in the flat voice of a woman entering a production, and the last one she read was her own, and she put the pen down and capped it.

## The Anger

"That is two," said Sun Meiling.

She had not raised her voice in five volumes and she did not raise it now.

"That is two documents in eight days and both of them name the people who sleep on this floor, and neither of them was asked for by anybody in this house, and both of them are in offices that make complete versions of this household and then act on them.

One came off an index and one came off a book and they were made by two different institutions and they were made for the same reason, and the reason is that a household with a plate on it is an asset that can be described.

"And I want to say the part that is mine."

She spread both hands on the table and left them there.

"On the seventh of this month I got a chair and stood on that landing and made a wall and a file agree with each other line for line, so that neither of them was a lie, and I did it for eight days and it was the best work I have done since I came into this house, and I did it because I believed that a thing which agrees with itself cannot be used against it.

"Eleven days ago Marit Kolb came up these stairs at ten past seven at night and read a wall and then asked the one question a district officer is not supposed to ask, and she asked it because a roll that agrees with a sworn answer is a copy, and a copy can be produced out of a file by anybody holding the file. I wrote it down that night. I have it in my own book.

"I built it," said Sun Meiling. "I built the thing that made them able to do this, and I built it out of the best idea I have had in four years, and I have been sitting at this table for nine days watching one institution after another walk through a door I made sure was unlocked."

## What She Does

He got up. He did not get up to say anything clever and he got up because she was standing at the end of a table with the whole house behind her and he had been the instrument of everything she was describing, and because the thing he wanted, in the only terms he had ever had for wanting anything, was for the room to be different in about nine seconds.

"Sun Meiling. Sit down."

"Sit down," said Sun Meiling. "There is a wall at the top of this stair with a nail in it and a roll on the nail, and it has got six names and a head's line and a seventh row in pencil in my own hand, and it is the only document in this city that is a household, and it is the only one of the three that somebody in this house has to carry up sixty-one steps to get at.

"A roll that lives at the premises is not an instrument and has no force. I learned that on the first of June from a woman of nineteen years who copied it into a book and told me so while she was doing it.

"So I am going to take it down off the wall and I am going to cut it at the ruled lines, and there is going to be a piece of paper with one name on it in each of seven pockets in this house, and there is going to be no roll."

"Sun Meiling—"

"The Office has the index," said Sun Meiling. "You told this kitchen on the ninth that the extract off the division's own index is the only complete version of this record in the city and it carries a standing on the foot of it. The Office has had the complete version since the twenty-first of May. The guild has had six names since the first of this month. The district has got a return with a blank on it.

"There are three complete versions of this household in this city and I have been standing in the middle of them for five weeks making sure they agreed with each other, and they agreed with each other, and it did not help, because three copies that agree are one document in three places.

"And the moment I take that roll off that wall there is not one single copy of this household in this city that anybody in this house controls," she said, "and that is the first thing I have wanted since I came into this building, and I am sorry."

She went up the sixty-one steps with her coat on and came back with a roll and put it flat on the kitchen table and squared it to the edge, and then she took a knife out of the drawer and cut it at the ruled lines, and it took her about eleven seconds, and she did not do it in a hurry and she did not do it badly.

Seven pieces. She gave the head line to him without a word, because it had his name and his premises and the number on it.

She kept one.

"The seventh row is in pencil and it is mine and it says what I have been since the twenty-ninth of last month, and I am taking it out of the middle of a roll and putting it in my own pocket.

"Twelve days ago I got that name into a guild's book in ink in a card the size of my palm, and as of this afternoon it is a piece of pencil in a coat, and I did that to it on purpose and I would do it again."

She went round the table and put one piece of paper in front of each of them, and she put the last one in front of Marguerite Vale.

Marguerite Vale looked at it for about four seconds and said, "That is my name and it is on a piece of paper in my own kitchen and it has been in three books since the first of June, and I would like to know, Sun Meiling, whether you have any idea what you have just done to the only version of this household that anybody here can hold in a hand."

"Yes," said Sun Meiling. "I have done it to all of them. Including mine."

Nadia Ferrous had not moved from her chair and when it was over she opened her notebook, and shut it again, and opened it, and said one thing, and it was the only thing she said in the whole of the afternoon.

"I have kept six of them for four years," she said. "That is what six means in this house. Six notebooks, six copies, six times somebody in this room wrote a thing down and somebody else read it back. And I have been putting them on this table and saying *which list are you on*, and I have been doing it because I thought the lists were the problem.

"The lists were never the problem. The lists were the only thing in this city that belonged to us. I want that in a book tomorrow with my name on it and with the word wrong in it, because I am the person who said it, and because a thing said and then taken back is worth about a quarter of what it is worth written down."

She wrote it down herself, in the end. She did not ask anybody to check it.

The kitchen was extremely quiet, and out on the landing the nail was still in the wall, and the roll was not there, and nothing in the world had changed except that a thing which had been true twice was now true once, and the once was in Cobb Street.
