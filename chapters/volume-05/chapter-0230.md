# The Twenty-Sixth

He went up the sixty-one steps at ten past seven in the morning on the twentieth with his coat on to look at a nail.

There was a clean rectangle on the wall where the roll had been, about nine inches by fourteen, and the paint inside it was very slightly paler than the paint round it, because every other thing on that wall had been repainted at some point and the nail had been there since the twenty-ninth of last month.

A week ago that rectangle had a name in it. It had six names on it and a head's line and a seventh row in pencil, and it had agreed with a file in Cobb Street and with a copy in a guild's month book and with a sworn declaration made at a counter on the thirtieth of May, and it had been the only one of the three that a woman had to climb sixty-one steps for.

He put his thumb in the middle of it and it was cold, and the paint was cold, and there was nothing there at all, and it was the first thing in a month that had made anything in this house lighter.

## Nine Sentences

Ines Duarte came in at half past seven and did not take her coat off and read the position out loud without anybody asking her anything, which she had told this house on the ninth of June she would never do again.

"Fifty-three of the sixty are gone. Seven to the certificate. Eleven to the valuation and three after that to a date in a coat pocket. Six days to the twenty-sixth.

"There are three copies of the household's record in this city. The audit division's index has the complete one and it has carried it since the twenty-first of May. The guild has six names in ink since the first. The district has a return of the third with six names on it and a blank on it.

"There is now a fourth, which is the sworn answer of the fourteenth, in your own name, with six on it, and it is the only one of the four that anybody in this house wrote deliberately.

"And there is not one copy left in this house, which means that from this morning there is not a single thing in Ostara that can be checked against the sworn answer except a Bureau index which was itself corrected on the thirteenth out of a blank line a district officer left, and which I read correctly on the twelfth and said one word to, and that word was seven."

She put the position down on the table like a woman putting down an instrument.

"A head who answers for six with a roll in the room has made a false statement and the roll is the proof and the roll is in this house. A head who answers for six with no roll in the room has made a statement that cannot be checked, and in this city those two things are the same piece of paper, and it is the same piece of paper either way.

"That is the whole of it. I have not said one word about the schedule or the auditor of record or the twenty-seventh, and I am not going to, and I would like it noted that I said nine sentences in a kitchen at half past seven in the morning and every one of them was about a number and none of them was about me."

## The Twenty-Third

Sun Meiling had it out by nine o'clock, because she had been awake since five and she had been doing what she does, which is to hold four things up at once and see which one has a shape.

"Wren Kallo countersigned the correction," she said. "And a countersignature is not a signature, it is a check, and the whole of her division is a room where people look at the history of a document instead of at the document. And on the eighteenth, at about half past three in the morning, she told you at a wall that she has written up what she found in the history of your file, and that it goes to the head of the counter-forensics division on the twenty-third of this month."

"What did she find."

"That every edit ever made to your record is a removal, and that there is not one addition in it in five years." Sun Meiling sat down on the edge of the table. "That is not about the household. That is about you. And when that arrives on a desk it is going to be read by a woman whose entire professional life is the question of who has had access to a record that has been protected, and the answer to that question is on this floor."

"Every woman in this house has had access," said Runa Sato. "And a clerk of the assessment side who has been in that guild nineteen years and copied six names off a wall into ink on the first of this month, and one more on a tram who felt a weight arrive on the seventh and has never once written down what she felt. That is the answer to her question and it took about eleven seconds."

"And one that is on no roll at all," said Beatrix Halm, from the stair. "Because there is a woman at the back of this house who has had access to everything that has ever happened in it for eleven years and has never once written any of it down, and that is either the strongest position in this house or the reason it is going to come apart, and from this morning I cannot tell you which."

## The Hundred And Thirty-Third

Petra Ilyin put the bundle on the table at about ten and took the thirteenth docket out of the top of it and squared it to the edge of the table, and the space at the foot of it was two inches deep and empty.

"Three days," she said. "It goes onto a desk on the twenty-third of this month, and the twenty-third is three days before the schedule goes up, and this is what will be on it."

"The guild does not sit on the twenty-third," said Marguerite Vale. "It is in a book and the book goes to whoever holds it, and I hold it, and I am not going to have the day of my own book explained to me in my own kitchen by a woman who has been an officer for eleven years and discharged for a fortnight."

"I know what day it is," said Petra Ilyin. "I have said it three times because I have watched three people in this house get a day wrong this week and I am not going to be the fourth. It is the twenty-third."

She put two fingers on the empty space.

"You know what goes in here. You have put a hundred and thirty-two of them in and you have never once asked who was producing them, and I have never once been able to ask you, and that was eleven years of the two of us not asking each other a question and we are not going to do it tonight either.

"So here it is without the question. The mark goes in on the first of July, in the real book, in ink, in your own hand, at whatever hour you are in that building, and it is the hundred and thirty-third and it is the last one anybody will ever be able to check against, because after the first of July it will not be a question any more, it will be a history.

"Mark it, or do not, and the plate comes off on the fourth of July either way, because an account that is not produced at a valuation is not an account anybody can answer for, and there is a broker in this house whose examination is entered against it, and there is a signal operator in this house whose hearing is on the morning of the first."

Marguerite Vale took her gloves off and put them on the table and squared the docket to the edge of it herself, which she had never done.

"I will mark it," she said. "At nine in the morning, because the desk is open at nine and I have never once in thirty years done anything to a real book before the building was full."

"I know," said Petra Ilyin.

"I know you know," said Marguerite Vale. "And you are going to come down those sixty-one steps with me and you are going to stand at the bottom of them at ten to nine on the first of July with a bundle under your arm, because you are the only person in this city who is on no roll of any kind and who has a reason to be standing on a pavement at eight in the morning, and I would like somebody there who is not paid by anybody."

Petra Ilyin said, "Yes," and picked the bundle up.

## Six Days

He came in at about nine that night with wet feet and stood in the kitchen, and there was a piece of paper with his name on it in his coat pocket and it was the head line of a roll that had been cut up in a kitchen on the previous afternoon, and there were six days to the twenty-sixth and seven to the twenty-seventh and eleven to the first of July.

Then Runa Sato, who had been counting out loud in that kitchen since the seventh of June because she had said in advance that the count did not have to live in one head, put her hand on the table and did it one last time and got it right without stopping.

"Fifty-three of sixty. Seven to the certificate. Six days to the twenty-sixth, which is the schedule, and the schedule is the thing that goes up, and the certificate is the thing that comes after it, and those are the two days that are going to decide whether this household exists as a household or exists as a number with a page two attached to it."

"That is two days and not one," said Sun Meiling.

"It is two days and not one, and there is nothing in this city that can move either of them, and there is a woman in a division whose finding goes onto a desk on the twenty-third, and a docket in a coat pocket dated the first of July with an empty space at the foot of it, and a clean rectangle of unfaded paint nine inches by fourteen on a wall at the top of sixty-one steps.

"I would like somebody to tell me which of those five things is going to matter."

Nadia Ferrous got her notebook out and shut it, which she had been doing a lot this month, and then opened it again, which she did about twice as often.

"Which list are you on," she said, to the room, in the voice she used about twice a month and had never once liked.

Seven women on the roll. Six of them with an address at Ferrier Street, and six of them with a notebook, and the sixth notebook is hers. One of the seven is in pencil on a seventh row that is now in a coat pocket. One woman is in this house on no roll of any kind and has been since the fourth of June. One woman is the eighth partner, and is on nothing at any time, and has never been on anything.

And one woman is asleep behind a door in a building she has not left in eleven years and is on no list at all, and a district officer left a line blank for her on the third of this month, and that is the reason this entire month is happening.

"Say that again slowly," said Runa Sato, "and I will write it down, because it is the only sentence anybody has said in this kitchen this month that I have not got written somewhere already."

"Say it again slowly," said Nadia Ferrous, "and I will write it down myself," and she did, and it took the whole of the back of a page, and when she had finished she put the notebook on the table face up, which she had done four times in a month now and had never once done before June.
