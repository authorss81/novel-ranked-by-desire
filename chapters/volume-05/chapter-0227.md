# The Space At The Foot

The counter at Cobb Street took the fourth page at twenty past one in the afternoon of the seventeenth. The clerk was not the one from the ninth of June. He read the figure, put a date against it, initialled it, and pushed it under a grille, and gave her back a slip with a number on it and did not read the whole of it back, which she had been told to expect and had told two other people to expect.

"That is the last anybody in this building says about it," she told them at half past four. "There is no copy. It goes into the index this afternoon and the index is the only complete version of it in the world, and there is a figure on it and a head's name and a date, and the woman at the counter did not ask me a single question, and I have been an officer for eleven years and I have never been so frightened of a counter."

Then she went up the sixty-one steps of a different building with a bundle of a hundred and thirty-two dockets in a bag, and a woman on a landing watched her go out of the door at the back of the house and did not say anything, because the door at the back of the house is open and it stays open and she was standing behind it.

## Sixty-One Steps

The guild keeps its rule book on a shelf by the ground-floor counter where anybody may read it, and it has been there for longer than the building and it is the single most boring object in Ostara, and Petra Ilyin read for about forty minutes.

Everything she needed was on the ninth page.

A docket is answered at the foot by the mark of the holder, in ink, in the holder's own hand. A docket that reaches its date without that mark is not produced, because it has not been answered, and an account that is not produced at a valuation goes on the dead list on the fourth day after it, and every security standing against an account comes off it when it goes on the list.

Four days after. Which meant the first of July was the date on the docket and the fourth was the date it went on, and the plate came off on the fourth of July, in a chained book, in a hand that was not his.

She read it twice and then she put her finger on a second thing, which was not on the page at all and which she had been carrying down sixty-one steps in a bag.

The hundred and thirty-two dockets were numbered in ink in the left-hand corner of the foot, under the space for the mark, one number to a month, in sequence, with no gaps, and the last of them was a hundred and thirty-two and was dated the first of this month.

The twelfth was dated the first of July.

And it was not numbered a hundred and thirty-three. It was numbered one hundred and thirty-three, in a different ink, in a smaller hand, above a line that had been drawn under the number as though it had been entered afterwards.

She put her finger on the ink and then she found the thing that finished it, and it was on the back, where a docket carries the month it was issued in a stamp because that is how the guild's run is checked, and the stamp on the back of the twelfth said JUNE, and the stamp on the back of the hundred and thirty-second said June, and the hundred and thirty-second was dated the first of June.

There was a docket dated the first of June in the bundle in her bag.

There was one dated the first of July, issued in June, out of the same run, with a number written in afterwards in a smaller hand.

"Somebody put a month's docket in early," she said to the shelf. "And put it in as though it were the next one, so that the run would read right. And it came out of a drawer with the June dockets in it, which means it was written in this building in June, and it was dated forward into July, and it is the first paper in eleven years that has ever come to me out of order."

## The Corridor

Ortwin Halb was on the second landing and she was not surprised, because Ortwin Halb had been up sixty-one steps of this building at four in the afternoon on the fourth of June with a summons and had gone up them anyway, and Petra Ilyin had been in this building eleven times in eleven years and had never once seen her on a landing.

She said good afternoon, and Halb said good afternoon ma'am, and then Halb said, "You are discharged."

"I am."

"That is not an accusation and I want you to hear it as one, because you have got a bag."

"I have got a bag," said Petra Ilyin, "and I am not producing anything and I have not come to any counter and there is no rule on the ninth page that stops a person reading a book off a shelf, and you can check the ninth page, and I have read it, and I have had it open in front of me for forty minutes and I have not written in it."

"I have not accused you of anything," said Ortwin Halb. "I have known you for eleven years and I have never once accused you of anything and I would like to keep it that way. That is the whole of what I have to say and I am saying it in a corridor instead of at a counter because at a counter it goes in a book and in a corridor it goes in a pair of ears."

She looked at the bag.

"That is a run."

"That is a run of a hundred and thirty-two and a thirteenth."

"Then somebody has made you a thirteenth early," said Ortwin Halb, "and I am going to tell you the one thing I am allowed to tell a person who is not an officer, and then I am going to go back down."

"Say it."

"On the ninth of this month a request came into this building from an office that is not this guild for the production file of an account that is entered against the broker of record and has been on the dead list for eleven years." She said it flatly, the way a woman says a thing she is required to say and has thought about saying.

"It was refused, because the file of a dead-list account is not a production anybody is entitled to, and the refusal was made in about four minutes by a woman at a counter who had never heard of the account in her life and found it funny.

"And I was asked to look at it afterwards and I did, and the request had been made out properly, and it had a form number on it, and it came from the division whose index has held that schedule since the twenty-first of May."

The corridor was very quiet for about four seconds and then Petra Ilyin said, "The ninth," and Ortwin Halb said, "The ninth," and neither of them said the rest of it, which was that two offices had found out about that house inside six hours of each other by two entirely different roads and neither of them had told the other.

"Go home," said Ortwin Halb.

## The Bottom Of The Steps

He was standing at the bottom of the sixty-one steps at about five past four with his coat on and his bag over his shoulder and he had been there for twenty minutes, because he had come to find out what a valuation was, and the Vant was the only building in this city where the word was printed in a rule book that anybody could read.

She came down sixty-one steps with a bag and she was two thirds of the way down before she saw him and she did not stop, which was the first thing that had happened in eleven years that she had been frightened of.

She stopped on the fourth step from the bottom instead, which was not the first thing out of a room, and stood there with the bag in her hand and the door of the building at her back.

"Four things," she said. "I have been in there an hour and a half and I have four things and I am going to say all four before I come down any further, because if I come down to you I will not be able to say them as well.

"A docket is answered at the foot by the mark of the holder, in ink, in the holder's own hand. That is the mark you have seen a hundred and thirty-two times. On the thirteenth there is no mark, and a docket without its mark is not produced, and an account that is not produced at a valuation goes on the dead list on the fourth of that month, and every security standing against it comes off on the day it goes on.

"So there is a date in July that this house is not looking at, and it is not the first, and it is not the twenty-seventh, and on that date the plate comes back to you whether you have had a good month or not.

"The second thing is that the thirteenth was issued in June out of the June run and dated forward into July and numbered in afterwards in a smaller hand, and the run of that account is one a month for eleven years with no gaps, and this is the first paper that has ever come to me out of order.

"The third is that on the ninth, the same morning the notice went up at Cobb Street, somebody outside this guild asked this guild for the production file of that account, and it was refused in four minutes by a woman at a counter who had never heard of it."

"And the fourth," she said, "is that I am not going to say who. Not because I have been asked not to.

Because I have been an officer since I was twenty-two and there is not one thing I have ever seen in a building like this one that I was not told to keep, and I have spent the last eleven years being the instrument that takes a thing off a person, and I am not going to start now by handing a piece of paper round a kitchen table and letting the women of this house look at a hand."

She came down the four steps.

"Fourteen days," she said. "To the first. And then three more, and on the third of July a plate at eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight comes off a thirty-year account and goes back to being a plate, and a woman with a false sworn answer answered against it stops having a broker's tail on his lie, and Beatrix Halm's hearing is on the morning of the first, and I do not know what any of it is for and I have not asked and I am not going to."

She got the last two steps and stood next to him.

"Say something," said Petra Ilyin. "You have been standing there twenty minutes and you came here to ask me a question and I have been talking for two minutes and I have not once asked you anything, and it is the first time in eleven years that has happened to me twice in one day."
