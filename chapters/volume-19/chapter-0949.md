# What Is Still Owed

Four thousand and two hundred and seventy-six was the seventeenth of this one and the twenty-fourth of this one is day four thousand and two hundred and eighty-three, and four thousand and two hundred and eighty-three less four thousand and two hundred and seventy-six is seven, and twenty-four less seventeen is seven by the month count.

## The Last Ordinary Morning

The twenty-fourth of December is a working day and it is the last working day of the year that is not the thirtieth, and the days round it have about four hours of useful light in them, and that office is open between a quarter to ten and half past five on working mornings, five days in seven.

About nine people came up those four flights that morning. Four of them came about a paper and got what they came for inside about six minutes each.

One of them came about a person who would not come, and the row went in with the date and the hour and the words used and nothing under the hour, and it will stand there until that building comes down, and it is the only row in that book with nothing under it and it has been there since the twenty-sixth of August.

At about twenty past four he noticed that the light had gone off that window about half an hour ago and that in fifty minutes that card's hours would be over for the year.

## Twenty Past Four And About Nine Minutes Left

The man who came up at about twenty past four had a piece of paper in his hand and he had it folded, and he did not unfold it, which Lin Hao noticed.

"I want to ask you one thing and I have been standing at the bottom of that stair about nine minutes deciding whether it is a cheek."

"Ask it."

"Is it shut at the turn of the year."

It went about four seconds to arrive, because a thing like that has been standing at the bottom of a stair in a lot of people all December and this was the first one that had come up four flights to say it to the man who wrote the card.

"That is the one thing on that card that is not written," said Lin Hao. "It says a street and it says a door at the head of four flights off a third court and it says the hours, which are a quarter to ten to half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour."

"That is not the same as telling me."

"That is not the same as telling you," he said, "and I am not going to, and I want to say why out loud while you are standing there, because I have made a habit of it and it has cost me more than it has saved me."

## What He Said He Was Not Going To Do

"I have spent this last month working out whether that card is any good," he said, "and I have got about nine minutes of your question left and I am going to answer it in this order. First: the card does not say anything about a turn of the year, and I am not putting a fourth line on it about a turn of the year, because a fourth line is a thing about the turn of the year and I have not decided one."

"That is the whole of your answer?"

"Second," he said. "You are asking me to promise you a thing I have not decided, and I have spent nine years not promising people things I have not decided, and that is the only practice in this room I have never broken."

"You might decide by tomorrow."

"I might. If I do you will not know about it until you come up here and look, and you will be able to tell, because a man who has promised something says four words at a quarter to ten and a man who has not says four words at about twenty past four."

## What The Man In His Building Has Been Telling People

The man put the folded paper back into his coat.

"I will tell you why I am asking," he said. "It is not because I want it shut. There are about nine people on my stair and a woman on the ground floor has been telling all of them since about the ninth that your office is closed over the turn, and she has got it off a man who read it at a counter on the second."

He did not ask to see the paper.

"I am not going to ask you what it says, because you told a man on a stair in this building four days ago that this office does not correct a copy, and if I put it on that table you would have to say something about it and I would have to carry something away from here."

"Give me about nine minutes and I will say something about it, and not that."

"I do not need you to say something about it," said the man. "I have come up four flights to tell you that it is going round, and I have come up four flights about six minutes before your door shuts on the last ordinary morning of the year, and that is not an accident and I am not sorry about it."

He got to the top of the stair and turned round.

"Whatever you have decided about the turn of the year, somebody in this city has already decided it and put it in about nine hands, and one of those hands is mine, and I am telling you that instead of asking you to fix it."

## What The Office Does With A Wrong Copy

"It has been going round since the ninth," said Lin Hao, "which means eleven days that this office knew about it would have cost the same thing and nine days of that was free, and I am not going to argue with you about the days because you are right and I was not listening."

"I am not here about the days."

"I know you are not. You are here about whether that is going to be true in nine days' time and I am telling you the true answer, which is that I do not know and I have not decided and there is a card on that door and what is on it is what is on it."

He said the hours then, out loud, in the ordinary voice, at the top of his own stair on the last ordinary morning of the year, to a man who had come to be told something about a turn.

A quarter to ten to half past five on working mornings. Five days in seven. At no other hour, on working mornings, and the card says nothing whatever about any other day in the year because a card is three lines and this one has been three lines since the thirtieth of December.

"And I am not adding to it," he said, "and I am not taking anything off it, and that piece of paper in your coat stays exactly as wrong as it is, and I am going to want my name off it in about four years because it is on it, and I am not going to be in a position to do anything about that either."

## The Drawer, Which Was Sixty-Two Months Shut

He went back to the table at about half past four and the drawer was where it has been since the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, shut under an instruction with a man's name under it, and it was sixty-two months and three days shut that afternoon.

He looked at it for about four seconds.

There is no hour of his working day in which he could open it, and there has not been for five years, and he has not known what is in it since the middle of the seventh year, and he is going to go on not knowing, and that is the arrangement and it is not a hardship and it is the same arrangement as a woman who is on no roll reading nine hundred names a week into a stone four hundred yards off.

He did not put a hand on it, and he went back to the table and wrote nothing down about the drawer or about the turn of the year or about a woman on a ground floor, because there are three things in that afternoon that have happened and none of the three of them is going into a book with a date and an hour at the head of every line.

## What He Went Down Four Flights To Find Out

He went out onto that landing at about twenty to five to look at a piece of card.

The street along the top was the only line of the three that had nothing wrong with it anywhere, and both top corners of the second one were soft with the ink gone out of the fibres, and the three nails were straight and had always been straight, and the holes round them were about a quarter of an inch wider than they had been in November.

It was a piece of stiff grey card about the length of a forearm, on a door, in a stairwell, four flights up, with three lines on it and not one name.

It would still be there on the thirtieth, when the rail broke and the forty-eighth sheet went up on the same two nails as the forty-seventh, and on the morning after that if not one person did anything about it.

He went in and said the hours out loud to four people on that stair and then got the book down at half past five and carried it four flights and about four hundred yards and it was not upstairs overnight.

Four thousand and two hundred and eighty-three days. Less four thousand and two hundred and seventy-six, the seventeenth of this one, is seven, and a man went up four flights on the last ordinary morning of the year to ask whether a door would be there after the turn, and the man who wrote the card had about nine minutes and did not answer him, and a woman on a ground floor had been telling about nine people for about a fortnight that it would not.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September, is eighty-four, and the forty-seventh sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last eighty-four of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of December, which is six days after this one.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand eight hundred and sixty-three days, which are sixty-two months and three days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
