# The Door Used By Everybody

Four thousand and two hundred and twenty was the twenty-first of this one and the twenty-eighth of this one is day four thousand and two hundred and twenty-seven, and four thousand and two hundred and twenty-seven less four thousand and two hundred and twenty is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven by the month count.

## Two People Up Four Flights

She came up at about ten past ten with a man a pace behind her, and she had not come up that stair before, and he had, and both of those were visible in that room inside about four seconds.

The way up had cost her something and she had not tried to hide what it was.

"You do not have to do this," she said to him on the second landing, loud enough to carry down, and he had said something back that the stair gave away, and she said, "Then come up and say that to a man in a white plate, and then it is a fact and not an argument."

He came up.

There was frost on the third-floor sills that morning and about nine people had been up that stair since a quarter to ten, and not one of them looked at the two of them, which is what four flights does for a person who does not belong on them.

## The Question She Carried Up There

She put it on the table the way a person puts down a parcel they have carried a long way and is finished with.

"It is about my husband," she said. "I want to know whether there is anything about him in this office at all. He was here in the spring and I have not been able to ask, and he would not come, and I have got tired of waiting for him to be a different man."

Then she turned and said to him, not to the room: "Say the thing."

He said nothing. He had a hat in both hands and his coat on and he had not looked at the book and had not looked at her.

"That is why I brought you up," she said. "Because if I ask about him and you are not in the room, then he is right about it for the rest of his life."

Wenna Raut had her pen down and had not written anything, which Lin Hao noticed and was grateful for in a way he had not time to look at.

## The Rule Has A Shape It Was Not Built For

"It is the eighteenth of August," he said. "An answer about a person is given in this room once, with the person it is about in the room, and there is no exception for anybody and there has never been an exception for the dead."

"That is what I read on a wall."

"That is what is on a wall and it is right, and you read it correctly."

"Then it is answered," she said, and she turned to him. "It is answered and you are standing here. That is the whole of what I came up for."

"That is not what the rule says."

"It is what it means."

"It says the person is in the room," said Lin Hao. "It does not say the person asked."

He put his hands into his coat because that was the only thing he had done all morning that he had planned.

"Since the spring I have been standing in this room telling people we will not send anybody to a house, and that we keep no list of who comes, and that neither of those two things can be written down anywhere at all. I said it in a passage in August with about nine people going past and the clerk told me what it would cost before I had finished the sentence."

"And today somebody has done it for us."

"Somebody has done it for us," he said, "at a cost of two landings and forty minutes and her own morning, and it worked, and I want you both to hear that it worked."

## He Asks The Man Instead

Then he did the thing the practice has no place for.

"Do you want the answer," he said to the man.

It went quiet in that room in a way it does not usually go quiet, because there were about four people on that stair at that moment and every one of them had stopped on the second landing, which is what people do.

"I did not ask to come up," said the man.

"I know you did not. I am asking you now. You may say no, and I will enter that you said no, and I will not look at her while you are saying it."

The clerk's pen was about an inch off the page and stayed there.

"Say it again," said the man.

"I am asking whether you want the answer to the question your wife has just asked about you."

"No," said the man.

Then he said the rest of it, and he said it to the floor, and nobody in that room stopped him.

"I have been to this office twice. Once in the spring, and once at two in the morning in the eighth month, because a piece of paper told me the hours and I went on the hours that were on it and got here in the dark to a shut door."

"You were angry."

"I have not been angry once," said the man. "I have been embarrassed, which is worse, and I have been embarrassed with her at home. I came up this morning because she asked me in a kitchen and I could not find a way of saying no that did not sound like that."

"So you said yes."

"I said I would come and get it over with," said the man, "and this is what getting it over with looks like."

## What The Clerk Asked Him Before She Wrote Anything

"You will want to know what I am going to write," said Wenna Raut, to him, with the pen still an inch off the page.

"Write what they said."

"I have four possibilities and about two hours to choose between them, and I am going to choose the one that makes this office look worst, and you are going to let me, and I want you to say so out loud now while the two of them are still in the room."

"Write the worst one."

"I am going to write that an officer of this office offered a man standing at that table a way of refusing an answer that had been asked about him, at his own asking, which is not in any practice and has never been in any practice, and that the man refused it in front of the person who asked."

"That is accurate."

"It is accurate and it is the third thing this year that has come out of this room because you said it out loud instead of doing it quietly." She put the pen down on the page and left it there. "I am not saying that as a complaint. I am saying it so that you know I noticed, because the other two are in that book and the other two have made it a harder room to work in than it needs to be."

## What Went In The Row

The clerk entered it in the words used, which is the practice: a question asked by one person, and an answer refused at the asking of another, with a date at the head of it and an hour against the date, and nothing underneath that hour that either of them had not said out loud in that room.

He said, before she went, what he was not going to do, because a thing decided quietly in a room is decided by whoever wants it decided.

"I am not going to tell you what this office holds about your husband. I am not going to tell you that it holds nothing. And I am not going to put one line in that book saying that he was here because he was brought."

"That last one is a kindness."

"That last one is the only thing I have got for you this morning, and it cost me the only thing I had, and I would rather have had the morning."

She said thank you and he said there was nothing to thank him for, which is the sentence that room says about four times a fortnight, and she said that was correct and that she was not coming up again.

He walked them to the door because walking them to the door was four flights and neither of them had asked for that.

"You will come back in the spring," she said from the top of the last flight, not turning round.

"No," he said.

"That is what I told you I would say to him and I want it on the record that I said it first." She got her hand on the rail. "And you are wrong about one thing and I have let you have it all morning because you were being decent at it."

"Tell me the one thing."

"You told me she would not come again." She went down two steps. "She will not. That part is mine to say and not yours, and I will not be here next spring, and there will not be a person at that counter who knows that you were kind about it."

## Going Down The Second Landing

They went down together because there was only one way down and about nine people were going up it.

At the second landing the woman got about four steps ahead of him and stopped, and he did not stop, and she said it to his back in a voice that carried about nine feet and no further.

"You could have answered me."

"He said no," said Lin Hao.

"He said no in a doorway, because you gave him a doorway to say it in. I have been asking him in a kitchen for two days and there was no doorway in a kitchen."

Then they went out into the street together and that was the end of it, and about four people came up those four flights in the hour after that and not one of them knew any of it had been in the room.

## What Is Left Of The Twenty-Eighth

The book went down at half past five by his own hands and it was not upstairs overnight, and at about ten past five a man came up with a slip about a division and had an answer inside about six minutes, because he had come at the right hour about a paper.

He noticed the other thing at about twenty to five, standing in the doorway with the light behind him.

The light went off that street at about ten past four now, and the card on that door runs to half past five, and for the last fortnight of that month there had been about an hour and a quarter at the end of every working day in which that door was open and that card could not be read by anybody standing in front of it.

Nothing had been done about it and there was nothing on that card that could be done about it, and he had about a week to decide whether he was going to stand in that doorway in the dark four times a night and say it out loud himself.

Four thousand and two hundred and twenty-seven days. Less four thousand and two hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of this one, is seven, and a woman carried a man up four flights on the strength of a sentence on a wall four hundred yards off, and this office gave him the one thing it has never given anybody, which is a doorway in which to refuse.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September, is twenty-eight, and the forty-seventh sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty-eight of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of December.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand eight hundred and seven days, which is sixty months and seven days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
