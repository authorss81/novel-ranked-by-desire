# The Board Bare With Paste

Four thousand and two hundred and sixty-nine was the tenth of this one and the seventeenth of this one is day four thousand and two hundred and seventy-six, and four thousand and two hundred and seventy-six less four thousand and two hundred and sixty-nine is seven, and seventeen less ten is seven by the month count.

## Half Past Three And Getting Dark Early

He came down the hill at about half past three on the seventeenth, which is an hour earlier than he came in September, because the light goes off that kerb at about four in December and there was not going to be time for it if he started at four.

The board stands nine feet back from the kerb and it has two ruled squares on it and it has had them for about nine years, and about once a month somebody comes up that hill and puts a month's form in the upper one, and the form goes up on the seventh and it stays up until the next one goes over it.

There is no seventh between the twenty-eighth of October and the thirty-first of December.

## What Was On The Rail In September

The rail of that board is four inches above the upper square and it has a lip on it about two inches deep for holding things that are not the form.

In September there had been a sheet of paper on that rail, folded once, held on with two drawing pins that were not the pins that came with it, with writing on the outside of it, about the length of a forearm.

The rail was bare and about four inches of it had never been covered by anything at all in about nine years, and the other two-thirds had been.

Under it, along the top batten, there was a strip of board about the length of a forearm and about two inches wide where the wood had been covered in paste and the paper was not, and the paste along it had gone from tacky to hard and had gone grey at the edges and had begun to lift at one corner in a curl about the size of a thumbnail.

Somebody had taken that sheet down off that rail. It was not the wind, because the wind takes the top corner first and the bottom corner second and it does not leave the paste behind in a rectangle.

The two ruled squares were empty. The upper one and the lower one.

Under the lower batten her sheet was held against the wood where it has been for about nine years, with a date at the top of it and a ruled pencil line across the foot of it in her own hand, and it had not moved.

## The Boy With The Paste Brush

There was a boy at that board with a paste brush.

He had it in his right hand and he had not been using it. He was standing about two feet off with his head on one side looking at the top batten, and there was grey paste on the ferrule of the brush and there had been grey paste on the ferrule of that brush every time Lin Hao had come down that hill since the ninth of March.

Neither of them said anything.

There was nothing in the world at that corner to say. He wanted to ask a boy with a paste brush four things and he had been wanting to ask him three of them since the ninth of March and had not asked one of them, because a question from a man in a white plate at that kerb is the first thing anybody has ever asked anybody there.

He asked him none of the four, and he had gone down that hill about nine times in nine years, and there is not one thing anybody has ever said to that boy at that corner and there was not going to be one this afternoon.

The boy went at the curl of paste with the flat of the brush handle and it came off in one piece and he put it in his coat pocket, and then he looked along the batten for about four seconds and then he went down the hill with the brush.

He goes up that hill about once a month for the board and about four times a month for the gutter and he has been doing it since before the card went on the door, and there is no arrangement anywhere in this city by which a man in a white plate at that kerb is entitled to ask a boy with a paste brush where he was on the second of September.

## Four Inches Of Dressed Stone

The stone is four inches deep and about a yard long and it has about sixty words cut into it in seven hands.

The seventh of them stops in the middle, the way it stopped in March and the way it stopped in June, and what is cut after it is what has been cut after it for years, which is a dressed face with nothing on it.

He went along it without stopping, which is what he has done every time, and he did not go hunting for the two words that are cut hard and small and well clear of the other seven, because that is a question about who cut them and why and he decided about nine years ago not to ask it and has not asked it since.

## The Thing He Came Down With

He had come down that hill in September with a question and had gone up without an answer. He had come down it in June and in July and in October with no question at all and had told a room he had no question.

This time he came down it with something to put there.

He stood in front of that board at about ten past four with about nine minutes of light left in it and worked out, standing there, that he was going to write the three lines on a piece of paper and gum it to that rail, which would have put an address in a public place at the foot of a hill where about four hundred people a week go past it.

It would have taken about nine minutes.

It would have been the second copy of three lines in this city that anybody had ever made, and he would have made it himself. There would have been a rail at the bottom of a hill with the hours on it where anybody could stand and read them in any weather, which is the exact thing the card on that door four flights up is for. That one has three nails and a date. This one would have had a thumbtack and a boy with a paste brush.

He did not do it.

The reason was not a rule. He had a rule about the card and a rule about the roll and a rule about the two places where people cannot read it, and not one of them had anything to do with a board.

The reason was that a rail is for things people take down.

## What Was Left Of The Light

He stood there until the light went, which took about four minutes, and about four people came up the hill in those four minutes and went past that board without looking at it.

One of them was going to the board itself, and found the two squares empty, and stood at it for about nine seconds, and went on. That is what that board is for and he had understood it in September and had been angry about it, and standing there in the dark on the seventeenth of December he found that he was not angry about it any more.

When the stone was a shape and the board was a shape, he looked at the bare strip along the top batten and at the curl of grey paste in the boy's coat pocket about a quarter of a mile away, and he went up the hill.

## Back Up Four Flights

He went up at about ten past four and came in at about half past with grit on his boots, and told Wenna Raut standing, with the door open.

"The sheet that was on the rail of that board in September has been taken down. The rail is bare and the paste is on the batten and the squares are empty and her sheet has not moved."

"Then that is a thing that happened at a board."

"That is a thing that happened at a board that has my street in it, possibly, on the outside of a piece of paper I never read, and I am not going to find out."

"You are not going to find out."

"I am not going to find out," he said, "and the reason is that finding out means asking a question about a person in this city, and this office refused to send one person to one house in the fourth month of the year before last, and it kept that refusal in a book, and I am not going round it at the foot of a hill with a lamp."

He put his hands in his coat.

"And I want to say the other thing before I stop, because I stood down there for about nine minutes with a piece of paper in my head."

"Say it."

"I am going to say I nearly put those three lines on that rail. And I did not, and the reason is that a board is a place things get taken down off, and that is all the reason there is, and it is not a good enough reason and I did it anyway."

Four thousand and two hundred and seventy-six days. Less four thousand and two hundred and sixty-nine, the tenth of this one, is seven, and a board at the foot of a hill that had had a sheet of paper on it since September had a bare rail and a strip of grey paste on the top batten, and a boy with a paste brush went down the hill with a curl of it in his coat, and not one question was asked at that corner.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September, is seventy-seven, and the forty-seventh sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-seven of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of December.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand eight hundred and fifty-six days, which are sixty-one months and twenty-six days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
