# Corrected Wrong Twice

Four thousand and two hundred and six was the seventh of this one and the fourteenth of this one is day four thousand and two hundred and thirteen, and four thousand and two hundred and thirteen less four thousand and two hundred and six is seven, and fourteen less seven is seven by the month count.

## A Paper That Is Not A Copy Of Anything

She came up those four flights at about eleven on the fourteenth with a paper in a frame, which is not a thing a person carries about loose.

"You are the man on the card."

"That depends on what you have come about, and I can see from about nine feet away that it is not the hours."

"I have come about the hours." She held it where he could see it without touching it. "And I know you are going to tell me you do not correct copies. I have got that from a wall four hundred yards off and I have read it about nine times and I have come anyway, because the paper in that frame is not a copy of your card. It is a correction of your card."

## What Is Wrong With It In The Other Direction

He looked at it standing up, on her side of the table, with his hands in his coat.

Three hands on it. Three different inks. The first one was the hours, and the second one had been written in beside them with a bracket, and the third one had been written in underneath with a bracket, and there were two lines under the second one that had gone through a copy of the third one because somebody had not thrown the first out.

"That is three versions of the same seven words," he said. "It came off a form, and then a clerk at a counter corrected it, and then somebody else corrected the clerk."

"The clerk's one is right."

"It is right at one end. Look at the other end of it. That is half past six."

"The hours say half past six."

"The hours on the door say half past five and they have said half past five since the thirtieth of December of last year, and this piece of paper has been wrong twice, and the second time it was wrong by half an hour in a direction that could not have been guessed from the first one."

## Four Seconds, And The Reason Again

He said no in about four seconds.

"I am not going to say no about the paper," she said quickly. "I am going to say it in the ordinary voice and I am going to say the reason after it, because there are four people on that stair and I have been up two of them since June and the reason is the thing that travels."

"Then it is the reason you have already got, and it is on a wall, and I am not going to invent a new one for a woman holding a frame." He said it to the passage. "This office does not confirm, correct or supply the hours on its address in any copy. A copy of those hours is a second door, and a second door that I have said the words to is a door I have built."

"Then correct one line. One end. Not the frame, one line, and I will take it back to the counter it came from and they will take it out of their book."

"No."

## She Is Early, And Not Careless

She put the frame down on the table, which is the first thing anybody had done to that table in about four years that was not a book or a paper from that office.

"I went on the third of this month at about half past four in the afternoon," she said. "That piece of paper said a quarter to eight. I stood in that street from half past four until about a quarter to seven and I watched the landing and then I watched the door and then I went home."

"Correct."

"And it says here that you are there from a quarter to ten, and it does not say anything about half past six being wrong, and I have been carrying this frame up four flights for eleven days because the paper and the door cannot both be right, and you are the door."

"You were on that street at half past four because that paper told you to be, and the paper was wrong, and the door has never once been wrong." He looked at the three inks. "You were not careless. You were early. That is the whole difference between you and the person who wrote it, and there is about nine of you and about nine of them in this city every week now and only one of those two groups ever has to stand in a street."

## What She Was Supposed To Tell The Person

"What am I supposed to tell the person who gave me that."

"That is the whole of what is left and I am going to answer it properly rather than refuse it."

"I would take an answer."

"I have not got one, and I would rather you had the true shape of that than a sentence I made up at about four seconds' notice." He turned round so that the four on the stair could hear it.

"The only thing in this city about those hours that is not wrong is a card nailed to a door four flights up this one. It has three lines on it, and there is one of it, and I did not correct the counter and I am not going to correct this frame."

"Then the person who gave me this is going to go on giving it to about nine people a week."

"Yes. And about nine of those nine will be in a street on a working afternoon, and every one of them will be there on time, because that paper is being handed to them by somebody who means them well."

"That is the worst of it," she said. "They will be on time. If it was a bad paper nobody would trust it."

## What That Costs, Said Where It Is Heard

"And every one of those nine is going to be in a street at half past four on a working afternoon," said Wenna Raut from the end of the table, "for about an hour and three quarters, on the strength of a piece of paper that this office will not correct."

"That is the cost and I have just heard myself say it and I am not going to argue with it."

"I am not arguing with it either." She had not written anything down. "The clerk at that counter corrected a paper because a person stood in front of them and looked at them. That correction is on your frame with somebody else's ink on it and it is wrong at one end in a way the first one was not. You have just watched a copy get worse because a person tried to be fair about it."

"Yes."

"That is what correcting a copy is," said Wenna Raut. "It is not that it stops being wrong. It is that a second person has now taken it seriously, and the second person is standing in the street now instead of the first one."

## The Man Who Came Up With Four Hands On It

He had it on the table for about four seconds before he said anything at all about it.

Four hands had written those hours on it and the four hands did not agree about either end, and the one at the bottom was in the same ink as a correction somebody had made in about the eighth month of the year before last, which meant the thing had been round this city more than once.

"Where did you get this."

"A man in a corridor off Cordwain gave it to me in the last week of the month before last," the man said, "and he has given it to about nine people since, and I am the ninth, and I am here because I do not want to be the tenth man standing in a street."

He did not put it on the table. He held it the whole time.

"I am not going to touch it," said Lin Hao, "and there is nothing I can tell you about it, and the reason I can tell you nothing about it is on a wall four hundred yards off and it is the reason this office is the way it is and it is not going to change before the end of the year."

"Then I will take it back to him."

"Do not take it back to him."

"He is going to give it to somebody else this evening."

"I know," said Lin Hao. "I am telling you not to take it back to him because you will have to say to him why, and he will say the reason was a man in a white plate, and then the next nine have a name on the reason and the paper. Go home with it and stand in the street about an hour and three quarters like she did, and that is what this city gets instead of a correction."

The man looked at him for about four seconds.

"You would rather nine people stood in a street than that paper be corrected."

"I would rather nine people stood in a street than that there be a second copy with my office's name on it," he said. "Those are not the same cost and you have been in this city long enough to know it."

## The Frame Went Down Four Flights Whole

She picked the frame up off that table and looked at the three inks one more time.

"You have corrected it twice in your own head while you have been talking to me," she said, "and you have not told me either of them, and I am not going to ask, because you will not give me one and I have got nothing to do with it."

"That is correct and I would have said no."

She went down four flights with a frame in her hand and a street in her that she was not going back to that week, and about half an hour later a man came up the same four flights with a piece of paper in a different frame with the hours written on it in four hands, and the four hands did not agree with each other about either end.

Four thousand and two hundred and thirteen days. Less four thousand and two hundred and six, the seventh of this one, is seven, and a piece of paper came up four flights in a frame to be corrected and was not, and a woman who was early went down with it, and about half an hour later a man came up with four hands on it and the four hands did not agree.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September, is fourteen, and the forty-seventh sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fourteen of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of December.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand seven hundred and ninety-three days, which is fifty-nine months and twenty-three days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
