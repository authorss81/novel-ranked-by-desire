# The Sheet She Leaves Alone

Four thousand and one hundred and seventy-two was the third of this one and the tenth of this one is day four thousand and one hundred and seventy-nine, and four thousand and one hundred and seventy-nine less four thousand and one hundred and seventy-two is seven, and ten less three is seven by the month count.

## A Woman Who Can Speak Perfectly Well

She came up those four flights at about eleven on the tenth with a holdall in one hand and no paper in the other, which is the arrangement about half the people who come up that stair have now.

"I want to ask about my brother," she said. "I cannot write. I want that said before anything else so that you know why there is no paper."

"Noted," said Wenna Raut, "and not written down, because a note to yourself is the first of a record." She turned the sheet in front of her round so that the blank side was up. "Say it and I will take it in the words used."

## What Is On That Table, And What It Is For

The twenty-sixth thing on that table is a sheet of office paper, unfolded, undated, with nothing on it at all.

It has been on that table for about three years. It came out of that drawer when that drawer was opened for something else, and it went back on the table and it has not been touched since, and there are three people alive who know what it is for.

One of them was a woman on a goods platform at the back of a market in the twelfth month of the year before last, stopped in the middle of her run, who told him about four seconds' worth of it and told him not to touch it.

He had wanted that sheet to be right for three years, and it was right, and he had been afraid of it the whole time.

## The Reason He Does Not Put It In Front Of Her

She had told him everything. She could not write her own question down and she was telling him the words of it in a room with about nine people in it. It was the one thing that sheet is for and the whole of why it exists.

He had his hand about four inches from it.

"There are two reasons and I am going to say both of them out loud," he said, "and you are not going to like either of them, and you are not the person I am talking to."

"Go on."

"The first is that the moment that sheet comes out of this room it becomes a thing this office hands to people who cannot write. Not today. Somebody is going to carry it up four flights and hand it to somebody who cannot read, and that is a good thing, and in about four months it is going to be standard, and a standard thing that this office hands to people who cannot write is how you begin to know which people cannot write."

"And the second."

"The second is that a sheet with a question on it is a thing that has to be carried back to the woman who wrote it." He took his hand away from the table and put it in his coat. "This office can carry an answer. It cannot carry a question, and the job of finding out whether a question arrived anywhere is not anybody's job in this building."

## She Asked Him What It Was For

"And what is that sheet for," she said.

He looked at it.

"It is for a person who cannot write their question down, so that they can say it and have somebody write it," he said. "It has been on that table for about three years and I have never once given it to anybody, and I did not know that until the twelfth month of the year before last, and I am telling you what it is for because you are entitled to know it is on that table."

"Then why do not you give it to me."

"Because I have just told you two reasons," he said, "and I would be telling you a third one to get out of saying it to the room."

She did not push it. She is the first person in about four years who has not pushed anything at that table, and she did it by turning round and looking at the sheet for a while and then not touching it and not saying anything about it.

## Her Brother And The Door He Will Not Come To

They got to her brother at about eleven twenty.

"He is not coming," she said, before he could put it as a difficulty. "He will not come and he has told me he will not come and I am not going to put you through it twice, so do not ask me to bring him."

"Then the answer goes in with the question and the space under it stays as it is."

"There is no answer."

"There is an answer," said Lin Hao. "The answer is that this office holds nothing about him that we can give anybody without him in the room, and that will be true on the tenth of next month as well, and on the tenth of the month after that, and I would rather you heard it than not."

She put her hand on the strap of the holdall and left it there.

"That is what I got on the bus for," she said.

## At The Bottom Of The Stair, With A Holdall In One Hand

She stopped on the second landing, which is where people stop who have been up a stair in that building before and did not like it.

"You told me that sheet is for me and then you did not give it to me."

"You did."

"That is the second time somebody has done that to me this year, and the other one was a man in a different office with better manners than yours." She shifted the holdall onto her other arm.

"I am not being clever. I am saying that I would have taken it. I would have taken it and I would have got a pencil from somewhere and I would have got the words down badly, and it would have been the only paper in my kitchen with my own handwriting on it. It would have been wrong, and it would have been mine."

"You would have got the words down badly and it would have been correct."

"That is worse," said the woman, "and that is the whole of what I have learned about offices this year, and I am going down four flights now and I am not going to come up again for it."

She went down. He stood on the landing and listened to the holdall catch the banister twice on the way.

## The Entry He Asked For

She went down at about half past eleven. After she had gone, and with the door open, he said out loud what he wanted written down.

"The date, and the hour, and that a person came up four flights this morning who cannot write her own question down, and that this office did not ask her the question."

Wenna Raut had the pen in her hand and she had it over the page and she stopped.

"Read that back to me."

"A person came up four flights who cannot write her own question down, and this office did not ask her the question."

"That is not a refusal."

"It is not."

"It is not an answer either, because nobody was answered and what was asked could not be answered." She wrote the date and the hour and then she wrote the sentence in his words and left the space under it empty. "It is a statement this office made about itself, in this room, with the door open, and the rule from the fifth of this year of last says that is how anything gets in here. It is in."

## Two Spaces On Two Pages

"There are two rows on these two pages with a date and an hour and nothing under them," he said, "and one of them belongs to a woman who was in this room on the nineteenth of the last month and one of them belongs to a woman who was in this room this morning, and both of them are about people who will not come to a door four flights up."

"They are."

"And neither of them has anything under it and both of them have been left that way on purpose."

"On purpose, and permanently, and by the same man." She squared the pen against the table. "You have just written down underneath them that this office is the reason, in your own hand, on a page that goes nowhere except into a bound book on a table. That has not happened before. In about four years a stranger is going to be able to point at it."

## What He Does Not Do With It

He did not offer the sheet to anybody else that week, and he did not put it away, and he did not throw it out, and it is still on that table because it is the twenty-sixth thing on that table and nothing is taken off it and nothing is put on it.

At about half past four a woman of about sixty came up with a man of about thirty and neither of them had any paper, and he heard himself tell them that the hours were the hours on the door and that the office would not put them on anything, and then he heard himself say the second sentence, the one from the twentieth past four, about being able to come up and say a thing and have a person in the room write it down.

He did not get the sheet out of his coat because it is not in his coat. It is on the table, about three feet from his right hand, in the middle of that room, where it has been for about three years.

What he did do, at about twenty past four, with about nine people going past on that stair, was say out loud that anybody who cannot write a question down can come up and say it and a person in that room will write it in the words used, and that they will not be handed a piece of paper to carry away afterwards.

The clerk entered it. It is in that book in her hand, and it is not a question and it is not an answer, and it cost nothing at all, and it is the first offer that office has made that the card does not carry and does not need to.

Four thousand and one hundred and seventy-nine days. Less four thousand and one hundred and seventy-two, the third of this one, is seven, and a sheet of paper that has been on that table for about three years was not given to the woman it was made for on the morning it was most nearly needed, and the reason was not sentiment, and the reason was entered in the book in his own words.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and nine, the thirtieth of June, is seventy, and the forty-sixth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of this month.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand seven hundred and fifty-nine days, which is fifty-eight months and nineteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
