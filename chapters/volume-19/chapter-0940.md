# A Rule She Cannot Keep

Four thousand and two hundred and thirteen was the fourteenth of this one and the twenty-first of this one is day four thousand and two hundred and twenty, and four thousand and two hundred and twenty less four thousand and two hundred and thirteen is seven, and twenty-one less fourteen is seven by the month count.

## The Return That Wants One Figure

She came up at about half past ten on the twenty-first with the hard-backed book open to a page with a line ruled on it and a bracket at the end of the line.

"A return has been raised for the quarter and it wants one figure out of that office," said the board's clerk, "and it wants it for the twenty-eighth of this month, and it does not want a name and it does not want a street and it does not want an hour. It wants a number of people who came up that stair in a quarter, and I am here in person because I was told to ask in person."

"How many does it think there will be."

"It does not know either," she said. "That is the whole of why you are being asked. There is a figure that this building used and has stopped using, and there is a figure about a stair four hundred yards off that the two of you have been arguing about since June, and somebody in a hall has put the two together and drawn a bracket."

## The Figure Exists

Wenna Raut turned the book round so that the line with the bracket was facing up.

"I can give her that in about four seconds," she said. "Nine a fortnight is nine a fortnight and it has been nine a fortnight since the fourth of March and it has not moved once, and there are two people in this room who could put it on that line and mean it."

"Say the number."

"Nine a fortnight," she said, "and about nine a fortnight have been knocking wrong once on their first morning for about eight months, and that is about two out of the two."

The board's clerk had her pencil out before the sentence was finished.

"Then I will write it."

## Why A Count Of Comers Is The First Line Of A Roll

"No."

"You agreed with the number."

"I agreed with the number," said Lin Hao, "and I am refusing the line, and those are two different things and you will want them both and you may not have them both."

He turned round and looked at the passage.

The door was open, because it stands open between a quarter to ten and half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour. Both carriers were in it with the strap, waiting, because the book does not go up at a quarter to ten if a woman from a division building is standing at the end of that table.

There were nine people on that stair at half past ten.

## No List Of Comers, Ever

He said it in the ordinary voice, and he said it slowly enough that it could be taken down.

"This office keeps no roll of who comes through that door. It keeps no index of it and it has never kept one and it is not going to start this month."

He turned back to the table.

"From today it keeps no count of it either. Not a number, not a total, not a figure for a quarter, and not a number handed to anybody in this room for any reason. It will not be given for the reason that a number is better than a name, because that is not a reason."

"That is a different thing from the wall."

"That is the same thing as the wall, and the wall is about nine months old and it was wrong for about eight of those months, and this is the version of it that is actually going to happen."

## What It Closes

"Then tell me what you get instead," said the board's clerk.

"Nothing." He said it flat and he did not improve it afterwards.

"From this morning this office does not know how many people come up that stair. Not for one morning, not over a week, not over a quarter, and not over a year."

"I had that in my head since the fourth of March, and she had it, and the woman who carries that book up two flights five days a week had it."

"Then from today there is one place in this city where that number can be said. It is about nine people's memories, and it does not have a clerk."

"That number is not a list of anybody."

"No. It is the first line of a list of anybody," he said. "One quarter of strangers counted and reported becomes four quarters. Four quarters is a year."

"A year of strangers with a street at the end of it is an index of who found a door, and I have spent nine years not making one, and I am not starting this month with a bracket at the end of a line."

## The Price, Named Before It Is Spent

"Name it," said Wenna Raut, "because you do not get to spend it quietly and you have not been able to since March."

He turned to the passage again.

"About nine people a fortnight will go on knocking at the wrong end of those hours for the rest of this year. They will come at half past seven because that is what the paper says, and they will go back down four flights, and nobody will be able to tell them they were nearly right, because telling them is correcting a copy."

"That is the first price. There is a second one."

"There is a second one." He looked at the two of them and did not look at the stair. "There are two places in this city where this office is being reached by people who cannot read the three lines on that door. A woman at a market end who has said them out loud about eleven times. A woman two streets off who has written a date on the back of about nine slips in her own hand and has said she is not going to stop."

"You have just told a woman from a division building where two of them are."

"I have just told a woman from a division building that this office keeps no count of who comes and that two women are standing outside the count doing it for nothing," he said. "That is the price of saying it with the door open, and I said it with the door open on purpose, and I would say it again."

## She Took Nothing Down

The board's clerk put her pencil away. She shut the hard-backed book and held it under her arm.

"I have been doing this for nineteen years," she said, "and about nine institutions have refused me something this year, and every one of them gave me a reason, and about four of them were good reasons, and I have never once been refused by a person who said the price out loud before saying no."

"That is not an advantage."

"It is a great deal better than being right," she said. "I am going back to a hall and put nothing on that line, and about nine people in that hall are going to look at the bracket and I am going to have to say something to them, and what I am going to say is that the office at the top of that stair does not count, and that is a sentence they can check by coming here themselves, and about nine of them will."

She went down four flights.

## What The Carrier With The Bad Back Said About It

The strap came off the book about a minute after that and the two of them got it up onto the table, and the woman with the bad back said what she had been holding since half past ten.

"You have just made a rule that I cannot keep for you."

"I know."

"I count the noise of that stair because there is nothing else to do with my arms. I have got a quarter of it and I have not written it down and I was never going to." She rolled her right shoulder. "From this morning it is not a count I am keeping. It is a thing I do with my hands, and that is a worse place for it to be and you have put it there."

"I have."

"Then I am going to go on doing it anyway, and if you ask me for it in the spring I am going to tell you what it is, which is nine people a week standing on a landing saying nothing to anybody, and you can put that on a return and it cannot be checked."

## What Is Left Of The Twenty-First

At about ten past five a man came up those four flights with a slip in his hand and he had been at that door on the previous afternoon at half past seven, and he said so before Lin Hao had said anything.

"I know what your card says," he said. "I have read it at a counter. I came yesterday at about half past seven because a piece of paper said so."

The card says a quarter to ten to half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour.

"That is right," said Lin Hao, "and the piece of paper was wrong, and this office does not correct copies of that card in any form, and there is no third piece of paper with your name on it coming down these stairs today."

The man stood there for about four seconds and then he went down.

Four thousand and two hundred and twenty days. Less four thousand and two hundred and thirteen, the fourteenth of this one, is seven.

A bracket at the end of a line in a hard-backed book went back up four flights empty. A room that has kept no list since the fourth of March will now keep no count either, and about nine people a fortnight will go on arriving once at the wrong end of the hours, and there is no longer anything in that building that could notice.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September, is twenty-one, and the forty-seventh sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty-one of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of December.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand eight hundred days, which is sixty months and no days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
