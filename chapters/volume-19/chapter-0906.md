# A Third Figure In Chalk

Three thousand nine hundred and seventy-seven was the eighteenth of this one and the twenty-fifth of this one is day three thousand nine hundred and eighty-four, and three thousand nine hundred and eighty-four less three thousand nine hundred and seventy-seven is seven, and twenty-five less eighteen is seven by the month count.

## Nine Stools And A Slate

The lane off Cordwain has nine stools in it, a delivery coat on a nail, a cart behind the third stool, and a slate four feet by three leaning against the wall with a figure of merit on it in four-inch letters.

Nine stools, nine districts, twelve books that are not the district's, and a rag that lives in an apron pocket.

The slate had been wiped and re-chalked that morning, which it is not every day, and Brack had put his own four days of the month along the bottom edge in small chalk where anybody can read them and check them against their own, and under them, in the same small chalk in his own hand, the figure he had rubbed out on the thirtieth of December and written sooner.

Lin Hao came down the hill at about eleven and stood in front of that slate until Brack had finished giving a ration out of the middle of the pot to a woman at the fourth stool, and then he said what he had come to say.

"I have come to ask you what a card on a door does to a lane, the way I came on the thirtieth of December, and I would rather ask it now than in the spring."

## Nine A Week And All Different

"About nine a week," said Brack. "In my line. And that has been true since the middle of the month before last and I have left it standing where it is."

"Has it moved since the card."

"It has not moved." Brack took the cloth and wiped the end of the slate, which is a thing he does while he is about to say a thing he means.

"The nine has not moved. The character of it has. Before the card, about four in nine were asking whether you carried anything, which is a porter's question, and about four in nine were asking whether the room upstairs was open, which is a question I could not have been asked twice. Now it is about nine in nine asking where it is, and they ask it well, and they go away."

"They go away."

"They go away, and they can hand it to somebody else, and that is the whole of what has changed, and it is not a small thing, and I have been chewing on it since the second." He squared the slate against the wall. "A man who is lost is a man with a problem. A man who has been told where it is has a fact, and he cannot give the fact away, he can only give the address away, and the address is on a board."

## The Third Figure

"And what will you chalk."

"About nine a week, same as it was. Nothing."

"Brack."

"I am going to tell you the thing I have been turning over since the second, and then you can go up that hill." He put the chalk on the ledge. "Since the turn of the year there is a third thing happening in this lane and I cannot put it on that slate, and I have known what it is since the ninth of this month and I am not going to chalk it."

"Tell me what it is and I will not ask you to put it up."

"It is how many of my own neighbours have been told where that door is by somebody who was told first. Not by me. By a delivery man, by a woman at a counter, by a woman with a bundle who stood in the middle of the passage this week and said it out loud and walked off." He wiped the end of the slate again without putting anything on it.

"Nine a week is a number of people walking in. That is a number about the lane."

"The other one is a number about the same lane, and it is the larger of the two, and it grows whether I write it or not, and there is no unit I could put after it."

"That is not a figure. That is a feeling."

"That is why it is not going on the slate."

He looked up.

"There was a woman in a counting house on Cordwain. I priced her in my head and to one other man and not one further, and she said it out loud in front of two rooms, and from that afternoon every woman at a bench in this city has kept her own arithmetic to herself."

"Your book went quiet honestly. That is what that evening bought you, and it is the only honest quiet anybody has ever been able to buy."

"And now there is nothing left in Ostara that can tell a true quiet from a careful one, and I am not putting a second thing in this lane that a person can be given a number for and then repeat out loud."

"I am not putting a second unpriceable thing in a public lane this year. That is my whole answer and you did not have to work for it."

## Respect Plainly Given

"I am not going to ask you to rub out the sooner figure," said Lin Hao. "And I am not going to ask you to chalk the third one."

"Good."

"You told me on the thirtieth of December that you would rub out four years and write sooner, and you picked up the chalk and did it, and you have not mentioned it since and neither have I." He stood in front of that slate with his hands in his coat. "I came down here in December afraid you would take it out on me. You did not. And I have been carrying that about for a month and I would rather say it in your lane than in mine."

Brack looked at him for about four seconds.

"You are not going to get it twice," he said. "Once is what a man gives. If you come down here next month and start being decent at me, I will take your business and you can go up the hill."

"That is fair."

"It is also the arrangement, and it has held since the middle of the first year." He turned the slate round so the figures faced the lane. "And the office not close. Still."

## The Cost Of A Deferred Figure

Then he said the part that was going to be in that lane all year whether Lin Hao heard it or not.

"I will tell you what carrying it costs, since you have asked me a question and I have answered it. I have had that third figure in my head since the ninth of this month and I am not going to write it."

"Every day I do not write it, I lose about one in fifty of it, and there is no way on this earth to put a figure back into a man who has been carrying it too long, and on the day I do chalk it it will be wrong, and it will be wrong by about a month."

"And a person will not be able to check it against anything, because the only record of what was said in that passage in this month is in about nine heads in a lane and they were all in it."

"So it will be a wrong figure on a public slate."

"Or it will be nothing at all, which is the thing I have chosen, and a man who has chosen nothing is still a man who has chosen." He set the chalk down. "You did not make me carry it. You made it a thing to carry. That is different and you know it is different."

## And What Lin Hao Was Going To Ask

"I was going to ask you for it," said Lin Hao. "That is why I came down. Not to ask about the nine, which was never going to move. I came to ask you to chalk a third figure so that somebody would be holding the number who was not me."

"I know you did. You have wanted somebody else to be holding a number for about nine years and you have been refused every time by somebody who was right."

"You should have said so before I got here and made you say it twice."

"I should have, and I did not, because you asked me a real question first and that earns a man a fair answer to the second one."

Brack took the rag out of his apron and folded it once.

"And I am not your instrument. That is the whole of the ninth sentence and it is the last one for this quarter."

"A figure on my slate in my chalk is a fact about this lane that anybody can check by standing here. A figure in your head that I put there because you asked me nicely is a thing two men know about and about four hundred people do not, and that is how every figure in this city gets into a rent."

## The Man With A Card

At about one a man came into that lane with a delivery of something that did not weigh anything, and stopped at the third stool, and took a piece of stiff grey board out of his coat and looked at it, and asked Brack whether the hours on it were right because he had written them out wrong somewhere in the third court and did not want to put a wrong thing in front of somebody.

Brack took the card, read the three lines, gave it back, and said the hours were right.

"Then I have made a mess of it," said the man, and rubbed the back of his own hand with his thumb for a while, and then asked Brack, not Lin Hao, whether the man would mind.

"I would not," said Brack. "He would not mind at all. Write them on the back of your hand and do not put them down again, because a thing that is written twice is wrong inside a month and he will not correct it."

The man went up the lane with the card still in his hand and his own handwriting on the back of his fingers, and Brack looked after him and then looked at Lin Hao standing there with his hands still in his coat.

"Well," he said.

Three thousand nine hundred and eighty-four days. Less three thousand nine hundred and seventy-seven, the eighteenth of this one, is seven, and a third figure has formed in a lane off Cordwain that will not be chalked, and a man with a card has the hours written on the back of his hand wrong at one end.

*Less three thousand nine hundred and twenty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is fifty-five, and the forty-fourth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-five of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand five hundred and sixty-four days, which is fifty-two months and four days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
