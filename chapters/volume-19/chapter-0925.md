# Seven Days Out Of June

Four thousand and one hundred and nine was the thirtieth of last month and the seventh of this one is day four thousand and one hundred and sixteen, and four thousand and one hundred and sixteen less four thousand and one hundred and nine is seven, and thirty less thirty is none, and seven with none carried out of the month is seven by the month count with the crossing named as itself.

## Four Days On A Wall

The wall in the counters hall of that division has had the quarter's figures on it since the morning of the third, and the figures went up at the end of the quarter the way figures go up at the end of every quarter.

It is eleven feet of painted board from the floor, and there are about four hundred people a week past it going in off the street and out to the counters, and none of them stop, and the ones who stop are there for a different reason and read the whole thing from the top down.

He had asked for the number that was not on the wall, and she had asked him what it counted, and he had told her what it counted, and she had written it down twice.

He went and stood at about half past nine in the morning on the fourth day and read his own four words under somebody else's number.

## What People Do With A Caption

He read the caption out loud once.

He did it because a sentence in a Bureau record is a thing that has not been checked out loud, and because he had promised a clerk in a filing room with no window that it was all of it, and he wanted to hear it in a hall where about four hundred people a week go past.

That is the whole of it. Four words, and the count is the paper and not the people, and the only thing the sentence is doing up there is stopping a reader in a hurry from putting a second number next to the first one and calling the pair a total.

It took about nine seconds and about nine people heard it and two of them stopped.

## The Sentence Doing The Opposite Of Its Job

The man who stopped was young and he had a delivery docket in his hand and he asked, not unkindly, whether the figure was the number of people who went in and out of a door, because that is what a figure over a door on a wall is in this city.

It is not. It is a count of pieces of paper and a caption written in a small hand four hundred yards off by a woman who has never seen the door.

And it is going to be a number about people going in and out of a door inside a month, because he had said four words to a filing clerk to stop that happening and the four words had a shape to them that a man with a docket standing in a hall could not get past, and a count of things that are not people is a thing people read as being about people.

"You have written it in a way that invites the wrong one," he said. "That is on me and not on you and I am going to be careful how I say it."

## Why He Was Not Going To Correct It

He could have corrected it, and he worked it out on the spot in about four seconds, and the correction would have been one sentence.

A count of sheets, and not of people who come and go — and there is nothing in the correction that is not in the caption, and the correction would have gone into that division's index the way the caption went into it, in a hand, under a column, on a sheet, and in about four months there would have been two sentences about that figure on one wall and about nine people a week would have read both of them and believed neither.

He did not correct it.

Then he said it out loud in that hall, in the ordinary voice, with about nine people within earshot, because a thing left uncorrected in a hall is a thing a hall can decide about.

"That caption is mine and it is true and I am not going to change it," he said. "If it reads as something about the people who come and go that is my doing and not the clerk's, and she wrote down what I said and she wrote it twice to be sure of it."

A man at the fourth counter looked up, and the man with the docket said thank you and went off up the hall, and nobody in that hall asked him anything else.

## The Price, Said Out Loud

At about half past two he was at the end of that table again, door wide, both carriers on the stair.

"I put four words into an index in another building on the thirtieth," he said, "and they were true, and they are on a wall now, and a man asked me a quarter of an hour after I had read them whether they were about people, and they are not, and I did not fix it."

"What would fixing it have cost."

"A second sentence in the same column on the same wall," he said. "That is all it would have cost, and it would have cost about four hundred people a week an argument for the next four months instead of a mistake, and I chose the mistake, and I chose it in about four seconds standing up next to the wall, and I did not enjoy it, which I want written down, because I have been enjoying saying things out loud since March."

"You have."

"I have. It went out of me about a quarter of an hour ago and I noticed it going and I let it go."

Wenna Raut wrote the hour and the words he used and left the space under them empty, because a row about a man being pleased with himself is not a question anybody asked.

## What The Week Did To That Stair

It was the busiest first week after a quarter-end that room had run in about two years, and it was busy in a way that had nothing to do with the quarter.

About nine people came up those four flights between the fourth and the seventh who were there about the figure and not about a person, and every one of the nine had come with a question rather than a request, and all nine wanted the same single thing, which was to be told that the caption on that wall was what it said it was.

Four of them said they had only wanted to look at it. Two of them were annoyed about it and said so. One of them was a man who had already said so out loud in that hall on the fourth day and had come up four flights a second time to say it to the man himself, which is the most frightening thing that has ever happened to this office and is also the only response in nine years that was worth the paper.

He told all nine of them the truth, which is the count of sheets, and nine of them went away satisfied, and he understood about two hours after the ninth of them that being satisfied was the dangerous part.

A person who is satisfied by an answer does not ask the next question. A person who is annoyed about a figure goes and looks at the caption underneath it, and reads it carefully, and goes away and repeats it badly to somebody, and there is not a man standing at that end of it.

He had swapped one kind of person going about with another and both of them cost the same.

The month's form came down at the start of the day and went back up on the seventh and was not an event.

## The Person Who Was Going To Ask

A woman stopped him at the foot of the hill on the sixth.

She was not the filing clerk and she was not at the returns counter, and she had a boardman's coat on and she had clearly come out of that hall on her own time.

"You read the caption out loud in there yesterday," she said. "About nine people heard it and two of them stopped and one of them went off up the hall talking."

"I did."

"Then you know what is on that wall next to a figure and a caption that says what it does not count," said the woman. "There is nothing else on it. Four hundred people a week go past a number and four words telling them it is not about people, and not one hour of it, and the only thing they would want on that wall to stop knocking at the wrong end of a morning is three lines of hours."

"You are going to ask me for them."

"I am going to ask you for them properly," she said, "and not in a passage, and with my supervisor's reasons and not my own, and you are going to have to say no to her face instead of to mine."

"Come up four flights."

"I know which stair," said the woman. "Every person in that hall knows which stair. That is not a thing you did and it is a thing you caused, and we have been arguing about it on the floor since the second."

He stood at the bottom of that hill for a while after she had gone, because the sentence had come up the hill with her and he could not get rid of it.

Every person in that hall knows which stair. About nine of them a week had already knocked on it, and he had sent nine of them away kindly, and he had refused a woman a paper in a doorway on the seventeenth of last month, and there was nothing wrong with any of that and it had all of it in it.

## About Eleven Feet Back From It

He went back into that hall for about four minutes on the way down the hill, because he wanted to see what a wall looks like after four hundred people have walked past it in a week, and the paint round it is the only honest count anybody takes of a thing in this city.

There were about nine people standing in front of the wall and two of them were reading the caption out loud to a third, and the second of the two had it in the wrong order, because he had heard it once from a man with a delivery docket on the fourth day and had taken it that way round.

It counts the people. Not sheets.

That is the wrong sentence and it was going out of that hall about nine times a day, and there was not going to be a man in that hall who stopped him, because a man reading a Bureau wall aloud to a colleague in a public hall is not doing anything that can be complained about.

Lin Hao stood about eleven feet back and let it go. He did not go over and say anything, because a second sentence in that hall would make two sentences about one caption on one wall, and he had worked that out in a lane in June.

Then he went down the hill, and it was some days after that when people began coming up the stair to be told the caption was what it said it was, and that was the first of the ways that sentence was going to leave that hall without him.

Four thousand and one hundred and sixteen days. Less four thousand and one hundred and nine, the thirtieth of last month, is seven, and four true words went up on a wall four hundred yards off on the fourth day, and a man read them and asked a question they were written to prevent, and a woman in a boardman's coat told him at the bottom of a hill that his own refusal to make a copy had put a copy in every head in a hall.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and nine, the thirtieth of June, is seven, and the forty-sixth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seven of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of September.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand six hundred and ninety-six days, which is fifty-six months and sixteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
