# Not One Name

Four thousand and two hundred and eighty-three was the twenty-fourth of this one and the thirtieth of this one is day four thousand and two hundred and eighty-nine, and four thousand and two hundred and eighty-nine less four thousand and two hundred and eighty-three is six, and thirty less twenty-four is six by the month count.

## The Six Lines At The Foot

The six lines at the foot of that standing sheet were read out first, in the ordinary voice, in the order they are read in, and it is not changed because it is a quarter-end.

Then the head.

"Eight hundred and fifty-two," said Lin Hao. "It has not moved since the thirtieth of March of the fifth year, when the sheet it is printed on was one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine."

Then he stopped, because the next part of that quarter-end is said in a passage off Cordwain and not in that room, and it has been said in a public passage and in a filing room with no window and in a lane, and this time it is said at a counter under a window with about nine strangers standing in it.

## Three Nails Out On The Thirtieth

He did the door at about eleven, with the room behind him and about nine people on that stair.

Three nails. He had put the cloth over the holes first because the grit off about four people's boots gets into that stair all through December, and the second nail turned twice, and it came out and left the pale ring round it that was about a quarter of an inch wider than it had been in November and wider again than it had been in the summer.

The winter card came off and he held it up in the light going down that stairwell and looked at it.

The street along the top was sound. Both top corners of the second line were soft with the ink out of the fibres and the fourth word and the fifth word were standing about the depth of a coat button apart. The three nails were straight and had been straight the whole year.

He put it in the inside of that coat on the right and shut it.

## What He Wrote And What He Did Not Write

There is a piece of stiff grey card in a drawer in the bin room off that branch, about the length of a forearm, and it has been there since the autumn, and he took it up four flights at about a quarter to ten on the twenty-eighth of October and has carried it in the inside of that coat on the right every morning since the twenty-ninth.

He wrote the three lines on it at that table, in ink, with his own hand, in about four minutes.

The street. The door at the head of four flights off a third court. The hours, which are a quarter to ten to half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour.

He did not write a name on it. He did not write a fourth line. He did not write an hour about the turn of the year. He did not write anything about the second of December of the ninth year, or about a division return with a bracket at the end of a line, or about two women standing outside the count.

And then he did something he has not done in nine years, which is that he wrote a thing down.

The hand that did it was his and it did not shake, and there are three lines on it, and it goes up on a door on the same three nails in about nine minutes, and the only part of this office that has ever been written on by him is a bound register with about four hundred and something rows in it and a book with two written refusals in it and now this.

## The Same Three Nails

The holes in that door were a quarter of an inch wider than the nails and about nine months older than the nails themselves, and the new card went on the same three nails because there are three nails in that stair and they have been the same three since the first year.

It stood about a sixteenth of an inch off the door. It did before.

He stepped back about nine feet and looked at it in the daylight that was left, which was most of it, because the thirtieth of December has about four hours of useful light and that landing gets a good deal of it.

"You have not added anything," said Wenna Raut.

"I have not added anything."

"You have replaced it."

"I have replaced it in the same three lines and not one name, and that is the whole of what a card renewal is and there is nothing to be said about it in a room." He put the screwdriver back in the tin behind the door. "And I want it said out loud with the door open that this is the first time this year that I have written a thing down that stays."

"Say the cost of it," she said, "because that is what you have said every other time."

## What It Costs

"It is this," he said. "For one year that office could not be found by anybody, and I put a street on a door in about nine minutes on the thirtieth of December, and about nine strangers a fortnight have come up four flights and found it, and wrong versions of those three lines now exist all over this city in more hands than this office has fingers and not one of them is mine."

"You will want a number."

"I could give you one in about four seconds and it would be wrong by whatever the answer is worth, and I am not going to give you one, and the reason is not the rule. The rule is about who comes up that stair. This is about how many pieces of paper about that door exist in a city of about nine hundred people a week going past a hall, and nothing in this city can count them, including me."

"Then that is not a cost, that is a confession."

"It is both, and they have been the same thing at this table since about four minutes ago." He put the tin back behind the door. "The cost is that the card comes down in the spring if I take it down."

"That is a card coming down and not a card staying up."

"That is exactly what it is, and that is the first half of it," he said. "The second half is that nothing in the next twelve months stops a stranger walking up that stair. There is no instrument in this city that stops it. About nine people a fortnight will go on arriving at the wrong end of those hours and not one thing in that building can notice."

## At The Counter Under The Window

He went down at about half past two and crossed to a passage off Cordwain where there is a counter under a window and a rail and about nine people who stand there in every weather, and the man behind that counter is paid nothing and has read the foot of that sheet four times a year since the beginning of the second year.

The counterman did the service first, because it comes first.

He said the stair and the third door off the landing in nine words to a woman who had no card in her hands at all, in about four seconds, and she wrote about half of it on the back of her hand and got the half wrong.

Then Lin Hao stood on the other side of that counter with the forty-eighth sheet face up on the rail and about nine people in the passage.

"Thirty-two sheets and thirty-one quarters and they differ by one, because the thirty-second has not gone up yet and will go up at four this afternoon on the same two nails as the forty-seventh."

"Four thousand two hundred and eighty-nine less one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine is two thousand seven hundred and ninety, and two thousand seven hundred and ninety is thirty-one times ninety."

"It is a count of pieces of paper with a striking at the foot of them," said Lin Hao. "It is not money and it is not people and it is not anybody who has come through that door."

The counterman wrote it on the back of the quarterly sheet in his own small hand, and put the sheet face up on the rail where a person could stand and read it, and about nine people who had come in to ask about something else read it standing there.

## The Forty-Seventh Comes Down At Four

At about four that afternoon he took the forty-seventh off that rail in the flat file room four hundred yards off and put the forty-eighth on the same two nails.

A young filing clerk with no window was in that room and she wrote the date at the top of the sheet that went up and the hour against it and nothing else, because that is what the sheet that went up last quarter had on it, and because he told her in June that a caption under a figure is a sentence the office cannot take back.

Same two nails. It has been the same two nails since the first year.

## The Woman At The Bottom Of Four Flights

She was standing at the bottom of that stair at about twenty to five on the last working day of the year, and she had come up it once before, on the twenty-fourth, with a folded paper in her coat that she had not unfolded.

"About twenty minutes ago I put three lines on a card and three nails in a door," he said, from about four steps up.

"I know. I was on that stair when you did it. I was going down." She did not come up. "I am not going to come up and I have not come up."

"Say what you came up the four flights for."

"I told you last week what I came up for."

"You asked me whether it would be here after the turn of the year," he said, "and I told you that the card does not say and that I had not decided. It has not changed what it says. I am telling you that it is there, and it is there because it has never not been there since the thirtieth of December, and I have decided that it is going to be there after the turn of the year."

"How do I know that."

"You do not," said Lin Hao. "There is nothing to check it against and I am not going to make one."

"You are going to have to say more than that to a woman standing at the bottom of your own stair."

"There is no register of who has read it and no page anywhere that says what that card says, and in about four years a stranger will come up here and ask why a room with nothing at the foot of its page for nine years has a door anybody can walk up to."

"And what will you tell him."

"That I decided it on the last working day of the twelfth year," said Lin Hao, "and that not one person alive is going to be able to tell him when."

That was the whole of it and it was said at the bottom of his own stair in his own voice and about four people heard it.

He went up the four flights and said the hours out loud to them, because that is what he does now, and then he got the book down at half past five by his own hands and carried it four flights and about four hundred yards and it was not upstairs overnight.

Four thousand and two hundred and eighty-nine days. Less four thousand and two hundred and eighty-three, the twenty-fourth of this one, is six, and thirty less twenty-four is six. A card came down off three nails at about eleven this morning, and another card with the same three lines and not one name went up on the same three nails at about a quarter past eleven, and nothing entered the office's hand at the foot of that standing sheet in ten chapters.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September, is ninety, and the forty-seventh sheet was face up on that rail for every one of the last ninety of them and came down at four this afternoon, and the forty-eighth is up on the same two nails.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand eight hundred and sixty-nine days, which are sixty-two months and nine days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
