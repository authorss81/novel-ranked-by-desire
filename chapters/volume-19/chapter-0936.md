# The Stone Holds Seven Hands

Four thousand and one hundred and eighty-six was the seventeenth of this one and the twenty-fourth of this one is day four thousand and one hundred and ninety-three, and four thousand and one hundred and ninety-three less four thousand and one hundred and eighty-six is seven, and twenty-four less seventeen is seven by the month count.

## Quarter Of A Mile Down The Hill

He had not come down that hill with a purpose. He had come down it because the light goes off that kerb about eleven in the morning and it is a quarter past four, and because he had done it in March and in June and in July and had stopped pretending there was a reason.

There is about a quarter of a mile of hill between that corner and the top of it, and he knows the length of it to within about nine steps and he has not walked it at speed since the winter of the year before last.

## Seven Hands, And The End Of The Last One

About sixty words are cut into four inches of dressed stone at the foot of that hill, in seven hands, and they are a year old and they are not about him.

The newest of them sits low and to the right and was cut small and in a hurry, and it is nine words long and says that a woman came up four flights and was read to and went home, and then it stops with the cutting clean behind it.

He went along the stone past the end of it the way a man goes along a wall hunting for the next thing on it.

There is nothing cut there. That last inch and a half has been dressed flat for years, and whoever comes next to that stone would have to bring a chisel to a face with no history on it, which is another act altogether from writing on a wall this city already walks past.

Two words are cut hard and small and well clear of the other seven. He did not go looking for them and he did not reach out towards them, and he is not going to start this afternoon with a question about who cut them and why.

## A Sheet In The Upper Square

The board stands nine feet back from the kerb and it has two ruled squares on it.

The month's form went up on the seventh and it is still up, and the paste along its top batten has gone from tacky to hard and is about seventeen days into it, and the form itself is thin and has begun to curl at the top corner where the wind gets under it.

The upper square is empty.

The lower square is empty.

Under the lower batten her sheet is held against the wood where it has been for about nine years, with a date at the top of it and a ruled pencil line across the foot of it in her own hand, and it has never been folded, never turned over and never taken, and it did not move this afternoon either.

What is new is on the rail of the board, about four inches above the upper square, held on with two drawing pins that are not the pins that came with it.

A sheet of paper, folded once, with writing on the outside of it.

## The Light Going Off The Kerb

It was going. By the time he had stood there for about four minutes the stone was a shape and the board was a shape and the sheet on the rail of that board was a rectangle about the length of a forearm with a darker rectangle at the top of it where the writing was.

He could see the length of the first line.

He could not read a word of it, and he could not have read a word of it an hour later, and the light off that kerb in the fourth month of this year does not come back.

He stood there.

## Why He Has Not Taken A Lamp Since The Ninth Of March

He worked it out on the way down, about twenty minutes earlier, and had it ready, and it did not feel like a rule because no rule exists anywhere that says it.

It is that a lamp held over that board at dusk is the one thing done down there that the hill itself would keep. Somebody would remember the man holding it, and a year later a stranger would come down that hill asking after him by name. On the day that happens, that corner stops being the one place in this city that has never wanted anything off him.

He also worked out, standing there, the second thing. A lamp is a way of taking a thing off a board, and taking a thing off a board at the foot of a hill is the same act, done smaller, as picking up a piece of paper off a half-landing between two floors.

He had thought about that one in the sixth month. It had not improved with time.

## The Woman With The Short-Handled Broom

She was about eleven feet off, at the kerb, with a short-handled broom, working the gutter edge from the wall out to the road in about four strokes and back.

She comes up that hill about once a month for the board and about four times a month for the gutter and she has been doing the gutter for about nine years.

He wanted to ask her three things. She would have answered two of them.

He did not ask her any of them, because she is the only person at that corner who comes every day and knows nothing whatever, and a question from a man in a white plate at that kerb is the first thing anybody has ever asked anybody there, and it would be the end of the only corner in this city that has never asked him for anything.

## What He Does Not Do

He did not take the lamp out of his coat, because it is not in his coat. He has not put a lamp in that coat since the ninth of March.

He did not take the sheet off the rail of the board. He did not ask a person to take it for him, and there was nobody on that hill he could have asked. He did not cut anything into that stone and he did not carry a chisel and he did not go up to a division building about it.

He stood about nine minutes at that corner, which is longer than the work there requires, and about the same as the ninth of March.

## Back Up Four Flights, And The Opposite Of The Ninth Of March

He went back up that hill at about a quarter past four with the grit on his boots and came in at about half past.

"Nine seconds ago I would have said nothing," he told her, and he told her the whole of it standing, with the door open and about four people going down on that stair behind him.

"There is a sheet of paper on the rail of that board. It has been put on with two pins that are not the pins that came with it. It has writing on the outside of it. It is about the length of a forearm and the first line runs the width of it, and there are about nine words on that stone and the last one stops in the middle, and there is nothing cut past where it stops and there has not been for years."

"That is the stone," said Wenna Raut.

"Everything about the stone is the stone and I have been down there six times since the ninth of March for it. It is the paper." He put his hands in his coat. "Somebody has written my office down at that kerb on a public board and I have stood four feet from it and I cannot read it, and I am not going to find out what it says, and I am telling you that I am not going to find out what it says."

"You are not going to send anybody."

"Nobody goes down that hill on my account, and there is not going to be a person sent to that kerb by this office in the spring either."

## What Is Left Of The Afternoon

He took the chair at the end of the table and put the heels of his hands against its edge, which is the only thing he does with his hands in that room.

"Why," said Wenna Raut.

"Because a lamp there is me asking that corner a question, and I have not asked it anything for about nine years, and in about four years the asking is the only thing anybody will remember up there."

He looked at the board on that wall, which is a different board four hundred yards off and is not the same board at all.

"And because a sheet about that office on a public board is not a mistake I can correct. If it has my street on it, then a stranger has written down where a stranger's paper said we were. The only correct copy of it is a door four flights up with three lines on it."

"It is still there."

"It is still there, and it has been since the thirtieth of December, and the nails are straight, and I am not putting a seventh line on it in the spring." He took his chair again. "Tell me what happened on that stair today while I was gone."

Nine of them. The same as the last three mornings, and the same four who waited, and two who came and left.

And then he asked her, out loud, for the first time in about four months, not to keep anything at all about that stair, not for him and not for anybody, and she said that was not in her gift and that she had been trying to tell him since March.

Four thousand and one hundred and ninety-three days. Less four thousand and one hundred and eighty-six, the seventeenth of this one, is seven, and there is a sheet of paper on the rail of a board at the foot of a hill that he has not read and will not read and has told a room he will not ask about.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and nine, the thirtieth of June, is eighty-four, and the forty-sixth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last eighty-four of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of this month.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand seven hundred and seventy-three days, which is fifty-nine months and three days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
