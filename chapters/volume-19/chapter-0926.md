# The Copy Asked Plainly

Four thousand and one hundred and sixteen was the seventh of last month and the fourteenth of this one is day four thousand and one hundred and twenty-three, and four thousand and one hundred and twenty-three less four thousand and one hundred and sixteen is seven, and fourteen less seven is seven by the month count.

## Two On The Landing And A Third

They came up those four flights at about eleven in the morning on the fourteenth and there were three of them, and one of them was the woman in the boardman's coat who had stopped him at the foot of the hill on the sixth, and the other two were in front.

About nine people were in that passage, four of them waiting, and the door stood wide because it stands wide between a quarter to ten and half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour.

He knew the whole of what was coming before the second of the three had taken his hand off, because he had been on that landing on the sixth thinking about it.

## The Ask, In Four Seconds

"We would like the three lines for the wall," she said. "The street, the door, and the hours. In your hand or ours, either is fine, and we will take it down if you prefer and hang it under your own figure."

"No."

That is the whole of it and it took about four seconds and he did not soften it, because a thing said at volume in a passage goes out into that passage and does not come back to be tidied.

The supervisor did not move. She had a folder of her own under her arm and she had come up four flights to be refused and had known that on the street, and her face did what faces do in a passage when a man says one word to three people.

"I want you to hear the reason," said the woman in the boardman's coat, "because you gave it to me at the bottom of a hill and I have written it down and it is right, and she has to be able to write the same words on a wall after she has heard them from you."

"Then she has it and she does not need me to say it again."

"She does need you to say it again. I have had it from you once, in the open air, off a street corner, on your own time, with my hands in my coat." She put her own hand on the rail of that landing. "Say it where the four of them can hear it."

The four of them were not the three who had come up. Four other people were on that stair between the second landing and the third, and there is nowhere on four narrow flights to have a conversation that about nine people do not get.

## Why He Gave The Reason Out Loud

"About nine people a fortnight find that door by something that is not the door," he said. "Not one of them is doing anything wrong. Somebody told a neighbour, or a man read it out of a counter, or somebody wrote it out on the back of a form, and every one of those copies has the hours an hour wrong at one end, because that is what happens to a thing that has been written out twice."

He stopped and looked at the three of them.

"A wall does not stop that. A wall starts it. The moment those three lines are on that board, every copy of them already in this city is wrong against a wall in a building where about four hundred people a week go past it, and every person in that hall holding a bad slip can say, and will say, that the office gave them different hours from the ones on the wall."

"That is the reason."

"That is the reason and it took me about four seconds and it is true, and I have not improved on it since June."

## What She Was Already Going To Do

"That is correct," said the supervisor, "and it does not get you the wall, and I would like it on the record that I told you on the seventh that the wall would stop about nine people a fortnight from knocking at the wrong end of a morning, and you have told me today that the wall would start about nine a fortnight being told they were wrong."

"I have not told you the wall is wrong. I have told you what a wall does."

"You have told me what a wall does," she said, "and the wall does that, and we are going to have it up, and there will be nothing on it but a number and four words about what the number is not."

"Then say that to the four hundred people a week and see what it does for them."

"We are going to say something," said the woman in the boardman's coat. "A hall that has asked a thing and been refused needs something on the wall where the ask is, because otherwise the next person who comes down it with a bad slip asks us the same thing and we have to refuse it again from nothing."

## Say It Again So That I Can Write It Down

He understood what was coming and he had about nine seconds to decide what to do about it and what he did about it was stand still.

"Say it slowly," she said, "and I will take it down here on the landing where it cannot be said that I took it down in the room."

"I am going to say it once more and you may write it."

"This office does not confirm, correct or supply the hours on its address in any copy, because a copy of those hours is a copy of a door, and this office keeps no roll of who comes through it. That is the whole of the position and there is a form of it anywhere else in this city that anybody may have."

She wrote it on the back of her hand, and then in a book, and the book was a hard-backed one that a board's clerk carries.

"Say it again."

"I said I was going to say it once more."

"You said you were going to say it once more and you said it about eleven seconds ago and I have written down about half of it and I am not going to put half of a thing on a wall."

So he said it again, on the fourth-floor landing, with the door wide behind him and about nine people in that passage, and she wrote down the second one, and the two of them are nearly the same and not the same, and the wall in that hall is going to have one of them under it for ever.

Then the supervisor put her own hand on the rail on the other side of him and asked him a question that was not about the wall.

"Is that the whole of it? Or is that what you are willing to have on a wall."

"It is the whole of it."

"Then it is on the wall at four o'clock this afternoon and it is the first thing that has ever been written about your office by anybody who is not you," said the supervisor. "I have spent nineteen years asking one institution in this city for a statement of what it is and you have handed it to me in a stairwell in about ninety seconds, and I want you to know that I would not have got it out of you in a room."

"I would have said it in a room."

"You would have said it in a room to about four people who work for you," said the supervisor, "and you would have said it again next week, and we would have gone without, and the wall would say that the office declined to say, which is a sentence people believe."

She went down the stair with her folder under her arm and the board's clerk went down with the book open in her hands reading the two versions against each other, and they were nine words apart, and the nine words were the whole of the difference between what he had meant and what a wall would say.

## What Is Left Of The Morning

Wenna Raut entered the date and the hour and the words that had been used on the landing, and left the space under them empty, and he did not correct her for doing it, because there was nothing to correct. It was a question asked in words and a refusal given in words and both of them were in the words used.

"You did not try the second time to make it shorter," she said.

"I made it longer. Nine words longer and it is the nine words on that wall."

"That is what I have been looking at since they went down the stair." She put the pen down on the line she had written. "You said the hours twice, and the first time it was about the hours and the second time it was about the office, and there was a woman with a book on that landing and she took the second one because the second one was about the thing she had come for."

"I heard it happen."

"You heard it happen and you went on saying it anyway. By then there were about nine people on that stair and you had decided that a reason said once in a passage is a reason four people have, and a reason said twice is a reason a division building has for ever."

She squared the pen against the table. "That is the first time in nine years I have watched you make a thing bigger on purpose to keep it from being changed, and it worked, and I want it in the book that it worked."

"Enter it."

"I am going to enter the refusal and not the reason you gave it, because the reason was for that wall and not for me." She wrote the hour against it. "And that is the whole of what this office did on the fourteenth of this month, and it is four words in my hand and about nine people on a stair, and every one of the nine heard a thing about a door that they will be able to tell somebody next week."

## What Went Up On The Wall

The wall went up on the eighteenth.

It has a figure on it and four words under the figure saying what that figure does not count, and under both of those a paragraph of about seventy words in a board's clerk's hand with no name on it, and the paragraph says that the office at the top of that stair does not confirm hours in any copy, does not correct a copy, and keeps no roll of who comes through the door.

It is on the wall because he said it twice on a landing, and the first of the two was true and the second of the two was nearly true, and a hall that has been refused a thing will always find somewhere to put the refusal.

The second of the two also said that this office keeps no roll of who comes through it, and that sentence has now been read out loud by about nine hundred people a week going past a wall for about a year, and every one of them who repeats it will describe a place with no list in it, and there is a place four flights up with no list in it, and they will find it inside a month.

And in a street two districts off there is a piece of paper in a woman's hand, folded twice and opened out again, with a building's name and the words RETURNS DESK at the bottom of it in a different hand, and a girl of about nine is going to be on that step on a working morning two hours before that office opens.

Four thousand and one hundred and twenty-three days. Less four thousand and one hundred and sixteen, the seventh of last month, is seven, and a division building was refused three lines of hours to its face at about eleven in the morning and was given seventy words to put on a wall instead, and about nine people a fortnight still arrive once at the wrong end of the hours.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and nine, the thirtieth of June, is fourteen, and the forty-sixth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fourteen of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of September.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand seven hundred and three days, which is fifty-six months and twenty-three days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
