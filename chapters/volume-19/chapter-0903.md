# A Promise Kept Eleven Years

Three thousand nine hundred and fifty-six was the twenty-seventh of this one and the fourth of the next is day three thousand nine hundred and sixty-three, and three thousand nine hundred and sixty-three less three thousand nine hundred and fifty-six is seven, and four days out of a month that ends on the thirtieth is seven by the month count with the crossing named as itself.

## Three Floors Of Washrooms

The branch office off the third court has three floors of washrooms, a stairwell and a bin room, and at half past three in the morning the four names on that rota page are spread across four floors and the only sound on any of them is water.

Petra Ilyin had the bottom floor and Lin Hao had the stairwell, which is what a man gets who has asked for the shift and has not asked for anything else.

She came down at about four with her sleeves pushed back past her elbows and a cloth over her shoulder, and found him at the bottom of that stair with a bucket, and stood there while he finished wringing a cloth she did not need him to wring.

"You are staying after it," she said.

"I am staying after it."

"Not for the floor. I know you are not for the floor." She took the cloth out of his hand and wrung it again herself, harder, and gave it back. "I have asked the woman below to hold my shift and I have said I will not be back before six, and she wants to know why, and I did not tell her, and she will work it out on her own before the week is out."

## The Woman At The Counter Below

"On the second of this month a woman asked her where to go," said Petra. "She had a piece of stiff grey card in her hand with three lines on it and she asked the woman below if it was true that there was a room four flights up where a person could ask about a paper. The woman below said she did not know, and the woman showed her the card anyway, and said, that is what it says, the hours."

She put the cloth on the bucket.

"I stood there and listened to my own address being read out in a shop by a woman selling bread, and I understood that the thing I have wanted to find for eleven years is now the easiest thing in this city to find, and that it is being handed round a counter with no name on it, and that the twelfth of this month is the day I go or I do not go, and I have told the woman below as much."

"Then go," said Lin Hao.

"I am going. That is why I have kept you on this stair until four in the morning."

## What Cannot Be Produced

She came down and perched herself on the bottom step with her boots off her heels and her back against the cold brick, which is a thing Petra Ilyin does the way other people take their coat off.

"I have been under a promise for eleven years," she said. "Not a paper one. Not one anybody can hand to a clerk and have read out in a corridor. One said to a person in a room, and it holds my body from that hour, and there is nothing in the world that shows it is there."

"Who made it."

"I remember the month. I remember the light in that room, coming in low and to the left, from a window with the catch broken. I do not remember the face. I remember saying yes and meaning it, and I have been meaning it every day since, and I cannot produce the person I said it to and neither can any office in this city and neither can I."

She turned her left wrist over. There is a pale band of skin there where a cord was worn years back, and it has been there since before he knew her and has never been written down anywhere.

"A promise binds without a paper," she said. "You say it to a person and it holds you. And you discharge it the same way, by saying to the person you said it to that it is done, and it is done, and not one other person alive can do it for you. A stranger cannot witness it, because a stranger cannot be spoken to the way that person has to be spoken to. A clerk cannot enter it because she was not spoken to at all."

"About four years back you went to a woman who keeps an index."

"And she said a sheet is not a person, and she was right, and she sent me home." Petra made a short sound that was not quite a laugh. "That is the whole of what this city has for it. A woman who keeps an index and a woman in a ward who told me what is taken out of a body is not itemised and the person it is taken from is never told. I left that room before she could ask me one more thing."

## What She Wants Him To Do

"Then what do you want from me," said Lin Hao.

"Not that." She looked up at him. "You cannot get me out of it and I know that, and if I had wanted a man to get me out of it I would have gone to a man on a white plate four months ago instead of standing on a stair in a washroom at four in the morning."

She stood up and put her boots back on properly.

"I want you to hold the week with me. I am going up four flights on the twelfth of this month and I am going to say out loud, in a room with a clerk at a table and the door open, that I have been under a promise for eleven years that no paper can produce and that I cannot find the person, and I know in advance that the office will not be able to do one single thing about it."

"And you want somebody there."

"I want somebody there who is not an instrument," she said. "That is all."

"You are rank one, the work you have been given is three floors of water and a bin room four nights in seven, there is something wrong behind your breastbone that a doctor would find in about four minutes, and in nine years you have never once offered anybody a thing you could not deliver."

"That is the whole of why I am asking you and not asking anyone else, and I am saying it out loud in a washroom because I would say it out loud on the stair too and I have never in eleven years said a sentence like this one in daylight."

"You will have me there on the twelfth."

"You will have me there on the twelfth," said Petra, "and you will not have solved it, and I am going to have to do the hardest part of it, which is stand up in front of two people who are good to me and be told no, and come down that stair with nothing in my hands, and I have wanted one person to be standing there for that for about nine years and I have not wanted him to fix it for about eight."

## The Door She Would Not Come Past

Her rooms are two rooms above a bakery off Cordwain with a stair that smells of yeast at all hours and a door that shuts with a wooden catch she has oiled herself.

Before the fire had gone down that night she would not come past her own threshold until she had said it twice, which Lin Hao understood completely and did not argue with, and he stood on the step in the cold while she said it again with the door open, and then he came in.

"You understand what that was," she said, with her hand still on the catch.

"You told me once at a table and once at a door and the rule of that room is that what is said on one side of it does not travel past it, so you are going to have to say it twice and you will not have said it to me until it has been said twice."

She laughed then, properly, and shut the door behind them both, and that was the threshold and neither of them named it and neither of them ever would.

## What She Wanted

"Now come here," she said, and took the cloth off her own shoulder and dropped it on the chair, and her hands were cold and her mouth was not. "I have wanted you since about ten o'clock on the second and I have had four nights of a stair and a woman selling bread telling me what to decide in the spring, and I am not going to sit in a chair and be brave at you tonight."

He said that he had a chest that would stop him twice, and she said she knew, she had known for years, she would wait for him and she would not wait politely.

She had his coat off and her own dress up over her head in about eleven seconds, and there was nothing reluctant in any part of her, and she said so out loud, twice, because she has never in her life let a thing about her own body go unsaid in front of a man.

## The Back Room

There is a bed against that wall, a stove with the fire banked down, a lamp on the shelf beside the shutter, and the rug in front of the stove with the weave gone through in the middle of it where two people have stood for years.

They used the rug because she was already standing on it.

He got his shirt open and she got the rest of it, and she ran her hands over the place under his breastbone where the old spent thing sits under bone and did not pity it and did not go round it, and he felt his breath shorten, which was not his chest.

"You have stopped once," she said. "Do not tell me about it."

He was hard in her hand before she had finished the sentence and she made a pleased noise at the ordinary end of it and got up onto her toes and took the weight of him backwards against the shuttered window with her fingers closed in his hair.

"Say the hours out loud," she said, and he said them, all of them, the start and the stop and the five days in seven, into her open mouth, and she laughed against his jaw and said that was not what she had meant and that she wanted it anyway.

Then she turned him round and took him from behind with one hand on his hip and the other reaching down and around for him, wet and hard in her palm already, and she pushed him into her from behind in one long slow stroke that made both of them swear, and the shuttered window gave a small knock against the frame each time she did it again.

She was tight and hot and very wet inside, and she rode back onto him with her fingers dug into his hip and her mouth open against the back of his neck, talking the whole time in a low voice about the twelfth of this month and what she was going to say in that room and how she was not going to cry.

"I am going to say it twice," she said, "because once is for the room and once is for me, and if I only say it once then it is a thing I did in a corridor."

He got his hand round her front and found her cunt swollen under two fingers, slick and open, and worked her in slow circles while she pushed back onto him, and her thighs began to shake against his hands and her breath came short.

She came first with her own fingers closed on herself, her body gone tight around him, a cry she did not try to hold back, and she held it four, five, six beats past it before she let go.

He put her down on the bed with her wool cover and her hair everywhere and came in over her, and she lifted her heels and took him inside her and told him to be slow and then told him not to be slow, and he managed neither for long.

When he spent she had her legs locked and her arms round his head and she said his name, and afterwards she lay with him a long time without wiping anything and without saying anything clever, and he lay with her and did not go, which is the thing he is worst at.

## The Week In Hand

Afterwards she warmed water on the stove and washed them both with it, and they dressed in the cold front room with the lamp turned right down, and he stayed until the ovens below went out and the whole of that stair smelled of cold yeast and old wood.

At the door she took his face in her two hands.

"You will come up four flights on the twelfth," she said, "and there will be a clerk at that table and the door will be open and there will be about four people in the passage, and I am going to say the thing, and you are going to stand there and not say one word that helps me, and afterwards I am going to walk home down that hill on my own."

"That is the arrangement."

"And on the second," she said, "a woman read my address out loud over bread. That is what made me brave. Three lines with no name on them, in a shop, out loud, by a woman who has never seen the door. It took eleven years for me to learn where to go and it took a piece of stiff grey card to make me go."

He went down that stair at about two in the morning with the cold in his coat and the taste of her still in his mouth and the twelfth of this month standing in front of him like a thing with a door at the end of it.

At the bottom, on the cobbles by the bakery's back door, a delivery man was telling another man where to go, out loud, in the street, from a piece of card he was holding in his fist.

He said the street. He said the door at the head of four flights off a third court. He said the hours, and he got them right, in about four seconds, to a stranger, in the street, at two in the morning.

Lin Hao stood on the cobbles and listened to his own door being given away for free and could not think of one thing to say about it.

Three thousand nine hundred and sixty-three days. Less three thousand nine hundred and fifty-six, the twenty-seventh of the month before this one, is seven, and a promise eleven years old was put on a table in two rooms above a bakery and not produced, and a delivery man in the street gave that door away in about four seconds.

*Less three thousand nine hundred and twenty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is thirty-four, and the forty-fourth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirty-four of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand five hundred and forty-three days, which is fifty-one months and thirteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
