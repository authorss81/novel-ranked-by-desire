# The Night Asking

Four thousand and one hundred and fifty-eight was the nineteenth of this one and the twenty-sixth of this one is day four thousand and one hundred and sixty-five, and four thousand and one hundred and sixty-five less four thousand and one hundred and fifty-eight is seven, and twenty-six less nineteen is seven by the month count.

## The Three Nights He Is Not In The Building

He had four nights in seven at a branch office off a third court and three nights off, and the twenty-sixth of this month was one of the three, and that is the whole reason anything happened.

There is no clock in that building. He had been awake since about one in the morning for the reason he is awake most nights, which is not a reason anybody else can use, and he came down four flights at a quarter past one for air and found the yard door open and went out into it and came back up four flights again because it was colder than the passage.

He was standing on the third landing at about two in the morning with his coat on and nothing in his hands.

The card on that door says a quarter to ten to half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour.

## Three Knocks On A Door Four Flights Up

Somebody knocked.

Not the office knock, which is two knuckles and a pause, because the people who live in that building do not use the office knock. Three knocks, ordinary, on a door at two in the morning, which is a door somebody lives behind.

He went up the last flight. The man on the other side of it was dressed for work with his collar up, and he had come in off a night shift and was not out of breath, and he knew exactly where he was.

"You are the man on the card," he said. "I have got about forty minutes before I have to be back out and I would rather say this standing up."

"Say it."

## Two Questions And The Second One

"My brother," he said. "He says this office is not a Bureau office. He says it answers questions and not forms. Is that right."

"That is right."

"Then does it hold anything about my brother."

And there it was, at two in the morning, on a landing, from a man who was about to have to go back out into the night.

The practice from the eighteenth of August of year eleven says an answer about a person is given with that person in the room, and no exception has ever been made, and there is no clerk in that building at two in the morning and no brother in that building at all.

"No," said Lin Hao. "It cannot. Not because you are here and not because of the hour. Because the answer would be about him and he is not in the room."

"Where is he."

"He is asleep, or he is at work, or he is in a room with a woman he does not speak to. I do not know which and I am not going to guess at it in a passage at two in the morning."

The man took that in about four seconds and then asked the question he had come for.

"And does this office keep anything about who comes up this stair."

## What He Said In The Dark

"No," he said.

It came out of him before he had decided on a shape for it, which is what happens to a man who has said a thing about nine times a fortnight for two months.

"Then how do you know how many come."

"I do not."

"You have got a book in there."

"The book has got rows in it. It has not got a roll of who walks up that stair in it, and it is not going to get one, and if anybody in this building ever asks me again for one I am going to say the same sentence in the same words, because a sentence that changes its words gets copied as two."

He heard himself do it and could not take it back and did not want to.

"Which division building."

"The one with the counter under the window in the passage off Cordwain, where a man is paid nothing and tells strangers where things are in about four seconds." The man said it without heat.

"I went in there on the nineteenth of this month because a woman at my work had written the hours out on the back of a form and got one end of them wrong. The man behind that counter told me which door in nine words without looking up. Both of them were being kind to me."

"And the form is still wrong at that end."

"The form is still wrong at that end," he said, "and he told me where to go anyway, and that is the arrangement, and you have just told me you are not going to do the other half of it. I came up four flights at two in the morning to find out whether that was true."

"That is a good answer," said the man. "That is the best answer anybody has given me in about four years, and I have been told the opposite by a man in a division building with a floor to it."

## What Opening A Door At Two In The Morning Does

He stood in that doorway for about nine seconds after the man had gone down.

The building has about nine people in it at two in the morning, counting the man who had just left. They are awake because they work nights, and every one of them knows which door on that fourth floor is the one with three lines on it. He had opened that door in the dark for about four minutes in a building where it has been shut at two in the morning for nine years.

A person who lives on the second floor was awake for it, and a person on the third, and neither of them will ever mention it. Both of them know.

There is no version of putting a door back.

"I will come in the morning," the man said, from the fourth landing, not going down yet. "At a quarter to ten. I want to ask it again in daylight and I want you to say the same thing."

"You will get the same thing."

## The Morning After, And The Hole

She got to the table at a quarter to ten with her coat off and her sleeves back and she said the hour out loud before she took her chair, which she does.

"What did you write in the book last night."

"Nothing at all."

Wenna Raut stopped with her arm going into her other sleeve.

"Nothing in it."

"There is no row from the twenty-sixth in that book and there has not been one since a quarter to ten this morning, and I have been down those stairs twice looking for it."

## Why It Cannot Go In, And Why It Still Could

He said it standing up, with the door open, because he had learned in the sixth month what happens to a price nobody hears.

"A man asked about his brother at two in the morning on a landing, and I said no, and then he asked whether this office keeps anything about who comes up that stair, and I said no, and then he asked how I know how many come, and I told him I do not."

"That is four sentences and it is the best thing you have said in six weeks."

"I cannot enter it."

"You cannot enter the answer, because the answer is about a person who was not in the room. The practice has been doing that job since the eighteenth of August of the year before last and it will go on doing it for ever." She took her chair and squared the book. "You can enter the asking. Not the words. The asking. The date, the hour, that a person asked at that door at that hour, and left."

"That would be the first row in nine years that is not in the words used."

"Yes," said Wenna Raut. "And you would get away with it, because the practice says both are entered in the words used and does not say what happens when there are none, and a clerk may only enter what she is given. You have given me the asking and withheld the rest, and that is the whole of your decision and you made it in about four seconds on a landing in the dark."

He stood there.

"Then I have left a hole in the first thing in that book."

"You have left a hole in it," she said, "and it is the first gap in that book since the second of December of the ninth year, and I am not going to paper over it, and I want you to say out loud in this room that you put it there. In about four years a stranger is going to come up four flights and ask why a row about a person can have a date and an hour and nothing in it."

He said it.

## What Is Left Of It

He did not say her name, and she did not ask it, and at about a quarter past ten the two of them worked through nine rows with the space under the first one empty and the date and the hour on it, which is not a hole so much as a door with the light off behind it.

About a quarter to six that evening he went down four flights with the wire round the buckle twice and did not put the book in the flat file until about ten past, because the flat file keeps a bound book and not tonight's, and tonight's was late.

The card on that door said nothing about two in the morning. The building has about nine people in it at two in the morning and from the twenty-sixth of this month it has a door that will open, once, without anybody deciding to open it.

He could not put that back and he could not find out how many people had heard it, and both of those were going to be true in the spring.

Four thousand and one hundred and sixty-five days. Less four thousand and one hundred and fifty-eight, the nineteenth of this one, is seven, and a door four flights up that had been shut in the dark for nine years was opened in it at about two in the morning, and the question that man brought was not entered, and the asking was, and the space under it is where the rest of it is.

*Less four thousand and one hundred and nine, the thirtieth of June, is fifty-six, and the forty-sixth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-six of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of this month.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand seven hundred and forty-five days, which is fifty-eight months and five days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
