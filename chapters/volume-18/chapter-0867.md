# A Row With A Space In It

Three thousand seven hundred and one was the twelfth of this month and the nineteenth is day three thousand seven hundred and eight, and three thousand seven hundred and eight less three thousand seven hundred and one is seven, and nineteen less twelve is seven by the month count.

## A Man With Something He Wanted To Give Somebody

He came up at about eleven with a small bundle in a cloth and he had it in both hands, and he had the look of a man who has decided that he is going to leave it here whatever anybody says.

He runs a cart on a third court and he has been in and out of that yard for about six years and in that time he has seen one man come out of that building in the dark every working night at about eleven, and in six years he has never seen a second person go in after him.

“I have something for him,” he said. “It is not a paper. It is a coat and a pair of gloves and it is his and it has been in my cart since the middle of last year and I have brought it down four hundred yards twice a month and I cannot put my hand on his shoulder because he is not a man you put a hand on.”

“We cannot take it.”

“You cannot take it,” said the man. “I have not asked you to take it. I have asked you whether you have ever written anything about him.”

## What The Office Holds About A Man Who Works Nights There

Wenna Raut went back through it. It took about four minutes and she did not have to look for it, which told her something she did not put into words.

“There is one row,” she said.

She turned the book round so that the page faced the man at the end of the table and she put her own finger on it before his hand was anywhere near the wood.

The twenty-third of August of the ninth year. A date at the head. An hour against the date.

And then a space in the middle of it.

Nothing has ever been written under it or in it. It has stood in that bound Register for six hundred and twenty-six days, which is twenty months and twenty-six days, with no hand going near it, and it is the only row in that book that has ever been entered and left empty by a person who was in the room.

The man with the bundle looked at it for about nine seconds.

“That is not an answer,” he said.

“No,” said Wenna Raut. “It is a row.”

## What The Space Is For

He looked at it again and then he looked at her and then he looked at the man at the end of the table.

“What is the space for,” he said.

The three of them looked at one another and the row answered nothing.

That is the whole of the answer and it took about four seconds and not a word was said in it, and the man with the bundle waited longer than that, because he had come down four flights with a coat and a pair of gloves and he wanted to go home able to say that somebody told him.

“It is for something,” said Lin Hao, “that was not written.”

“Written by who.”

“That is not a thing anybody in this room can tell you and you have been in the building nine minutes.”

The man nodded at that and took a while over it.

“Would it help,” he said, “if I told you when he comes out. I have seen it every night for six years and I could tell you the night and I could tell you what he was carrying and whether anybody was with him.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut.

“Why not, when you cannot tell me anything.”

“Because you would then be a person this office has written down,” said Wenna Raut, “and in about four years you would be a name against a date against an hour in a book, and he would be a second one.”

## The Question He Did Not Ask Out Loud

The man at the end of that table had been standing at the end of it for about eleven minutes and he had not said one word, and both of the sentences that were available to him were available for all of that eleven minutes and he knew the price of each of them to about the word.

He is a man with a spent chest who cleans washrooms at a branch office off a third court four nights and three off, and the plate in that coat has his name on it and nothing cut into it, and every row in the book on that table has a date and an hour against it and not a name on any of them anywhere.

If he says it, a row about a person gets a sentence in it in a clerk’s hand in the words he used, and that sentence would be the first thing anybody ever put in that Register that this office could be wrong about.

If he says nothing, a man who has run a cart on that court for six years goes home with a maybe, a coat and a pair of gloves stay in that cart for another month, and about four people in this city go on describing him by what he does at night.

He said nothing.

“I can take it back down,” said the man with the bundle.

“Yes.”

“And in four years I could come up and ask again and you would tell me whether there is a row, and you would not tell me whose.”

“Yes,” said Wenna Raut.

“That is the same arrangement as the fence and the man at the counter and the four years.”

“It is the same arrangement,” she said, “and you have worked out a thing about this office in nine minutes that about four hundred people have not, and I am not going to reward you for it, because the reward is the thing that makes people start.”

## Six Years Of A Man Coming Out Of That Door

The man with the bundle talked for about nine minutes without being asked to, and neither of the other two stopped him, and what he said was this.

The man comes out at about eleven and never before. He comes out through the side door and not the yard gate and he has never used the gate.

He stops on the cobbles outside the side door and washes his hands in the trough under the downpipe for the best part of a minute, which he does not need to do and has done every night in six years. Then he walks to the end of that court and stands there a while looking up the street, and then he eats, and then he goes, and he has never been drunk and he has never once been with anybody.

He carries things. In the spring he carried a box, and in the summer a folded sheet under his arm that looked like bedding, and once in the autumn he came out with a broom, put it against the wall, went away without it, and came back for it in the morning.

He has never been asked any of that by anybody in that yard in six years, and the man with the bundle said so twice, and the second time he said it he said it to Lin Hao and not to the clerk.

The book on that table had a date and an hour against the row and a space in the middle of it, and the hour was about eleven at night.

That is what the space is not for, and he did not say so in that room, and there were about nine seconds at the end of that man’s nine minutes in which the two of them could have said it and neither of them did.

## What The Man With The Bundle Did About It

He did not go straight back down to the cart.

He stood in the passage outside that door for the best part of nine minutes with the bundle in his hands, and about four people went past him in that time and every one of them let him stand there, which is the arrangement of that passage and has been for nine years.

Then he went down four flights and about four hundred yards and put the bundle back in the cart under a folded mat, in the place it has been since the middle of last year, and he kept it to himself in that yard.

He told one person. A man loading at the other end of that court asked why he had been away four hours and he said *it is nothing*, and the man loading did not ask again, and the sentence *it is nothing* is in this city now in one more mouth than it was that morning.

## Half Past Five

He went down at about twenty past four with the bundle still in both hands and he did not leave it, and at the bottom of the four flights he turned round and looked up the stairwell for about four seconds and then went out.

At about half past five the two people came back for the book and the strap went round it twice and was done up, and the woman with the strap looked at him where he was standing at the end of the table.

“You do not have to be here for this part,” she said.

“I know.”

“You have been here for every day of it.”

“I have not,” said Lin Hao. “I have been here for nine weeks out of about nine hours a day and the other part is a filing floor four floors down and about four hundred yards and nothing of mine is in it, and I have not been down there at half past five in nine years, and I am not going to start this week.”

She looked at him for about four seconds longer than the strap took.

“That is a strange thing to want,” she said, “and I am not going to ask about it because you will tell me, and I have got a cart of my own to be at by six.”

*Three thousand seven hundred and eight days. Less three thousand seven hundred and one, the twelfth of this month, is seven, and a man with a coat and a pair of gloves in a cloth asked whether this office held anything about the man who works nights on that third court, and it holds one row, and there is a space in the middle of it, and the three of them could not tell him what the space is for.*

*Less three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is forty-nine, and the forty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last forty-nine of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand two hundred and eighty-eight days, which is forty-two months and twenty-eight days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
