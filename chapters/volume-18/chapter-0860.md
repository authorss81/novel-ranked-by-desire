# The Register Comes Upstairs

Three thousand six hundred and fifty-three was the twenty-fourth of this month and the thirtieth is day three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine, and three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine less three thousand six hundred and fifty-three is six, and thirty less twenty-four is six by the month count, and the thirtieth is the last working day of the month and of the quarter.

## Two People In A Corridor Four Feet Wide

The book came up at about a quarter to ten on the morning of the thirtieth, carried by two people, one at the bottom edge and one at the strap, up four flights and about four hundred yards off, and it took about nine minutes and no one in that building said anything to either of them about it.

He met them in the corridor with the door open. It is about four feet wide and the stair turns twice below it and there is no window on the landing, and about four people go up and down it in an ordinary morning.

“Set it down on that table,” he said, “and not on anything else, and not under anything, and leave the strap where it is.”

Then he said the rest of it at the end of that table with the door open and about four people within earshot of him, which is where he has said every decision he has made since the second year.

“From this quarter the Register is carried up four flights and about four hundred yards off on working mornings by two people, and it stays in this room until about half past five, and any person may ask to have the page their own answer is written on turned in front of them, and not one row on it is read sideways.”

He had it worked out, both at the bottom of his own stair and at the end of that table, and it had taken Wenna Raut four sentences and him six days.

“That is the decision,” he said. “It is a practice, and a practice is not a line, and nothing goes under the foot of a page today.”

## What It Closes

“It closes one thing,” said Lin Hao, “and I am going to say it in one sentence and I would like the people in that corridor to get it, because it is the whole of what nine years has been for.”

“For nine years this office has said true things about pieces of paper and has had exactly one copy of them, and four hundred yards between that copy and the person it was about, and not one word of it could be checked from the paper it was given from. That is shut this morning.”

“If a man asks me in about nine years what we said to his wife in this room, I can put the book on that table, turn to the page, and let him read it, and the answer and the paper it came from will be the same object in his hand.”

He turned the book about a quarter on the table so that the page faced the two of them, and went near nothing else on it.

“That has never been true here before. Not once in nine years.”

What he had done this morning was put the only copy of what this office has ever said on a table in a room anybody may walk into, and the bill for it goes to a clerk who cannot leave that room.

He is still rank one, four nights and three off at a branch office, on the shift he asked for before anybody thought of offering him one, and the plate in that coat carries his name and nothing cut into it anywhere.

Nothing about that moved today, and he had not asked for it to.

## What It Costs, In About Nine Seconds

“And here is what it costs, and it takes about nine seconds and I am saying all of it because the door is open.”

“If that book is in this room then a clerk cannot leave this room, and about nine hours of every working day I am not able to go out of a door I have been out of nine thousand times, and I am paid the same wage.”

“That book is about nine years old and it is signed out of a flat file by two people who both have to be back before this door shuts. At about six o'clock it either goes down four flights in the dark by two people or it stays in here until the morning.”

“About four rows in every nine are about a person and not about a piece of paper the person brought up themselves. And a book lying out in a room anybody can walk into can be read from the doorway by anybody standing in the doorway.”

He stopped there and neither of them said anything else about it.

“Every one of us in this room knows that last one,” he said. “We are doing it anyway, and I did not find it and write it down, and I would not have said it if I had.”

## The Number At The Top Of It

The two people who had carried it up were still standing in that corridor at the end of the table with their hands empty and nothing to do, and he turned round and looked at the top of the page.

That number has not moved since the thirtieth of March of the fifth year. Eight hundred and fifty-two.

He said it to the two of them rather than to the room, because they were the ones holding the thing it was printed on.

“Unmoved across twenty-five sheets and twenty-four quarters. Those two counts are both right and they differ by one, and the reason is that the first sheet ever written in this city is a sheet and has never been a quarter-end.”

“Three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine less one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine is two thousand one hundred and sixty, and two thousand one hundred and sixty is twenty-four times ninety, and the twenty-four is the number of quarters and the ninety is the days in one, and the whole of it has not moved and it is not going to.”

“Is it on every page.”

“It is at the top of the page that goes out to about two thousand counters four times a year,” he said, “and it is not going to move and it is not going to be given a second copy, and you two have just carried nine years of it up four flights and you may both look at it for as long as you like.”

## At Four O'Clock In The Passage

At about four he went down about four hundred yards to the passage off Cordwain.

The fortieth of those sheets had been face up on that rail for ninety days and it came off at four, and the forty-first went up in its place, and it carried no new line in that office’s hand, because the sixth line of the second of December of the year before last is still the last one this office will ever make at the foot of one of its own pages.

The counterman read all six lines of the foot of it out at the speed he reads everything else to about eleven people standing in that passage in the last of the light on the last working day of the quarter, which is what a quarter-end carries and not nine, and he did not know that there was a book in a room four hundred yards away with that same six lines on the bottom of it, and he was not going to be told.

## Half Past Five

The book was on that table from about a quarter to ten until about half past five, and in that time about nine people came up four flights.

About four of them came about a piece of paper they had brought up themselves. About two of them came about another person, and one of those two asked to have the page her own answer was written on turned in front of her.

It took about nine minutes to find and it was the right page, and no row on it was read sideways, because about four people were standing in the way of it and could not have read a bound page at a glance if they had tried.

At about half past five the two people came back and lifted it and the strap went round it twice and the cord was done up about nine thousand and one times, and they carried it down four flights and about four hundred yards and put it into a steel drawer with a handle on a filing floor, and it was signed out and signed back in on two lines that are not in that book.

He stood in the corridor after they had gone down.

Four people were on that landing waiting for the stair, at the end of the third month, with the door of that room open behind them. Behind them, in the room, a clerk squared a page against the wood and turned it, the way anybody turns one at the end of a day, and the sound of it came out into a corridor about four feet wide and reached all four of them.

One of them asked the other what the noise was, and the other one said *a page*, and the third of them said *a book*, and none of the four knew where it had come from or where it had gone or that a woman four floors up had had her own page turned in front of her for the first time in the history of that building.

*Three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine days. Less three thousand six hundred and fifty-three, the twenty-fourth of this month, is six, and the thirtieth of March is the last working day of the quarter, and a bound Register went up four flights about four hundred yards off at about a quarter to ten on two pairs of arms and came down again at about half past five, and about nine people came up those stairs in between.*

*And about one of them had her own page turned in front of her.*

*Less three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is ninety, and the fortieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last ninety of them and came down at four this afternoon, and the forty-first went up in its place carrying no new line, and a man who is paid nothing read all six lines of the foot of it out to about eleven people at the same speed he reads everything else.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand two hundred and thirty-nine days, which is forty-one months and nine days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
