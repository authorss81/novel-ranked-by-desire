# The Two Men At One Rail

Three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine was the thirtieth of the month before this one and the first of this one is day three thousand six hundred, and three thousand six hundred less three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine is thirty-one, and no month in this city has a thirty-first in it, and those thirty-one days are a door standing open and not an error in a sum.

## What Came Of The Thirtieth Of December

On the thirtieth of December, at about four in the afternoon, at a counter under a window in a passage off Cordwain, a man who is paid nothing and a man with four words of pencil paper in his coat stood about four feet apart for about eleven minutes while about nine people watched, and nothing in this city ended it.

Not one of those nine could have told you afterwards which of the two had read that page right. Two of them said afterwards that the man behind the counter was right, and two said afterwards that the man in the coat was, and it is the same four people in both halves, and in about four months none of the four will remember which half they were in.

One of the nine said one thing out loud that afternoon, and it is in the city, and it cannot be got back in, and it is not going on a page and no person who was in that passage is going to say what it was.

Whether either of the two has been back to that rail since is not a thing anybody in that room knows, and it was not said out loud this morning either, because a man who says that at the end of his own table at nine in the morning is buying himself about four minutes and will pay for them somewhere else.

## The Morning He Is Not On

He was in that room at nine in the morning on the first of February and he is not on the rota at all today, and the rota page on the wall behind the door carries four names and his is the first of them, and the shift is four nights and three off at a branch office and he asked for it, and the plate in his coat has his name on it and no figure cut into it anywhere.

That matters about nine times a year. It matters on the first morning of a month because a month is the one thing a man on a night rota can be sure of, and he had come up four flights at about a quarter to eight on his own time to hear what came of the thirtieth of December before about nine people arrived and made it into a thing other people had to hold.

He had wanted it ended. He had wanted it ended since the following morning and he had not managed it and had not slept badly, which was the part he found he had not expected.

## One Hundred And Twenty Months

He said the next of it out loud because Wenna Raut was in the room and the door was open and about four people heard all of it, which is where he says the things he has decided.

“This office was a room on the second of December of the second year and this morning it is one hundred and twenty months old. Three thousand six hundred days is thirty times one hundred and twenty with nothing over, and that is exact.”

“And what of it.”

“It is the fifth time in nine years that a figure about this office has come out exact,” said Lin Hao. “The four before this one were two thousand one hundred and sixty, and two thousand five hundred and twenty, and two thousand eight hundred and eighty, and three thousand two hundred and forty, and each of the four was thirty times a number with nothing over, and each of the five has been written down once and has never once been asked for.”

“Who wrote them down.”

“I wrote them down and you entered them,” he said, “and not one person in this city has come up four flights in nine years and asked what any of the five was for.”

Wenna Raut put a month and an hour on the outside of a fold and nothing else anywhere on it, which she does about four times a morning, and this one had no question behind it and went down to a woman who had asked about a drain.

## Nine In The Month That Ended Yesterday

She told him the month at about a quarter past nine while she squared the standing sheet back onto the shelf behind the door.

“Nine came up those four flights on the days the door stood open all last month. All nine went into that book in the words they were used in, and I have not counted them since and I am not going to.”

“Not one of the nine was about anybody who is dead.”

“Not one of the nine was about anybody who is dead,” said Wenna Raut, “and I know that without being told, because a question about a dead man has to arrive as a question about a piece of paper first. The paper is the only door in and the man cannot come up it and say what he would have said.”

“That is the arrangement and not an opinion.”

“It is the arrangement and it has held for the whole of the time you have had it,” she said, “and this is the first month it has held for nine questions in a row with nothing odd in any of them, and I wanted you to have the good month before the other kind of morning arrives.”

“Where is the other kind.”

“You know where it is,” said Wenna Raut, “and I am not going to hand it to you on the first of a month to save you about four minutes.”

## A Woman At About Eleven With A Death On A Paper

The eleventh one came up at about eleven and she had a sheet of her own in her hand that had been folded once and folded back, and folded once and folded back, and folded a good many times before that.

She took the chair at the end of the table before she said anything, which about two people in a month do.

“I want to know what this office holds about my husband.”

“What is his name.”

“That is not the question I came up with,” said the woman, and she said it without any weight on it at all, as though she had told the stairs on her way up. “He died on the fourth of the month that ended yesterday. I have the paper and it is in this. I want to know whether you have ever been asked anything about him, and I want to know whether he is in that book.”

“He died twenty-seven days ago.”

“Yes.”

Wenna Raut took her pen off the tray and squared it against the edge and then did not put it in the book.

“We have about four hundred and seventy rows in there,” she said, “and that is the last time anybody counted them, which was the third of June of the year before last, and there have been rows added since and not counted, and I am the person who would know and I am telling you now instead of at four o'clock this afternoon because you have not got anybody with you.”

“Thank you.”

“Do not thank me, I have not done anything yet,” said Wenna Raut. “I have told you the size of it. What I have not told you is what is in it, and there is a difference, and the difference is the rest of this morning.”

“Then tell me the rest of it now.”

“No, because you have come up four flights on a morning you are not on and you have not slept and you have decided something before you got here,” said Wenna Raut, “and if I tell you the rest of it in the next four minutes you will make a decision about this office at about eleven o’clock on the first of February because a woman you have never met has a piece of paper in her hand.”

“I have not decided anything.”

“You decided to come up at a quarter to eight,” said Wenna Raut.

He took the chair at the end of the table, which he does about four times a year.

The woman turned her sheet round and pushed it about four inches along the wood towards him, and he did not touch it.

“He was in a trade,” she said. “Not one you would know. And he was not ill for long and I was not there for the end of it, which is a different thing and I am not asking you about that either. What I am asking is whether anybody in this building has ever had his name.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut.

“That was quick.”

“It is quick every time,” said Wenna Raut, “and it has been quick for nine years, and the reason it is quick is that a book with names in it is a book that can be asked about a person instead of a piece of paper, and this one was a list once, and I was here when it was.”

She opened the book on the table and squared it to the wood, and there was about four hundred and seventy rows in it with a date against every one and an hour against every date, and not one name on any of them anywhere, and she had the corner of her own forearm along the spine to hold it open.

And neither of the three of them had said so yet.

*Three thousand six hundred days. Less three thousand five hundred and seventy-three, the fourth of the month before this one, is twenty-seven, and that is the day a man died, and nine questions came up four flights in the thirty days around it and not one of the nine was about him.*

*Less three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is thirty-one, and the fortieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirty-one of them, and two men stood at that rail on the afternoon of that thirtieth and nothing came of it.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand one hundred and eighty days, which is thirty-nine months and ten days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*

*Less three thousand and eighty-two, the twenty-third of August of the ninth year, is five hundred and eighteen days, which is seventeen months and eight days, and a row with a space in the middle of it stood in that bound Register for every one of them with no hand going near it, and the inside of a coat on the left is empty and has been since the third of December.*
