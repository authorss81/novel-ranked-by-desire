# The Question That Is Not About A Paper

Three thousand eight hundred and ninety-six was the twenty-seventh of the month before this one and the third of this one is day three thousand nine hundred and two, and three thousand nine hundred and two less three thousand eight hundred and ninety-six is six, and three days out of a month that begins on the first is three by the month count with the crossing named as itself.

## She Asks For It In Writing

She came up at about ten with a shawl round her shoulders and a paper of her own folded in her fist, and stood at that bench with the door open and about three people in that passage, and said it plainly, the way people say things they have rehearsed on the way up four flights.

"My husband died in the tenth month," she said. "A woman in my lane has a stamped paper that says her husband's rent was paid. I do not want a paper about rent. I want this office to say in writing that my husband was a good man."

Wenna Raut stopped with the pen half up, and Lin Hao came to the end of that table, and the room went quiet in the way it does when a person has asked for the one thing that room cannot give.

"Say your question again," said Lin Hao, gently, "so the clerk may enter it in the words you used."

She said it again. The clerk entered the date and the hour at the head of a row, and the words, exactly as she used them, and left a space under them where an answer would go, and put the pen down.

## The Worst Thing

He stood there with both sentences available to him and felt the whole of nine years narrow to the width of that table, because since the tenth of June an answer about a person is read out in that room once, in the voice that office reads everything in, with the person it is about in the room and the person who asked in the room, and if the person it is about will not come then there is no answer and the row stands as it is.

Her husband will not come. Her husband is dead. And since the eighteenth of August an answer about a person is given only with that person in the room, with no exception for the dead, and none for anybody.

"I cannot give you what you ask," said Lin Hao. "Not because it is not true. I do not know whether it is true, and this office has never once been able to say what a paper is about, and it has never once been wrong about a paper, and neither half reaches a man."

"Then say he was good. You have his row. You read it to me in the autumn."

"Three times," said Lin Hao. "On the twenty-fifth of August, in a room with the door open, with four lines about a boundary, and the practice had been standing seven days at that point, and you were not to have been read to at all."

"I did that," said Lin Hao, and did not go straight on with it. "It was me at the end of that table, and I have not found a way to put it right since, and you have come up tonight for a paper about a man and I am going to have to tell you no, and the reason I have to is the thing I did to you in August."

She looked at him for a while.

"You read it three times because he would not come up the stair."

"He would not come up the stair," said Lin Hao. "And I read it to you three times because it was mine to give and not hers, which is the whole error, and I did not have the practice yet that says so."

"I made that practice on the eighteenth of August with about nine people in that room as witnesses," said Lin Hao. "And it is that practice which is stopping me answering you tonight, and I made it three months after I had done this to you and not one day before."

She looked at him, and he saw her understand, the way people understand in that room, that he was going to refuse her and that the refusal was correct.

"Put the refusal in writing," she said. "If you will not put the other thing in writing, put that in writing, so I can carry something down four flights besides my own words."

And that was the thing that had not happened in nine years. Every refusal that office ever made was said out loud, in the voice it reads everything in, once, with the door open, and left nowhere. This was the first refusal in nine years that was asked to be refused in writing instead of said out loud.

He wrote it himself, on a paper of that office, in his own hand, that the office cannot say in writing that a man was good, and that the row stands as it is, with the date and the hour on the outside and nothing else, and Wenna Raut witnessed the date and the hour and nothing else, and he gave it to her with both hands.

She took it, and folded it once, and put it in her shawl, and went down four flights with a refusal in the office's hand where no refusal has ever been before, and it was correct, and it was the worst thing that office has ever done.

## The Row After She Goes

Wenna Raut entered the hour the woman went down, in the clerk's hand, under the row, with the date against it, and left the space under the refusal empty, because there is nothing further that room can put to a question that is not about a paper.

"She will show it in the lane," said Wenna Raut. "To strangers. To neighbours. To the woman with the stamped paper about rent. And not one of them will learn whether her husband was a good man, because that is the thing a piece of paper cannot do, and we have just handed her the paper to find out."

"And what will she say about us."

"That we read to her when she came up, and refused her in writing when she asked for writing, and both are true, and both will go home in heads that cannot be got back out of them. A refusal on paper is heavier than a refusal said out loud, and it travels further, and we have just learned how far."

Lin Hao stood at that table with his hands behind his back and felt the room larger and colder than it had been in the morning, as if the walls had moved a finger's breadth outward to make place for the first written refusal in nine years.

After she had gone Wenna Raut looked at the row with nothing under it but a refusal, and Lin Hao stood at the window and hated himself with a clean, steady hatred that surprised him by how much relief was in it, because at least he had not lied to her.

## What Writing Does

Wenna Raut took the copy of the refusal that office keeps, in the clerk's hand, with the date and the hour and the words used, and entered under it, in the same hand, that the refusal was given in writing at the woman's asking, and that no refusal in that room has been given in writing before.

"That is the second thing today that has never happened," said Wenna Raut. "The first was her asking. The second is us answering on paper that will go down four flights into a house where we cannot follow it."

"And what will come of it."

"About nine people a month come up those four flights, and about four of the nine are about a person. Every one of them will hear, within half a year, that a refusal went down in writing, and every one of them will ask for the same, and this office will have to decide whether the first time was the rule or the exception."

He stood at that window with the yard empty below him and felt the weight of what he had done come down over him like a coat that does not fit, because he had been correct and it was the worst thing, and correctness had never felt like that before.

## The Evening After

That evening he asked the carriers to go ahead without him and came down late, alone, with that book against his chest in the dark, and the strap cut into his shoulder through the coat, and at the half landing with no window in it he stopped because his left arm had gone, and he put that book down on the step for about ten seconds, and picked it up again.

## The Shawl At The Bottom

He thought all the way down about the shawl she had folded the refusal into, and about hands that fold paper once before putting it away, and about what she would find when she opened it at home in lamplight with neighbours round her.

A refusal in an office hand, with the date and the hour on the outside and nothing else. Correct. The worst thing. The first in nine years to go down four flights on paper instead of living one hour in air with the door open and then living only in heads.

At the bottom he stood in the yard with his arms hanging and looked up at the four flights of windows without moving, and the cold took the smell of ink off his fingers, and he knew he would remember the weight of the pencil in his own hand longer than he would remember any finding that year, because the pencil had done what nine years of voice had never done.

A book that is put down once is a book a person knows how to carry. A woman with a bad back told him that on her way out of that room with the strap over her shoulder, and he had not understood her until his arm went on that landing and the step took the weight without judgment.

At the bottom he got that book into the flat file with the heel of his hand and stood there in the dark with his breath going white, and thought about a woman in a shawl going down a hill with a refusal in an office hand in her fist, and what she would say at the bottom of that hill when somebody asked her what she had been given.

He stood there longer than the work required, with the file drawer shut and the yard empty, and thought about nine years of refusals said out loud with the door open, and how light they were beside one sheet of paper going down a hill in a shawl, and how heavy correctness can be when it is carried in the hand instead of spoken in air.

Three thousand nine hundred and two days. Less three thousand eight hundred and ninety-six, the twenty-seventh of the month before this one, is six, and a woman asked that office to say in writing that her husband was a good man, and it refused, correctly, in writing, for the first time in nine years.

*Less three thousand eight hundred and thirty-nine, the thirtieth of September, is sixty-three, and the forty-third sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last sixty-three of them, and it does not move until the last working day of the year.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand four hundred and eighty-two days, which is forty-nine months and twelve days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
