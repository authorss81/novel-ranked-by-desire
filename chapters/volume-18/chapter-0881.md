# The Book Does Not Go Down

The Register was lying open on that table on the twenty-fifth of this month with a page spread and nothing whatever on it, which had not happened in that room since the thirtieth of March, and Wenna Raut had two fingers on the edge of it in case the air in the room got at the corner.

It was twenty minutes to five. The stair had been loud since four and had gone quiet in the last few minutes the way it does when the last person of the day is about to be called.

"Read it to me again," said the woman.

"That is the third time, Mrs Agger."

"I know it is the third time. My husband is at the bottom of your stair and he will not come up it, and you have now told me twice that you will not put one word of him in my hands without him in this room, and I would like the third time before I go back down and ask him why."

She said it without any temper in it, which he had not expected, and the two of them went back two hours.

## Two O'Clock On A Half Landing

He went down at about two for a second lamp for the corridor, because the one at the stair head has been failing since the spring, and on the way he came to the half landing between the second and third floors, which has not had a window in it since the building went up, and the man was on the step with his parcel under his knees.

Three years of working afternoons at about two. That landing is not on any wage in this building and was not put there by anybody in it. Lin Hao had gone past that man about two hundred working afternoons and had not learned one thing about him, in a building that holds the words of four hundred people who came up those stairs and cannot say what any of them are about.

He stopped, which he had not done before.

"Good evening," said Lin Hao.

The man lifted his head about an inch. "Evening."

And that was the whole of it. Lin Hao stood there another four seconds with a lamp in his hand like a man who has been handed something and has not found the pocket for it, and then went down the rest of that flight and across the yard and back up, carrying the lamp, and thought about the four seconds for the rest of the afternoon.

## What The Ninth Month Held

She came up at about half past three with a paper wrapped in a cloth and did not open the cloth, and about nine people had come up those flights that day before her, and she was the last of them, which was unlucky for everybody in the room.

He took the paper off her and looked at it without reading it, which is what you do with a paper that whoever brought it has already read, and set it on the corner of the table where it could not be confused with anything else.

"He has not brought it up himself today."

"He has not been out of the house since the spring. He says if I say anything about a border he will go and sit in the front room and not come back to it. He does that now about most things and I have stopped arguing him out of it."

Then the finding began.

The row was dated the eleventh of the ninth month of last year and it had taken the best part of an hour and a half, because that winter's hand was small and tight and four pages had to be gone through twice.

What was on the page was a man asking whether a boundary ran where a paper of his said it ran, and under it four lines in Wenna Raut's hand, and the boundary did run there, and that is true nine times in ten, and it tells his wife at the end of that lane that she has been right for two years.

"Read the four lines."

"I read the four lines. I read them twice and I read the words above them three times. That is the whole of what is on that page and it does not carry his name."

"And you have told me that before."

"And I will tell it you again at the end of the day if you want it at the end of the day."

She put her fingers on the table on either side of the Register without touching it, which is as near as a polite person gets to a stranger's book, and Lin Hao understood why and said nothing about it.

## The Fold That Is Not Here

"He came up on the eleventh of the ninth month at about two in the afternoon," said Lin Hao, "and he brought the paper himself. And because he brought it himself the answer went into a sealed fold, gummed at the flap, with a date and an hour written on the outside of it and nothing else, and the carrier wrote down whose hand took it and in what order."

"And I have looked."

"You have looked. That fold is not in this house. That is a true thing and I am sorry it is a true thing."

"Then what is it, then."

"That is the office's own copy of what was said to him, and it exists because every answer goes in twice, once for him and once here. It carries no name because nothing in that Register carries a name, and it is the only copy in this city of those four lines, because this office burned the other one in November of last year and is not going to make another."

She looked at the page for a long moment and then said the thing he had known she would say.

"Then you have a piece of paper about my husband and I am not allowed to hold it."

## Five To Five

She went down at five to five and he let her go and stood at the window and watched her cross the yard and out through the arch, and then he counted, and the count took four minutes.

She came back up those steps at about two a minute, with her coat open and the cold of the yard still on her and the look of a woman who has asked a man a question and got the answer she expected.

"He says no."

"Does he want to know why."

"He says he does not want to know why. He says if it needs him in a room then it is about a boundary, and a boundary is a thing you say out loud in a lane." She looked at the Register without touching it. "He says and tell them I have not the legs for four flights and I am not going to be got."

"He has said that before."

"He has said it about three hundred times since the spring. This is the first of those three hundred times that anybody in this city has written down what he said."

Wenna Raut stopped writing.

"You have got about four minutes with her and then she goes," said Wenna Raut. "Say it now and say it short."

## What She Was Given Instead

There were three things and he gave her all three, in the order there were only three in.

"You may have the reading as often as you will come up for it, and the finding is done and does not have to be done again, so it is only the walking."

"You may have the date and the hour in your own hand on a paper of your own if you write it yourself, and the clerk will witness that you wrote it and not that it came from here."

"And you may have my name and the room and the hours, and you may send anybody with the date and the hour and ask for the reading, and I will do it as if you had come up yourself."

"You will do it as if I had come up myself."

"Yes. And that is the one of the three that costs me anything, and I am telling you which one it is so that you can decide whether you want it."

She thought about that longer than he expected, and then she said she would take the first and the second and not the third, because she did not want to be a woman who sends somebody.

"Then take the first and the second, and come up at the start of next week, and if I am not here I will leave word at the bottom of that stair, in words, where anybody standing there can read it."

## Half Past Five And The Carriers Going

They came at half past five with the strap and stopped in the doorway when they saw the page out and the two of them at the table, and the one with the bad back said the thing she says every time she comes into a room and finds a page out, and said it without knowing she had said it.

"Take it down at the end of the week," said Lin Hao.

"No," said the woman with the back. "A book that has been open gets carried open, and it went into my shoulder in April on the second landing and I have thought about it every day since. Take it down now or it sleeps in that room, and if it sleeps in that room you will be having this argument with me at seven one morning in the winter and I will be the one saying it."

"I will carry it."

Neither of them said anything for a moment. The strap went back over the woman's shoulder, and they went down four flights and about four hundred yards on their own two pairs of legs, and the door of that room stood open behind them.

## Down Four Flights Alone

He waited until their footsteps had gone off the yard stones, and then he got the strap round it himself, which took three attempts because the buckle is on the wrong side of the book for a man working alone, and then he stood in the doorway at about twenty to six with nine years of other people's words against his chest and the last of the light going grey off the glass.

Four flights. About four hundred yards. Nine minutes with two people who both have to be back.

He went down the first two fast, because that is the easy part and there is light in that stairwell, and then the stair turned at the half landing with no window in it and he was in the dark with his arms full and the whole weight of that book under them and nowhere to put it down.

It took him fourteen minutes and he was at the bottom with his left arm gone entirely.

He stood in the yard and could not get his fingers open, which was a new thing and lasted about a minute, and he could not have told anybody what his hand was doing because he could not feel it doing it.

The yard was cold and empty and the lamp in the filing room was out, and he found the flat file by the door and got the book into it with the heel of his hand and stood there in the dark with his own breath going white in front of him.

He stood there longer than he meant to. Nine years of other people's words in a flat file in a room on a filing floor, shut the way the drawer in that room upstairs is shut and for a different reason, and the difference was that this one is opened every morning by two people who both have to be back.

Then he came back up, four flights, on his legs, and at the half landing between the second and third floors in the dark he stopped, because he could not help it, and he said good evening out loud to an empty step.

There was nothing on it, which he had known before he opened his mouth, and he had said it anyway, and it went up the stairwell and came back at him off the newel, and it was the first thing he had said all evening that was not a sentence about work.

Three thousand eight hundred and four days. Less three thousand seven hundred and ninety-seven, the eighteenth of the month before this one, is seven, and the book did not go down at half past five, and it went down at about ten to six by one pair of hands instead of two.

*Less three thousand seven hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is fifty-five, and the forty-second sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-five of them, and that rail does not move until the thirtieth of this month.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand three hundred and eighty-four days, which is forty-six months and four days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
