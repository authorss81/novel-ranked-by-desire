# Three People Who Will Carry It

Three thousand eight hundred and forty-six was the seventh of this one and the thirteenth of this one is day three thousand eight hundred and fifty-two, and three thousand eight hundred and fifty-two less three thousand eight hundred and forty-six is six, and thirteen less seven is six by the month count.

## The Question Put To Three People

He asked three people between the seventh and the thirteenth of this month and he asked them the same sentence each time, and he wrote it out first on a piece of paper of his own so that the three of them were answering the same words.

The sentence was: *From this week, that book goes down four floors and about four hundred yards at half past five every day of the week, in the dark, five days in seven, and I am asking whether you will do it.*

He had already decided that he would be doing it himself. He had not decided that on paper and he wrote the sentence out anyway, because a man who has decided and still asks is a man who has not decided, and he wanted to know which of the three of them would tell him that.

## The Paper He Wrote It On

He wrote it on a piece of paper of his own, which is not the same thing as writing it in that book, and the difference is worth about nine years to him and about nothing at all to anybody else.

Nothing was entered.

The clerk may only enter what she is given, and he gave her nothing, and a practice that begins on a slip in a man's own pocket is not a practice in a room anybody can walk into, and if it is not in a room then it is not in this office, and if it is not in this office then it can be spoken about on a hill by two people and it has to survive being spoken about, which is a harder thing than being written down.

He folded it into four and put it in the breast pocket on the outside of that coat, and he understood as he did it that the pocket on the inside left of that coat has not held anything since the turn of last year and is going to keep being empty while he is the man who stands at that table, and that a man who has just been told no three times keeps his evidence where anybody standing in the room can see it.

## The One With The Back

The woman with the back had been carrying it since the seventh of April and had said in June, once, that she could not do this in the autumn.

"No," she said. "And you did not need to ask me, and I am not going to say anything about your asking, because you asked, and that is a different thing from being told."

"What do you mean."

"I mean that if you had told me in June I would have said no and then carried it anyway for another four months and hated you by the end of the fourth."

"And if you had told me in June I would have said no and been sacked for it, because you are rank one and I am a woman who carries paper for a wage, and you can end that with one sentence."

She shifted the strap off her shoulder and put it over the back of the chair.

"Asking means I get to have said no, and there is a great deal of difference between that and being told, and the difference is about nine years of my life and it is not a small thing to me."

"You will not do it at all after this week."

"Not at half past five in the dark, five days in seven, not with what I have got in that shoulder, and I will not pretend otherwise to be kind to you about the other mornings."

## The Clerk In The Room

He asked Wenna Raut on the second working day and asked her at the table with the door open, because a thing put to a clerk in a passage is a different thing from a thing put to a clerk at her table.

"I cannot," she said, and then she gave him the four minutes of reasons in the order she had them, and the fourth one was the one that mattered and she put it last on purpose.

"From the thirtieth of March that book is in this room from a quarter to ten until half past five and there has not been one morning when a person came up and found a shut door."

"If I am on that stair at half past five then the last asking of the day waits, and from the eighteenth of August the last asking of the day is a question about a person, and a question about a person is the one that cannot wait until tomorrow, and I am the one who says so at the door."

"You could say it at half past six instead."

"I could, and the answer would be the same, and you know it is, because you are asking me to be somewhere else between half past five and half past six every day for the rest of the year, and no version of that leaves the room open at half past five." She set the pen down.

"And I will tell you the other thing, since you have asked me in front of the door. You are not asking two people this week. You have asked two people and a man at a counter, and all three of us are going to say no, and you have known that since the twenty-fifth of August when you carried it down that stair on your own, and you are doing this because the twenty-fifth happened and you have not put it down since."

## The Man Who Is Paid Nothing

The counterman had never been inside that room and had to be fetched up four flights, and he came with the quarterly sheet in a flat box under his arm because that is what he had with him.

He read the sentence on the paper twice and then handed it back.

"Yes," said the counterman. "And I will tell you the true thing about it, because you have asked me and I have nothing else to give anybody, which is the only reason I say yes to anything."

"Go on."

"A book that weighs what that book weighs does not want two people. It wants one person who does not stop."

He put the flat box on the end of that table like a man putting down something he has been carrying.

"Four hundred times I have watched two women do that landing with it. Twice one of them has had to stop and put it down on the step and the other one has taken the weight on her own hip and they have gone on."

"And every time that has happened a person has come past that landing who was not going to, because a person carrying a thing with somebody is a small thing and a person carrying a thing alone is a big thing, and this city stops for the big one."

"And the other half of it."

"The other half of it is that you will not do it," said the counterman, "because a man who is paid nothing and reads the foot of a sheet out loud to whoever stands at his rail is the last pair of hands nine years of other people's words ought to go through every evening."

"You would not put that on me at half past five and you have not asked me that, and I would have said yes anyway, and that is how I know you will not do it and I have not been insulted."

"Why not."

"Because a man at a rail gets asked things." He said it in the same flat voice he reads the foot of that sheet in.

"You get asked whether you can go in without knocking. I get asked whether you can go in without knocking, and if I am the one who carries the book down four flights every evening of the year then in about two months there is a woman in this city who knows that the stair at the back of the court is open at half past six and not at ten, and she will tell a man at a counter, and the man at the counter will be me."

He looked at the four walls of that room as though checking them.

"You said no," said Lin Hao.

"I said yes and then I said the part that makes yes a bad answer," said the counterman. "You asked me one question and I have given you two, which is more out of that room than anybody has given me in nine years, and I would like the morning to end now."

## The Sentence That Decided It Without Deciding It

The woman with the back came up on the thirteenth at half past four to get the strap and found him standing in the middle of that room with the book shut on the table and the lamp on.

"You have been up here all afternoon not doing anything," she said. "That is not a thing you do."

"I have been doing the sum. Five days in seven, about two hundred mornings in a year, and I have a chest that was spent in the fourth year and four nights a week on a branch office that I asked for, and the sum does not close."

"It does not close because you are doing it wrong." She got the strap off the chair and over her shoulder the way she has got it over her shoulder about four hundred times.

"I have carried that book five months. Five months of the two of us taking it up four flights in the morning and down four flights at night, and every morning I have thought it was heavy and every evening I have thought it was heavy and not once in five months have I thought *where is it going*. It goes up. It comes down. It lives on a flat file on a filing floor and everybody is very pleased."

"That is what it does."

"That is what it does," said the woman with the back. "A thing you only ever carry one way is not being carried. It is being stored in a person."

He stood there and let that go round the room, and he understood that it had been in that building for about three years and that it had been said by a woman who carries paper for a wage and has never been thanked, and that he had needed about four seconds and one evening and a book going up four flights alone to hear it.

"Five days in seven," he said.

"Five days in seven," said the woman with the back, going into the stair with the strap over her shoulder and one hand on the rail at the half landing where a man used to sit. "And if you are going to do it, do it tonight, because I am not carrying it down for you and I am not staying behind to."

Three thousand eight hundred and fifty-two days. Less three thousand eight hundred and forty-six, the seventh of this one, is six, and three people were asked the same sentence at a table with the door open, and two of them said no with the reason given before the question was finished, and one of them said yes and was right, and the sentence that decided it was said by one of the two who said no.

*Less three thousand eight hundred and thirty-nine, the thirtieth of September, is thirteen, and the forty-third sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirteen of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand four hundred and thirty-two days, which is forty-seven months and twenty-two days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
