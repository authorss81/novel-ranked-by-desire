# The First Morning Of The Book

Three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine was the thirtieth of the month before this one and the seventh of this one is day three thousand six hundred and sixty-six, and three thousand six hundred and sixty-six less three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine is seven, and seven days out of a month that begins on the first is seven by the month count, and that seven is the whole of what there was between the last morning that book came up and this one.

## The Board By The Kerb

The boy came up the path at about a quarter to ten with a board under his arm and a paste brush shut in his other fist, and the path goes down in three turns and the light has been off the wall behind that board since about eleven.

The one hundred and fifth of those forms went up on it that morning and the one before it came off on the same morning, and both of the ruled squares printed at the foot of it were empty, which is what those two squares have been since the eighth of October of the first year and what they will be on the last working day of this one.

He leaned it against the kerb, found the bottom edge square with the side of his boot without looking at it, and went back up the slope with the brush shut in his other fist.

Nobody asked him anything.

## Two People And A Turn That Is Too Narrow

The Register came out of the flat file about a quarter to ten, signed out on a line that is not in the Register, and two people carried it up four flights and about four hundred yards off.

One of them had it under the bottom edge with both forearms and the other had the strap. It took about nine minutes and it took the same number of minutes on the last working day of the month before, and the difference between that morning and this one was the strap.

The stair turns twice at the half landing between the second and third floors, and that landing has no window in it, and the whole of the difficulty of the morning lives in about four feet of it.

A book that has lain flat under nine years of other people’s words is longer than the turn is wide once the strap is round it. The strap drawn out of the drawer four floors down on the thirtieth of March was a length that goes round that book twice at the very limit of it.

It slipped at about the second of the nine minutes.

The woman who had the strap put her shoulder under the corner and took the whole of the weight in one movement and did not put a hand out. The man with it under the bottom edge took two steps down without being told to and turned about four degrees, so that the length of the thing lay along the stair instead of across it.

It did not go over the newel. It came to rest on the tread above it with the woman’s shoulder under one end and the man’s forearms under the other and both of them standing still on a half landing with their breath going.

## He Was At The Head Of It And He Did Not Get A Hand To It

He was at the top of that stair and he has thought about the two or three seconds since.

There was about nine seconds in which he could have gone down four steps and taken a corner, and he has been down four flights of stairs in the dark carrying a heavier thing than that, and he knows what nine years of other people’s words weighs when it goes through a hip.

He did not do it, because two people carry it and not three, and because the practice he made on the thirtieth of March says two people carry it, and because a man who takes a corner off a book in a stairwell is a man who has decided the two people who agreed to do this are not enough, and they agreed to it, and they are paid the same wage either way.

He has not worked out whether that was the right reason. It has been a week.

## The Drawing Of It

He asked about it in the corridor with the door open and about four people going past, which is where he asks everything.

“Where did you get it.”

“Out of the drawer,” said the one with the strap. “There are about nine in that drawer and four of them go round twice. I took the one that goes round.”

“Does it fit.”

“It goes round,” she said. “Going round and fitting are not the same thing, and you will find out which one you have got in about four months, and so will I, and I am telling you this morning instead of that morning, because in four months I will not be able to tell you anything. I will be able to carry it. I will not be able to tell you.”

He said nothing at all to that, which is not what he does.

“You have asked me which strap,” said the woman. “You have not asked me which book. I have been thinking about it since I got to the third floor and it is the book that is wrong and I am the one who chose wrong, because there are four straps in that drawer that go round twice and no line anywhere says which book goes in which one.”

“I drew the same one out of the drawer on the thirtieth and I drew it again this morning without knowing I had, and if you had not asked me I would have gone on drawing it out until it went.”

“All right.”

“All right is the whole of what you are going to say and I have heard it before.”

## What She Carries On Other Mornings

She has been going up four flights with a strap since she was a girl in that trade and she does this one six days a week for a firm off Cordwain that does not know that this office exists, and the firm’s book is a third of the size and it goes round once.

“A thing you have carried before gets lighter,” she said. “Not in your arms. Your arms do not know. It gets lighter about the second week and stays that way for nine years. That one has been up and down that stair once and it is already the heaviest thing I have ever carried and it will get heavier and I know exactly what will make it lighter and I am not going to be the one who tells you.”

“What will make it lighter.”

“About four hundred and seventy rows with nobody’s name on them and a date and an hour on every one, and that is the last count anybody took of them, which was the third of June the year before last,” said the woman. “And two pair of hands that are not mine. And somebody upstairs who says the arrangement stops, plainly, in a corridor, on a working morning, while I am still in the building and can go home knowing which day it was.”

He looked at her.

“I am not asking you for that,” she said. “I am telling you what I would want, so that when it is gone you know that I told you, and so that nobody in this building can say afterwards that I did not say.”

## The Table, And What Was Not On It

They set it down on that table and not on anything else, and left the strap where it was, and about four people went up and down that corridor in the next nine minutes, and any one of them would have had to stand over it for a quarter of an hour to read a bound page at a glance.

There was no blade on that table and no weight on it, and nothing of this office’s own came up that stair in anybody’s hands except the strap, which went back into the drawer four floors down at about half past five with the other eight.

Between a quarter to ten and half past five about nine people came up those four flights. Four of them brought a paper of their own with them. Two came about somebody else.

That was an ordinary morning and it will be an ordinary morning for a year now, and no page in this city carries a figure that would tell anybody so, and a book that has been on a table for nine hours is heavier on the fifth morning than it was on the first, and that room will not be the one to say so.

## A Boy Going Back Down The Slope

He went down that hill at about eleven and about four hundred yards and the boy was still on it, coming down with the brush in his fist and his other arm empty, and the two people carrying the book went past him at the kerb going the other way.

One of them said *he is always up about this time* to the other one, and the other one said *he is*, and neither of them said one word to him, and he did not look at two people carrying nine years of other people’s words up a hill past him.

That is not an event. It is not on any page. It has gone on being the arrangement for nine years and nothing this morning changed it.

*Three thousand six hundred and sixty-six days. Less three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine, the thirtieth of the month before this one, is seven, and the bound Register came up four flights about four hundred yards off for the first time on a standing arrangement, and the strap it went up in is a length that goes round it twice because it is the wrong one, and the drawer it came from holds about nine of them and nothing whatever saying which book goes in which.*

*Less three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is seven, and the forty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seven of them, and that rail does not move again until the thirtieth of June.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand two hundred and forty-six days, which is forty-one months and sixteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*

*Less three thousand and eighty-two, the twenty-third of August of the ninth year, is five hundred and eighty-four days, which is nineteen months and fourteen days, and a row with a space in the middle of it stood in that bound Register for every one of them with no hand going near it, and the inside of a coat on the left is empty.*
