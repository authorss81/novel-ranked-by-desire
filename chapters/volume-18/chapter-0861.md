# The First Morning Of The Book

Three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine was the thirtieth of the month before this one and the seventh of this one is day three thousand six hundred and sixty-six, and three thousand six hundred and sixty-six less three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine is seven, and thirty less thirty is nothing at all, and the seven days are the first week of a month that has thirty of them and stops on the thirtieth.

## The Board By The Kerb

At ten in the morning the boy came up the path with a board under his arm and a paste brush shut in his fist, and the path goes down in three turns and the light has been off the wall behind that board since about eleven.

The one hundred and fifth of those forms went up on it that morning and the one before it came off, and both of the squares printed at the foot of it were empty, which is what those two squares have been since the eighth of October of the first year and what they are going to be on the last day of this one.

Nobody asked him anything.

## Nine Minutes For Four Flights

The Register came out of the flat file on the filing floor at about a quarter to ten, signed out on a line that is not in the Register, and two people carried it up four flights and about four hundred yards off — one under the bottom edge and one at the strap — along a corridor about four feet wide, and it took about nine minutes.

The stair turns twice at the half landing between the second and third floors, and that is where the whole of the morning lives.

A bound book that has lain flat on a shelf for nine years is nine inches longer than the turn is wide once the strap has gone round it twice, and at about the second of those nine minutes the strap slipped a finger's width on the shoulder of the woman who was carrying it,

and the bottom edge of it swung out into the middle of that stair and stopped against the wall, and the wall is plaster over brick and it took most of a second to decide not to hit it.

They went back down four steps and came round it again and neither of them said anything while they did it.

They came off the fourth floor last, which is right, and the last door on that landing is about four feet wide and has been opened by somebody coming out of it about eleven thousand times in nine years and has not once been opened by anybody carrying anything.

Nine minutes. It has been nine minutes every morning since the seventh of March, and it was about nine minutes and a half this morning, and that half minute is not going to be written down anywhere at all.

The woman who had it under the bottom edge went down at a quarter to five and the man who had it at the strap went down at about five to five, and both of them had to be back somewhere by six and neither of them said so in that corridor.

## On The Table

The strap came off and went on the floor by the leg of the table and stayed there, because a thing that is on the floor does not have to be explained to the next person who comes through that door at about two in the afternoon.

It stayed shut.

Wenna Raut turned it about a quarter on the wood so that the head of it faced the empty chair at the far end of that table, and that is the whole of what she did with nine years of other people's words before about nine o'clock.

The strap left a mark in the dust on that wood about nine inches long and about the width of two fingers, and there has been a mark on that table every working morning since the seventh of March and it has been getting slightly darker each time, and it has never been mentioned in that room, and it is not going to be on anything.

## What The Nine Asked Instead

Nine came up those four flights between about a quarter to ten and about half past five. About four of them brought a paper up themselves, which is the ordinary traffic and always has been, and about five of them brought nothing.

None of the nine of the nine asked to have a page turned in front of them.

The promise had been standing for five weeks and had been used once, on the thirtieth of March, by a woman who got the right page and did not read it, and this morning, on the first morning of the arrangement, it went entirely unused in that building, and he had known that would happen and had got the arithmetic wrong about when.

The first of the nine came up at about twenty past ten with a lease and wanted to know whether a clause in it had ever been contradicted by anybody who came up here, which is a question about a paper and is answered in about nine minutes and is the whole reason that room exists.

The second came up at about eleven with nothing.

The third came up at about one.

And the woman at eleven with nothing was the one who asked the question, and she asked it in a plain voice in the middle of that room, and about four people in the corridor went past without stopping, which is what about four people a day do on an ordinary working morning.

## The Question That Was Asked Instead

It came at about eleven and again at about one and twice after two, in three different mouths and in three different sets of words, and every version of it was the same question, and it was about neither a paper nor a person, and nothing he has ever built has a clause that answers it.

Who else has come up these four flights.

## The Answer He Gave, Which Was The Right One

He said nothing at all to any of the four, in four different rooms, in a voice you could hear over the corridor, and he was right, and Emil Brack had told him at the head of that lane on the fourth of last month that he would be right and that it would not help a soul, and it had taken a month to come true and it had come true on the first morning.

He said it to the fourth of them, who had come in with a document of lease and a question about a clause in it, and the two questions were about four inches apart in that room and the wrong one was the one he was thinking about.

A man had been up that stair at about eleven and had been told that nine people come up it in a month, and had told somebody, and about a week later there would be a man at a rail off Cordwain who had been told it by nine people and had no instrument in the world to check a single word of it.

And the four of them were all going to go home and repeat the thing they had asked, because that is what a person does with a question they were not answered, and in about four days the question would be in a lane and in about four weeks it would be in a kitchen, and none of the four of them knew they were carrying it and he could not ask any of them to put it down.

He had made one instrument in nine years. It was a refusal, and it was the best thing he had ever done, and it had just come downstairs and was standing in a corridor about four feet wide.

## Four O'Clock, And A Woman Sends Word

At about four a boy of about nine came up that stair and said that Enid Salter could not be at half past five and had said so at the counter downstairs and was not coming up to say it herself, because she had a thing to be back for at six.

Enid Salter had carried it up at about a quarter to ten and had told him at the door that she would be there at half past five, and she had said it the way people say a thing when they have already decided.

The other carrier stood there.

He is a man who works on the same floor as that flat file and he had it under the bottom edge for nine minutes that morning and cannot lift it alone, which is not a thing about his back, it is about the shape of a nine-year-old binding coming round a turn.

“Then the book stays in this room tonight,” said the man who had carried it.

“No it does not.”

“There is not a third person coming up those stairs and we both know it.”

“There is a third person if I say there is a third person and say why,” said Lin Hao, “and I am not going to say it at four o’clock on the first morning of something and have it be a habit by the second week.”

## Half Past Five

At about half past five a third pair of hands came up that stair and the reason was said out loud at the end of that table with the door open, because a thing done without a reason on the page is a thing done for no reason at all.

The flat file cannot be locked with the Register in it. It has a drawer and the drawer has a lock and the key is in the same building, and a book that spends a night on that table in that room is a book that anybody in this city can come up four flights at two in the morning and ask about, and no room of that width has ever had to answer that question before.

So it went down at about half past five, three sets of shoulders on it, by three people, and the third one was not going to do it again tomorrow and had not offered.

## At The Yard Gate

He went out into the yard at about five to six because the room was a room with the table bare in it and he had nowhere to be.

The woman who had asked the question at about eleven was standing at the gate with the man who had carried the book under the bottom edge, and she was asking him the same question, in the same voice, with the same three hands going the way they had gone that morning.

He could not answer it either. He had been up that stair for nine years and was barred from saying one word about any person who had ever used it, and he had carried that book up the stair himself on the seventh of March and had been in the room when it came off the flat file, and he had watched two people take it down at about half past five and had not gone out of that door either.

She looked up, saw the white plate on him, and understood in about four seconds that the man at the gate was not going to be told anything by the other one either.

“Twenty-nine years,” she said. “They will tell you the same thing you told me and it will be the right thing and it will not help me.”

Then she went out through the gate and the man at the rail with the slate said nothing, because he is paid a grain a head and has said nothing at that rail for nine years, and there are two women in this city who have now asked about that room on the same morning and neither of them got anything out of it, and one of them has a husband and one of them has not, and that room knows the difference and has not used it.

*Three thousand six hundred and sixty-six days. Less three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine, the thirtieth of the month before this one, is seven, and a bound Register went up four flights and about four hundred yards off on two pairs of arms at about a quarter to ten and came down again at about half past five on three, and about nine people came up those stairs in between and not one of them asked for a page.*

*Less three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is seven, and the forty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seven of them and has not been changed since it went up on the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand two hundred and forty-six days, which is forty-one months and sixteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*

*Less three thousand and eighty-two, the twenty-third of August of the ninth year, is five hundred and eighty-four days, which is nineteen months and fourteen days, and a row with a space in the middle of it stood in that bound Register for every one of them with no hand going near it.*
