# Nine Beds And No Names

Three thousand seven hundred and forty-two was the twenty-third of this month and the thirtieth is day three thousand seven hundred and forty-nine, and three thousand seven hundred and forty-nine less three thousand seven hundred and forty-two is seven, and thirty less twenty-three is seven by the month count.

## The Back Of The Dispensary

The dispensary stands two streets over from the lane with the nine stools, and the day room at the back of it holds nine beds along two walls, with a window at the far end that was open to the cold though it was the thirtieth of June.

Tessa Okonkwo keeps the room in the afternoons when the morning round is done, and she was at the third bed with her sleeves up past the elbow when he came in, washing a man's feet in a basin, with the man's blanket pulled decently up and the man's face turned to the wall.

The skin is gone from the base of the nails on both of her hands and has been gone for years, which is what soap and hot water do to a person who does not wear gloves indoors, and it is the only mark she has and no person in that building would call it one.

Two floors up a clerk writes the names down, which is the rule of that house, and for that reason no person in that room says the name of a person who is not lying in it, and the nine men in the nine beds are the only people who can be spoken of by anything but a bed number.

"Bed four is asleep," said Tessa Okonkwo without looking up. "Do not wake him. The window stays open because the air in here goes bad by two. You can put your coat on the chair."

## What The Sheets Carry

He put his coat on the chair and stood with his hands behind his back, because the room has a way of making visitors hold themselves, and he looked at the nine beds and the nine blankets and the nine charts at the feet of them, and at the light coming in cold over the top of the sill.

"I have come about the sheets," said Lin Hao. "The forty-first came down off that rail at four this afternoon and the forty-second went up in its place carrying no new line in the office hand. That is the second quarter-end of the four."

"I know what day it is," said Tessa Okonkwo. She finished with the basin and dried her hands on her apron and pulled the blanket straight. "And I know you have come to say the head beside it, because you say it four times a year and this is the second of them, and you want somebody in the room who will not make you say it about a person."

"The head stands at eight hundred and fifty-two," said Lin Hao. "Unmoved since the thirtieth of March of the fifth year. Unmoved across twenty-six sheets and twenty-five quarters, both right, differing by one, with three thousand seven hundred and forty-nine less one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine is two thousand two hundred and fifty, which is twenty-five times ninety."

She nodded, wringing out the cloth.

"And three thousand seven hundred and forty-nine less three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine is ninety, which is three times thirty and nothing over, three months exactly, and a sheet going up at four this afternoon carries no new line."

"That is the cleanest interval in your year," said Tessa Okonkwo. "And the first exact quarter figure since March. And you have said it in a room where no person may name the name of a person who is not lying in it, which means it cannot be said about anybody and therefore has to be said about the sheets."

"That is the fifth way I have said it," said Lin Hao. "And it will not be said again until September."

"Good," she said. "Now say what you came for."

## What A Dead Person's Row Is

He told her about the man whose wife died in the sixth month and whose row was dated the second, and about the mother who could not hear, and about the page turned away on the tenth, and she listened the way she listens to everything, with her hands still and her eyes on his face.

"What you hold about the dead is nothing," said Tessa Okonkwo at the end of it. "That is the whole of it and kindness does not change its shape. A row with a date and an hour and some words is not a person after the person has stopped. It is paper that remembers being written."

"I have refused to write down that a person is dead," said Lin Hao. "Even on a slip. Because the first row anybody could be identified from would be a row saying a person is dead."

"And you are right to refuse it," she said. "The day you write that word against a date you have a column of the living and the dead, which is a list, and your book was a list about nine years ago and could not be got back."

She came across to where he stood and stopped about a foot from him, and the room went quiet in the way it does when everybody in it is asleep or pretending to be.

"You are rank one," said Tessa Okonkwo. "On a night shift you asked for. With a chest spent since the fourth year and no permission of any kind. And I have been absorbing other people's injuries for forty chapters of your life and I am tired of watching you carry paper up stairs."

"Yes," he said.

"So I am going to ask you for something that is not a service," she said. "And you are going to say yes or no before I have the sentence out."

## Yes Before The Sentence

"Yes," said Lin Hao.

She laughed, a short sound, and it was about four seconds and it was enjoyable to her and written as a fact about her body, because she is a woman who says what she wants and takes pleasure in the saying of it.

"You said it before I asked," she said. "That is the first time in nine years you have not made me finish."

She took his hand and pulled him behind the screen at the end of the room where the linen is kept, where the nine beds cannot see and the clerk two floors up cannot hear, and the cold from the window came over the top of the screen and raised the hairs on his arms.

A stool stands behind that screen where the night nurse sits to fold, and she pushed it aside with her foot to clear the floor, and set her chart and pencil down on it, because even in this she keeps the figure and the name apart the way that house demands.

She got his shirt open with both hands and put her mouth to his collarbone and then lower, and her hands went below his belt and pulled him out and held him the way a person holds a chart they already know the answer to, and he was hard in her hands inside half a minute.

"You are slower than you were," she murmured against his skin. "And you have not told me about it and I am not going to ask you about it in a room with nine men in it."

She went down to her knees on the folded linen and took all of him into her mouth, and her tongue went along the underside and stayed there, and she swallowed and took more, and looked up at him over him once while he braced one hand against the shelf and kept the other clear of her hair.

He put his fingers to the inside of her thigh and she pushed against them, wet through the cloth, and the noise she made was not a small one and she did not try to make it one.

"Now," she said, standing, pushing her skirts up with one hand and keeping the other on his shoulder. "And be quick about it because bed four wakes at half past and I want this done before he does."

She turned and bent over the folded stack with her legs apart, and he set himself against her from behind and came into her in one movement, inside her to the root, and she gasped and then laughed low and pushed back against him.

"Feel me," she said. "My cunt knows you. It has known you since spring. Come into me properly and do not be careful, because no person in this house is careful with what is theirs."

"There," she said. "That is what a dead person's row is not. This is warm and it is now and it will be gone in a minute and no hand in this house will write it down."

He moved in her with his hands on her hips, and she worked back against him, and the linen shifted under her elbows and she braced herself and told him to go harder, in the plain voice she uses on a round, and he did, and she came first with a cry bitten off against her own wrist, her body clenching round him, wet and tight.

He spent in her a minute later with her name in his ear, and she held him there and counted it out under her breath the way she counts everything, and took all of that too and did not let go for a little while afterwards.

There was seed on the inside of her thigh and on his own wrist and neither of them wiped anything yet, because the cloth by that screen has been there since the building was put up and neither of them trusts it.

## The Chart At The Foot

She straightened her skirts and washed her hands at the small basin behind the screen, and he did up his shirt, and they came out from behind it to find bed four still asleep and the window still open and the cold still coming in.

"What you asked me for," said Lin Hao.

"I asked you for something that is not a service and you gave it," said Tessa Okonkwo. "And what it changes is that I will go on taking a share off people and holding it for them, and you will go on refusing to write the word, and neither of us will pretend the other is doing the same thing."

She picked up the chart at the foot of the third bed and made a mark on it, and the mark was a figure and not a name, because in that house a figure is a figure and it is not a name and there will never be a column with a name in it while she is in it.

He left her at the door of that room with the nine beds behind her and the clerk two floors up writing down whatever the house needs, and he went down the two flights and out into the cold of the afternoon, carrying the head he had printed and the three months he had counted, and the warmth of her still on his hands.

*Three thousand seven hundred and forty-nine days. Less three thousand seven hundred and forty-two, the twenty-third of this month, is seven, and the head stands at eight hundred and fifty-two, unmoved across twenty-six sheets and twenty-five quarters, both named in words, both right, differing by one, with three thousand seven hundred and forty-nine less one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine is two thousand two hundred and fifty, which is twenty-five times ninety.*

*Less three thousand six hundred and fifty-nine, the thirtieth of March, is ninety, which is three times thirty and nothing over, and the forty-first sheet came down off that rail at four this afternoon and the forty-second went up in its place carrying no new line in the office hand.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand three hundred and twenty-nine days, which is forty-four months and nine days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
