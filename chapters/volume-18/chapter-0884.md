# The City Felt It In April

Three thousand eight hundred and eighteen was the ninth of this one and the sixteenth of this one is day three thousand eight hundred and twenty-five, and three thousand eight hundred and twenty-five less three thousand eight hundred and eighteen is seven, and sixteen less nine is seven by the month count.

## The Man Who Came Back For Nothing Else

He came up at about eleven on the sixteenth of this month with his coat over his arm, and Lin Hao knew him the way he knows everybody who has stood on that floor, and then had to stop himself from calling him the man of the twenty-third of June with his family behind him, because no family has stood in that room since the eighteenth of August and none is going to.

"You are the one whose wife died in the sixth month," said Lin Hao.

"I am. And I am not here about her."

"Say what you are here about."

"I am here because a woman on my street asked me on the last day she had working whether it was true that there is a book up four flights in that building and a clerk who cannot leave the room, and I said I did not know, and I have not been able to put it down since. It is not a thing I am carrying. It is a thing I do not have and I said I did not have it."

"Who asked you."

"A woman I have spoken to twice about a drain. I am not going to be asked a third time and say I do not know." He put his coat on the back of the bench. "That is the whole of it and it took me a week to say it and it is not about my wife and it is not about a paper."

## What He Did About It

Lin Hao got it out of him in about four minutes and it was not difficult, because a man who has come up four flights with something and been told it is not about anything will say what it is about.

"Two people have said it to me since the end of the month before last," said the widower. "Once in a lane, once at a counter, and the one at the counter said it as though it were about the weather, the way people say a thing about the weather when it is not about the weather."

"Any idea where they heard it."

"No. And that is what brought me up four flights, because I have spent the whole of the summer standing in rooms telling people what this office does not hold, and I could not go on standing in them if half the street has heard the other half."

"Then hear it from me," said Lin Hao.

"From the thirtieth of March there is a bound book on a table in that room from about a quarter to ten until about half past five on working mornings, and a clerk enters what people ask and what is answered, and a person may come up those stairs and ask one question about any paper they bring. That is the whole of it. That building holds nothing else, and a record of who has asked would be a list, and this office does not make one."

The widower listened to the whole of it without moving.

"That is a description of an address," he said, "and you have just given it to me, and I live two streets off."

## Where It Came Out Of

Wenna Raut had been standing at the far end of that table with the pen down and had not said a word until he had gone.

"You had it on the table in about four seconds."

"I had it on the table in about four seconds because he had come up four flights to be told a thing I already said in that room in June, and because I did not think about the word street until it had gone out."

"Would you have said it to a stranger who asked you about the weather."

"No."

"He was not a stranger. He is a man whose wife this office holds a row about and who came up this morning specifically to be told that he did not have to keep a secret." She capped the pen. "That is a real difference and I am not being sarcastic, and it is also the size of the whole of the difference between a thing that stays in this city and a thing that does not."

"Who has heard it, though."

"That is the part I have been turning over all morning." She put the pen down again and did not look at him.

"Not the four people who came in here. It has come out through the passage and the corner and the yard, and there are two people who carry that book past about nine people a day every working morning and back down four flights again at half past five, and they have been doing it since April, and one of them has a bad back and goes slowly on the second flight, and that is a thing about four hundred people a day can watch a person do."

## The Practice He Did Not Make

"Do you want it to stop," said Wenna Raut.

"That is a strange question to be asked at about twelve."

"It is the only question. You can want that in four ways and only one of them is a practice." She turned the cap on the pen over and over. "You can want it to stop and say so out loud to everybody who comes up that stair, which is a practice, and about nine people a month would hear it and about four of them would repeat it, and the four would be a column you built in a fortnight."

"I am not doing that."

"You can want it to stop and go looking for whoever said it first and send a clerk to a passage, and I will not enter that either and you will not ask me to."

"No, and you would not ask me to either."

"You can want it to stop and decide that the arrangement has to hold still now, and the arrangement is two people and a strap and a stair, and there is nothing to hold still." She put the pen down for good.

"And there is the fourth way, which is to go on exactly as you are and let it be known in a lane that this office has been up those stairs since the thirtieth of March and means nothing by it, and that is the one you are going to choose, and I am not going to argue with you about it."

"How do you know which one I am going to choose."

"Because you said it out loud in this room in June, to a man with a coat over his arm," said Wenna Raut. "You told him that the voice at the end of that table makes a thing a decision and the ordinary voice makes it a fact, and in nine years you have not managed to leave one of them a fact."

## At The Counter Off Cordwain

He went down at about four and stood at the rail in the passage, where about four thousand people go past on a working day, and the man who is paid nothing had already seen him coming.

"You have come about the weight," said the counterman.

"Who has asked you about it."

"Four people in a fortnight. All strangers. All of them asked it differently and all of them asked it as though they were checking something they had already half heard." He put the quarterly sheet down and turned it over, which he does four times a year and not otherwise.

"One of them said it is a filing office. One said it is an office with a book in it. One asked me whether you could go in without knocking. And one of them did not ask me at all. She stood where you are standing and said *the stair at the back of the court*, out loud, to the passage itself, and then went."

"And you answered her."

"I answered the three. The fourth one had gone by the time I had my hand off the sheet."

## The Part The Promise Does Not Reach

He walked back up that hill with the thing he had gone down for turning over the whole way, and he got it clear on the third landing and then lost it twice and got it clear again at his own door.

About four thousand people a day past that counter. About four in a fortnight asking. Two carriers and a second flight and a bad back, five days a week, since April. One woman at the end of a tram line who felt the whole of it on the seventh of April and would not write one word of it down, and one man on a half landing in his own building who has stopped coming and about whom this office cannot begin a row.

And a promise made in words to a city in which no sentence says that anybody will ever find the door.

He stood in the dark of his own hall with his coat still on and worked out the shape of it the way he works out everything now, which is to say he got there in about nine minutes.

The arrangement can be found. It can be found by a woman who watches a second flight, and by a woman at a counter who says a phrase out loud to whoever is nearest her, and by a man in a lane who asks four times a week because a price has gone into a rent. A person standing in a lane can now be shown to a building.

And the only thing in this city that would make it harder to be found is an address on a door, and an address on a door is a card with a street on it that any stranger may carry, and no instruction in nine years permits it, and in nine years the request has not been made of him by anybody at all.

He had wanted a thing he could not have at the end of that hill and he had wanted it so hard that he had to stand in a hall in the dark with his hands in his coat pockets and be honest with himself about how long it had been in him.

Three thousand eight hundred and twenty-five days. Less three thousand eight hundred and eighteen, the ninth of this one, is seven, and a man came up four flights who was not asking about anything this office holds, and about four people in a fortnight have asked at a counter off Cordwain, and the promise he made in words has nothing at all in it that reaches the person who asked.

*Less three thousand seven hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is seventy-six, and the forty-second sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-six of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of this month.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand four hundred and five days, which is forty-six months and twenty-five days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
