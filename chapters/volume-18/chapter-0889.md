# She Cannot Leave That Room

Three thousand eight hundred and fifty-two was the thirteenth of this one and the nineteenth of this one is day three thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight, and three thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight less three thousand eight hundred and fifty-two is six, and nineteen less thirteen is six by the month count.

## Said Out Loud At The End Of The Table

She said it at about one on the nineteenth of this month, standing at the head of that table with the door pushed wide and about four people going past outside, which is the way a fact gets said in that room.

"I cannot leave this room," said Wenna Raut.

She put the pen down before she said it, which she had not done before.

"You cannot leave this room while the book is in it, from a quarter to ten until half past five, five days a week, and that has stood since the seventh of April and you have not said it out loud in it before."

"I have never said it out loud in it."

"Say the rest of it."

"The rest of it is that I go home at half past six and I come up at half past nine and I have done that five days a week since the seventh of April, and there has not been one morning when I could not do it and there has not been one evening when I wanted to more than I wanted anything else."

"And the wanting does not stop at six. It sits in me from six until about half past nine and then it goes off and I am a person again at about ten, and for about nine years before the seventh of April I was a person at all of those hours, and I did not know how much of me had gone."

## What She Wants Before The Year Turns

"Then say what you want," said Lin Hao, "because I am going to say no to most things this year and I would rather be refused than managed."

"One night."

"One night of what."

"One night out of this room. One whole night, with the book not in it, so that I can be a woman in my own rooms and not a woman who is waiting for a quarter to six to be one." She said it fast and did not look at him.

"I have been thinking about it since the spring and I have not said it out loud because a clerk who says a thing like that out loud in a room with the door open is a clerk asking for something, and I do not want to be a clerk asking for something."

"What would you do with it."

"Sleep first. Eight hours of it, and I have not had eight hours since the fourth month, and I would like to know whether I can still do it." She looked up then.

"And then go down to the water and sit, and I would like to go to the counter off Cordwain and stand at that rail and hear a man read the foot of that page out loud to about eleven people, because I have entered those four lines for nine years and I have not heard them read by anything except your voice in this room, and I would like to know what they sound like in a street."

## Why He Cannot Give It

He could not give it and it took him about nine minutes to get the reason into the shape where she could use it, and she let him take all nine.

"The first reason is the room. From the eighteenth of August an answer about a person is given only with that person in it, and every answer in that book is about somebody. The last asking of any day is a question about a person, and a question about a person comes at half past four and at half past five, and if this room is shut for a night then the morning it opens is the morning somebody finds out that a question was waiting."

"And the second reason."

"The second reason is worse."

He said it out loud because she had asked him out loud.

"A book in a room overnight is a book anybody may ask about at two in the morning. At two in the morning somebody stands on that stair, because that is when people stand on stairs, and this room does not answer anybody at two in the morning."

"Every question that needs a page waits for the morning. And the people whose rows those questions are are the ones who cannot come in the morning, and that is the whole of what we agreed on the eighteenth of August, and I am not going to be the one who quietly undoes it to get a clerk a night off."

"So the answer is no."

"The answer is no, and I am sorry, and I am going to try to find you something else before the year turns, and I do not know yet what it is and I am not going to promise you a shape I have not got."

## What She Says To That

"You are not going to promise me a shape," said Wenna Raut. "In nine years that is the first time you have refused me and told me the truth in the same breath, and I have been waiting for both."

"I am sorry."

"Do not be, it is a good refusal." She picked the pen up again and went back to the page. "Now go and do whatever it is you are going to do about two carriers who have both said no to you inside eight days, because that is the arithmetic in this room that is actually wrong and not this one."

## Six Days To Put Something In Her Hands

He had six days to put something in that clerk's hands before the twenty-fifth of this month, and on the twentieth he thought he had it, and on the twenty-second he had lost it, and on the twenty-fourth at about four in the afternoon he had it again and it came out of the third flight of that stair rather than out of his head.

The twenty-first was the day he tried the honest version, which was that he would have the room open on a day off for the two of them and read out anything either of them wanted, and it was not enough and he knew it was not enough within about nine seconds of saying it, because the thing she wanted was not a reading. It was a room with a window and eight hours in it and a door that she closed herself.

The twenty-second was the day he nearly wrote it down, and he did not write it down, and the reason he did not write it down is that the only person who could enter it was standing on the other side of that table and he would have had to hand it to her to enter, and handing a sentence to a clerk is how a sentence becomes this office's.

The carriers came down that stair at half past five on the twenty-fourth with the strap between them, going down fast because they were late for something else, and the half landing with no window in it was right there in the middle of it with about nine minutes of dark stair above it and four flights of lit stair below it.

A thing said there belongs to neither floor and cannot be fetched back up or carried down. He had heard a woman say that about a turn in a different stair in the month before, on a night, and he had not understood a word of it at the time, and he stood on that landing on the twenty-fourth of this month and understood all of it.

It is not a rule and it is not ink and it is not a practice and it cannot be handed to a person. The only thing it does is make a thing said there belong to neither floor.

Which meant that a thing said there could be about her, and about her only, and could not be carried up four floors into a room with a book on a table in it.

## What He Took Down The Hill

He went down that hill at about five with the last of the light going off the top batten and thought about a night.

The book went down at half past five and came up at a quarter to ten. Between those two clocks the room was shut and the book was four floors down in a flat file and the stair was empty. After the last asking of the day there is nothing anybody can bring to that room that needs a page until about nine the next morning, and every night of the year is that same night, and she could have one of every one of them.

Not a night with the book out of the building. A night with the book at the bottom of four flights and the woman at the top of them, and the two of them as far apart as they can be made without anybody in this city being able to walk from one to the other in under half an hour.

He got to his own door and stood in the dark of his own hall and worked out that this was not a gift. It was a gap in a day's work that had already been agreed to be shut, and he was proposing to make the gap permanent on the strength of a sentence said on a landing by a woman who carries paper for a wage and has never been thanked.

And then he worked out the other half of it, which was that the gap did not exist unless the book came back up those four flights at a quarter to ten by one pair of hands instead of two, and that the whole thing turned on the sum he had been doing for eight days and could not make close, and that the sum closes at about a quarter to six in the evening, in the dark, in a yard, on the twenty-fifth of this month.

Three thousand eight hundred and fifty-eight days. Less three thousand eight hundred and fifty-two, the thirteenth of this one, is six, and a clerk put her pen down at about one and said the plainest thing she has said in nine years, and asked for one night out of that building before the year turns, and was refused in about nine minutes, and went back to the page without being managed.

*Less three thousand eight hundred and thirty-nine, the thirtieth of September, is nineteen, and the forty-third sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last nineteen of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand four hundred and thirty-eight days, which is forty-seven months and twenty-eight days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
