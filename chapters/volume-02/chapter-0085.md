# The Locked Office Opens

On the fifth of the month Colm Deveraux unlocked his office door at the branch for the first time since the ninth of the season, and Lin Hao was mopping the corridor when the key turned.

He felt a jolt of something between hope and dread. He wanted the man to come out and say the thing plainly.

Colm stood in the doorway with a bad shoulder and a duster in his hand he had clearly not used. The office behind him was dark and smelled of paper left shut.

“You can come in,” Colm said. “You cannot clean it. I am going to clean it.”

Lin Hao put the mop against the wall and stood in the doorway. He did not go in.

Colm looked at the corridor board with the two lines on it, typed, and the strip on the spike.

“A woman in a dark coat came here on the twentieth of last month,” Colm said. “She asked me whether anybody had asked me for names. I said no. She said that was very useful. Then she asked me about arithmetic and I told her.”

“I know.”

“She came to the counter and then she came to the floor and then she stopped coming to me and started on the porters.” Colm put the duster down. “The Office asked me the same three questions last week. I answered them. I did not tell them about her until yesterday, because I was frightened.”

For Colm, who had kept a door shut for months, opening it meant every clerk on the floor could see how small the room was.

Lin Hao kept his voice level. “What did you tell them.”

“That I gave her two answers for nothing in an ordinary voice at my own counter. That I have been frightened since. That I am still frightened.” Colm looked at the floor. “You refused them in a room and it cost you a calendar. I gave them away at a counter and it cost me nothing at the time, and now it is going to cost me the room.”

An attendance clerk came down the corridor with a sheet and stopped when he saw the open door.

Colm handed over the duster. “I am opening the office. I am not asking to be moved. If there is a finding against the arithmetic I held, I will answer it.”

The clerk wrote that down and went on.

Colm sat on the edge of the desk. “I am not asking you to forgive me. You told me once to write my own episode up. I did. It is in the drawer. If the dark coat comes back I am going to tell her to go to the desk downstairs, and if she does not go I am going to call the runner.”

Lin Hao picked up the mop. The hope stayed and the dread stayed with it, because a man who had hidden for months had come out and the coming out did not fix anything.

“You washed that date off twice,” Colm said. “The eleventh. I saw you do it. Do not wash it off again where I can see you.”

He went back to his washrooms with the office open behind him, and the corridor was different for a door being open, and it was worse, because now there was nowhere for either of them to pretend.
