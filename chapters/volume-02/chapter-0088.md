# The Wing Stair

On the eighth of the month Drant took him down to the second sub-level stair and stopped at the top, and Lin Hao stopped with him because the clerk stopped.

He felt curiosity pull hard against caution. He wanted to see the drawer without opening it.

The stair went down past a landing with a lamp on it to a corridor with doors on both sides. At the bottom of the first flight a thin dated sheet lay in an open drawer of a cabinet against the wall, face up but not turned round, prepared and unsigned.

Drant did not go down. “That is as far as I go with you. I have been down there nine years ago and I have not been down since.”

Lin Hao looked down the stair. He could see the date on the sheet but not the words, and the signature line empty.

“What is it,” he said.

“A holding sheet with a date on it and no name signed to it.” Drant kept his hands on the rail. “It was prepared for the account with one digit. It has never been turned round. Nobody has signed anything on it.”

For Drant, who entered everything anybody did in a room, standing at the top of a stair and not entering it meant there was a paper his book could not take.

“Why show me.”

“Because you asked what the order was on the thirty-first, and the order came up from three floors, and this is the floor it came past.” Drant did not move down a step. “And because Ruta’s map is closed and Runa’s minute is entered and the finding number is doing its work, and the next paper will come from down there.”

Lin Hao stood at the top with the lamp light coming up. He could feel the cold off the lower corridor.

He did not descend. He did not turn the sheet round. He did not sign anything.

He looked for a count of seconds and then looked away, because looking longer would have been a decision.

Drant shut the cabinet drawer with his foot, without going down, and the sheet went out of sight with its date still on it.

They went back up to the inventory room in silence. At the top Drant entered nothing, because nothing had happened that a book could hold.

He walked the long road to the branch with the stair behind him, seen and not descended, and the thin sheet stayed where it was, prepared and unsigned, and the fact that he knew where it was changed the weight of every Thursday after it.
