# The Dot On The Map

On the second of the month Ruta Vesely took the wall map down in the inventory room and laid it flat on the table with the ring round the Review square still on it, and Drant stood at the end with the thin register open.

Lin Hao had come for the strip and found a room set up for something else. He felt a thin fear, the kind that comes before a measurement. He wanted to see the instrument before it was read.

Ruta did not apologise. “The room attendances are discharged. That finding stands. This is not that room.”

She put three buff squares on the map. They had the nineteenth on them and the fourth box empty, and under each name a line in a clerk’s hand.

“The nineteenth happened,” she said. “Three people, one at a time, in four rooms. The fourth box was empty because there was no source. Your acknowledgement says the hand. The hand cannot be in three places. So the map of the nineteenth is not a map of a room.”

Drant turned the register round. It had four lines for the strips, each with the Exchange under it, and a fifth line started for the thirty-first.

“The paper leaves here,” he said. “Four times it has left here and gone to the Exchange. The figure on it has been eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight four times running. The tolerance is three.”

Ruta put her finger on the dot in the square. “That dot was a spend. One spend since the start of the season. It sat in a room and the room is closed. A second spend would be a second event. Two events is a line.”

She lifted the finger and put it on him. Not on his chest. On the table in front of him, on the empty boards.

“The attention has moved,” she said. “Not the room. The man who stands in rooms.”

Lin Hao looked at the three squares. “You have three addresses. You have no source. What does the office do with that.”

“It watches the addresses,” Drant said. “It does not watch the room any more. It watches where you were on the days you attended, and who you spoke to, and what was sent where you stood.”

Ruta took a pencil and drew a second ring on the map. It went round nothing. It went round the white margin beside the Ferrier arc, where the thirty-one marks ran along his walking line.

“That is not a place,” he said.

“No,” she said. “It is where a place would go if there was one. The map has thirty-one marks of where you walk and one mark of where you stood still long enough for something to happen. The office cannot file a margin. So it files you.”

She wrote on the bottom of the map in her own hand, where the confirmation box had been left empty before.

Subject confirms the room and declines recurrence. Distribution of one is not a distribution. Watch the attendee.

Drant entered it. The pen made the sound it always made.

For Nadia, who had given a name in a lift on the tenth, the move meant the office would no longer ask what room she stood in.

Ruta rolled the map. “A second spend makes this true. Not a room. A line with your name at both ends.”

He walked out with the strip in his hand and the figure on it unchanged, and the fear stayed with him all the way to the branch, because a room can be closed and a man cannot.
