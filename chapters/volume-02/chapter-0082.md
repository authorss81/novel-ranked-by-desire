# The Dot On The Map

On the second of the month Ruta Vesely took the wall map down in the inventory room and laid it flat on the table with the ring round the Review square still on it, and Drant stood at the end with the thin register open.

Lin Hao had come for the strip and found a room set up for something else. He felt a thin fear, the kind that comes before a measurement. He wanted to see the instrument before it was read.

Ruta did not apologise. "The room attendances are discharged. That finding stands. This is not that room."

She put three buff squares on the map. They had the nineteenth on them and the fourth box empty, and under each name a line in a clerk's hand.

"The nineteenth happened," she said. "Three people, one at a time, in four rooms. The fourth box was empty because there was no source. Your acknowledgement says the hand. The hand cannot be in three places. So the map of the nineteenth is not a map of a room."

Drant turned the register round. It had five lines for the strips, each with the Exchange under it, and a sixth line started for the seventh.

"The paper leaves here," he said. "Five times it has left here and gone to the Exchange. The figure on it has been eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight five times running. The tolerance is three."

Ruta put her finger on the dot in the square. "That dot was a spend. One spend since the start of the season. It sat in a room and the room is closed. A second spend would be a second event. Two events is a line."

She lifted the finger and put it on him. Not on his chest. On the table in front of him, on the empty boards.

"The attention has moved," she said. "Not the room. The man who stands in rooms."

Lin Hao looked at the three squares. "You have three addresses. You have no source. What does the office do with that."

"It watches the addresses," Drant said. "It does not watch the room any more. It watches where you were on the days you attended, and who you spoke to, and what was sent where you stood."

"Two of them have gone out."

Ruta did not look up. "Two of them went out on the first. The third is in the tray and it goes at four, and by tomorrow morning three people in three buildings will each have a buff square with the nineteenth on it and a name under it that is not yours."

He felt the number of people in the room go up by three and the room itself not change at all.

"Who are they."

"That is the answer to the wrong question and you know it is the answer to the wrong question." Ruta took her finger off the map. "If I gave you three names you would spend the rest of this month deciding what to do about three names, and you would do something decent and stupid with them. I would have handed a set of addresses to a man who is already the subject of a distribution."

She squared the three buff squares with the side of her hand. "What you get is the shape. You are standing in the position of a man who has been sent three squares and is not going to be told who got them, because the not telling is the only protection those three women have left."

"Whose decision is that."

"Mine," Ruta said, "and Drant's, and if he says no I will drop it."

Drant looked at the register for a moment and then closed his hand flat on it. "My decision is that he finds out from a corridor at the Vestry that a woman he works beside has been sent a square about him. I have watched that happen twice this year in this building and both times it came out of a canteen inside the hour." He took his hand off. "So it is hers."

Ruta turned a sheet of buff round so the writing faced up, and it was a list of dates in a column with a column beside it headed *attendee present*, and the column was empty down all of them.

She took a pencil and drew a second ring on the map. It went round nothing. It went round the white margin beside the Ferrier arc, where the thirty-one marks ran along his walking line.

"That is not a place," he said.

"No," she said. "It is where a place would go if there was one. The map has thirty-one marks of where you walk and one mark of where you stood still long enough for something to happen. The office cannot file a margin. So it files you."

She wrote on the bottom of the map in her own hand, where the confirmation box had been left empty before.

Subject confirms the room and declines recurrence. Distribution of one is not a distribution. Watch the attendee.

Drant entered it. The pen made the sound it always made.

For Nadia, who had given a name in a lift on the tenth, the move meant the office would no longer ask what room she stood in.

"And what does watch the attendee mean to me," he said. "In practice. On an ordinary morning, with a strip on the machine."

"It means a second office sends a person to sit where you sit," Ruta said. "Not to the Exchange. Your Exchange attendance is the part that is already discharged. It means wherever you go that you have chosen to go, there is now a question about why you went there and who you went for, and the answer is not mine to give and not his to refuse, because the person asking is not accountable to the room they ask in."

"You think they will send somebody to the Vestry."

"I think by the end of next month there is a woman who has been asked what time you arrive at a building on your days off, and I think she will not be from this office, because this office does not ask questions in corridors." Ruta set the pencil down. "And I think that is what watch the attendee is for. It is not for the office. It is for the people who come behind the office and ask first."

Drant turned the register back and squared it against the table.

"There is a worse thing in it and you have not asked me for it," he said.

"Give it to me."

"The line I entered is not mine." Drant looked at Ruta and then at the page. "The office entered it. Ruta dictated the words and the office entered them, and it is in the district line now, and a distribution goes to every office that reads the district line."

He turned the page a quarter turn so the entry was the right way up for Lin Hao. "This one says a subject has declined recurrence and that one of three is not a distribution. The next office that reads that has three squares and a room that is closed and a man who attends it, and what they make of it is not a thing I can predict. Two readings of that sentence are equally available and the office has no reason to prefer yours."

Lin Hao sat with that for a moment.

"So I have stopped being one room and become a sentence other people are arguing about."

"You have become a line," Ruta said. "With your name at both ends, which is the only kind of line that can be drawn without a source."

Drant shut the book. "For what it is worth, and it is worth very little, the standing has five dates on it and no end date, and the sixth is the seventh, and I have entered an attendance for it, and if the office wanted a line out of you the cheapest way to get one would be for somebody to make an offer and for you to take it."

"You have thought about that."

"I have thought about it and I have not written it down, and I would like it noticed that the not writing is costing me more than the writing ever would have."

Ruta rolled the map. "A second spend makes this true. Not a room. A line with your name at both ends."

"Where would it come from."

"Would you take one if I offered it."

"That is the second wrong question in ten minutes and it is worse than the first." Ruta did not raise her voice. "If I take one off you it is a spend made for an office, and an office will put it in the book as a spend made for an office, and then the finding gets harder to argue with rather than easier, because you will have moved while being measured."

"Then what would you do with it."

"I would refuse it and I would not tell you which day I refused it on," she said. "A refusal that is reported is a direction, and this office has four directions it can already give me."

She tied the cord round the rolled map. "It means we wait. It means you keep not spending while an office gets very interested in a settled figure and a man who has stopped generating anything. The Office's instrument at this stage is not a plate and not a warrant. It is the calendar, going on."

He put his hand on the table. "And what did the second ring cost you."

Ruta stopped with the cord in her hands.

"It cost me a map that was a record last week," she said. "It is a drawing now. A record belongs to the office that made it and goes where it goes. A drawing belongs to the woman who drew it, and if the next woman to come into this room cannot tell whether the ring is a finding or a suggestion, she will treat it as a suggestion, and a suggestion is the thing an office acts on when it has nothing else."

"Will your division read it."

"My division will read the line I entered and not the map," Ruta said, "because I have not shown them the map, and I am not going to, and if anybody asks me why I drew a ring round a blank piece of paper on the second of the month I am going to say it was a figure of speech, and they will write that down."

Drant capped his pen. "And I am in it too, for the same reason, at the same cost."

He walked out with the strip in his hand and the figure on it unchanged, and the fear stayed with him all the way to the branch, because a room can be closed and a man cannot.
