# The Wall Map

She was at the table with her coat still on when he came in, and she had the wall map flat in front of her and three pencils on the cloth, and she had been waiting long enough that the tea in the jug had gone cold and she had not replaced it.

"Attendant," she said. "Ruta Vesely. I am the one who drew the ring and you have never met me and I have had your plate for a month, so I am going to be extremely direct and you are going to decide whether that is offensive later, when you have something else to think about."

"I know your name," Lin Hao said. "It is on the sheet you sent up and the second sentence on that sheet is your name in full."

"Then you have read my sentence and you know I am not permitted to write anything else." She squared the map. "Sit down.

Drant is going to ask me in about a minute whether I have an authority to keep you here past your attendance and I do not, and he is going to say so out loud, and then you are going to leave, and I am asking for eleven minutes first and I am asking for them in front of him so that they are minutes and not a favour."

Drant came in with the attendance sheet and put it under his arm.

"I have heard it," he said. "Eleven minutes. I am entering this as attendance kept, because it is in the room, and I am entering the eleven minutes as office time and not as yours." He looked at her. "Do not put him anywhere."

"I am going to put him in a square."

"Then do not put him in a building."

She took the three pencils off the cloth and put them in a line and did not touch them for a moment.

"Thirty-one marks," she said. "In a band, along the Ferrier arc, in the first colour, over eleven months. That is not a pattern of you, that is a pattern of where you walk, and I want to put that on the table before I put anything else on it, because I have been staring at it for a month and it took me three weeks to work out that it was boring."

"Then what is the interesting one."

"The one in the square." She put her finger on the newsprint. "A single mark, in the middle of the Reading, under the room. And a ring round it in the second colour, drawn on the eighth of last month, with three small marks inside the ring in the third colour, because three people reported an episode in one week in three buildings three streets apart and all three of them had been in that room on one afternoon."

"You did that off my plate and my band and the date of a room."

"I did that off your plate and your band and the date of a room, and there was no ledger, and you should sit down and be frightened, because if I could do it without a ledger then anybody in this building with a map and a month could do it, and about four people have one." She let her hand lie flat on the newsprint.

"And now I am going to tell you that I do not believe the map, and I have written it down in the third colour, and I have not sent it up."

The room had three people in it and two of them were looking at the sheet.

"Why not," said Drant.

"Because a map of a person and a map of a room are the same piece of paper and I cannot tell them apart with what I have." Ruta Vesely took one of the pencils out of the line and did not use it. "Mr Lin Hao.

On the thirteenth of the month you made a spend, in that room, in front of sixty people, and three people in three other buildings felt it four seconds later and reported it, and I put a ring round the place where it happened, and the ring is correct. The room is where it happened."

"Yes."

"What have you spent since the thirteenth?"

Lin Hao looked at her.

"Not one grain," she said. "Not to anybody. In a month. And I know that because you have been at this desk four times and you have told two clerks and a Field officer that you would not describe yourself by your means, and a man who has refused four ways of describing himself has spent nothing in a month."

"That is not the same thing."

"It is the same thing for my purposes, because the three marks inside the ring happened inside four seconds of each other in three buildings, and four seconds of cold in three buildings is not three people being unlucky. It is one thing leaving one place." She put the pencil back in the line. "So. One event. One room. One dot.

And I have built a nine-month map out of it, and I would like to show you what a nine-month map looks like when it has got one dot on it."

She turned the map round.

"The band along the arc is where you walk. The dot is where you spent. Those are not the same kind of mark and I have been drawing them in the same colour for three weeks and calling the whole thing a distribution."

Drant said, from the door, "That is the first sentence anybody has said in this building in a month that I would have bet money against."

"It is also the sentence that closes my file, if I am honest." Ruta put her hands flat on the cloth either side of the map. "Because if the dot is a spend, then the map has one event on it, and an office cannot keep a finding open on one event, and the correct thing for me to write is insufficient.

And if the dot is a person, then the map has a place, and you are at the place, and then this office has you, and everything else in this building stops being necessary."

She looked up.

"I do not know which one it is and I have known for three weeks and I have not been able to ask, because the only person who can answer is the man on that chair and the only question I have is a question that gets me removed."

"Ask it," said Lin Hao.

"I cannot."

"Then I will tell you what is on the map and you can use it or you will not."

Drant put his pen down on the attendance sheet, which he had never once done in a room.

"It is the room," Lin Hao said. "It was the room. The thirteen of the month and a Thursday afternoon and sixty people and nine chairs, and I answered a question on the floor of it and three people took something out of it, and that is the only time it has happened in your map's nine months, and I have not spent one grain on anybody since."

"That I have. What I cannot tell you is whether it will happen again." He put his hands on the table, one on either side of the edge of the newsprint, and did not touch it.

"If it happens again, it will happen to whoever is nearest me when it happens, and it will happen in four seconds, and it will be in three buildings, and you will be able to draw it.

And if it does not happen again, you have a map with one dot on it and eleven months of a man walking down a street, and there is nothing on that paper about me at all."

"You have just told me how to get you."

"I have told you the truth and you are an analyst and you have been doing it for a month." He looked at the third colour. "Do you want me to lie about it instead, so that your map stays one dot and your file stays open?"

Ruta Vesely did not answer for a moment.

"No," she said. "I want you to do something much worse and I am going to ask you for it and you are going to say no, and then I am going to do it anyway and we will both have to live with it."

She turned the map round again and put the third pencil in her hand.

"There is a line at the bottom of every map I send up that says place of event confirmed by subject. I have never written it. It is a box. If I write in it, your file closes on your mouth, because a subject who confirms the place has confirmed the place, and an office is allowed to stop." She wrote the box and left it empty.

"If I write unconfirmed, the map goes up with a hole in it, and a hole is not a refusal, it is an open question, and this office keeps open questions on the standing schedule, which means it keeps sending people to that square, for years, every nine days and then every seven, asking the people who work in it."

"That is the cost of not confirming."

"Yes. Nine clerks set that room up and take it down, and two of them are in the Reading Office, and one of them has already been asked about a Thursday." He did not raise his voice. "And one of them has already been asked twice, and she did nothing wrong, and she has been asked because I stood in a room in her building on the thirteenth of the month and spent the whole of a true answer on myself."

The room was extremely quiet.

"You are telling me that the cost of the box I am not filling falls on a woman who cleans chairs," said Ruta Vesely.

"I am telling you the cost of the box. I am not telling you to fill it in and I am not telling you what to write, and you are going to do what you are going to do, and I am a man who cleans washrooms and I have been telling you the price of things all day in a room where nobody has asked me to."

"That," said Ruta Vesely, "is the second time today that somebody has said a true thing to me and declined to make it useful, and I want it noted that the first one was Drant and he did it with a rubber stamp."

Drant said, "I have a sheet and a date and eleven minutes of office time, and I am going to say the one thing I am entitled to say, and then I am going to go and stand behind my own counter.

An empty box is not a description and it is not a refusal, and this office has been reading an empty box as a refusal for nine years and it has been wrong nine times, and this time it will be wrong in the correct direction, and I will be the clerk who wrote that down and I would like it written down that I said so in advance."

"You have not filled it in," said Lin Hao.

"No."

"Then write the true thing that is in the box and not the thing you want in the box."

Ruta Vesely wrote for about nine seconds, and then turned the map round so that the man in the chair could read the line.

Place of event confirmed by subject. Subject confirms the room and declines to confirm any recurrence.

"That is true," she said. "Every word of it is true and it is the only sentence in three weeks of that map that I have not had to argue with myself about at eleven at night."

"It is also the only sentence in it that gives you the room and takes back the map."

"Yes. Thank you. I had worked that out and I had hoped you had not." She signed underneath it and put the pencil down. "The third dot stays. The ring stays. The map goes up as a map of one event, in your own words, over my initials, and there is no mark on that paper anywhere between your name and a room with nine chairs in it."

She looked at him with her hands on the cloth.

"And I am going to write the third thing in the third colour and send that up as well, and it is not on the map."

"Show me."

"No." She picked up the third pencil. "It is one line and it is my finding and it is not yours and you have had three documents in a month and you are not getting a fourth out of me in a room with a clerk in it.

What it says is that a distribution of one event is not a distribution, and an office that treats it as one will keep attending a room in which nothing can be found, and I think that is true and I would like it on the record in my own hand before either of us knows whether it is."

Drant stamped the attendance sheet once.

"Fourth attendance kept, fourth on the schedule, and it is not a standing Thursday," he said. "And the strip from Thursday went into the tray with no envelope and I am entering it as received, and the figure on it was the same figure, and it has not moved since the Review, and I am a clerk and I do not have a view about the ninth week."

He went out and shut the door.

Ruta Vesely rolled the map up along its grain and put a rubber band round it, and then stopped with her hand on the roll.

"I am going to ask you one thing and it is the only one I am going to ask you and I am asking it as the analyst on this file and not as anything else, and then you can go up the stairs and keep the standing you have got."

"Ask."

"Will you do it again." She was not looking at him. "The spend. The one in the room. Not today and not for money and not because you want a rank. Will you do it again."

The room had no instrument in it that could read a thing and there was a woman waiting for an answer that would be on a map either way.

"I do not know," Lin Hao said.

Ruta Vesely nodded once, as though he had read a figure off a machine, and picked the roll up off the table.

"Then it goes up as unknown," she said, "and unknown is the only word in this building that the standing schedule has to keep answering, and I have written it and signed it and I am going to take it up myself, because a man at a desk at the end of an inquiry carries the finding's weight and it should not be carried by a clerk in a sub-level who has been doing this nineteen years and has three chairs."

She put the roll under her arm and went out in front of him and did not wait at the stairs.

Upstairs, on the corridor board outside the brass counter, the line that had been typed by somebody with a machine said his Thursdays and his job, and on a printed sheet in a roll under a woman's arm there were two sentences in his handwriting that nobody in that building could unwrite, and the second of them was about the rest of his life.
