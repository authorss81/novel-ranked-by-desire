# The Cost Of Keeping

On the tenth of the month he walked Petra Hall to the Exchange at nine in the morning with Tessa Okonkwo on her other side, and the clerk was in, and the date closed in eleven minutes with a stamp.

He felt joy when the stamp came down, brief and disbelieving. He wanted to see Petra get on a tram home.

She did. She got on at the stop outside with her shoes still laced and waved through the window, and the tram went on.

Tessa stood in the street with her hands in her coat. “One night. That is what the room is for. Ilse on the twenty-ninth. Petra today. The book balances.”

They walked back to the Vestry in silence. At the ward desk the runner with the sprained wrist was sorting charts one-handed, slow and stubborn, and Tessa stopped to correct her grip.

Then Nadia Ferrous came up the stairs with a sheet in her hand and her face set.

“It came this morning,” she said. “To the lift. A copy of the sheet that left at four on the thirty-first. My name at the bottom in an analyst’s hand. With a request for the days I worked the tenth and the persons I spoke to.”

Lin Hao felt the joy go out of him like light off a stair. He wanted to take the sheet and tear it and knew he could not.

Nadia held it out. “This is the cost of keeping. Not your rack.” She looked at Tessa. “Yours is boards and a wrist. Mine is this. A paper with my name on it in a building four streets away, asking me who I spoke to in my own lift.”

Tessa took the sheet and read it and gave it back.

Nadia turned to him. “Say it back to me now. In a room. No paper. Just you.”

He said it. “You gave her the building on the tenth on purpose. You worked it out four seconds before she asked. You told me on the twenty-second. You claimed it on the thirtieth. I put it in a file on the thirty-first. If she uses it, it happens to you first. That is minuted in this house and it is true in that building.”

Nadia folded the sheet and put it in her pocket. “Good. That is the first time. It cost you eleven days to say it, and it cost me a file, and now we are even, and I am still running the lift.”

For Nadia, who had asked twice and claimed once, being answered out loud meant she could stay where she was without pretending the paper was not there.

She went down to the lift. Tessa went to the rack. He stood in the corridor with the closed date behind him and the open inquiry in front.

At four o’clock the branch machine printed the strip with a bell, and the runner brought it up the long road to the Vestry because Drant had forwarded the copy to the inventory room and the inventory room had sent it on.

He took the strip in the corridor with four beds at the end of it, and the figure on it was eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight, and the instrument had reached him where he stood.
