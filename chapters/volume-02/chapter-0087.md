# The Finding Number

On the seventh of the month, a Thursday, Lin Hao attended the inventory room with Runa Sato beside him and the strip in his hand, and Drant put the thin register on the table without being asked.

He felt a flat calm, the kind that comes after a thing is written. He wanted to get through the attendance without giving the office another sentence.

Drant read the strip aloud. Eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight. Fifth week and then a sixth line started, because the standing had five dates and no end date and the sixth Thursday was today.

Runa put her minute sheet face up on the table. Drant read it twice.

"You stood between two papers," he said.

"I stood between two papers and I chose one of them," Runa said. "The Field retains the file. The Wing retains the inquiry. That is the whole of it."

Drant entered it. His hand was steady. For Drant, who had kept a desk for nine years without a third thing, entering a disobedience in plain words meant the book had a line it was never built to hold.

"Minute of the sixth," he said, reading it back. "Subject attended to inventory under standing date. Field declined delivery to holding. Field retains file. Household line at the subject's word, retained by the Field." He looked up. "That last one is yours, not mine."

"It is mine," Runa said. "He said it and I wrote it. If anybody in my division wants to know why a field operative retained a household for a man with a settled figure, they can come and ask him, and he will not be able to help them, and that is the correct shape for it to be in."

Then Ruta Vesely came in with the finding number sheet. It had ruling one hundred and twelve at the top in red ink beside the thirty-first register line, and under it the plate figure and the margin.

"The finding number is the join," she said. "It joins the machine to the register. A plate in a corridor and a line in a book. Any office anywhere can read it."

Lin Hao looked at the sheet. "What does it do now that it has not done."

Ruta turned the sheet over. On the back was a column of dates in her hand: the third, the tenth, the seventeenth, the twenty-fourth, the thirty-first, and the seventh of the new month.

"Six strips," she said. "Six identical figures. The Exchange has built a return out of the flatness. The return asks for the place of score and the cause. The cause is not at the counter. The cause is time."

Drant nodded. "The instrument of this step is not a plate. It is a duration. Forty days from the last movement. Fourteen days past the twenty-third this morning. Every Thursday the strip prints the same figure, and every Thursday the finding number sits beside it, and the two together say the account has stopped moving."

"That is the sentence they are writing."

"That is the sentence they are writing and they have not written it yet." Ruta put her finger on the red number. "A settled figure is publishable. A published figure is the only kind anybody can do anything about. The finding number is how a settled figure gets published. That is the work it has not done before."

"What is published, exactly."

"That is the part I cannot tell you, and I am not hiding it from you out of kindness." She squared the sheet. "A settled account with a finding number against it is a line in a district table. A line in a district table is a thing offices draw down from. It stops being a case and starts being a reference. And a reference is read by people who were never told anything about you and who will never ask you anything, because a reference is not a case."

"Whose desk is the district table."

"It is not mine and it is not Drant's and it is not a desk. That is the difficulty." She turned the sheet over and put her thumb on the reverse.

"It is assembled. A clerk in the Reading Office takes the lines that have come in from four offices and rules them into one page, twice a month, and the page goes to six floors. She has done it for years and does it well and has never once been asked to justify a line in it, because a district table is not a decision."

"It is a list that happens to be true."

"And a list that happens to be true is more dangerous than a finding."

"A finding is a thing you can answer," Ruta said. "You can appeal a finding. You can bring a witness. There is a man in this city who has been appealing the same finding for two years on a technicality about a date, and everybody in three buildings knows his name, and he is safe, in the way that a man who is publicly wrong is safe."

She put the pencil down and squared the strip that was still on the table.

"Whoever reads that page will read three lines about you that are not about you. That your figure is settled. That a finding number stands against it. That a distribution went out with three squares in it. They will not read a sentence and they will not read your name more than once, and the thing they will carry away is not an accusation. It is a shape."

He understood what it meant for one named person. For Ottilie Prebble, three years and four months at a counter, it meant another weekly return to sign with her name at the bottom.

"So it goes out."

"It goes into a table. Then somebody, twice a month, reads the table and decides what to do about the rows in it." Ruta held the sheet up and then put it back down flat, which was a way of saying she had already decided what it was. "I have been in this building nine years and I have watched two settled accounts become references. Neither of them was closed. Both of them got easier, because a reference is a thing you can work from without asking anybody anything."

Drant said, "It also stops being yours alone."

He looked up.

"That is the part nobody says out loud." Drant put both hands on the table. "The moment a figure is in a district table with a number against it, it stops being a question about a plate in a corridor and it becomes a question about a plate and the office that holds it and the corridor it passes through and the branch that forwards it and the street it runs down. There is no version of a published figure where the people who handle it are outside it."

Lin Hao looked at the thin register, which had six lines in it now, and thought about a corridor on the second floor at the Exchange where ninety people walked past a spike with a strip on it.

"How many people handle it."

"For the strip, three. For the sheet, four. For the figure itself, I have never counted and neither has anybody else, because the figure has been in a great many hands in a year and each of those hands thinks it is the first." Drant turned the register a quarter turn. "I will tell you the honest number for the plate itself. A man at the Exchange, a woman at the Reading Office who rules the table, a clerk on this floor, me, and a file upstairs that nobody opens."

"Five."

"Five, and a figure on a black tile in the Reading Square that a hundred people a day walk past and cannot read." Drant capped the pen.

"That is the whole of the handling. Five people and a tile, and not one of the five can be got at, because not one of them did anything. It is a figure in the hands of a machine, and the machine does what it does on a Thursday."

He looked at the sixth line in the register, which was open and ruled and had nothing in it yet.

"Do not look at that line," Drant said.

"I have looked at it."

"Then look at it once and remember that it is a line in a book and not a prediction."

Drant put the book down on top of it. "I open that book once a week and I write what you did on the days. If this week was a week you did nothing, there is nothing for me to write and the lines stay as they are, and that is the whole of what a clerk can do for a man in your position."

"Be dull in it," he said. "Be so dull that there is nothing for anybody to carry."

Runa said, "It has been published," very quietly.

"It has been referenced," Ruta said. "Published is next and you are correct to be frightened of it and I am not going to tell you it has happened."

"There is a way to check," Drant said. "When a table line is quoted, it is quoted whole, with the finding number and the date it was assembled. If somebody upstairs quotes yours with a date, it has been tabled. If somebody quotes it without one, it has been copied by hand out of a page somebody has been carrying about."

"Which is more likely."

"The second," Drant said. "A page in a book in this building travels. A table on a desk on the sixth floor does not come down without a reason."

Runa stood. "Then the Field's line holds today. He attends inventory. He is not delivered to holding. Write that."

Drant wrote it. Attendance kept, sixth of the standing, order prepared and unsigned and unissued, Field minute received and entered.

"And one more line, because I am in the room and I can see the page." He wrote it without asking. "Exchange weekly return for a settled account raised this morning, figure and finding number, counter-signature Ottilie Prebble. Entered as information. Not as an instrument."

"You had no authority for that."

"I have had the same pen for nine years and no authority for anything," Drant said. "It is entered. It cannot now be unentered by anybody who did not see it entered."

Lin Hao walked out with Runa into a corridor where the board had two lines and the second was typed, and the instrument had reached further than a room. It had reached a number that joined him to every office that could read, and behind him a woman with a bad shoulder was going to keep a door open for six more mornings before a room was made for him, and neither of those facts had anything to do with him and both of them were in the table now.
