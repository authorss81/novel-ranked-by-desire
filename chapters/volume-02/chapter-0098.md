# What He Will Not Spend

On the eighteenth, Drant put a third paper on the counter face up and did not take his hand off it, and Lin Hao felt the cold in the room go out of his face entirely, because he had been told about this one the previous afternoon in a corridor by a clerk who was not permitted to read it, and had spent a night deciding what he was going to do about it.

The demand was dated the seventeenth. He had been told about its existence on Thursday by a clerk who was not permitted to read it, and it had come today with a finding number in the corner, which meant it was not the paper the Wing had been carrying since the ninth. It was a new paper made out of the old one, and the new one had a number on it.

"Read it yourself," Drant said. "I have read it. I am not going to read it aloud this time."

Lin Hao read it. It was four paragraphs and the fourth one was the whole of it.

The first named the office, the inquiry, the standing and the account. The second named the day and the hour and the place: the twentieth, the hour at which the plate hold expired, the inventory room on the level below the Reading's third floor. The third gave the conditions, and the conditions were four, and they were printed as a list, which told him that somebody three floors up had sat down and decided this was a procedure and not an errand.

Attend at the hour named. Present the plate at the place named. Repeat the episode recorded on the thirteenth of last month, at the place named, under the control of the office. Name the instrument that will read the mass before and after, and the instrument must be one the office can send an officer to.

The fourth paragraph said what happened if it was done.

That the office would describe the account once, in its own hand, from its own observation; that the description would be the only one ever made; that the inquiry would close on the making of it; and that the member would be discharged of the standing and of the inquiry at the hour of the description.

Lin Hao read the fourth paragraph three times and then put the paper down on the counter with the printed side up, which he had never done with a piece of paper in that building.

"There is no offer in it," he said.

"There is the best offer anybody in this office has ever made to you," said Drant. "That is what you are holding. Read the fourth one again and read it slowly. The inquiry closes. The standing goes. You walk out of this and there is no sixth Thursday and no strip and no fourth floor and no woman with a card on any corridor in this city.

In five weeks that is what is on this counter and it is the first thing anybody has put in front of you that was worth having."

"I know what it is worth."

"Then you know what it costs." Drant put his own hand flat beside the paper. "Because it is not a promise. It is a description and the office makes the description, and a description is not the same as a finding. A finding can be answered. A description cannot.

You will attend on the twentieth and they will watch you do a thing you have done once in your life, and they will write down what they see, in their words, and that will be you for the rest of time in every office in this city."

Lin Hao looked at the paper.

"And if I do it," he said, "there is a description of me on the twentieth, and it is in their words, and it is not true, because I have not done that thing once in my life. I have been in a room. They will write down that a man did something to sixty people.

They will write it as an act and they will write it with a number on the front and it will be true in every office in Ostara for the rest of my life and it will be false."

"You cannot prove it false."

"I cannot prove it true," Lin Hao said. "That is the difference and it is the entire difference and it is why I am not doing it."

He heard himself and stopped.

Drant had gone very still on the other side of the counter.

"Say that again," he said.

Lin Hao said it again, and heard it the second time, and understood with a clarity that arrived like cold water what he had just walked into.

The demand had four conditions and the fourth one was the instrument. He had answered the third one and said nothing about the fourth, and he had said nothing about the fourth because the fourth was the door. Name the instrument that will read the mass before and after.

There was one instrument in Ostara that could read the mass and it was in a drawer off a corridor with four beds at the end of it, and it belonged to a woman who had carried a rack to twenty-seven on the wall and had kept a girl off two flights of stairs since the second of the month to keep a floor looking like a floor.

Name the instrument and he puts her in a Wing file.

"I am not naming an instrument," he said.

"Write it down," said Drant.

The room had three people in it and two of them were a clerk and a runner who had come in with a tray, and the runner put the tray down and left without taking anything, and the door stayed open.

"What I am about to say, you are going to enter as a refusal," Lin Hao said, "and I want you to enter it as a refusal and not as a third thing, and I know what you said on the thirty-first about refusals being answers and about answers keeping me here. I have thought about that for a night. I am choosing the answer. I would like you to enter it in the form of words I have said out loud."

Drant took out the thin book and not the tray book, and squared it on the counter, and uncapped the pen.

"Say it, then," he said. "Say it in order and I will write it in order and you may read it before I close it and I will correct anything you do not recognise."

Lin Hao put his hands flat on the counter, one on each side of the paper, the way he had done in a Field room on the sixth and in a room with no chairs in it on the twenty-sixth.

"Inquiry of the third of last month, standing of the same date, account of one digit, in the Classification Wing. I have had a demand dated the seventeenth of this month put in front of me. I refuse all four conditions of it.

"I refuse the attendance at the hour and the place named, because the hour is the hour at which the plate hold ends and a man who attends at the hour his own plate is released is attending an office and not an inquiry.

"I refuse to present the plate there, because a plate presented at a place of the office's choosing is not a reading, and I am willing to be read in a corridor ninety people walk past and I am not willing to be read in a room.

"I refuse to repeat the episode of the thirteenth of last month at the place named under the control of the office, and my reason is that there is no episode.

There was a room with nine chairs in it and sixty people in it and a man at the front who was the mouth of a thing that was being given away, and no office in this city watched it and no office will watch me do it again, and a description of a thing that only happens once cannot be made by watching it happen a second time in front of people who came for a hearing.

"And I refuse the fourth condition by name, and I refuse it by name so that the refusal is on the record as a refusal of that condition and not as a general objection. I will not name an instrument."

He stopped. Drant wrote for a while.

"Read it back," Lin Hao said.

Drant read it back. It was correct and it was in order and he had put there is no episode in as he had heard it and had not improved it and had put in a corridor ninety people walk past in as he had heard it.

"Correct anything," Drant said.

"Nothing."

"I am entering it as refusal, all four, given in person, on the eighteenth, with the demand present, and I am entering the fourth as a refusal by name and not as an objection to the whole." He wrote for another thirty seconds. "And I am entering one thing you did not say and I am telling you I am entering it, and you can object after I have said it aloud.

"I am entering that the member was offered a closure of the inquiry and declined it, and that the offer was made on the fourth paragraph of the demand and was made in good faith by a clerk who had no authority to make it or to refuse it."

Lin Hao looked at him.

"That is the fourth paragraph. That is in the demand."

"It is in the demand and the demand has four paragraphs and the fourth one is the price," said Drant. "And in nineteen years I have entered about nine hundred of these and every one of them has been a thing a man did to a room, and I have never once had to enter a man refusing a price, because in nineteen years not one person on this floor has ever turned one down.

That is what goes up three floors at four o'clock instead of a piece of paper with nothing on it."

"Then enter it."

"I have entered it." Drant capped the pen and squared the book. "And now I am going to tell you what it costs, and I have written it out before I say it, so that you are hearing a thing that has already been decided and not a threat."

He turned the thin book round and turned it back again and did not let Lin Hao read it.

"One. The standing is discharged today. The five dates, the sixth, the seventh, all attended, closed at ten past eleven this morning, and the fourth floor is discharged with it, and the strip does not go to the inventory room any more because there is no standing to forward it under. You will have no Thursdays in this city after this morning.

"Two. An inquiry without a standing is not an inquiry with no dates. It is an inquiry with dates that somebody else sets, and the somebody is the wing that drafted the fourth paragraph, and you will be summoned there, and the dates will not be Thursdays, and I will not see them.

"Three. Your band." Drant put his hand flat on the counter. "You are held at six on an instrument, not on merit, and the instrument is a requisition and a finding from the nineteenth of last month, and there is no clause in it that survives a refusal of a closure offer, because a refusal is an answer and an answer is a fact about a member, and a member who refuses a closure is a member whose standing is suspended pending description. Suspended is not lost.

It is not loss and you will not lose the six and I want that in front of you clearly. Suspended means the band is held by a paper and the paper is now about whether you will let yourself be described.

"Four, and this is the one I cannot do anything about." He looked up.

"Anneke Roos will have this on a desk in three days and it will be entered, and it will be entered correctly.

There is a sixth space in the fourth book and it is ruled and empty, and it is going to get a line in it. If the office has been wrong about one thing for a month and a half then it is wrong about the account, and this is the first document that anybody anywhere is going to be able to point at and say the account has been described."

Lin Hao felt it land. Not the band. The space.

"Then it is not true."

"It is not true and it is the truest document any of them will have for months," said Drant, "and that is the whole of the trick and I have been watching it work on other people for nineteen years. A false thing, written down in the right order, by a man who has refused nothing in his life, is worth more than a true thing nobody has signed."

He put the book down.

"There is a fifth thing and it is not mine and it is not this floor." He looked past Lin Hao at the corridor. "There is a woman in this building who is going to be asked about a mass this afternoon, because a demand that asks a member to name an instrument is a demand that has already been put to every instrument-holder in the district, and they do not wait for the member to name one. They go and ask.

And they asked your clinic on the eleventh and it went back on the twelfth and it is going back again this afternoon with a different question on it."

"Who told you that."

"Nobody told me anything," said Drant. "I have been a clerk in this building for nineteen years and I know what a demand looks like on the day it is written, because I have watched three of them, and on all three there was a day when somebody else's desk got a piece of paper about it, and I have never once been told, and I have never once been wrong."

Lin Hao picked the demand up off the counter and read the fourth paragraph one last time, the words about the description being the only one ever made and the inquiry closing on the making of it, and then he put it down square with the printed side up.

"I want it on the record that I said no to a closure," he said, "and that I knew what it was worth, and that I said it in a room with you in it rather than in a corridor on the seventeenth with a woman telling me it existed."

Drant wrote that down too, and read it back, and did not ask him to sign it, which was the fourth time in five weeks, and then he squared the tray on the left, which was the one tray, which was for everything, and put the demand in it, and the demand went up three floors at four o'clock with a refusal on it in six lines in a clerk's hand.

"You have until the twenty-first to see what comes back," Drant said. "And I will tell you the thing I have been not telling you since the eleventh, and then I am going to go and do the fourth floor, because there is nobody else to do it and I have been doing it since the twenty-third of last month and nobody has asked me to and nobody has stopped me.

"You asked me in the corridor on the eleventh what the six days were for. They were for you. They were never for the figure. A figure prints every Thursday and costs nothing. They were for the machine, and for the man who runs the machine, and for the ninety people who would see a figure and believe it.

They were to take the reading off a floor where it can be checked by anybody and put it somewhere it cannot, and then they sent a clerk who works from a paper down the middle of it, and I would like you to understand that you were looked at on the seventeenth and I have not been able to work out since whether that was the point or the accident."

He picked up the tray.

"I do not think it was an accident," he said, "and I think you already know that, and I am not going to be the one to say it in this building again this month."
