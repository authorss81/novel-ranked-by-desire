# Every Paper With The Person Who Brought It

Two thousand six hundred and forty-two was the third of June and the tenth is day two thousand six hundred and forty-nine, and two thousand six hundred and forty-nine less two thousand six hundred and forty-two is seven, and ten less three is seven by the month count.

## He Had It By Nine And He Did Not Write It Down

He had it by nine and he did not write it down and he went down four flights at about half past ten and came back up them and went down them again, which is about nine hundred steps a day that nobody counts.

He had carried it since the twelfth of May, which is the day a clerk spent the one question she has in a morning on a field in a book and was told no three times by people she had not asked.

He had not written it down anywhere, including on the back of his own hand, and the reason he had not was that a list on the back of his own hand is a thing a clerk asks to see and Wenna Raut has asked him for nine things in two years and this would be the tenth.

“It is not about the column,” he said, at about eleven. “Say that first.”

Wenna Raut had the book open and she did not look up.

“It is never about the column.”

“It does not go in the column and nothing goes in the column and there will be three lines in it when the thirtieth of this month squares that sheet at about half past two.”

“All right.”

“It goes in the Register and it goes in from today and it is not going to wait for the quarter-end, because the whole point of it is that it starts now, and there are four hundred and thirty rows in that book and every one of them has nothing in it but a day and an hour, and the eleven newest of them are seventy days old.”

## The Instruction

*From the tenth of June of this year every piece of paper received through the front door of this office is to be entered as received, with a date against it, and with a description of the person who brought it, written in the words that person used, whether or not it is true, whether or not it is complete, and whether or not anybody is able to check it.*

*Lin Hao.*

He put it down face up on the table and it was the second version of it and he had not crossed out the first one and there was no third one.

“Enter that. Exactly. You may not improve one word of it and you may not leave any of it out and I am not going to be in the room while you do it.”

She read it where it lay. She read it a second time and it took her nine seconds and he watched every one of them.

“Where does that go.”

“In the Register.”

“Not in the column.”

“No,” he said. “The column has three lines in it and it will have three lines in it at the thirtieth of this month, and there is nothing in this room today that is going to put a fourth one in.”

She picked the pen up.

“I am going to say two things and then I am going to enter it.”

“Go on.”

“The first is that you have written *written in the words that person used*,” said Wenna Raut. “That is not the same as *written as that person described themselves*, and there is a difference between those two and it is the whole of my life.”

“If I write what somebody said, I am entering their account. If I write what they looked like, I am entering mine and calling it theirs, and about two hundred years ago in this city somebody decided those are the same thing and every counter in it has been wrong ever since.”

“Yes.”

“And the second is that the drawer in that wall has four hundred and nine sheets in two boxes under the window that were entered as nothing, and this instruction does not reach them, and you have known that since you wrote it, and you have written the instruction anyway.”

*Two thousand six hundred and forty-nine days. Less two thousand six hundred and forty-two, the third of June of the eighth year, is seven, and about four hundred pieces of pencil paper were sitting in about four hundred kitchens with not one line about any of them in any book in this city.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is seventy days, which is two months and ten days after them, and the figure at the head of the standing page has not moved for one day of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two hundred and twenty-nine days, which is seven months and nineteen days after them, and a drawer has been shut under an instruction with a man's name under it for every one of them and this instruction does not reach it.*

## What It Closes And What It Costs

It took her about four minutes and she read it all back in the order it stood and his name was in it, and it went into the bound Register and not one stroke of it went into the column.

Before this morning there was nothing in this city that could say anything at all about a person who had brought a piece of paper to a building with no door onto a street. Not their name, which they may not give. Not their trade. Not whether they have been before. There was a day and an hour and four hundred and thirty rows and nothing whatever else in any of them.

From today there is a description.

That is the thing it closes. It is not possible again for a piece of paper to stand in a book in this city as a fact about paper only, and there will not be another building in this city where a person is invisible and a sheet is visible.

And about four hundred people are now in a book, described in somebody else's words, in a building that is not on any street, in the one place in Ostara where their claims can be found.

And there is exactly one person in Ostara who may ask that office any question about any of it.

He worked that out standing at the end of the table at about a quarter past eleven and it took him about four seconds, and he had worked it out before he wrote the instruction and had known it for about four weeks, and nobody in this room was going to tell him he was wrong and there had not been anybody in this city entitled to since the sixth of February of the first year.

The figure at the head of that page stands where it stood. It has not moved since the thirtieth of March of the fifth year and it has now been unmoved across thirteen of these sheets — that is thirteen sheets, the twenty-ninth is the one on the rail and sheets are counted by taking sheet seventeen itself.

The quarters run to twelve and the house keeps that figure too, and both are right, and they differ by one, and a book that prints one of them has to say which.

This is not a sheet day and it is not a quarter-end. The thirtieth does not go out until the end of this month.

## The First One Under The Instruction

She had the pen down and the book open and she said, “Then stand there, because the first one under it has been at the door since half past ten and I am not going to put a person into that row in front of the person who wrote the instruction if he is in the room.”

A woman came in and put a piece of pencil paper on the counter, which is what that door has been for seventy days now, and it was about the size of a receipt.

“Where do you live.”

“That is not a question.”

“It is the only question,” said Wenna Raut. “It is not being asked for anything and it is going in as what you said and not as where you are. Say it or say nothing and I will enter that you said nothing.”

The woman looked at him standing four feet off with his hands in his pockets and then at the clerk.

“Off the third court,” she said. “The one with the stair at the back.”

He did not move and he did not say anything and he had no right to say anything and there was no form in this city that would have let him.

Wenna Raut wrote it in, in her own hand, in about nine seconds, against the eighth of June and the hour, and she entered it exactly as it had been given, and *off the third court, the one with the stair at the back* is what is in that book and it is not a mistake and it is not going to be corrected.

He looked at it for about four seconds.

“It is not an address.”

“It is what she said,” said Wenna Raut, “and that is the whole of what an entry of that kind is, and you wrote it, and I entered it exactly, and there is nothing in this city that will take a thing out of a row once it is in.”

*Two thousand six hundred and forty-nine days. The first morning of the lane off Cordwain was the third of October of the year before the first, which is a hundred and eighteen days before the first of February and is therefore day minus a hundred and eighteen.*

*Two thousand six hundred and forty-nine less minus a hundred and eighteen is two thousand seven hundred and sixty-seven, and counting the first morning in itself, which is the only thing that has changed by counting it, is two thousand seven hundred and sixty-eight mornings.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is two hundred and fifty days, which is eight months and ten days after them, and the third line of a second column went onto a page at four that afternoon and has been on a rail in Cordwain for every day since.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and eighty-one, the twelfth of September of the seventh year, is two hundred and sixty-eight days, which is eight months and twenty-eight days after them.*

## And The Four Hundred And Nine That It Does Not Reach

He went down to the two cardboard boxes under the window at about a quarter to twelve and put two fingers on the lid of the top one and did not lift it.

Four hundred and nine pieces of rag paper. Entered as nothing, in the fourth month of last year, by a clerk who could only enter what she was given, on an instruction he had not yet written.

And there is no form anywhere in this city for undoing an instruction, which is why he shut the drawer himself on the last working day of last year with his own hand at eleven minutes to three, and which is why the instruction he wrote this morning cannot reach backwards into two boxes under a window, and he wrote it that way on purpose and did not write the words *from now on* and had thought about writing them for about nine seconds.

Four hundred and nine people brought paper to that door before the thirtieth of March and there is not one line in any book in this city that describes any of them, and from this afternoon there will be a line about every one that comes through it, and the door has no street on it, and about nine hundred people in this city can read every word of it, and the only person any of them may ask about it is him.
