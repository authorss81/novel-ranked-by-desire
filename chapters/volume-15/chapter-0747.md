# The Cost Is Said Out Loud

Two thousand eight hundred and twenty-two was the third of December and the tenth is day two thousand eight hundred and twenty-nine, and two thousand eight hundred and twenty-nine less two thousand eight hundred and twenty-two is seven, and ten less three is seven by the month count.

## Four Flights Down And A Door With No Name On It

The girl who carries linen brought a message up to the laundry on the tenth of December and it was nine words long and it said that the woman who keeps the third table would be in the room off the third court at about eleven in the morning, and that she was coming down on purpose and had not been asked to.

She had been in that building once, on a day in the third month of the seventh year, to give a sheet to a counter. She came in at eleven with her coat on and a pencil behind her ear and nothing else, and she stood inside the door for about four seconds and looked at the room.

“There is not much of it,” she said.

“There is not much of it,” he said.

“You do the floors here.”

“I do the floors of three washrooms and a stairwell and a bin room, at night, at a branch office off a third court. This is the other building.”

“Then this is the office,” said Perla Quist, “and I have been in it once and I did not know it was the office until about two minutes ago.”

## Nine Seconds, In This Room, To His Face

She did not sit down and she did not take her coat off.

She said it standing up, in about nine seconds, the way a woman says a figure at a market end.

“A woman has a name at the head of a line on a sheet that goes out four times a year to about two thousand counters, and about nine people a week hear the bottom of it read out loud in a passage by a man who is paid nothing.”

“In about four years a person will be standing at a counter saying that a building keeps her name because she is the reason about four hundred people trusted it, and she will be right, and there is nothing in this city that anybody can put on a table to say otherwise.”

That was eight seconds. She stopped.

“I have had seven days to say the rest of it to somebody and there is not somebody, and you are the only person in Ostara I am allowed to say the rest of it to, and I am not going to, because it is four years and it is mine and it does not help.”

He stood at the end of the table and said nothing for about nine seconds himself.

“You are not going to ask me to take it out,” she said, “and I want to say that you were going to ask me and I want to say it out loud that you were going to, so that there is one person in this room who has heard it.”

“You were right. I was going to.”

“There is no form anywhere in this city on which a line comes out from under a heading on a printed sheet,” said Perla Quist, “and a row may be added to and may not be taken from, and I have known both of those since the seventh year and I did not need you to say either of them to me this morning.”

## What She Came Down Four Flights To Ask For

“I want one sentence,” she said.

“That is not what I expected.”

“That is what I came down four flights for and it has taken me since the seventh day of this month and I have said the price first because a price is a thing you say and a request is a thing you ask for, and I did the first one so that you would not have to decide about the second one before you had heard the first.”

He waited.

“There is a name at the head of a line on that page and there is a sentence under it in a clerk’s hand and it is true, and then there is your name, and then there is nothing,” said Perla Quist. “I want a sentence after your name that says I did not agree and was not asked.”

“That is not in my gift.”

“No it is not,” said Perla Quist, “and I worked that out on the eighth and I came anyway, because I want to say it to your face before the thirtieth and not after it.”

“Because of the hearing.”

“Because of the hearing,” she said.
“Anybody may read the bottom of that sheet out loud in a passage, in a kitchen, at a counter, in about nine years. A name with nothing against it can be said by anybody at all.”

“A name with a sentence against it can only be said by somebody prepared to say the sentence as well. There are about nine people in this city who would be prepared, and one of them is a man behind a counter in Cordwain who is paid nothing, and that is the whole of the market.”

She put the pencil from behind her ear back on the bench-side of nothing and did not draw with it.

## Why He Cannot Write It, Said Plainly

He told her the reason in about eleven seconds and he did not dress it up, because she had come down four flights for the truth and not for a sentence.

“Everything on that sheet is public and it does not come back. If there is a sentence under my name that says a woman refused and was not asked, then about nine people a week will hear that sentence at a rail.”

“Four of them will say it in a kitchen within a month, and in about four years it will be in about four hundred kitchens in somebody’s words, and one of those words will be *refused*, and that is the word that will do the damage and not the other ones.”

“So a sentence protects me and does not protect you.”

“No,” he said. “A sentence protects nobody. It converts a name into a thing somebody can argue with, and arguable is the same word as useful, and a thing that can be argued with is a thing somebody can be asked to stop, and I have had four years to work that out and it took me about eleven seconds to say it to you.”

Perla Quist stood very still in the middle of that room.

“Right,” she said. “Then it is a name on a page with nothing against it, and that is what I will have.”

## Nobody Is Going To Thank Him For This And She Is Not Going To Say It

“I am not going to thank you,” she said, “and I want to be extremely clear about why, because you are going to spend about four days assuming that it is because you did it, and it is not.”

He looked up.

“It is because this is what happens,” said Perla Quist.
“You did a thing and I did not ask you to do it and you did it anyway, and I have come down here and said the price to your face, and if I thank you for that then I have thanked a man for letting me be damaged and told him it was all right.”

“About nine years ago a woman in a house off Ferrier Street read a definition of an unrecorded deed out loud to about four people and thanked a man for it, and it turned out she had been thanking him for the wrong thing for six years.”

She got her coat collar up.

“I have been a person who can be asked to stop for six years on purpose and I was extremely good at it and it was the correct position and it worked,” said Perla Quist, “and there is a line under a heading on a piece of paper with my name at the top of it, and it was not done by a magistrate and it was not done by a landlord and it was not done by anybody with a form.”

“It was done by me.”

“It was done by the only person in this city who had the standing to do it, who could not be refused, and who was told the price nine seconds before he did it. That is the whole of what happened.”

“There is nobody in this room who is going to be thanked and there is nobody in this building who is going to be thanked, and I am going to walk out of that door in about four seconds and I am not going to say anything on the stair.”

She did not say anything on the stair.

*Two thousand eight hundred and twenty-nine days. Less two thousand eight hundred and twenty-two, the third of December of the eighth year, is seven, and a woman came down four flights on purpose to say the cost of a name in about nine seconds and did not ask for it to be taken out.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is two hundred and fifty days, which is eight months and ten days after them, and the thirty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is four hundred and thirty days, which is fourteen months and ten days after them, and a copy carrying four sentences and no receipt has been older than that afternoon for every one of them.*

## What She Left On The Table

He stood in the room off a third court for a while after the door shut and understood the arrangement he was now living inside, which is that the cost of it had been said out loud, once, in a room, by the only person in Ostara who had a right to say it and no standing at all to enforce it.

There is no person in this city who can put a word against that line.

Not a magistrate, who would have to be shown a private index of nine sheets under a third table that may not be cited. Not a landlord’s man, who is right about every fact and has never heard of the laundry. Not a clerk at a window, who has a day book and takes returns and has never once been asked a question about a paper nobody else saw.

And not her, which is the part that is going to cost him the rest of his working life, because she could have refused the whole thing in about four words on the twenty-sixth of November and he would have carried it down four flights and put it in the drawer at the back of the bench with the other eleven things under a table, and he would be the same man on the third of December that he was on the second.

She priced it, and then she came down, and then she did not ask.

And at about half past five he stood at the table with the drawer shut in the wall on his left and twenty-six things on the bundle beside it, and there was a sheet squared under the window with a name in the second column that was going to be read out loud in a passage in about twenty days by a man who would not stop at it, because he reads what is on the page and has never been paid for it and has never been thanked.
