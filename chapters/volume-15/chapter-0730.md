# A Denial In His Own Hand

Two thousand seven hundred and eight was the ninth of August and the sixteenth is day two thousand seven hundred and fifteen, and two thousand seven hundred and fifteen less two thousand seven hundred and eight is seven, and sixteen less nine is seven by the month count.

## What He Has Had For Nine Years

He has had one thing for nine years that nobody else in this city has had, and it is the ability to say nothing at all about the two words cut at the foot of a wall, because the two words have never been printed in this city and the only copy is a rubbing in the inside of a boy’s coat, and there is no form on which anybody may ask about a coat.

For nine long years that silence held without a break. A claim went round a passage in May that a heading and two words are about the same thing, and it was wrong, and he said nothing, because a man in a white plate correcting four people about a wall is the one thing that would make the other three believe it.

A pencil went onto the bottom two inches in June in a hand that is not anybody’s, and he read past it, because the counterman reads what is on the page. A woman asked a boy what he has in his coat, and the boy gave the reason in about nine seconds and stopped, and he added nothing.

On the cold morning of the sixteenth he understood at last that the silence was finished, because about nine people now believe there is a thing under that wall that the office has been keeping, and a claim with a day beats one without at a counter.

The receipt he entered is carried by hand into cases about rooms, and the fourth column can date a copy but cannot correct a kitchen, and every one of those is a door he opened himself with instructions he wrote in a clerk’s hand with his name under them.

He came into the room off a third court at about eleven with his coat still open from the yard and told Wenna Raut what he wanted, in the order it stood, and he had gone over the whole of it twice at the bench before he came up the stairs.

She may only enter what she is given, and he wanted her to have it exactly, and an entry with his own name in it is the one entry in nine years he would not trust himself to write.

He set out for her the two halves plainly, first what the entry closes and then what the entry costs, because she enters exactly what she is given and cannot improve one word of it, and a denial that does not know its own cost is not an entry but a wish.

“What it closes,” he said, “is nine years of saying nothing. What it costs is that a denial is a claim, and it carries my name in a hand of this city, and it goes where about nine people a week read to the bottom.”

## The Denial

*No page of this office and no stone in this city is connected to any other, and a resemblance is not a figure.*

*Lin Hao. The sixteenth of August of the eighth year.*

He put it down face up square on the table and it was the second version of it and he had not crossed out the first one, because the first one had said more and more is worse, and there was no third one.

“Enter it,” he said. “Word for word. Alter nothing and omit nothing, and I will wait outside while it is done.”

She studied it where it lay without touching it. The denial sat square between them, short and level. Neither looked away from it while she read. The clock in the passage struck somewhere below. She went through it again slowly and it took her nine seconds and he watched every one of them, the way he watched in June, because an instruction with his name at the foot of it is a thing he watches even when he is not in the room.

“Where does that go.”

“On the page,” said Lin Hao. “At the foot of it, under the four sentences, under the name, in the office’s hand, with my name and the date, where about nine people a week read to the bottom.”

She picked the pen up and put it down again without writing.

“You have had nine years of saying nothing about two words,” said Wenna Raut. “That is the only thing this office has ever had that nobody could argue with, because a thing nobody says is not a claim, and the third of the four sentences was written to stop a claim being a thing you can argue with, and a denial is a claim about somebody else’s claim.”

“Yes.”

“And it carries your name in a hand of this city and not in pencil,” she said. “And it goes on a page that goes out four times a year to about two thousand counters, and a claim is a thing you can argue with, and you have just used it.”

He nodded gravely, because she had said the cost exactly and had not improved one word of it, and the cost was his and not hers, and there was nobody in this city entitled to tell him he was wrong.

*Two thousand seven hundred and fifteen days. Less two thousand seven hundred and eight, the ninth of August of the eighth year, is seven, and a denial went onto a page in the office’s hand with a man’s name and a date.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is a hundred and thirty-six days, which is four months and sixteen days after them, and the thirtieth sheet has been face up for every one of the last forty-six of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is three hundred and sixteen days, which is ten months and sixteen days after them.*

## What It Closes And What It Costs

It took her about four slow minutes and she read it all back in the order it stood and his name was in it, and it went onto the standing sheet and not one stroke of it went into the column, which has three lines in it and will have three lines when the thirty-first squares it at about half past two on the thirtieth of September.

That is the thing it closes. The office’s ability to say nothing at all about the two words, which it has had for nine years, is gone from today, because a denial is a claim, and a claim is a thing you can argue with, and he has now used the exact sentence the third of the four was written to stop, in the office’s hand, with his name under it.

And about nine people a week read that page to the bottom, and from the thirty-first that denial is on it, in a hand of this city and not in pencil, and anybody in any kitchen may argue with it, and the man who put it there is the only person in Ostara who may ask that office one question about any of it.

He saw it standing at the table end at about a quarter past eleven, taking some four seconds, having seen it before the denial was written and carried it for some four days, with nobody in that room able to say he was wrong.

The bundle on the table holds twenty-five things of which the last has never been opened by anybody, and the sheet that goes out four times a year is the sheet it was in March with seven lines under the four sentences, and every person who reads to the bottom of it from today will find his name standing at the end of it.

None of that is touched by what he did today, although all of it will be read differently from today, because a page with a denial on it is a different page from one without, even when every figure on it stands where it stood.

He went down four flights at about noon and told Perla Quist with the nine sheets out between them, in the order it had happened, because she keeps days and a denial with a day is a day she can hold even when she cannot cite it, and because she ruled nine sheets under a third table in October and headed them in February and wrote a line under the heading in May that went nowhere, and every one of those was a denial in its own shape.

She listened without writing anything down, and when he had finished she drew the pencil slowly along the edge of the bench and looked at him for a while, and he saw that she was not angry and not glad, but tired in the way people are tired who have kept nine sheets under a table for a year, and who have said no five times and are about to be asked a sixth.

“You have put yourself twice on paper now,” she said. “Once in pencil, under a striking, where a clerk entered your name because you wrote four words in September, and once in the office’s hand, under four sentences, where you wrote your own name yourself. And the two are not the same instrument and you know the difference because you were the one who said so.”

He nodded and went back up the four flights and sat down at the table without touching anything, because a denial is a claim about somebody else’s claim, and he had known that when he wrote it, and had written it anyway, because nine years of a silence kept by a building with no door onto a street is not a denial at all, it is only an absence, and from today anybody at all may put a name to that absence and argue with it in the open.

Wenna Raut squared the sheet carefully against the edge of the table although nobody had asked her to at all, because a denial with a name under it is an entry that has to sit straight.

From today onwards there is now a claim about two words with his name on it in a building with no door onto a street, and absolutely anybody at all may argue with it openly now, and he simply cannot take it back, because a row may be added to and may not be taken from, and a page that goes out four times a year does not come back.
