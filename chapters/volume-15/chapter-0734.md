# Two Records And No Instrument

Two thousand seven hundred and thirty-four was the fifth of September and the eleventh is day two thousand seven hundred and forty, and two thousand seven hundred and forty less two thousand seven hundred and thirty-four is six, and eleven less five is six by the month count.

## What He Is Allowed Down Four Flights

He did not ask to see the book again and she had not expected him to, and on the stairs at about half past four he understood that he had been given a thing about a room and not a thing about a day, and that this was correct, and that it was going to be the shape of the rest of his life.

What he was allowed to carry down four flights was this, and he checked it on the second landing so that he would not add to it on the way down.

About forty pieces of pencil paper in this city carry any hand of the two words at all, and each of them carries a day at its foot in the handwriting of the person who made it. One of the forty came through the returns counter of a division building and was written down in a book that is not a return book by somebody who keeps that book.

That was all. There was no name and no date and no number of pages and no entry in it of anything said at a counter, and he had gone up that stair expecting to be given an instrument and had been given a fact with nothing in it but the fact itself.

## About Forty Lines And One

Perla Quist had the ninth sheet out and a second sheet squared under it, and the ruler across the edge of the bench, and about two hundred empty lines under the heading as usual.

“You have been up a stair,” she said.

“I have been up one flight to a room over the counters and down again, and I am not going to tell you whose it was.”

“Then do not,” said Perla Quist, “and do not tell me what the room was, because I will use it.”

He put the forty out for her in the order he had them, and she went down the fourth column with the pencil held above the bench the way she always did, and he stood at the end and let her work, because the ninth sheet is hers and a man who interrupts a column has to be told why.

It took her about forty minutes and she did it without speaking except twice, once to ask him whether a piece of rag paper could have come through a division counter at all, and once to ask him whether the person who had written it down was known to be alive.

“Yes,” he said.

“That is the whole of what I wanted,” said Perla Quist, and wrote one more thing at the foot and squared it, and then she did not say anything for about nine seconds, which from her is a shout.

## The Line They Do Not Agree About

Thirty-nine of the forty hold together. The man wrote the day he made the copy at the foot of it. She was handed it on a day, or a day or two after, and the day she wrote in the column is the day she took it off the counter.

On the one that does not hold together, both of them have the same date and they have two different things.

“The man made his copy on the twenty-eighth and wrote that on it,” she said. “That is what the column says, and the column is right, and it is what my four column is for.”

“And?”

“And he handed it in at a division counter on the twenty-eighth as well,” said Perla Quist, “and the day book has it as the twenty-eighth because that is the day the counter did something. Both of those are the same number.”

“One of them is the day a piece of paper was made in a kitchen by a man who cannot write it any other way. The other one is the day a clerk at a window did a thing to it.”

“Neither of them is wrong. Neither of them can be the other one.”

He sat down on the end of the bench, which he had not done in that room in a year and a half.

“Then how does anybody tell which is which.”

“By asking the person who wrote it, and there is nobody to ask,” said Perla Quist. “And so about one line in forty of the only instrument in this city that can put a claim true against a day has a day on it that means a counter was open, and about four hundred people are going to be right about most of them and wrong about one, and they will not know which one, because the number is the same number.”

*Two thousand seven hundred and forty days. Less two thousand seven hundred and thirty-four, the fifth of September of the eighth year, is six, and of about forty copies carrying any hand of the two words the two records in this city agree about thirty-nine and do not agree about one, and neither of them is wrong.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is a hundred and sixty-one days, which is five months and eleven days after them, and the thirtieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-one of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is three hundred and forty-one days, which is eleven months and eleven days after them, and a copy carrying four sentences and no receipt has been older than that afternoon for every one of them.*

## What A Number Does Not Say

“A claim with a day on it beats a claim without one,” he said. “A clerk at a counter with a window in it ordered that on the twenty-sixth of July and it went into a book that goes out to nobody. And the day on the claim is the day the copy was made.”

“The day on the claim is supposed to be the day the copy was made,” said Perla Quist, “and it is, in thirty-nine lines out of forty, and in the fortieth it is a day a clerk was at a window, and there is no column in this city that says which of the two a number is, and there is not going to be one, because the day book is not mine and is not in my room and has no heading and no keeper anybody may name.”

“I am not blaming you for it.”

“I know you are not,” said Perla Quist, “and I want to say the other half out loud before you leave this room, and then I am going to wind the bench and you are going to let me.”

She put the pencil down across the two sheets.

“I have been keeping days for six years and about a month ago a man went into a book at a counter and ordered two claims against each other and the one with the day won,” she said. “That was the twenty-sixth of July.”

“I have not slept properly about it since and I have not said it to anybody, and I am saying it to you because you are the only person who may ask this office anything, and the office is the only thing I have ever been afraid of.”

“You have.”

“And now I know that about one day in forty of mine is a day a clerk at a window was open, and the day does not say so, and I cannot make it say so, and if anybody ever comes at me with a figure off my fourth column I will not be able to put my hand on the one that is not mine.”

He wanted to say something to that and could not find a sentence that was not a form, so he let it stand.

## What He Asked For And Did Not Get

He asked her for one thing at about twenty to twelve, and he asked it badly, and he knew he was asking it badly while he was asking it.

“I want a heading on the column that says what kind of day it is.”

“No,” said Perla Quist, without looking up.

“You have not said why.”

“A heading is a thing somebody can ask about,” she said, “and a thing somebody can ask about is a thing somebody can be asked to stop, and I ruled the one I have and I said out loud in February, before anybody asked me for anything, that I am not an instrument.”

“That was the price of the one I have, and I am not paying it twice in six years for a column that would put a word in the mouth of about four hundred people who cannot check it.”

She squared the second sheet against the edge of the bench.

“And it would not be true,” she said. “A number with a kind written over it is a column that is guessing. I have got about forty pieces of paper and not one of them tells me anything except the day, and the day is what the man put there, and I am not going to stand over a man who cannot read and tell him what his own handwriting meant.”

He took the lift of her refusal at once because it was right, and because she had given him the reason and the structural reason together, in that order, which is her way and had been her way since the seventh year.

He went down the four flights after that and came back up at about half past five and stood at the table in the room off a third court with the thirty-first sheet squared under the window, the head unmoved at the top of it, three lines in the second column, and his own denial at the foot under the four words about about four hundred pieces of paper.

There are two records of a thing being true against a day in this city. Nine sheets under a third table with a heading a woman ruled on her own paper, and a grey cloth book in a pigeonhole over a counters hall, opened at the first page.

Neither of them is an instrument. Neither of them may be cited. Neither of them has a keeper who may be sent or a keeper who may be named, and he is the only person in Ostara who knows both of them are there, and there is no form on which that can be entered anywhere, and there is no person in this city entitled to be shown either of them.

And there is a man alive tonight in a kitchen a long way from Cordwain who wrote a day at the foot of his own copy with his own hand, and two books in this city hold that number, and in one of them it is the day he made a piece of paper and in the other it is the day a clerk at a window did something to it.

Nobody may go and ask him which one he meant, and nobody may tell him that the question exists.
