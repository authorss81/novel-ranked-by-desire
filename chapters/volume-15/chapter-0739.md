# He Opens The Drawer

Two thousand seven hundred and sixty-six was the seventh of October and the fourteenth is day two thousand seven hundred and seventy-three, and two thousand seven hundred and seventy-three less two thousand seven hundred and sixty-six is seven, and fourteen less seven is seven by the month count.

## The Second Instruction

He came in at about ten and told Wenna Raut what he wanted before she had the sheet squared, and he told it in the order it stood, because she may only enter what she is given and cannot improve one word of it, and a second instruction is not a thing a clerk should be handed in a hurry.

The first instruction was entered on the twenty-first of October of the seventh year in a clerk’s hand with his name under it, and it said that a drawer in the wall on the left of that table was not to be opened, and he shut it himself at about eleven minutes to three on the thirtieth of December and put the key on the table and did not keep it.

The second instruction said that on the fourteenth of October of the eighth year that drawer is to be opened by the officer named at the head of this sheet and by no other person, and shut again by him at half past three, and that the fact of the opening is to be entered in the bound Register with the date against it and the hour against the date.

“What it closes,” he said, “is the instruction of the twenty-first of October. What it costs is that there is no form anywhere in this city for undoing an instruction, so the only way I can get that drawer shut again is to enter a third one, and the third one cannot be withdrawn either, and after this afternoon that drawer cannot be opened by anybody ever again, including me.”

She read it back in about four minutes with the pen in her hand and then asked her question of the morning, and it was not about the wording.

“What is it for.”

He stood at the end of the table for a while.

“Because on the twenty-third of this month I told a clerk at a counter with a name over the door that this city holds none of those papers, and it was true, and he wrote it in a book,” said Lin Hao. “And I would like to know whether it was the whole of the truth.”

She nodded and put the pen across the book.

“That rule has not stopped you opening that drawer,” she said. “You are not asking anybody anything. You are asking paper. Enter it.”

## Four Hours

He opened it at about eleven with his own key and there were two cardboard boxes on the boards under the window with about four hundred and nine pieces of rag paper in them, and every one of them is a count of pieces of paper and is not a count of claims and is not a count of people, and he had written that down himself in August at a bench four flights below a laundry and it had not stopped being true.

He did not sit down for the first of it. He took a stack of about forty out of the top box and stood at the table under the window and read the bottom two inches of each one, because that is all any of them is: a piece of pencil paper with the last two inches filled in by a person in a kitchen, and no figure on any of them and no name on any of them, except where a person wrote their own name.

That was about one in nine.

Then he carried the stack down four flights and put it on the boards in the bin room and came up again, because his chest had been spent since the fourth year and he could not sit still with four hundred and nine things in a room and read them, and going up and down the stairs four flights eleven times between eleven and half past three was the only way he could keep his hands busy.

Three lines of the fourth column in a laundry had been the best thing anybody in this city produced in four years. Four hundred and nine pieces of rag paper read in about four hours by a man on a night shift was nothing at all, and it was the last time anybody in Ostara would ever read them, and he understood that within about twenty minutes and read every one of them anyway.

## The One He Reads Twice

Somewhere about a third of the way down the second box, in the early afternoon, he lifted a piece of rag paper out of the stack and turned it over and found that there was nothing on it at all.

Not a word, not a stroke, not an indentation where a pencil had been pressed and wiped out. A piece of rag paper about the size of a copy of the foot of a printed page, and empty, and it went into that drawer in August because everything went into that drawer, and it was entered as nothing because he had instructed that they were entered as nothing.

He stood at the table under the window with it in both hands for about a minute and a half, and then he put it on the boards to the side and went and got the whole of the second box out and put it beside it, and he went through about a hundred and ten sheets looking for another one, and there was not another one.

Then he came back and read the empty one a second time, because that is what a person does with a piece of paper that is doing what it is doing, and it was empty again, and there was nothing to read the second time, which is exactly why he read it twice.

Somebody carried this through a front door off a third court on the sixteenth of August in the eighth year and handed it to a clerk and said nothing whatever, or said a thing that was not written down, because a clerk could only enter what she was given and there was nothing given. And she put it in a drawer because a drawer is where a piece of paper goes, and that is the whole of what happened to it.

And he will never know which of the two things was true. That the woman who handed it in had nothing to write about. Or that she wrote what she had on another piece and kept this one, and kept it because of what she thought was on it.

This office may not put a question to that person about a thing somebody else has written down, and no office in this city may ask her why she brought in a sheet with nothing on it, and there is no page anywhere that either of them could be put on.

She is not traced and is not asked and will not be, and nobody in this city will ever know whether she came back to that door after the sixteenth of August or whether she went out into the street and did not, and this sheet will go on being empty in a drawer that cannot be opened again whatever the answer turns out to be.

*Two thousand seven hundred and seventy-three days. Less two thousand seven hundred and sixty-six, the seventh of October of the eighth year, is seven, and a drawer shut since the twenty-first of October of the seventh year was opened by the officer named at the head of the sheet at about eleven in the morning and shut again at half past three.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is a hundred and ninety-four days, which is six months and fourteen days after them, and the thirty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fourteen of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is three hundred and seventy-four days, which is twelve months and fourteen days after them, and a copy carrying four sentences and no receipt has been older than that afternoon for every one of them.*

## What It Closes And What It Costs

He read the foot of every one of them, all four hundred and nine, and about a hundred and ninety of them had a day written at the foot in the handwriting of the person who made them, and about two hundred did not, and the rest were about other things entirely and had been handed in by people who believed they were handing in the same thing and were not.

At half past three he shut it himself and turned the key and put the key on the table, at almost the same hour of the afternoon as the thirtieth of December, and stood there afterwards with his back against the wall.

What it closed is real. There is not a second way to open that drawer in this city or in any other, because the only way is an instruction, and he has just entered the second of them, and the second one says it is opened by him and by nobody else, and the first one says it is not to be opened at all.

The two of them have been in that book since this morning and nothing in this city takes an instruction out of a book.

What it costs is the ordinary cost, which is that he now knows what is in there and cannot have it out of his head, and that about four hundred pieces of paper are a count of pieces of paper and not a count of claims and not a count of people, and that one of them is empty and cannot be explained and never will be.

He did not sleep that night, and at about half past five he was at the table with nothing in his hands.

The only record in Ostara that he opened it is a line in a bound Register written by a clerk in her own hand, and it says that on the fourteenth of October of the eighth year the drawer in the wall on the left of the table was opened at about the hour of eleven by the officer named at the head of the sheet and shut again at half past three, and that no other person was present.

That is the whole of it. It will be read by nobody, it goes up a stair, and the drawer under it is shut now and cannot be opened again, and the only person in this city who knows that the four hundred and nine were read is a man standing at a table who is not going to be asked about it by anybody, ever, including the woman who brought in the empty one, who does not know it exists, and who is not going to be told.
