# He Does Not Read It Out

Two thousand six hundred and seventy-six was the seventh of July and the thirteenth is day two thousand six hundred and eighty-two, and two thousand six hundred and eighty-two less two thousand six hundred and seventy-six is six, and thirteen less seven is six by the month count.

## Six Days After The Asking

For six days the hill talked about nothing else, the way a hill talks, in kitchens and at doors and at the bottom where the hot water is sold, and by the thirteenth it had reached four flights down in a laundry off a street two streets wide, where Perla Quist keeps nine sheets under a third table and writes down when a thing was said.

She sent word up the stair by a girl who carries linen, and the boy came down at about half past twelve with the paste brush in his apron and chalk on his fingers, because a boy who puts boards up by a kerb is never far from chalk.

There were three of them in the back room. Perla Quist at the bench with the nine sheets squared. Lin Hao at the end of the bench with his hands in his pockets, saying nothing, because he had not asked the boy anything in seven years and was not going to start. And the woman with the yoke, who had asked, and who had come because Perla Quist had sent for her and not because anybody had told her to.

The back room smelled of boiled soap and damp linen with steam pressing under the door from the washroom front, and the ruler lay squared against the edge of the bench, and about two hundred empty lines waited under the four entries she had made since February, and the boy looked at those lines the way a person looks at a wall that has room left on it.

“I asked him,” said the woman, before anybody else had spoken. “I asked him what he has in his coat. My brother talks and I asked. I am not an officer and I am not a clerk and I have no form, and I asked him straight out on a hill.”

She put the yoke down against the bench although there was no need to, because her shoulders remembered it, and her hands were red from the cans.

“I sell hot water at the bottom of the hill. I have done it for about nine years. I have never asked anybody about a coat before and I did not know I was asking the one question nobody wants answered until the coal man told me on the way down.”

The boy stood by the bench with his eyes on the nine sheets and did not look at any of them. He had the paste brush in his apron and a lump of chalk in his pocket, and his fingers were marked with both, and he held himself very still, the way he holds himself on a hill when the paste is drying.

## About Nine Seconds

“Tell her why you did not answer,” said Perla Quist, gently, the way she speaks to people who bring her copies. “Not the words. The reason. About nine seconds and then stop.”

He was quiet for about four seconds and then he spoke, and he spoke to the bench and not to any person, and it took him about nine seconds and then he stopped.

“You have something in your coat,” he said, “and everybody in this city can see your face while you do not take it out. I have a piece of chalk and a board and nine people a week, and if I say it out loud it is only a thing somebody heard.”

Then he stopped, the way she had told him, with his hands at his sides and chalk on his fingers, and nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds afterwards.

The woman with the yoke looked at Lin Hao standing at the end of the bench with his coat buttoned and understood, all at once, that the folded sheet in the inside of that coat was the thing the boy meant, and that a man everybody in this city could read all the way down had something in his coat he would not read out, and that a boy with a paste brush had just said so to her face.

“I did not know,” she said.

“You could not know,” said Perla Quist. “That is not a thing anybody can know. It is the worst thing about a page that is copied by people who cannot read, and it does not need anybody to do it deliberately.”

Lin Hao said nothing at all, which was the hardest thing he had done in about four years, because the boy had given the reason exactly and had not improved one word of it, and anything he added would make it smaller.

He thought about the seventieth form going up in April with two empty squares at the foot of it, and about standing four feet off that boy with his hands in his pockets and not asking, and about the folded sheet in his own coat that had been there since December, and the three things sat together in his head without touching, the way three lines in a second column sit together without touching.

The woman with the yoke was crying now, quietly, without any sound, and nobody in that room looked at her directly, because there is nothing in a laundry that answers crying except work going on.

*Two thousand six hundred and eighty-two days. Less two thousand six hundred and seventy-six, the seventh of July of the eighth year, is six, and a boy stood in a back room off a street two streets wide and gave the reason in about nine seconds and stopped.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is a hundred and three days, which is three months and thirteen days after them, and the thirtieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirteen of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is two hundred and eighty-three days, which is nine months and thirteen days after them.*

## The Thing Nobody Wants Heard

Perla Quist wrote the day on the ninth sheet after the boy had gone back up the hill with his brush in his apron, and she wrote it in her own hand and did not improve it, and the woman with the yoke watched her do it and did not ask what it was for, because nine sheets under a third table are not a thing anybody asks about.

She entered that a question was put at a corner about a coat, and that no answer was given at the corner, and that the reason was given four flights down six days later in about nine seconds, and she put the day against all three, because that is what a column with a heading at the top of it is for, and the heading says what a copy carried on the day it was made.

“What happens to it now,” said the woman with the yoke.

“Nothing,” said Perla Quist. “It goes under my heading with a day against it and it goes nowhere, the way the line under my heading goes nowhere, because some months hence somebody will ask a woman who cannot read about a heading, and she will repeat a rail saying, accurately yet wrongly, while this entry waits here.”

Then she looked up and said the thing nobody in the yard wants heard, in one paragraph, because somebody had to say it and there was nobody else in that room who could.

A claim about two words is unanswerable, and the reason is a boy, and the boy is right, because the only copy is a rubbing in the inside of his coat and about nine people have been in a room where it was read out, and a man in a rank-one coat carries a folded sheet he has not read to anybody, and a thing nobody says out loud cannot be copied and therefore cannot be answered.

The woman with the yoke went out with the yoke on her shoulder and the cans empty, and Lin Hao stayed at the end of the bench for a while after that with his hands in his pockets, feeling the folded sheet against his ribs and thinking about the rubbing in the inside of a boy’s coat, and the two pieces of paper sat in two coats in the same city and neither of them could be put where about nine people a week could see it.

He had wanted anger and felt only a great tiredness, and underneath the tiredness a kind of pride he did not want, because the boy had stood in a back room and said in about nine seconds what he himself had not been able to say in nine weeks, and had stopped.

“You did not ask him,” said Perla Quist, after a while.

“No,” said Lin Hao. “She asked him. She is not an officer and she is not a clerk, and I have never asked him and I never will, and the reason is in your column now with a day against it.”

He went up behind the woman with the yoke, the boy’s nine seconds going round in his head, and left the bound Register shut where it stood on the table under the window, because a reason given out loud in a back room is the one piece of paper in this city with nowhere to be written on, and that was the whole of why the boy was right, and he had been carrying it since April without knowing what it was.

Wenna Raut watched him stand there and did not ask him what had happened four flights down, because a clerk who may only enter what she is given does not ask about a thing nobody gave her, and he was glad of it, because the nine seconds were not an entry and could not be made into one.

The sheet on the rail is the thirtieth, and the second column has three lines in it, and the drawer in the wall is shut, and the pencil went down with the twenty-ninth, and what stands where it stood is a question asked on a hill at half past seven on the seventh of July and answered six days later in a laundry by a boy with a paste brush.

The woman with the yoke had gone down the hill with her brother waiting at the bottom of it, and the reason had gone out of that room in her mouth, and a sentence cannot be put back into a mouth, and by the twentieth of that month it will be in forty kitchens in her words, and not one of them will be the boy’s.
