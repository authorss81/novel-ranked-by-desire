# He Did Not Ask

Two thousand five hundred and sixty was the eleventh of March and the seventeenth is day two thousand five hundred and sixty-six, and two thousand five hundred and sixty-six less two thousand five hundred and sixty is six, and seventeen less eleven is six by the month count.

## Four Streets And Four Doors

He put it in writing on the top landing of his own stair at about a quarter to eight in the morning, because there is nowhere else in this city he is allowed to put a thing down.

*I want to read about four hundred kitchens.*

Then he took it down with it in his own hand and did not write the reason, because the reason was ready in about four seconds and he had found it in November and it had not improved.

Four hundred is a count of pieces of paper and only of pieces of paper. It is not nine hundred people, and it is not nine claims, and there is no figure anywhere in this city that is all three at once and anybody who puts them together has made a fourth thing.

He had been at the office by nine and had got nothing done by half past nine, which is a thing that has happened about four times in two years and never on a morning when anything was due.

## Why It Is Not Cowardice

He knew where about four of them were.

One was two streets off Cordwain and he had stood at the end of it on the eleventh of this month and had not gone down it. One was up the hill. One was off a street where he had bought nothing for six years. One he had because a man had said a street name out loud at a counter about four months ago and had not known he was being useful.

He could get to four of them inside a week by walking.

A house is not a public place. That is the whole of it and no rule in this city says it, because a rule would have had to be written and nobody wrote one. What a man may not do is put a second question to a person about a thing she has already been asked about by somebody else, at her own front door, with his hat in his hand.

His own prediction, which he wrote down and which he was not going to let out of his own hand: if he did it in four houses this month he would be doing it in four hundred in about a year, and there would be a fifth house that knew about the first three before he got to the door of it, and a person who has been refused at nine counters does not need to be refused a tenth time to stop writing.

That was a prediction and it was not a figure and he had no way of checking it and he was doing it anyway, in his own head, every morning, which is the only way anybody in this city does arithmetic they cannot check.

## What She Is Not Going To Do And Why

He was down four flights at about ten past one and she had the nine sheets out and the fourth column with one word in it and about two hundred lines empty.

“I am going to say the thing first,” said Perla Quist, “because you have not got here yet and you have a face on you that I have seen about eleven times in four years and I have worked out what all eleven of them were.”

“You do not know what I came for.”

“You have come to ask me to go and get them,” said Perla Quist.

He did not say anything.

“You have come to ask me to go into four houses, or four hundred, and take a piece of paper off the back of something and bring it up these four flights and put it in a column I have ruled myself, and I have not been asked and you were not going to ask me, and I am going to say the no now so that neither of us spends the afternoon being polite about it.”

“Yes.”

“A woman who keeps a list is not a person who may be sent to fetch things,” said Perla Quist. “The moment somebody with a building behind him sends me, I am that building’s, and everything I have got on nine sheets becomes a thing that building had a look at.”

“There is no office to be an instrument of. If there were one it would be a building with no door onto a street, and in about four years everything anybody has ever brought me would be a thing somebody sent a woman in a laundry to collect.”

She squared the ruler.

“I have said that four times now and you have heard it four times and you have never once tried to get past it,” she said, “and I want to say the other half, because you have never noticed it. It has never once been about you.”

*Two thousand five hundred and sixty-six days. Less two thousand five hundred and sixty, the eleventh of March of the eighth year, is six, and a woman off a street two streets wide has said no to a man in a white plate for the fourth time in four years and it has not once been about him.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and forty-seven, the twenty-eighth of February of the eighth year, is nineteen days, and there has been one word in a fourth column for every one of them and it is the fourth word of a line of a page that went face up on a rail at four on the thirtieth of December.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and twenty, the first of February of the eighth year, is forty-six days, which is one month and sixteen days after them, and the man who came in by the only door that office has on the first of this month, holding a piece of pencil paper, was given four words by a clerk who may only enter what she is given, and the four words were that none of them is a deed.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is a hundred and sixty-seven days, which is five months and seventeen days after them.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is eight hundred and forty-eight days, which is twenty-eight months and eight days after them.*

## The Thing She Offers Instead, Which Is Worse

“All right,” he said. “Then tell me what you will do, and tell me the number as well, because I will not do arithmetic on an answer you have already softened.”

“I will read every sheet that is brought to this table,” said Perla Quist, “and I will put in that column what the page said on the day that copy was made. That is the whole of what I am offering and I am offering it before you ask because it is worse than the thing you came for and you should not have to find that out in a fortnight.”

“Why is it worse.”

“Because it is a thing about four hundred kitchens that is going to be true for the people who bring their sheets to a laundry,” she said, “and about a third of them will never come, and it will be true for those, correctly, for as long as I live, and wrong the first time a line goes on that page, and there is nothing anywhere in this city that can put a correction into a house.”

“About a third will never come.”

“About a third. That is the number of the ones who stopped in the middle of last year and it is not going to get better, because a woman who has stopped does not know there is anything to come back for, and there is no form on which I may go and tell her.”

She put the ruler down beside the pencil. “You wanted four hundred kitchens and I am offering you about two hundred and seventy of them. I cannot tell you which two hundred and seventy and neither can you, and that is the whole of the difference between the two things.”

He had not known that when he came down four flights and he had not known it on the top landing either, and it took him about four seconds standing at the bottom of them with his coat on and his hands in his pockets, and then he went out into a street that was wet in about four places and had nobody in it at all.

There is no figure in this city for what he wanted. Not the head, which stands at eight hundred and fifty-two and has not moved since the thirtieth of March of the fifth year. Not the second column, which has three lines in it and can take one more before the thirtieth of this month. Not the four hundred, which is a count of pieces of paper and neither of the other two things and does not become either of them by being said more often.

## Nine Words

She picked the nine sheets up off the second table and squared them and put them back under the third one, and then she stopped with one hand on the edge of the table.

“And I have kept something back, and I am going to say it the once and then I am going back to these two hundred lines, and you may ask me about it later or you may not and I would rather you did not.”

“All right.”

“Nobody else can get at those four hundred papers.”

That was nine words and she said them without any emphasis at all and then picked up the pencil and went back to the two hundred empty lines in the narrow column, and he stood about four feet off in a wet room and understood the whole of it in about four seconds.

He is the only person this office may ask. Every one of those four hundred people had been turned away at a counter and had been given a page to copy out by a building that has never once been given anything. There is nobody else in Ostara a door would open to and nobody else in Ostara who is permitted to be asked.

She had not suggested it. She had given him the size of it and the reason and stopped.

He did not ask.

He went up the four flights with nothing in his hands, which is how he has gone everywhere since the summer, and about nine hundred people in this city can read every line of about four hundred pieces of paper in about four hundred kitchens, and there is one door in this city that any of those people would open, and it is in a building with no door onto a street, and it is behind him in a coat.
