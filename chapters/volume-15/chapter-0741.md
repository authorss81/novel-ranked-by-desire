# One More Thing In The Bundle

Two thousand seven hundred and eighty was the twenty-first of October and the twenty-eighth is day two thousand seven hundred and eighty-seven, and two thousand seven hundred and eighty-seven less two thousand seven hundred and eighty is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven by the month count.

## Three Years On A Table

The bundle came off a table on the twenty-eighth of October of the fifth year and has been on that table ever since, apart from about nine days in the sixth year when it was put down a stair and forgotten, and it has been three years to the day this morning and nobody in that building thought of the date.

Twenty-five things were on it when he came in at about ten. A receipt for a coat. A folded public sheet with a corner torn. An envelope that had been opened and had nothing in it. A pencil. A tin that had held something and had rusted through at one corner.
Two keys that fit nothing in that building. And twenty more of the same kind, brought up the stair or handed in at the counter or picked up off a floor, and every one of them had gone onto that table within about nine seconds of arriving and had stayed there.

Not one of them has been opened in nine years.

That is not a rule anybody has written down. It is the way the table works, the same way a key board works, because the table is in a room where things are entered and a bundle is a thing that is not entered, and it has stood on that table through four quarter-ends and eleven sheets and two drawers and one instruction, and no clerk has ever been asked to look inside it, and none of them has ever wanted to.

At about ten past ten he put a twenty-sixth thing on it.

## What Came Out Of The Drawer

It was a piece of rag paper about the size of a copy of the foot of a printed page, and it has nothing on it at all, and it has been in the inside of his coat since the fourteenth of this month.

He did not take it out of the drawer. On the fourteenth the drawer in the wall on the left of that table was opened at about eleven and shut again at half past three, and the room was empty when he did it, and the Register says the drawer was shut at half past three and it does not say what came out of it in a clerk’s hand, because the clerk was not there.

He had carried it in the inside of that coat for sixteen days, in the fold where the docket used to lie, and he had taken it out about four times and looked at it about four times, and it was empty every time and there was no pencil mark in it and no indentation where a pencil had been pressed and wiped out.

Nobody will ever know which of two things was true of it.

That she stood at a door off a third court on the sixteenth of August with a piece of paper in her hand and had nothing to write and nothing to say, and a clerk entered what she was given, and what she was given was nothing.

Or that she had written what she had on another piece and kept this one, and kept it because of what she thought was on it.

## Four Doors He Went Past In His Head

He wanted to put it somewhere and he went through the four places in about nine seconds without leaving the table.

Wenna Raut may only enter what she is given and cannot improve a word of it, and what she would be given this time is nothing, and there is a row in the bound Register with the date against it and the hour against the date and nothing whatever against the hour and a space in the middle where her words were going to be. That row is done and it cannot be done again.

Perla Quist keeps a heading at the top of a column and the heading says what a sheet carried on the day it was made. This sheet was made by nobody and carries nothing, and there is no line under that heading for it, and putting one there would be inventing a day.

The drawer is shut and cannot be opened again by any person in this city, including him, and that is the third instruction in a book and the third cannot be taken out of the book either.

And the fourth place is a kitchen a long way from here, where there may be a person who put this on a table, and there is no form in this city on which this office may ask her what she has done with a piece of paper, and she is not traced and this office has never sent anybody to find out where she lives.

He did not try any of them with an argument, because he has been told no about the fourth of those four times and about each of the other three in one sentence, and he is not going to spend an afternoon of a night shift being refused a fifth time over an empty piece of paper.

## The One Question Of The Morning

Wenna Raut came in at about half ten with the sheet squared under the window and saw him standing at the table with an empty piece of rag paper in his hand, and she had a question and had had it since about eleven o’clock on the previous morning.

She did not spend it.

“There is nothing to enter,” she said, before he had said anything at all. “I have looked at it from here and there is nothing on it and nothing to write and nothing to date, and I have been standing here for about nine minutes working out whether I am allowed to ask what it is.”

“You are.”

“I am not going to,” said Wenna Raut. “I get one a morning and I am going to spend it on the thirtieth, when a page goes out and there will be a thing in it I cannot answer, and if I spend it now I will have none in hand at four o’clock.”

She squared the sheet again and did not look at the bundle, because a clerk does not look at a table of things that are not entries, and she has never once looked at it in nine years.

Then she said one more thing, which was not a question.

“You put that down knowing what was in it,” she said. “Everything else on that table went on without anybody knowing, and this one went on after you had read it twice, and that is a different thing and I wanted it said out loud in this room once.”

## The Only Place Left

So it went on the bundle.

And standing there with it in his hand he understood something about that table which he has been standing next to for nine years and had never once seen.

The bundle is an instrument.

It is the only instrument in this city that will hold a piece of paper without making it into a record of anything. Not the bound Register, which turns a sheet into a row the same minute it arrives. Not nine sheets under a third table, which turn a sheet into a claim true against a day.
Not a grey cloth book in a pigeonhole, which turns a sheet into a second claim and is worse than the first. Not the drawer, which turns a piece of paper into a thing a man has instructed about and therefore a thing a book has an opinion on.

The bundle turns it into nothing. It is the only place a piece of paper can sit in Ostara and stay a piece of paper.

And it has no heading and no keeper and no name and no form and it may not be cited in any room by anybody, and there is not one person in this city who is entitled to be told what is on that table, and that includes the man who put it there.

He has been guarding the route to about four hundred people’s claims for four years by holding a door shut with a piece of paper instruction, and there has been a table in the room the whole time with an open door on it, and he built the shut one himself and paid for it with an instruction that cannot be withdrawn.

And he has been proud, on and off, for nine years, of the sentence at the foot of the quarterly sheet that says where the Register has nothing in it then a person has been given nothing, and it is true, and it is the truest thing that building prints, and it is printed on a building that has a bundle on a table in it with twenty-six things nobody has ever been shown.

## Twenty-Six Things On A Table

The cost of the table is not a secret. It is a floor.

Nothing that goes onto that bundle can be found again. Not by him, not by the clerk, not by a magistrate’s clerk with a form, not by a woman with a pencil nine weeks later. In nine years the office has never been able to produce one item out of that table and has never once been asked to, and that is the whole of what it is for.

And the twenty-sixth thing on it at about ten past ten on the twenty-eighth of October of the eighth year is the only one of the twenty-six he has ever read, and he read it twice, and he is not going to read it a third time, and there is not a person in this city he could hand it to and no form on which handing it over would be a thing that happened.

There is a woman in this city who came up a stair to a counter off a third court in the fourth month of last year and put something across it and went back down the stair, and the only record in Ostara that she was ever in that room is a line in a bound book with a date against it and a space in the middle of it where her words would have been.

And the piece of paper she was holding is on a table in a room with no door onto a street, in a bundle of twenty-five other things that came from twenty-five other people and have never been looked at, and it is the twenty-sixth thing, and it is the only one there that anybody has ever looked at, and he has looked at it and put it down and will not look at it again.

*Two thousand seven hundred and eighty-seven days. Less two thousand seven hundred and eighty, the twenty-first of October of the eighth year, is seven, and a twenty-sixth thing went onto a bundle that has been on a table three years and has never been opened.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is two hundred and eight days, which is six months and twenty-eight days after them, and the thirty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty-eight of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is three hundred and eighty-eight days, which is twelve months and twenty-eight days after them, and a copy carrying four sentences and no receipt has been older than that afternoon for every one of them.*
