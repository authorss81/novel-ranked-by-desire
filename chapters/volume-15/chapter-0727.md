# The Older Claim Wins

Two thousand six hundred and eighty-nine was the twentieth of July and the twenty-sixth is day two thousand six hundred and ninety-five, and two thousand six hundred and ninety-five less two thousand six hundred and eighty-nine is six, and twenty-six less twenty is six by the month count.

## Two Claims For One Street

There were two separate claims for one stretch of street dug in the cold in the eleventh month of last year, and both came to the magistrate’s counter off a third court in the same week, and neither of the men who brought them had ever been in that room before.

The very first man standing there that morning had brought a copy of the foot of a printed sheet in his own pencil, made at a rail in a passage off Cordwain, with his own handwritten date along its foot, as people commonly date a thing they make, the fourth of February.

The second had a piece of pencil paper with the bottom two inches filled in his own hand, and no date on it at all, because he had made it in his own kitchen in the middle of last year out of what he believed, and nobody had told him a day was a thing anybody wanted.

Lin Hao stood about nine feet back from the counter with his hands in his pockets, because the first man had come to him on the fourth of February in the first week after the instruction and had asked him a question that started all of this, and he had promised the man he would stand there when it was heard, and he keeps promises of that kind even when they cost him dearly.

The clerk at the window had been sitting there for about five long years and had never once been wrong about what a counter is, and he is entitled to exactly one question a day about a thing and not two, and he read both papers where they lay, which is what a counter is for.

## What The Clerk Is Entitled To Order

He slid the first copy back round and nudged it a full two inches across the wood towards the man who had carried it in. The paper caught the light from the window as it moved. The second man shifted his weight and said nothing yet. The room smelled of dust and window polish. A queue had formed behind the two men.

“The day here is the day you wrote the copy,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Then it is not the day of the digging.”

“No.”

“What you stood at a rail to write,” said the clerk. “And that counts. A copy holds what the page carried when it was made.”

“The four sentences have kept that order since the ninth of November of the fifth year, and the four-word line reached that page at four on the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, so a copy holding four sentences and no receipt predates that afternoon.”

He put his pen across the book, which is what a clerk does when the one question has gone, and then he picked it up again, because ordering two claims against each other is not a second question, it is what a counter is for.

“The claim with a day on it is older than the thirtieth of September of the seventh year,” he said, writing it in the day book at the counter in his own hand. “The claim without a day has no age in this room. A claim with a day on it is ordered against one without, and the one without loses, and that is not a finding about the street, it is a finding about paper.”

The second man said, out loud, that he did not understand, and the clerk told him, not unkindly, that nobody in that room understood, and that a bound is not an age and an age is what a city does with a day when somebody entitled writes it down.

This was the first time anybody in this city had done it, and the ordering would go into the book behind the counter that goes out to nobody and is read by nobody and has been kept for about two hundred years.

The second man asked whether that meant his paper was worth nothing, and the clerk said it meant his paper had no day in a room where a day beats no day, and that out of that room the two papers were the same weight they had always been, which is the whole of why a counter is a small room with a window in it and not the city.

Lin Hao understood it standing nine feet back with the folded sheet in his coat against his ribs, and it took him about four seconds, and it took him the rest of the morning to sit down with it, because he had spent nine weeks being unable to put an answer to anybody about how old a claim is, and now somebody entitled had put one, and it was not him.

For four years the only figure this city has ever done anything with is an age, and now a claim has one, because a copy made on the fourth of February carries a day, and the day makes it older than a claim with no day, and the older claim wins, although neither man dug a different street.

*Two thousand six hundred and ninety-five days. Less two thousand six hundred and eighty-nine, the twentieth of July of the eighth year, is six, and a clerk at a window ordered a claim with a day against one without and the one without lost.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is a hundred and sixteen days, which is three months and twenty-six days after them, and the thirtieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty-six of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is two hundred and ninety-six days, which is nine months and twenty-six days after them, and that afternoon is the bound every copy with four sentences and no receipt has been older than.*

## The Person It Is About

The street was dug by men working for one house, and the house has an office above a shop off Cordwain, and the man who sits in that office writes every letter the other three have ever had, and none of that was said at the counter, because the counter is for paper and not for houses.

The person that pencil paper is about does not know there was ever a day at all, and he was not in the room, and nobody told him afterwards, because there is no paper in this city that tells a man a claim about nine pounds has been given an age in a book behind a counter that goes out to nobody.

Lin Hao walked out after the two men and stood about nine feet off the rail in a passage while a man who runs a desk under a window read the bottom of the page out to four strangers, in the ordinary way, without being asked, and paid nothing for it, and stopped at the striking.

The first man folded his copy along the crease he had made in it in November and put it back in his pocket, and the second man stood with his piece of pencil paper in his hand as though it had got heavier, and neither of them thanked anybody, and the day book closed at noon with a remark in it and an ordering and no finding about the street.

He went up four flights at about half past twelve and told Perla Quist in the order it had happened, because she keeps days and a day without its ordering is not a day she can hold, and she listened with the nine sheets squared and the pencil down, which means she is listening as a person and not as a keeper.

He laid out for her the two dates and the two papers with the clerk’s exact words in the order they fell, and about the book behind the counter and the day book at it, and about the two men going out with one copy folded and one sheet held as though it had got heavier, because nine sheets under a third table cannot hold a thing unless it comes with its day.

“So now a day is an age,” she said, when he had finished.

“A bound first, at the counter in May,” said Lin Hao. “And now an age, at the same counter, because a claim with a day beats one without, and the clerk was entitled to order them, and he did, and the man it is about was not there.”

She pushed the ruler a half inch along the bench and did not square it to anything, and he understood that she was afraid, because nine sheets under a third table can put a claim true against a day, and a magistrate’s counter can now put a day true against a person, and the two together are the whole of what she refused to be.

“What changes,” she said.

“That about four hundred kitchens hold pieces of paper and some of them carry days and most do not, and from today the ones with days beat the ones without, and nobody in any kitchen knows which kind they hold.”

He went back down the four flights with the older claim sitting on him and did the floors of three washrooms and the stairwell and the bin room, and at about half past five he stood in the room off a third court and looked at the standing sheet with the head unmoved and the second column with three lines in it and understood that the question in front of him had turned.

He must still find out who is using pieces of pencil paper in kitchens and against whom, or the receipt he entered goes on being the only answer in this city to a person asking whether what happened to her happened.

From the twenty-sixth of July the using has a new shape, because a claim with an age on it is a thing a counter will act on, and the house above a shop off Cordwain has never once been in that room, and the man who knows the four names has asked for them not to be printed, and he keeps promises of that kind even when they cost him dearly.
