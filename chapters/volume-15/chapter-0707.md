# The Person The Receipt Is About

Two thousand five hundred and fifty-three was the fourth of March and the eleventh is day two thousand five hundred and sixty, and two thousand five hundred and sixty less two thousand five hundred and fifty-three is seven, and eleven less four is seven by the month count.

## Eleven Sheets In The Fourth Month Of Last Year

Perla Quist had the nine sheets out from under the third table and had not put anything on them, which meant she had been sitting there for some time without working.

“You have been down four flights for nine days,” she said. “I have watched the stair. Sit down and I am going to say a thing and I have been not saying it for a week and I am not going to let you leave this room without it.”

She squared the ruler against the table.

“A woman carried eleven sheets of pencil paper through the front door of an office off a third court on the fourth month of last year. She carried them herself. She came up four flights with them and she stood at a counter with a bag and she did not come again, and the eleven went into two cardboard boxes and were entered as nothing, which is what happens to about eleven pieces of paper a week.”

“All right.”

“She was told in the first month of this year, at a counter, by a woman entitled to tell her, that that office has certified that none of them is a deed.”

He did not sit down and she had not expected him to.

“And she has gone home and told four people that nothing happened to her,” said Perla Quist.

## The Worst Direction A Sentence Can Go

“Show me the page.”

“You have got it in your head and you have had it in your head for four years and so have I.”

“There is a sentence at the foot of that page,” he said. “It amounts to this. Where nothing stands in the register, nobody was given anything.”

“Yes,” said Perla Quist. “That is it. That is the truest sentence on the sheet and it was on the foot of it before there was an office to put it on.”

“And the third line of the second column went onto that page at four on the thirtieth of September, in the office’s own hand, a man’s name standing under it, and four words in the middle of it saying that none of them is a deed.”

“Yes.”

“So a woman standing at a counter had both of those read at her on the same page by somebody entitled to read her either of them,” he said.

“She put them together inside about nine seconds and she got somewhere out of them that neither of them says, and what she got was that nobody gave her anything, and that therefore there was nothing there, and that therefore it did not happen to her.”

Perla Quist did not say yes.

“It is the worst direction that sentence can go and she went four feet down it in nine seconds and she was entitled to,” she said. “It is true of her. Nobody in this city gave that woman anything. Nine counters and two years and a woman in a white plate telling the truth about a figure on a wall is the whole of what she has ever been given, and if that is not enough then it is not enough.”

## Who Was Entitled To Read It To Her

“It was the division.”

“I know it was the division.”

“She went to a division building because she had eleven pieces of paper and she thought somebody there might look at them, and she had been to about eleven counters before that office and been turned away at every one of them. A counter is a thing you go to.”

Perla Quist picked the ruler up and put it down again without having measured anything. “And the woman at that counter read her the page. Not the third line out of a book and not a finding and not anything of her own. The page. Out loud, in order, because that is what a counter is for and that woman does it several times a day and has done for six years.”

“She read it correctly.”

“She read it correctly and she would have read it wrongly if she had wanted to, and she has no reason in the world to want anything by that woman being anything or not being anything,” said Perla Quist.

“That is the whole of it and there is no villain in it and you are not going to get one. If you go up there and ask her to stop reading the page out to people at a counter she will say no, and she will be right, and there is nobody in this city over her who could tell her differently.”

“I know that.”

“You know it since the fourth of March and I have known it since the fourth of February,” said Perla Quist, “and I did not tell you for a week because there is no instrument in this city that puts a thing like that in front of the man who caused it, and because saying it to you was not mine to decide.”

*Two thousand five hundred and sixty days. Less two thousand five hundred and forty-seven, the twenty-eighth of February of the eighth year, is thirteen days, and a woman off a street two streets wide has said out loud that a sentence he wrote in nine seconds has taken a woman’s account away from her, and the sentence is true and the page is true and the woman who read them at a counter was entitled to read either of them.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and twenty, the first of February of the eighth year, is forty days, which is one month and ten days after them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of August of the seventh year, is a hundred and ninety-one days, which is six months and eleven days after them, and four words went into a Register in this office's hand on that morning and have been on a page at a counter since the thirtieth of September.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is a hundred and sixty-one days, which is five months and eleven days after them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and thirteen, the fourteenth of October of the seventh year, is a hundred and forty-seven days, which is four months and twenty-seven days after them.*

## Four People Off A Street At Half Past Three

He went down and along and off Cordwain into a street of about two hundred feet with a washing line across it and four doors off it and a woman sitting on a step with a sheet over her knees that had something written on the bottom two inches of it in her own hand.

He stood at the other end of the street for about four minutes and then he went away, and he had thought about knocking for the whole of the four minutes and had not, and the reason was not a rule anybody made.

Nothing in this city entitles him to put that question to her through a door, and there is no paper anywhere that would make the asking lawful.

He got as far as the front door of that office at about four and there was nothing outside it and there was nobody on the step and there had not been anybody on that step on any of about nine occasions since the twelfth of September.

## What He Cannot Un-Enter

He could be told what was true. That was the whole of what was available to him and it was the best thing anybody in this city had ever handed him.

He could not un-enter a receipt.

He had entered it on the thirtieth of August at about ten past eleven, in nine seconds, with the back of his hand on the table, and it had been the right thing to do and he had known it was the right thing to do at the moment he said it, and four words in a clerk's hand had taken a woman in number four in an earlier year out of a room where four figures a landlord had invented were about to be used on her.

The sentence was true.

It was true of every person in this city who had ever gone to a counter and been refused and gone home with nothing, and it was true of the woman with the sheet over her knees, and it was being used on the only building in Ostara that prints it, and the office had certified the death of a claim in a straight face and a clerk's hand and had published it four times a year since.

She had believed it. She had told four people. And there is nothing anybody in this city can do about a thing being true.

He had come down a stair about six weeks ago wanting to find out who was using about four hundred pieces of pencil paper and against whom, and the answer had turned out to be standing at a rail at about ten past ten in the morning being told by a building.

## The One Of The Four Who Is Going To Stop

One of the four people she had told was a man who dug a street on the twenty-second of January and wrote in the bottom two inches of a piece of paper of his own and had the foot of a printed sheet copied out in his own kitchen in his own pencil.

He was going to stop writing in the two inches.

He was not going to stop because anybody had frightened him. He was going to stop because a building with a plate on it and a clerk's hand and four sentences at the foot had told him, in the only language this city has ever used on him, that the only thing anybody had ever given him was a piece of paper that was not a deed.

And he would be right to believe it.

That was the thing Lin Hao was carrying down a street at about ten past four on the eleventh of March, and there is no form anywhere in this city for undoing an entry, and there has not been one in six years, and he had known that since the seventh of April and he had used it about nine times since.
