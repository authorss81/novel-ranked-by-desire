# A Date In Front Of A Magistrate

Two thousand six hundred and seven was the twenty-eighth of April and the fifth of May is day two thousand six hundred and fourteen, and two thousand six hundred and fourteen less two thousand six hundred and seven is seven.

April runs to its thirtieth and there is no thirty-first. Twenty-eighth to thirtieth is two, the first of May is one, and the fifth is four more, and two and one and four is seven.

## A Counter With A Window In It

A magistrate's counter is a counter with a window in it and a clerk on the other side of the window, and the clerk has been there for about five years and has never once been wrong about what a counter is.

The man at the window had dug a street in the cold in the eleventh month of last year and had a copy of the foot of a printed sheet in a pocket, in his own pencil, made at a rail in a passage off Cordwain, and against the copy there was a date in his own hand at the bottom of it in the ordinary way people put a date on a thing they have made.

He had wanted nine pounds and the house had offered nothing and he was not a lawyer and had never been in this room before and had spent four days outside it deciding whether to come in.

Lin Hao was not there for him. He was standing about nine feet back with his hands in his pockets because the man had come to the counter on the fourth of February, in the first week after the instruction, and had asked him a question that had started all of this, and had come back on the twenty-ninth and told him where he was going.

“What is it,” said the clerk, before anybody had spoken twice.

“It is a claim for nine pounds for a street,” the man said, “and I have got the day on it.”

## How Old Is It

The clerk did not take the copy. He read it where it lay on the counter, which is what a counter is for, and he read it in about four seconds and then read the bottom four inches of it, which is longer.

“The day on this is the day you made the copy,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Then it is not the day you dug the street.”

“No.”

“It is the day you stood at a rail and wrote it out,” said the clerk, “and that is not nothing and it is not much. How old is the claim.”

That was the question, and it was the question the whole of this city has been asking since the fourth of February, and Lin Hao had spent about nine weeks being unable to put the answer to anybody.

He could not answer it here either. There was no instrument on the counter and there is none in this city that turns a copy into a day, and the only thing anybody had ever built that can say anything about it is nine sheets under a third table four flights up a street, and nobody in this building may ask for those nine sheets, and this clerk is not entitled to one question about them because his one question is going somewhere else and has not gone yet.

“Older than the thirtieth of September of the seventh year,” he said, “and not a day more than that.”

The clerk looked up for the first time.

“How do you know that.”

“Because a copy carrying the four sentences at the foot of that page and none of the receipt has to have been made before the receipt was on the page, and the receipt went on at four on the thirtieth of September of the seventh year. Anything after that has a line in it that this one has not got.”

“That is a bound and not an age.”

“That is a bound and not an age,” said Lin Hao. “And it is the first bound of its kind anybody in this city has ever had, and there are about four hundred people who could be given one.”

The clerk wrote it in the day book at the counter in about nine seconds, in his own hand, and did not improve it, and it went in as a remark against a claim and not as a finding, because a finding has to be given by somebody entitled to give one and he was not going to invent a second thing on the same morning.

## The One Question He Is Entitled To

He turned the copy back round and pushed it two inches towards the man.

“You made this at a rail,” he said. “Who was standing next to you when you made it.”

The man said the name of a man who runs a desk under a window in that passage and has run it for nine years and is paid nothing and has never been thanked.

The clerk wrote that down too.

Then he stopped writing and put the pen across the book, which is what a clerk does when the one question has gone and there is not another one coming, and everything after that is ordinary business and is done by other people in other rooms.

He did not ask Lin Hao anything. He had not asked Lin Hao anything at any point and he was not going to, and that was not politeness. It was that a man in a white plate with no figure cut into it is not a party to anything at a magistrate's counter, and the clerk knew it, and the clerk had been right about every part of it in about four seconds.

There is a book behind a magistrate's counter that is not the day book, and it is a book of things that were said at a counter and are not findings, and it goes out to nobody and it is read by nobody and it has been kept in this city for about two hundred years.

Two remarks went into it that morning. One said how old a piece of pencil paper could be and the other said the name of a man who runs a desk under a window in a passage off Cordwain.

Neither of them can be acted on. Both of them are in a book, and that is the first time in the history of this city that a claim with a day against it has been written into any book that is not a page going out to a counter.

## What The Man In The Queue Said Instead

There were two people behind him and neither of them had anything to do with the claim. The older of the two was a woman who had come about a week of work she had done in the ninth month of last year and who had been in and out of nine counters since then and had a piece of pencil paper in her handbag with a date on it that was the date she had written it.

She said, out loud, to nobody, that if the day you dug the thing was on the paper she did not know what else anybody wanted from her.

The clerk heard it. He did not turn round.

He said, “That is the right question to ask and it is the one that has never been answered in this city, and I am going to tell you why, and then I am going to put the book away, because I have one question a day and it went at about eleven.”

He said: “A copy will tell you when it was copied. A person will tell you when it was done. About four hundred people in this city have got the first and not the second and there is no form anywhere that asks a person for the second, and the reason there is not one is that nobody has ever wanted it badly enough to write it down.”

“Will somebody?”

“Somebody has wanted it every day since the fourth of February,” said the clerk, “and has not written it, and I have noticed that from this counter for about nine weeks and I cannot ask anybody about it, because a magistrate may put one question about a thing and that one went at eleven about a rail.”

*Two thousand six hundred and fourteen days. Less two thousand six hundred and seven, the twenty-eighth of April of the eighth year, is seven, and a clerk at a window wrote the first bound anybody in this city has had on a piece of pencil paper and did not make it a finding and has not been asked about it by anybody.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is two hundred and fifteen days, which is seven months and five days after them, and every copy with four sentences at the foot of it and no receipt has been older than that afternoon for every one of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and six, the seventh of October of the seventh year, is two hundred and eight days, which is six months and twenty-eight days after them, and the seventy-first form goes up on that date and there is no part of this room that touches it.*

## The Man Left With It In His Pocket

Nothing was decided. That is the whole of what happened at that counter between about eleven and about half past eleven on the fifth of May, and the claim went back into the man's own pocket folded along the crease he had made in it in November, and nobody was charged anything and nobody was thanked and the day book closed at noon with two remarks in it and no finding.

The man was in the passage for about four minutes afterwards and then he went back down to the rail, because that is where he had made the copy and he was not the sort of man who would take a thing back off the rail after he had written it out.

Lin Hao walked out after him and stood about nine feet off while a man who runs a desk under a window read the bottom of the page out to four people standing at it, in the ordinary way, without being asked, and paid nothing for it, and had never been thanked in nine years and was never going to be.

A day is the only figure this city has ever done anything with. A person can be asked for a day. He had known the second of those two things since the nineteenth of July of the second year, in a kitchen off Ferrier Street, and had put the knowing in a drawer with a man's name under it and a sentence about a drawer in the same office's hand, and it had gone out four times a year since, and it was the truest line on the page.

And there is still no form on which anybody may put that question to about four hundred people, and there is now a clerk at a window who would like somebody to write one, and one person in this city who could write it, and he stood about nine feet off a rail in a passage and did not go up four flights to do it.
