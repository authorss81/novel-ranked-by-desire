# A Man At The Rail Says It Again

Two thousand six hundred and twenty-eight was the nineteenth of May and the twenty-sixth is day two thousand six hundred and thirty-five, and two thousand six hundred and thirty-five less two thousand six hundred and twenty-eight is seven, and twenty-six less nineteen is seven by the month count.

## About Nine People A Week Get To The Bottom Of It

The sheet on the rail is face up in a passage off Cordwain and the reading is done standing up, and there is a hand's width of air under the bottom edge of it, and about nine people a week get all the way down to the last two inches.

The man at the desk under the window had his own copy face up on the wood and he reads the bottom of it out loud to whoever is standing at the rail, in order, because that is what he has done for nine years and nobody pays him for any part of it and there is nobody alive he could name who has said thank you.

There were four at the rail at about half past four in the afternoon and a fifth came up behind them and stood at the end and said it before anybody had asked him anything.

“There are two words cut into a stone at the bottom of a wall in this city,” he said, “and I have been told by two men this month that a woman has put the same two words at the top of a column, and that the office off the third court has known about the stone since before any of us were working.”

Nobody at the rail said anything for about two seconds.

“Who told you that.”

“My cousin,” said the man. “And a man at a counter in Cordwain.”

“There is no column,” said the counterman. “There is a page.”

“There is a page in your building and there is a column in a laundry and they are the same thing and you know they are, because you read the bottom of that page out to people every day and there is nobody in this city who has got the bottom of it by heart except you.”

## Four Minutes, The Same As The Last Time

It went round the passage inside four minutes. That is the figure, and this is the second time that figure has come back in this city about a sentence said at a corner, and the last time was in the fourth month of a year nobody here names, and it went round then too, correctly and wrongly at the same time.

The two things that came out of it were a sheet of paper with the wrong four sentences on it and a claim about two words that nobody has been able to answer ever since.

Four of the five had believed it by the time the fifth one had finished, and none of the five had seen a laundry, and none of them had seen a heading, and one of them had been in the office off the third court in the fourth month of last year and had stood at a counter with a bag.

That one said, “It stands to reason. There is a page and it has been face up since the thirtieth of December and it has got four sentences on the bottom of it and there is a woman writing down what that page says on the day people copy it, and if there is a woman writing down what the page says then somebody has told her about the stone.”

That is the step. It is nine words long and it is the kind of step people take when they want to be somewhere and have not got a road.

## What Cannot Be Answered

Lin Hao was about eleven feet back in the passage with his hands in his pockets and he had been there since four.

There are two things he could not do and he worked out both of them in about four seconds.

He could not say what the two words on that stone are, because he has not said them to anybody in this city in about four years and there is a rubbing of them in the inside of a boy's coat and about nine people have been in a room where they were read out and one of them cannot read.

And he could not go up the hill and look at them, because he was told in front of a counter in the middle of last year not to come back for the rest of it, and the arrangement was made out loud before a piece of paper moved from one hand to another, and he has kept it for about two hundred days.

So the claim stood in a passage off Cordwain for the rest of that afternoon and there was nothing anybody in Ostara could put against it, and it was not a lie. It was a thing with the shape of a truth in it and no more, and a shape is enough when there are five people and a rail.

He went down to the counter.

“Did you hear what that man said.”

“I heard all of it and I read the bottom of the page out to all of it before he said any of it,” said the counterman. “I read what is on the page. That is the only thing I have ever read out of it in nine years.”

“Right.”

“You are going to tell me it is not true.”

“I am not going to tell you anything at all about it,” said Lin Hao, “because I have not got a form, and I have not got a page, and I have not got a right, and a man in a white plate with no figure on him standing in a passage correcting four people about a wall is the one thing in this city that would make the other three of them believe it.”

*Two thousand six hundred and thirty-five days. Less two thousand six hundred and twenty-eight, the nineteenth of May of the eighth year, is seven, and a claim went round a passage off Cordwain in four minutes about two words in a stone and six words ruled by a woman in a laundry, and it was not a lie.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is fifty-six days, which is one month and twenty-six days after them, and the twenty-ninth sheet has been face up on a bracket in that passage for every one of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two hundred and fifteen days, which is seven months and five days after them, and nothing that happened in that passage on the afternoon of the twenty-sixth of May had anything to do with any of it, and about nine people in this city now believe a thing about a stone that nobody can put against.*

## Four Flights And Six Words And A Line Under Them

He went down four flights at about half past five and she was at the bench with the nine sheets out and the four sheets in the column and about two hundred empty lines under the four.

He did not tell her. He put the bench square and then he told her, in the order it had happened, because that is what she does with a day and she had not been asked to do it and he had not asked her to do it and he did it anyway.

She wrote down the day it was said. She did not write down who said it.

“That is a claim about two words and a heading,” said Perla Quist, “and the two words have never been printed, and my heading is the only thing in this city that has been written by a person about what a page said on a day, and somebody has put the two together and they have gone round a passage in four minutes.”

“Can you say it is wrong.”

“No,” she said. “Not without the two words, and the two words are not in this city in a form anybody may hand me, and the only copy is in the inside of a coat of a boy who puts boards up by a kerb and who has said three times to a man he knows that he has not been told what they are.”

She put the pencil down and squared it against the edge of the bench.

“So I am going to write one line under my heading,” she said, “and it is going to say what the heading is about, and it is going to say it in my own hand, and it is going to be on nine sheets that nobody may cite in a laundry off a street two streets wide, and it is going to go exactly nowhere, and I am going to do it anyway.”

“Why.”

“Because in about four months somebody is going to ask a woman who cannot read what a heading is and she is going to say what the man at the rail said, and she will say it correctly and it will be wrong and she will have had it from a person,” said Perla Quist. “That is the only thing I have ever been able to do about it and I have thought about it for about six seconds and I am doing it now.”

She wrote it in one line at the top of the column, underneath the six words, in her own hand and not in any printed hand, and it took her about eleven seconds and she did not improve it and she did not read it back.

*WHAT THE SHEET SAID THAT DAY IS WHAT A COPY OF A PUBLIC PAGE CARRIED ON THE DAY IT WAS MADE. IT IS ABOUT NO STONE. IT IS ABOUT NO WALL. IT IS ABOUT NO COLUMN BUT THIS ONE AND THE COLUMN BELOW IT. A WOMAN RULED IT IN THIS ROOM ON THE AFTERNOON OF THE FOURTEENTH OF OCTOBER OF THE SEVENTH YEAR AND TOLD NOBODY.*

She put the pencil down across the bench.

“That is a sentence nobody can argue with,” said Lin Hao, “and it is in a room off a street two streets wide and about four flights up, and the man at the rail will not read it and would not come and look at it if he did.”

“No,” said Perla Quist. “That is what a sentence is for when it is written by somebody who cannot be sent. It is for the next person who comes down those stairs and not for anybody who is standing in a passage this afternoon.”

## About Nine People, And One Of Them Goes And Tells Somebody

At about six o'clock he came back up the hill through about four hundred people going the other way, and at the kerb there was a board with the seventy-first month’s form on it and two ruled squares at the foot of it and nothing whatever in either of them.

One of the nine who believed it at the rail was a woman who had been in the office off a third court in the fourth month of last year with a bag, and she had not carried anything in and she had gone away again in about nine minutes, and she had told four people since the middle of February that the office had told her that her paper was not a deed.

On the evening of the twenty-sixth of May she told two more, and what she told them was not only about the four sentences.

She said that the building off the third court keeps the stone. That is what she said, and she said it as a fact and not as a question, and she had heard it in a passage from a man who had heard it at a rail from a man who had heard it from his cousin.

Nobody in this city can put a wall off a person's mouth once two people have said it to each other.

A Bureau officer had learned that on the fourth of February about nine people being in a room, and he had said it out loud to a woman with a pencil on a floor two flights under a sales floor, and she had agreed with him, and he had been saying it to himself for about nine weeks as the answer to a question he had not found the end of yet.

There is no form in this city that stops a person saying where a building is or what is in it, and about four hundred people have never been asked anything at all.
