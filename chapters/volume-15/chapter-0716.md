# The Question She Spends

Two thousand six hundred and fourteen was the fifth of May and the twelfth is day two thousand six hundred and twenty-one, and two thousand six hundred and twenty-one less two thousand six hundred and fourteen is seven, and twelfth less fifth is seven by the month count.

## It Was Not About The Four Lines

She had had it since about nine and she spent it at twenty past ten, and it was not about the second column and it was not about the drawer, and she said the word drawer nowhere in that room for about six weeks and everybody in it had noticed.

“I am going to ask it and then I am not going to tell you what you are supposed to do about it,” said Wenna Raut. “It has taken me three weeks to get it the shape of a question instead of a complaint.”

“All right.”

“There are eleven pieces of paper entered as received in that book and there is a row against every one of them with a day and an hour and nothing else on it. Not one of the eleven has a person in it. Not one of the eleven has anything at all that a person said.”

She turned the book round a quarter so the rows were the right way up for him, which she does about four times a year.

“The eighth of April came in at about eleven in the morning and stood at that counter for twenty minutes and said four things, and not one of the four was a thing I could put in the row, and she was not asking for anything and she was not refusing anything and she went out again with nothing, and the row says *received* and the hour and that is the whole of what happened in this room that morning.”

“She did not give a name.”

“She gave a street and not a number and she gave the month and not the day and she gave two of them and then she said that if I wrote the second one down it would be worse for her than if I did not.” Wenna Raut put the pen down on the right side of the book. “I did not write it down. I want that in your own head as well as mine.”

## The One Question Of The Morning

“May an entry of receipt carry a description of the person who brought the paper.”

It took her about four seconds to say it and she said it in the voice she keeps for the column, and she did not look at him while she said it, and then she looked at him.

“That is my question for the morning,” said Wenna Raut, “and I have spent it, and it is not about the four lines and it is not about the drawer and it is about a person, and I have never spent one on a person before in six years in this room and I have thought about it for three weeks and I could not work out whether I was allowed to and I have decided to ask and be told.”

Nobody answered her for about eleven seconds.

There were four people in this city who could have answered it and all four were in the room or within four flights of it, and every one of them was going to say no, and the four noes were not the same no.

“I may not improve what I am given,” said Wenna Raut, before anybody else had found a way in. “That is my half and it is the only one of the four I am certain about, and I would like it written down that I said it first.”

## Four Answers And Three Reasons

Yusuf Adeyemi had not taken his coat off and had not been at the table for two hours.

“The office has never, in nine years, put a question to a person about a thing somebody else had written down,” he said. “That is the only finding this office has ever passed. It is in a book in a corridor and there is no name against it. I have never once in six years been asked whether I agree with it and I have always agreed with it.”

“That is not a reason.”

“It is the reason,” said Yusuf Adeyemi, “and the reason it is a reason is that the day this office writes down what a person looked like while they were handing something over, this office has become a thing that watches people come in, and about four hundred people in this city have been turned away at nine counters and not one of them came to a counter to be looked at.”

“That is one.”

Lin Hao did not say anything for about four seconds and then said, “Perla Quist will say no and she will give you a structural reason and it will be the right one and I have heard it four times and it has never once been about me.”

“Then it is two.”

“It is two and I am not going to give you hers,” he said. “She has said no to me five times in four years about nine different things and each time it was the same reason in different words, and the reason is that a woman who keeps an index is not a person who may be sent.”

“If this office describes a person who has walked through that door then every sheet on nine sheets under a third table is a thing this office had a look at through her.”

Wenna Raut put the pen across the book.

“Three,” she said. “That is the reason I have not given and it is yours and I want it entered that you gave it to me before I asked you for it.”

“You did not ask me for it.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut, “and that is my half and it is the only one of the four I am certain about, and I have said it twice now and I am not going to say it a third time.”

*Two thousand six hundred and twenty-one days. Less two thousand six hundred and fourteen, the fifth of May of the eighth year, is seven, and a clerk who may only enter what she is given spent the one question she has in a morning on a field in a book and was told no three times and by two people who were not asked.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and thirteen, the fourteenth of October of the seventh year, is two hundred and eight days, which is six months and twenty-eight days after them, and the fourth entry in a fourth column has been standing on nine sheets for every one of them and nobody may cite it.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two hundred and one days, which is six months and twenty-one days after them, and a drawer in this room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*

## Fourteen Rows And A Field That Is Not The One She Asked For

There are five fields in the second column and they have not changed since the second year, and the fifth is the only one anybody has ever wanted, and it cannot be filled out of anything except the mouth of the person it is about.

That is not the field she has asked about and she knew it before she asked and she had put the row in the book in the right place on purpose so that nobody could tell afterwards that she had not.

“The fifth field is about what was done to a person,” said Wenna Raut. “It is not about who brought it in and it never was, and if anybody ever tells you that the fifth field is the one for this, they have misunderstood the column, and it will be me that they have misunderstood, because there are about nine people in this city who would say a clerk made a mistake.”

“It is not going in the column.”

“It is not going in the column. Three lines are in it and there will be three lines in it at the thirtieth of June and I have said that out loud every quarter for about two years.”

She had the book open at the eighth of April and she put her thumb on the row.

“There are eleven rows and every one of them has a day and an hour and nothing else, and eleven is a figure I have now got that I did not have on the first of April.”

“Eleven is not a count of claims and it is not a count of people. It is a count of pieces of paper that came through a door, and it will be about four hundred by the end of next year if this goes on at the rate it has gone on for eight days.”

“I have worked that out myself and I did not ask anybody to,” said Wenna Raut, “and I am not going to be told it is wrong by a person with a plate.”

He stood at the end of the table with his hands in his pockets.

At about half past four he went down four flights to a laundry and up them again and put the question to a woman with a pencil, in her own words, and she said no in nine words and gave the reason, and he did not argue with any part of it.

Then he went down them again and up them again and put it to her the way he had put it to her before, and she said no again in eleven words, and he noticed that it had been nine the first time and eleven the second, and that she had not said one word differently.

He had been told no by three people in a room and by one person twice on a stair in one day, and the fourth reason was his, and there was nobody in this city he was permitted to ask whether it was a good one.

The thirtieth of June is the end of this quarter and it is forty-eight days from this morning, which is one month and eighteen days, and after that day nothing goes into that column until the thirtieth of September, and the thing he had begun to think about at about twenty to five in the afternoon did not go into the column at all, and he had not decided anything yet, and he had forty-eight days.
