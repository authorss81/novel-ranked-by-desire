# The Receipt In Nine Hands

Two thousand six hundred was the twenty-first of April and the twenty-eighth is day two thousand six hundred and seven, and two thousand six hundred and seven less two thousand six hundred is seven, and twenty-eighth less twenty-first is seven by the month count.

## A Front Room With A Bench In It

She mended umbrellas in a front room off a street that runs down to the laundry, and she had been mending them there for about nineteen years, and she wrote, and she had learned the writing in a schoolroom and had not needed it for about six years until the middle of last year.

The first thing she wrote out for anybody was the bottom of a printed page.

A man came in with a copy in his own pencil, done at a rail in a passage, and the copy had everything on it except the bottom, because the bottom of that page is where the sentences are and a man copying out of a rail cannot get his hand down far enough to see them without kneeling on a public floor.

“You are going to want these,” she said. “Everybody who has one of those wants the bottom of it.”

That was in the middle of last year and it was the whole of how it started, and she has not charged anybody for it and nobody has ever asked her to and there is no form anywhere in this city on which anybody may ask her to.

Nine people have had it written out since the middle of February. She knows it is nine because she counted the sheets on the pile when she squared the bench on the twenty-second, and she does not know who the nine are beyond the fact that four of them came from one stair off a street where the houses are the same.

She is the fifth thing of its kind in this city that can put a piece of paper against another piece of paper, and she does not know that there are four others, and there is no office she would recognise and no form she would fill in.

## The Order Of Four Sentences

He came down four flights at about eleven on the twenty-eighth and she had the bench squared and about four sheets of her own writing face down under the glass of the case where she keeps the things she is not doing.

“Read me the foot of it.”

“That is not a thing you do to me any more,” said Perla Quist. “You have asked me to read you a page four times in four years and every time you wanted to know whether a thing was true. This time you want to know whether the order is right and the order is the easiest thing in the world to get wrong by hand.”

“The order on the page is the same every quarter.”

“The order on the page is the same every quarter and about four hundred people are copying the bottom of it out of a copy in a pencil, and one of the copies I have seen has the second and the third the wrong way round.”

He put the sheet down on the bench and she turned it round with two fingers and read it and put her hand off it.

“That is wrong.”

“I know it is wrong.”

“No. You do not, because you have never seen the page,” said Perla Quist. “You have seen the bottom of it, and the bottom of it has been in that order since before this office existed, and you cannot see whether a copy is wrong unless you have seen the whole of the thing it was copied out of.”

She got the ninth sheet out from under the third table and put it beside it, and the two of them lay on the bench together, which is the only thing in this city that has ever put a copy and the page side by side and it has happened nine times in about four months.

## What A Copy Can Be Told From A Page

“A copy carries what the page said on the day it was made,” she said. “That is the whole of what a copy is and it is why I keep the column and it is why nobody may cite it.”

She turned the ninth sheet round so the four words at the top of the column were under his eyes.

“Those four sentences have been at the foot of that page in that order since the ninth of November of the fifth year. Not the ninth of November of the sixth year, which is a different November and about three hundred and sixty days nearer, and there is a second November in this story that everybody gets wrong.”

“How long can a copy with those four sentences in them have been made.”

“As long as you like, up to a point, and the point is the day the third line went onto the page.” She put a finger on the margin of her own sheet and not on his.

“The four sentences were at the foot of it before there was a clerk’s hand in this city. The line with the four words in it and a man’s name under it went onto that page at four in the afternoon on the thirtieth of September of the seventh year.”

“That is the thirtieth of September and not the thirtieth of August, and the twelfth of September is the day it went into the column and it is a third day again, and if anybody puts those three together we will have a worse thing than we have now.”

“So a copy with four sentences and no receipt on it is older than the thirtieth of September of the seventh year.”

“A copy like that was made before the receipt was on the page, and I could not tell you by how much, and there is no form anywhere in this city that turns an old copy into a day,” said Perla Quist. “The check tells you when. It does not tell you how old.”

He looked at the two sheets lying together on a bench in a back room off a street two streets wide.

“That copy has the second and third the wrong way round.”

“It does.”

“So it was made out of another copy.”

“It was made out of something, and it was not made out of the page, because the page has never once had them in that order, and I have the page in front of me in nine sheets of my own copying and I have had it in front of me since the fourteenth of October of the seventh year.”

## A Person Who Cannot Read It

“He cannot read,” said Perla Quist. “That is what you have come down four flights to ask me and you did not ask me, and I am going to say it because it is a fact about a piece of paper and not about a person.”

She squared the two sheets together and the nine sheets under the third table.

“About four people in this city cannot read a line of that page, and one of them had it written out for him out of somebody else’s copy by a woman who mends umbrellas and charges nothing, and he has it now, and the two sentences at the foot of it are in the wrong order because he read the second one as the third one and the woman writing it out from his mouth wrote down what he said she had said.”

“He did not know.”

“He could not know. That is not a thing anybody can know. It is the worst thing about a page that has been copied by people who cannot read, and it does not need anybody to do it deliberately, and the only thing that makes it worse is that the page is still perfectly correct on the rail.”

That was the whole of it, and she let it stand about four seconds without saying anything to fill the gap.

“He has been in twice,” she said. “The second time he did not want it written out. He wanted her to come and write it out in his own kitchen on his own table, and she said she would come on a day when the bench is shut, and she has not been yet.”

“Where does he live.”

“I am not going to tell you that and you did not ask me and if you had asked me I would have said no,” said Perla Quist. “There is no form in this city on which anybody may ask a person what they have done with a piece of paper, and there is not one on which anybody may ask where they live either, and the second of those is worse than the first and I have thought about it about nine times this month.”

*Two thousand six hundred and seven days. Less two thousand six hundred, the twenty-first of April of the eighth year, is seven, and nine documents were in nine hands by that evening and there is still no list of them in any book in this city, and there is no form anywhere that there could be one in.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is two hundred and eight days, which is six months and twenty-eight days after them, and that is the day the third line went onto a page at four in the afternoon and a copy carrying four sentences and no receipt has been older than it for every one of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and thirteen, the fourteenth of October of the seventh year, is a hundred and ninety-four days, which is six months and fourteen days after them, and a fourth word was put at the top of a column on that afternoon.*

## A Person And A Desk And Two Fields That Do Not Join

“You went down there to ask me to stop her,” said Perla Quist.

“Yes.”

“There is nobody in this city who can stop her and there is nobody in this city who ought to, and the only person who could have asked her was you, and you did not ask her, and I have known that you were not going to ask her since about the ninth of April.”

He did not deny it and she did not wait.

“I am going to say the part you did not come for, and then we will do the two hundred empty lines.” She picked the pencil up.

“The copies are a count of pieces of paper. They are not a count of claims and they are not a count of people, and the reason I will not let them be either of the other two things is that the day I let them become a count of claims every person who brings me one of them will want to know what it is worth.”

“It is worth a day, and a day is not worth anything to anybody.”

“That is the whole of your trade.”

“That is the whole of my trade and it took me four years and about nine arguments and it is the only sentence I have that I am actually proud of,” said Perla Quist.

She wrote for about an hour with nobody in the room but the two of them, and at about twenty to one he went up the four flights with nothing in his hands, which is how he has gone everywhere since the summer.

Behind him, on a bench in a front room off a street that runs down to a laundry, there were nine sheets of a woman's own writing lying face down under the glass of a case, and four of the nine were in kitchens on one stair off a street where the houses are the same.

One of them had two sentences at the foot of it the wrong way round, and it belonged to a man who cannot read, and he has asked a woman who mends umbrellas to come and write it out at his own table.

He did not know her name and he did not ask and he will not.
