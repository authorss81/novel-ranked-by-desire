# Four Hundred And Nine In Four Hours

Two thousand seven hundred and seventy-three was the fourteenth of October and the twenty-first is day two thousand seven hundred and eighty, and two thousand seven hundred and eighty less two thousand seven hundred and seventy-three is seven, and twenty-one less fourteen is seven by the month count.

## The Row That Is Not There

There are two things about the drawer and only one of them is in a book.

The opening of it is entered, in Wenna Raut’s hand, on the fourteenth of October, with the hour against the date and nothing whatever against the hour, and it is a true entry and a complete one.

The sheet is not.

About four hundred and nine pieces of rag paper went through that front door between the sixteenth of August and the twenty-first of October. Every one of them was received as nothing, on his instruction of the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, because a clerk could only enter what she was given, and a piece of pencil paper with the bottom two inches filled in is not a deed and is not a claim and is not anything a division’s form has a box for.

None of them is in the bound Register. Not one of them has a row, a date, an hour, a description, or a line of any kind anywhere in this office.

Four hundred and nine is a count of pieces of rag paper. It is not a count of claims and it is not a count of people, and he said both of those out loud at a bench four flights below a laundry in August and wrote them down, and the drawer being open did not change either of them.

He had wanted, for about a week, to be able to say that all of them had been looked at. He cannot say that. Looking at paper is not a thing an office does, and the only record that it was done is a sentence in a clerk’s hand saying a drawer in the wall on the left was opened at about eleven and shut again at half past three, and that sentence does not have one of them in it.

## The One Thing He Does

He came in at about eleven on the twenty-first and said he wanted a row entered, and he set it out for her in the order it stood before she had the sheet squared.

A piece of rag paper was received through the front door of this office on the sixteenth of August of the eighth year and was entered as nothing. It bears nothing whatever. It is a count of one piece of paper and is not a count of claims and is not a count of people. And it came in with nothing on it and nothing said about it.

“Enter that exactly,” he said. “And leave the field empty.”

Wenna Raut had the pen in her hand and did not put it down.

“The instruction of the tenth of June,” she said.

“I know what the instruction of the tenth of June says. It says every piece of paper received through that front door is to be entered as received, with a date against it, and with a description of the person who brought it, written in the words that person used, whether or not it is true, whether or not it is complete, and whether or not anybody is able to check it.”

“And you want me to leave the description empty.”

“I want you to leave it empty because there is nothing to put in it and there is nothing I am allowed to put in it, and I am not going to invent nine words for a woman I may not ask about anything.”

She put the pen across the book and looked at him for about four seconds.

“Then say the reason out loud, because I may only enter what I am given and you have not given me a reason yet.”

*Two thousand seven hundred and eighty days. Less two thousand seven hundred and seventy-three, the fourteenth of October of the eighth year, is seven, and a row went into the bound Register with the description of the person left empty, which is the first empty field in that book in nine years and cannot be filled in afterwards.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is two hundred and one days, which is six months and twenty-one days after them, and the thirty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty-one of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is three hundred and eighty-one days, which is twelve months and twenty-one days after them, and a copy carrying four sentences and no receipt has been older than that afternoon for every one of them.*

## The Reason She Can Enter

“A description of a person is written in the words that person used,” he said.

“And that person used no words.”

“She came to that door on the sixteenth of August and handed a clerk a piece of paper and said whatever it is a person says when they have nothing written on it and no reason written on it. The clerk entered what she was given, and what she was given was nothing.”

“There is no form anywhere in this city on which this office may put a question to that person about what she brought in, and no office in this city may ask her why. She is not traced and is not asked and will not be, and the only description available to me is that she brought nothing to describe.”

Wenna Raut wrote it in about four minutes and read it all back in the order it stood, and she did not improve one word, and then she sat with the pen above the page for a while.

“It will go in empty,” she said.

“Yes.”

“And it is the first field in this Register that has ever been empty,” she said, “and there is no form on which a field may be filled in afterwards, because a row may be added to and may not be taken from, and if that woman walks into that room in four years and stands at that counter there is nothing anybody may do.”

“That is the cost.”

“That is more than the cost,” said Wenna Raut. “That is the office writing down, in its own hand and with a date against it, that a person stood at that door and cannot be described.”

“She will ask why, and nobody will be able to tell her.”

She entered it. The date went against it and the hour went against the date and there was nothing whatever against the hour, and the description of the person was blank in the middle of a column of eleven that had never been blank.

## And It Is Nothing

It is nothing, and it is worth more than nothing, and both of those are true at once and she said so out loud.

It does not put a correction into a kitchen. It does not make the drawer openable, which it cannot be, because a second instruction is in the book and a third cannot be taken out of it. It does not reach the rail in a passage off Cordwain, and it does not reach four hundred kitchens where pieces of paper with two words on them in two orders are being argued about this week.

And there are eleven papers in this Register that came through that front door in eight days in the spring and about four hundred and nineteen accounts above them that were entered as they stood, and every one of them is about a person or about a piece of paper in the words of somebody, and the twelfth is about a person in no words at all.

She said the count herself, unasked, because she is a clerk and a clerk counts what is in front of her.

“Four hundred and nineteen accounts, eleven since the thirtieth of March, and one with nothing in it,” said Wenna Raut. “And four hundred and nine pieces of rag paper in a drawer that cannot be opened again, and that is four different numbers and no two of them are the same thing, and if anybody ever in this building gets a hold of one of them and carries it into another one, that is the day this office stops being worth anything at all.”

“I know. You told me in August.”

“I told you in August at a bench four flights down and you wrote it on the ninth sheet,” said Wenna Raut, “and I would like it understood at this table that the ninth sheet and this Register are not the same instrument and that I am not hers and she is not mine and neither of us is anybody’s.”

## One Sentence About One Person

There is a person in this city who stood at a door off a third court on the sixteenth of August with a piece of paper in her hand and nothing at all on it, and she said nothing that a clerk was permitted to write down, and she went away again.

The only record in Ostara that she was ever there is a line in a bound Register with a date against it and an hour against the date and nothing whatever against the hour and a space where the words about her were going to go.

Nobody knows her name and nobody may ask for it and nobody is going to send anybody to find out.

She is not in any list and she is not a claim and she is not one of about four hundred anything, and she is a person who walked into a building that does not have a door onto a street and handed something across a counter.

The bundle was on the table where it has been since the twenty-eighth of October of the fifth year, and there are twenty-five things in it, and the last of them has never been opened by anybody in nine years, and nobody in that room is obliged to open one item of it.

He did not, and neither did she, and the drawer in the wall on the left was shut and had been shut since half past three on the fourteenth and could not be opened again by anybody at all.

At about half past five he stood at the table and looked at the row with the blank in the middle of it, and understood that the whole of what he had done in seven years of making this office findable came down to one line in a clerk’s hand that could never be filled in.

A man who is allowed to ask this office any question in the world cannot ask it about her. The one thing he is allowed to do is ask about the office. The one thing he is not allowed to do is ask a person.
