# No Instrument For Correcting A Kitchen

Two thousand seven hundred and two was the third of August and the ninth is day two thousand seven hundred and eight, and two thousand seven hundred and eight less two thousand seven hundred and two is six, and nine less three is six by the month count.

## Before Anybody Asks

Perla Quist found it before anybody asked anything at all, the way she did in the seventh year, when she ruled nine sheets under a third table and headed them herself and entered the first thing in them before anybody told her to.

She came down four flights at about eleven on the ninth with the ninth sheet in her hand and put it face up on the table in the room off a third court, squared, without letting go of it until it was all the way over, which is the only thing she does with these sheets, because a sheet let go halfway is a sheet half entered.

Wenna Raut looked up from the bound Register with the pen in her hand and put the pen across the book, which is what a clerk does when something is about to be said that is not an entry, and Lin Hao stood at the end of the table with his hands in his pockets, because the ninth sheet is hers and the table is his and the two meet only when she carries one down.

“One of them is wrong,” she said.

Nobody spoke. Perla Quist says a thing is wrong the way other people say a thing is true, and she does not say it unless she has the day, and the day was the fourth of February for one copy and the middle of March for the other, and a man who cannot read had put the second one out of the first one at his own kitchen table and had not known.

“A copy made at the rail on the fourth of February carries the four sentences in the right order,” she said. “A copy made out of that copy in a kitchen in the middle of March has the second and the third the wrong way round.”

“The page has never once had them in that order, so it was made out of another copy, by a man who cannot read, out of a woman writing down what he said she had said, and he could not know and there is no paper on which anybody may tell him.”

She put her finger on the margin of her own sheet and not on his, because nine sheets under a third table are hers and the standing sheet is his and the two are never put side by side except on a bench.

“I said so first,” said Perla Quist. “The way I did in the seventh year. Before anybody asked. Before any counter ordered anything. Before any landlord’s man put anything face up. And there is no instrument in Ostara that can put a correction into four hundred houses, and the only thing that can is me, and I am not an instrument and may not be sent.”

## Pieces Of Paper And Not People

Lin Hao looked at the ninth sheet with its heading in her own hand and its entries under it, every one with a day against it, and understood what she had brought him, because she had brought him a day and not a correction.

“Say the three counts,” he said, quietly, “the way you said them in April, because somebody has to say them and there is nobody else in this room who can.”

She said them all, in order, as three entirely different things, because the copies are a count of pieces of paper and they are not a count of claims and they are not a count of people, and the day she lets them become either of the other two things every person who brings her one of them will want to know what it is worth.

“About four hundred pieces of pencil paper in about four hundred kitchens,” said Perla Quist. “Every one copied by hand. And at this counter now a fifth hand’s copies are out there too, and they are not in anybody’s list, because a woman who mends umbrellas writes the foot out for anybody who brings her one, and she keeps no list, and she began in the middle of last year.”

Wenna Raut wrote absolutely nothing down. A column kept four flights down under a third table is not entered anywhere she can reach, and no row on the page behind the window may be cited against it, and she knows that without being told.

“And eleven,” said Lin Hao, after a while. “Eleven pieces through that front door in eight days in April, entered as received with a date, and still eleven on the last leaf in May, and the first row under the new instruction saying off the third court, the one with the stair at the back.”

“Eleven pieces of paper that came through a door,” said Perla Quist. “Not eleven people. Not eleven claims. Eleven is a count of pieces of paper and it is not either of the other two, and by the end of next year on the same rate it is about four hundred, which is the same figure as the four hundred and is not the same thing, and no page in this room will ever say otherwise.”

That was the collision, said out loud in a room off a third court on the ninth of August, with all three counts on the table at once and every one of them named for what it is, and nobody merged one into another, because the woman with the pencil would not let them.

The three of them stood close round a narrow wooden table and nobody said anything for a while. Perla Quist put two fingers on the wrong line of her own sheet and did not move them, and Wenna Raut put the pen back into her hand and did not open the bound Register, because the wrong copy in a kitchen was the first thing in four years that nine sheets under a third table could see, and no other paper in this city can reach it.

*Two thousand seven hundred and eight days. Less two thousand seven hundred and two, the third of August of the eighth year, is six, and the fourth column found one that is wrong before anybody asked and Perla Quist said so first.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is a hundred and twenty-nine days, which is four months and nine days after them, and the thirtieth sheet has been face up for every one of the last forty of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is three hundred and nine days, which is ten months and nine days after them, and no copy with the second and third the wrong way round was ever made out of that afternoon.*

## She Is Not An Instrument

“What happens to the wrong one,” said Wenna Raut, after a while, because she is a clerk and a clerk asks what happens to a thing, and because the bound Register behind her holds rows with days and hours and nothing else, and a wrong copy in a kitchen is the first thing anybody has brought her that has a day and an order both wrong at once.

“Nothing,” said Perla Quist. “There is no instrument in Ostara for correcting a kitchen. A counter can order a claim with a day against one without. A landlord’s man can put a receipt face up. A laundry can write down when a copy was made. But none of those puts a correction into a house where a man who cannot read holds a sheet with two sentences the wrong way round, and the woman who wrote it out will not go to his table, and nobody may send her.”

She picked the ninth sheet up and squared it against the edge of the table, because nine sheets under a third table go nowhere and she was going to take this one back up four flights.

“I am the only thing that can,” she said. “By going down there myself, on a day when the bench is shut, and sitting at his table and writing it out again. And I am not an instrument and may not be sent, the way I said in February before anybody asked me for anything, because a column with a heading on it is a thing somebody can be asked to stop, and I ruled it myself.”

Lin Hao nodded, and the shame of it came up in him hot and clean, because she had said no five times in four years and this was the fifth, and the reason has never once been about him, and he had heard the other four and was hearing the fifth now, and this time he understood it.

He went down four flights with her at about noon and stood in the back room while she put the ninth sheet back under the third table with the other eight, and the heading stood at the top of the fourth column in her own hand with one line under it saying what it is about, and about two hundred empty lines waited under the entries, and the ruler lay squared against the edge of the bench.

He thought about asking her to go to the kitchen on the day the bench is shut, and did not ask, because he has asked her for nothing since February except to read a docket, and she has said no five times in four years, and the fifth of them is standing in this room with a day against it.

There are four sentences at the foot of that page and they have stood there since before any clerk’s hand in this city, and in about four hundred kitchens there are pieces of paper with those four sentences copied out at the foot of them, and one in nine has two of them the wrong way round, and there is nobody in this city who can put that right except a woman with a pencil, and she may not be sent.

He came back up the four flights behind her and did the floors of three washrooms and the stairwell and the bin room, and thought about one kitchen and which two of the four sentences it had got the wrong way round.

The only person alive in this city who can put that right is four flights up, and she is not an instrument and may not be sent, and a thing that is true and cannot be corrected is the exact shape of about four hundred claims he has spent nine years on.

There are about four hundred kitchens, and one copy in one of them is now wrong, and the only instrument in this city that can put it right is a woman who may not be sent and would have to sit at his table herself, one kitchen at a time, on a day the bench is shut, of which there are about four left before the rain.
