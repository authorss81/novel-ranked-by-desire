# The Seventieth Form Goes Up

Two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine was the thirtieth of March and the seventh of April is day two thousand five hundred and eighty-six, and two thousand five hundred and eighty-six less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine is seven.

The month turned at its thirtieth. There is no thirty-first anywhere in this city and there is no day of the week, so the seventh of April is seven days on from the thirtieth of March and is not one day on from it, and anybody who wants to shorten that has to shorten a fact.

## Sixty-Nine Before This One

The sixty-ninth came down off the board at about half past six in the morning with a strip of paper along the top edge where the paste had let go, and the boy put it over the shoulder of the form that was going down and went up the render with the brush to the new joint and stopped dead at the bottom of it, the way he stops dead every morning, because paint will not hold on a face a mason has worked.

The seventieth went up over the top of it at about seven. A month’s form on office paper with two ruled squares at the foot of it, and nothing whatever in either, and both of them going to be empty at four o’clock that afternoon and at four o’clock on every afternoon after it for as long as the boy is willing to carry a jar up a hill.

Lin Hao stood four feet off with his hands in his pockets and did not ask the boy anything, and had not come up the hill to ask the boy anything, and was not going to.

“There is sixty-nine of them now,” the boy said, to the board.

“Yes.”

“Not that it is a figure anybody wants.”

## The Door Off The Third Court

Nobody had counted. That was the whole of it and it had been true for eight days.

On the thirtieth of March at about half past four in the afternoon an instruction had gone into the bound Register in a clerk’s hand with a man’s name at the foot of it, and the instruction was that every piece of paper received through the front door of that office is to be entered as received, with a date against it, whether or not it is a copy of the foot of the standing sheet and whether or not anybody is able to check it.

Since then, in that building, the front door had been used eleven times.

Wenna Raut had the eleven of them in a row along the back edge of the table at about a quarter to eleven, squared, and she had counted them twice and had not been asked to by anybody, and she said so before he had got to the table.

“You did not ask me to count those.”

“No.”

“I counted them because nobody had,” she said, “and I would like it on the record that I would have counted them anyway, because a thing that happens eight days running and is not counted is not an event, it is a habit, and in about four months there will be nobody alive who can say how many there were.”

Eight days. Eleven papers. The rate is what it is and neither of them wrote it down, because writing a rate down is the making of a figure, and this office has had two exact figures in its life and both of them were months.

“You have a door now,” said Wenna Raut. “That is what has happened. You have not got a queue. You have not got anything a person can find out about by standing in it. You have got a door that people have found out about, and a door is not a queue, and a queue is a thing that has to be somewhere to stand.”

“It is a court.”

“It is a court with a stair at the back of it and two floors and a bin room,” she said, “and there is nowhere in this building for anybody to wait who is not also being looked at, and I have thought about that for eight days and I have not written it down, and if you ask me what a person who came here this morning wanted I will have to tell you that it is not in the book and there is nowhere for it to go.”

## What There Is No Field For

There are five fields in the second column and they have not changed since the second year: when, where, the hour, what was done, and a person.

Three lines are in it. The third of them went onto the page at four on the thirtieth of September and it is a receipt in this office’s hand saying in four words that none of the pieces of paper received is a deed, with a man’s name under it and a sentence about a drawer put in under that in October.

The fourth line cannot go in until the thirtieth of June. Nothing goes into that column between one quarter-end and the next and she says that out loud once a quarter and has said it about nine times in two years.

And the drawer in the wall on the left of the table was shut, and had been shut since the twenty-first of October of last year, and four hundred and nine pieces of rag paper are in two cardboard boxes on the boards under the window that have not been moved since the thirtieth of December.

“You have eleven pieces of paper on that table,” said Lin Hao, “and about four hundred and nine in those boxes, and they are not in the same book and they will never be in the same book, and I made the second of those shut in October and the first of those legal in March.”

“I know what you made,” said Wenna Raut. “You made me enter the second one and you were not in the room.”

“Was it wrong.”

“It was the only instruction anybody has ever brought me in six years in this room that asked me to enter something I could not check,” she said, “and every other one asked me to enter something true, and I checked it before I wrote it. You have taken the check out of the job and left everything else exactly as it was. I said that on the thirtieth and I am not going to say it again until the thirtieth.”

*Two thousand five hundred and eighty-six days. Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is seven, and seven pieces of paper came through a front door that stands open on a court in those seven days and nobody in this building knew until a clerk counted them twice on the fourth day of April.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and six, the seventh of October of the seventh year, is a hundred and eighty days, which is six intervals of thirty exactly, and a seventieth form went up on a board by a kerb this morning with two ruled squares at the foot of it and nothing whatever in either.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is a hundred and eighty-seven days, which is six months and seven days after them.*

## A Paper With A Stamp On It

The eleventh of the eleven was not a piece of pencil paper.

It was a sheet of about the size of a sheet folded once, with a Bureau stamp in the corner at an angle that was not square to the edge of it, and it had come through the front door on the sixth of April at about eleven in the morning and it had been entered as received with a date against it, because that is what the instruction says, and the date against it is the sixth of April.

Nobody in that building may serve a paper and nobody in that building may return one. There is no instrument in this city for doing either of those things out of a room off a third court, and there has never been one, and the person who wrote that sheet had put it down in the ordinary way and would be entitled to expect it answered.

He did not read it.

It was face down on the table when he came in and it was face down when he left, and he had put it face down himself at about a quarter to eleven, and Wenna Raut watched him do it and did not ask.

“You have turned a Bureau paper over.”

“Yes.”

“That is not a thing anybody does in this room.”

“It is the fourth thing anybody has done in this room in nine years and I am not going to tell you what the other three were,” he said. “Tell me one thing about it and then I will go and do the floors.”

“The stamp is on the corner at an angle.”

“I noticed.”

“It goes on at an angle when it goes on by hand and not through a press,” said Wenna Raut, “and it goes on by hand when somebody has walked it to where it is going and not put it through anything. That is not a thing about the paper. That is a thing about a person carrying it up a stair and being angry at the stair.”

He stood about four feet off the table with his hands in his pockets and thought about a man carrying a sheet up two flights in a building with no door onto a street, which is this building, which is where it was carried, and about the fact that there is no instrument anywhere in Ostara for serving a document at a building a person can be found at instead of an address.

And he had known that for about six years and had never once had to write it down.

There were eleven papers on the table and about four hundred and nine in the boxes under the window and one of the eleven was stamped at an angle by a hand that got tired halfway up a stair.

He had gone up the hill that morning to watch a form go on a board and had come down it to find out how often a door he had opened eight days ago was being used, and the answer was eleven times in seven days, and one of the eleven was still face down on a table because he could not think of one single thing he was entitled to do about it.

Nobody had asked him to decide anything. He decided it anyway, standing up, in about four seconds, the way he has decided about nine things in nine years, and he went down the stair to do the floors.
