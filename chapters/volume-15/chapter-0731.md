# A Copy Of The Denial

Two thousand seven hundred and fifteen was the sixteenth of August and the twenty-third is day two thousand seven hundred and twenty-two, and two thousand seven hundred and twenty-two less two thousand seven hundred and fifteen is seven, and twenty-three less sixteen is seven by the month count.

## The Lane Off Cordwain

Emil Brack had nine stools in a lane off Cordwain with the nine districts behind them and a slate on a nail and twelve books that were not the district’s, and he was paid a grain a head for every head that stopped, and nobody had ever said thank you to him for it, and he had never once asked.

The morning was ordinary in the way that mornings in that lane are ordinary, with carts going through at the pace carts go through and a man selling nothing at all from a barrow at the top end.

“I want this not written down,” said Emil Brack, before Lin Hao had got the second stool out from under him, “and I want no figure on it anywhere, and I am not going to be paid and I am not going to be thanked, and that is the whole of what I have come to say.”

“You have not come to me about anything.”

“I have come to you about a thing I counted,” said Emil Brack.

## What He Counts And Why He Counts

He did not take the sheet out of anybody’s hand. He never does, because a toll is taken for stopping and not for showing, and the twelve books on the shelf behind him hold about nine figures a morning and not one of them is anybody’s business.

“In the first three weeks of this month,” he said, “about eleven people stopped at these stools with a copy in their hand, and I did not ask to see it and did not look twice. About four of the eleven had two words on them in somebody’s writing, and about four of those eleven had the two of them the other way round from each other.”

“They cannot both be the order in the stone.”

“That is what I have said to four people this month and not one of them wanted to hear it from a man who counts,” said Emil Brack. “I am telling you because you are the one who put something on a page on the sixteenth and I would rather you knew that the page is late.”

He picked his slate off the nail and wiped it with his sleeve, which he did about nine times a morning whether there was anything on it or not.

“Do not print it,” he said. “The day it is printed the four stop bringing them, because a claim everybody knows came from a building is a claim that waits to be told. The other seven find somebody with a better wall.”

## Running It Backwards

He was in the back room of the laundry four flights up at about half past eleven, and the ninth sheet was out on the bench with its heading standing at the top of the fourth column in Perla Quist’s own hand, and about two hundred empty lines waited underneath it.

She had a copy on the bench beside the sheet, made in pencil at a rail, with the day the man wrote at the foot of it.

“Your colleague counted something this morning and told me about it before he told me anything else,” she said, “and I have been sitting here since nine doing the only thing I can do with it.”

“What thing.”

“The check backwards,” said Perla Quist. “Every copy anybody has brought me has the day it was made at the foot of it in that person’s own hand, because they all date them, because that is what a copy is for. For a year I have been asking one question of each of them. Was it made after the fourth of February.”

She put a finger on the sheet, not on his copy.

“Tonight I asked a second question, and it is the only question in this room that has never been asked, and it is this. Was it made after the twenty-second of December.”

She said the day and not the year, and he did not supply it.

“There is one copy of the two words in this city and it was made on the twenty-second of December, out of a lump of chalk, on the back of a month’s form, and it is in the inside of a coat in a place you have not been and I am not going to guess at.”

“I have the day of it because you gave me the particulars of a thing in April and the day was part of the particulars. I have never been given the words themselves and I would not know them if I had.”

## About A Third

She went down the fourth column with the flat of the pencil held above the bench, not touching it, the way she does when she is counting something she is not going to enter.

“Of the copies that have been brought to me, about forty carry any hand of the two words at all. Of those, about a third were made before the twenty-second of December.”

“A piece of paper made before the only copy of a thing cannot be a copy of it,” said Lin Hao.

“It cannot,” said Perla Quist. “So it was written out of somebody’s mouth, or out of another piece of paper, and the chain runs backwards past the day the only version of those words was ever made in this city, and the links in the chain are in about four hundred kitchens and not one of the links has a name on it.”

He looked at the figure she had put at the foot of the column and did not say it, and she saw him not saying it and pushed the pencil half an inch along the bench.

“It is the best thing anybody in this city has produced in four years,” he said.

“It is nine sheets under a third table,” said Perla Quist, “and it goes nowhere. And it covers the copies that have been brought to me, which is not all of them, and I keep no list of the ones that have not been, and you are about to ask me for one, and the answer is that a woman who keeps a list of pieces of paper is a person who can be sent, and I am not a person who can be sent.”

She squared the ninth sheet against the edge of the bench and did not say the rest of it, and he said it for her.

“Nobody may be sent to any of them either.”

“Correct,” said Perla Quist. “And you know it, and you are standing there with your hands in your pockets, so I am going to put the pencil down and let you have the room.”

*Two thousand seven hundred and twenty-two days. Less two thousand seven hundred and fifteen, the sixteenth of August of the eighth year, is seven, and a check run backwards over nine sheets found that about a third of the copies in circulation that carry any hand of the two words were made before the twenty-second of December.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is a hundred and forty-three days, which is four months and twenty-three days after them, and the thirtieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-three of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is three hundred and twenty-three days, which is ten months and twenty-three days after them, and a copy carrying four sentences and no receipt has been older than that afternoon for every one of them.*

## A Denial Goes Out On A Rail

He came up four flights behind her and stood at the table in the room off a third court for a long while with the page squared under the window in front of him, and the head at the top of it unmoved, and three lines in the second column, and the four sentences at the foot with his own denial under them in the office’s hand and his name and a date under that.

Seven days ago he had given nine years of saying nothing up in exchange for one arguable sentence, and the sentence had been the right one, and a resemblance is not a figure, and every counter in this city will read it four times a year and about nine people a week will be told it out loud by a man who runs a desk under a window and is paid nothing.

And it does not go to a kitchen.

That was the part he had not costed, and it took him the whole of the afternoon to understand it, and it took him about four seconds to wish it back.

A claim about two words is in four hundred kitchens in two orders, and about a third of the pieces carrying either order were made before anybody in this city wrote them down anywhere, and the only answer the office has is on a rail in a passage where about nine people a week stop, and it says that the words are not a figure.

Nobody in any of those kitchens will ever be told that.

There was a woman at the rail at about four in the afternoon with a copy folded in her hand, and the counterman read the whole of the bottom of the page out to her the way he reads it, and she listened with her head slightly turned, and when he came to the denial she said out loud, to him and not to anybody else, that a building in this city had now said on paper that the two words were not a figure.

The counterman said that was what it said.

“Somebody in that building expects to be asked about them,” said the woman, “and they have not asked me, and I have been standing here since eleven with this in my hand.”

He read the rest of the page to her and stopped at the striking, and she put the copy back in her pocket and went, and he never found out whether she was one of the four or one of the seven, and the counterman never asked her either, because a counter reads the page.

His name was at the foot of that sheet in the office’s hand. His name was on about four hundred pieces of paper in a kitchen in pencil, under a line four words long and a sentence about a drawer. Both were his. Neither could be read by anybody who was entitled to read the other, and there was one person in Ostara entitled to ask about either of them, and he was standing in a room with no door onto a street wishing he had never been.

Two hands. He had put his name in both of them himself, and he had never once looked at them side by side until the twenty-third of August, which is the day a check ran backwards and found that about a third of the copies were older than the only copy there is.
