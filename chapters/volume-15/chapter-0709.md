# Nobody Picked It Up

Two thousand five hundred and sixty-six was the seventeenth of March and the twenty-fourth is day two thousand five hundred and seventy-three, and two thousand five hundred and seventy-three less two thousand five hundred and sixty-six is seven, and twenty-four less seventeen is seven by the month count.

## Six Days

Two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine is the thirtieth of March, and two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine less two thousand five hundred and seventy-three is six, and the twenty-ninth sheet is squared to the edge of that table at about half past two on that afternoon and there is no version of this office in which anything can be added to it after that.

Wenna Raut had the book open in front of her and the pen beside it. The drawer in the wall on the left of the table was shut and had been shut since the twenty-first of October, and there was a bundle of twenty-five things on the table that had not been touched since the twenty-eighth of October, and nobody in that building was obliged to open one item of it.

“You have six days,” she said, “and I have said that out loud in this room every quarter for about two years, and it is the fifth time this year and I have said it about five times in the last quarter alone, and I am not going to say it a sixth time because I am not a machine at this table and I would like somebody to notice that I have noticed.”

“Nobody noticed.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut. “You are the only person this office may ask and there are nine of us in this building and you have not asked one of us what the three lines in that column are for in about two years, and I have worked out what that is, and I have decided that it is not going to be one of the things I say out loud.”

She turned the book a quarter round and put it back.

“I am not being cold at you. I am telling you that the offer has been on this table eight times and that it has never once been picked up, and that after the eighth time a woman stops putting it out.”

## The Question She Spent And Was Not Asking

She had one question a morning. She had had it since about nine and by half past ten she had spent it and it had not been about the four lines and it had not been about the drawer.

“Is this office going to go on receiving.”

“Yes.”

“That is not an answer,” said Wenna Raut.

“It is the only one I have.”

“It is not, and I will tell you why it is not, and then I am going to go back to my column and you are not going to ask me a second question today.”

She put the pen down. “Yes is an answer about tomorrow. What I asked about is the part after tomorrow. You have answered me about a day, and I have watched you answer me about a day four times now, and every one of the four was correct and not one of them was what I asked.”

He stood about four feet off the table.

“If the answer is no then I can put something in that column in March instead of in June, and there is a field on it I have never had anything for and I would be able to fill it, and I have wanted to fill it since about the second year and I have never once been able to get an answer about anything longer than a day out of anybody at this table.”

“Write me.”

“I may not write you a form,” said Wenna Raut. “There is no form. I have looked, and looking is not a permitted reason for refusing to make one, and I have told the man at the end of that table the same thing about four times and he agrees with me and he cannot do anything about it either.”

Yusuf Adeyemi had not moved from the far end of the table and had not taken his coat off.

“The answer is yes,” he said, from the end, “and I am not going to improve it either, and neither of you is permitted to.”

## The Fifth Field On A Column Of Five

The second column is headed carried by and has five fields in it, and they have been the same five fields since the second year, and Wenna Raut had read them out to him once in February of a year neither of them names.

Five fields, in this order: when, where, the hour, what was done, and a person.

He had the other four and had had them since the twelfth of September.

The fifth is the only one anybody has ever wanted, it cannot be filled by anybody in this building except out of the mouth of the person it is about, and it goes in with nothing in it or the whole line does not go in.

That is a fact about the column.

It is not a permission.

He stood at the end of the table at about four in the afternoon with the list on the back of his hand in four versions, and he crossed out the first three without reading them and had the fourth one left, and the fourth one did not have a person in it either.

He was not going to give it to her. She would enter it exactly and she would enter it today and it would be on the twenty-ninth sheet at four this afternoon in about two hundred hands by the end of the month.

## The Room Off A Ward With A Door That Closes

There is a ward in this city with a door that closes and a small room off it with a cot in it, and a woman in that room who takes a fortieth off people and does not tell them that she is doing it. He has been in that small room about nine times in six years and has never once been asked anything about it, by her or by anybody.

It is not a thing he is going to put in a column, and there is no form on which it could go in.

That is the whole of what there is to say about it this quarter, and he said it once, in one place, and he was not going to say it again.

*Two thousand five hundred and seventy-three days. Less two thousand five hundred and sixty-six, the seventeenth of March of the eighth year, is seven, and the twenty-ninth sheet is squared at about half past two on the thirtieth of this month, which is in six days, and after that nothing goes on that page until the thirtieth of June.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and sixty, the eleventh of March of the eighth year, is thirteen days, and a woman off a street two streets wide has a fourth column with one word in it and about two hundred empty lines under it.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is a hundred and fifty-three days, which is five months and three days after them, and a drawer in this room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction in this office's hand with a man's name under it.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is a hundred and seventy-four days, which is five months and twenty-four days after them.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is eight hundred and fifty-five days, which is twenty-eight months and fifteen days after them.*

## The Sixth Time And The Seventh And The Eighth

He sat down on the second chair at about half past four, which he does about four times a year.

“Can I ask you something that is not a question of the morning.”

“You may ask me something,” said Wenna Raut, “and if it is a question I will tell you that I do not take questions in this room and you will have wasted about four seconds, and if it is not a question you may say it and I may answer it or not.”

“All right.” He turned the list over. “What are the three lines for.”

The pen did not stop and she did not look up and she answered it in the ordinary way, in about two seconds, as a woman tells a clerk what a form is.

“The first one is about a bundle and it is older than the two of us. The second one is a person and a division and a finding and it is older than this office. The third one was entered in September and has your name under it, and under your name a sentence saying that a drawer is not to be opened, and that sentence went in on the twenty-first of October.”

“That is what is in it.”

“That is what is in it,” said Wenna Raut, “and it has been in it for about five months and it has gone out at four four times and about nine people a week read that third line to the bottom because it is the only line on that page anybody has ever bothered to stop at.”

He put the list back in his coat.

He had come into that room on the twenty-fourth of March meaning to ask her what the lines were for, and he had been carrying the question since about eleven o'clock in the morning and he had asked it at half past four. It was answered in two seconds and it told him nothing, and he had known before he asked that it would tell him nothing, and he had asked it anyway.

It had cost him the only thing he had been carrying about all day, which was the belief that a woman who enters what she is given will one day hand him something she would rather not.

“Thank you.”

“You have not thanked me for that in two years and I have not thanked you for anything in six and we are not going to start,” said Wenna Raut. “Go home. It is four minutes to the corner and about two hundred and forty of them go past it between five and six.”

## Six Days And Three Lines

At four minutes past five he was at the corner with his hands in his pockets. Two hundred and forty people came past in the first hour, of whom ninety stood and read a month’s form for an address off a board and nine went down at the foot of the wall.

And in a room off a third court a column on a page that goes out at four on the thirtieth of this month had three lines in it and can take one more, and the fourth line, if it is going in at all, takes a person in its fifth field.

There is no person in this city he may put in it except himself and he is not going to, there is no form anywhere on which he could ask anyone about it, and he has six days.

He had spent none of them. There was nothing in the morning and nothing in the afternoon, and he had gone down four flights twice and up them twice and had asked one woman one question he had already known the answer to.

He stood at the bottom of a hill in the rain at about ten past six and understood that the sheet was going out at four on the thirtieth with three lines in the second column, exactly as the twenty-eighth sheet went out on the thirtieth of December, and that the six days were not going to be used for anything at all.
