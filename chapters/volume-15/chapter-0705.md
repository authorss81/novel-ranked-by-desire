# The Second Copy

Two thousand five hundred and forty was the twenty-first of February and the twenty-eighth is day two thousand five hundred and forty-seven, and two thousand five hundred and forty-seven less two thousand five hundred and forty is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven by the month count.

## Two Pieces Of Paper And The Dates On Both

A check needs two copies of one page and the day both of them were made, and the office keeps what it keeps and what it keeps is in a drawer that a man in a white plate instructed in writing on the twenty-first of October is not to be opened.

Perla Quist had both of them on the second table on the last morning of the week and she had not laid them out yet when he came down.

She had a pencil and a sheet of her own paper and a piece of pencil paper in a man’s handwriting with two inches of writing in it in the bottom, and she had her own copy of the foot of a page in her own handwriting with four days of the month at the top of it.

“There is no form in this city that says a person comparing two pieces of paper is doing anything at all,” she said. “You understand that before we start.”

“I understand it.”

“Good, because I am going to do it anyway and I have thought about it for four days and I have not once thought of a form.”

She squared the two sheets so that their bottom edges were in a line.

“This is his. He made it in a kitchen. He has put a lane and a number of months and a day in it and there is a figure at the top in his own hand and the figure is the twenty-second of January.”

“And this is mine.”

“And this is mine, and I made it at about ten past ten on the fourth of February at the rail in Cordwain with a man behind a counter reading the foot of that page out loud to him and about four other people who did not get that far, and I dated it the day I made it and I am the only person in this city who can say I made it on that day.”

## The Check

It took nine minutes and neither of them said anything for the first four of them.

She read down his sheet to the two inches. She read down hers. She put her finger on the third line of the second column in her own copy and read that out, and then she read the four sentences at the foot, and then the six lines under them, and then the striking, and then the name under the striking, and then she stopped.

“He copied his out of the same page,” she said, “on the fourth of February, from that rail, and every word of it that he could see standing up is in that column and in that column and nowhere else. There is not one thing in the two inches of his that has been contradicted by anything in nine days.”

“No.”

“There,” said Perla Quist. “That is the check. That is the whole of it and it took nine minutes and it is the first time in the history of this city that anything has been true against a day.”

She wrote it in the narrow column, four words against the row, in her own hand.

## What It Says And What It Does Not Say

“His claim is about the twenty-second of January,” she said, “and the check has put it true against the fourth of February.”

She turned the sheet a quarter round so that it faced him.

“Those are two different days and they are thirteen days apart. The check does not know anything at all about the twenty-second of January. The twenty-second of January is the day a street was dug. The fourth of February is the day a man stood at a rail and wrote down what a page said.”

A street dug on the twenty-second of January is still a street dug on the fourth of February.

What the check has proved is not that it was dug.

It is that on the fourth of February there was a piece of paper with a claim in it, and a page that said the same thing it says now, and two people in a passage.

That is what it says. That is all of it, and it is not a softened version of the whole of it, and I am not going to be asked to say a bigger one because you have come down four flights.

“All right.”

“You are going to want a bigger one and there is not going to be one and I have had four days to work out what I was going to say if you asked me for one,” said Perla Quist, “and what I was going to say is the sentence at the top of that page, and it is the only sentence I have got.”

## Four In The Afternoon At A Table Off A Third Court

He went down at about four and put the back of his left hand on the table face up in front of Wenna Raut, which he has done four times in eight months, and read out what he wanted entered.

*That a piece of pencil paper, made by a person in this city, has been compared with a copy of the foot of the twenty-eighth sheet and found to say the same thing the page said on the fourth day of this month. That the comparison was made by a person and not by this office. That it is a comparison of two pieces of paper and it is not a deed.*

She read it where it lay and read it a second time and then said, “Say the last part again.”

“It is not a deed.”

“That is a sentence,” said Wenna Raut, “and I am not permitted to improve a sentence, and I am going to enter it as you have given it to me and I am going to read all of it back to you in the order it stands.”

She entered it in four minutes in the order it was given and read it back in the order it stood.

“It is not a deed,” she said, “and I am going to say one thing about that and then I am going to go back to my column.”

“About nine hundred people in this city can read what you have just put in that book. In about four years one of them will be standing at a counter holding a piece of pencil paper in his own hand, and he will say, I have had that checked against a day, and he will be right. Nobody will be able to tell him what it was checked against, including him.”

“I know.”

“You have known it since the fourth of February,” said Wenna Raut, “and you have brought me a sentence that says it in words anybody can read, and I have entered it, and that is my whole afternoon.”

## The Question She Spent At Four

She had one question and she had had it since about nine, and she spent it at about four past four and it was the correct question and it went straight through him.

“Who made the second copy.”

“The woman with the pencil.”

“Of the page.”

“Yes.”

“That is not what it is,” said Wenna Raut. “What it is, is one paper a man wrote in a kitchen set against a second paper a woman wrote standing up at a rail, off a page a man behind a counter read out loud to him. He gets nothing for it, and he is the only witness to either half of it.”

“If he had read one line of it wrong on that morning, then your four minutes would have been the first thing anybody in this city had ever put true against a day, and it would have been wrong.”

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

“You have one question a morning,” he said.

“I have one a morning and I do not take them in this room, and I took that one because it was about paper,” said Wenna Raut. “A question comes to me and I enter it as it arrives or I do not enter it at all. This one goes in the book word for word as I have said it and you may not tell me afterwards that it does not count.”

*Two thousand five hundred and forty-seven days. Less two thousand five hundred and twenty, the first of February of the eighth year, is twenty-seven days, and a woman off a street two streets wide has entered nine hundred words of a thing that is true against a day and it is not a deed and it is the first of its kind in this city and there is no form anywhere that says what a person is for when she does it.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of August of the seventh year, is a hundred and seventy-eight days, which is five months and twenty-eight days after them, and four words went into the Register of an office off a third court on that morning in a clerk's hand and have been read out at a counter and on a rail since.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is a hundred and forty-eight days, which is four months and twenty-eight days after them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and thirteen, the fourteenth of October of the seventh year, is a hundred and thirty-four days, which is four months and fourteen days after them.*

## Running It Backwards

He went back down at about ten past five because there was nothing else in the city he could do about it and because she had said there was one more thing and he had wanted it.

She had the sheet out and the fourth column had one word in it.

“The check runs backwards as well,” said Perla Quist. “That is the part that matters and you have all afternoon to notice that you did not ask me for it.”

“How.”

“Take any copy anybody in this city has made out of the foot of a page. The third line of that second column went onto the page on the thirtieth of September. If a copy has that line in it, that copy was made on or after the thirtieth of September. If a copy has not got it, that copy was made before the thirtieth of September and does not know about four hundred and nine pieces of paper or about a drawer or about a man’s name.”

She put the pencil down beside the ruler.

“That is a claim that can be put true against a day without asking anybody anything. Not about what happened to them. About when they wrote it down and what was on the page when they did.”

“Nobody in this city has ever needed to run a check backwards.”

“Nobody in this city has ever had four hundred pieces of paper in four hundred kitchens with a day on them before the last week of last year,” said Perla Quist. “And I have, and about a third of them are going to come to this table over the next two years and about two thirds of them are not, and a copy that nobody ever brings me is a copy that stays exactly as it was written.”

She squared the nine sheets and pushed them back under the third table.

“You have handed about nine hundred people a tool,” she said. “You did it by coming down four flights twice and asking me for nothing. It was not for me. It was not for them.”

“It was because a man asked you a question in a doorway on the first of this month and you have not been able to put it down since. I have watched you not put it down for about nine weeks and I am not going to be the one who praises you for it.”
