# Nobody Says It Came From Him

Two thousand six hundred and thirty-five was the twenty-sixth of May and the third of June is day two thousand six hundred and forty-two, and two thousand six hundred and forty-two less two thousand six hundred and thirty-five is seven, and three less twenty-six is seven by the month count.

## The Lane And About Nine Figures A Morning

The lane off Cordwain keeps nine stools and nine districts and a slate on end and twelve books squared beside it, and a slate is a thing you cannot rub a mistake out of, which is why Emil Brack uses one, and the whole of his method depends on there being no way back.

A grain a head. About nine figures a morning, and about four hundred people in this city who turn up because he is standing there, and every figure goes on the slate with its reason said out loud in front of all of them before it is written down.

He squared the ninth book at about a quarter to eleven and looked up.

“I have got something and I am going to say what I want first,” said Brack. “I want it not printed. I want nothing else. There is no figure here for you and I am not going to take one, and you are not going to thank me and I have been in this lane for about nine years and I have never once been thanked and I am not going to start.”

“All right.”

“You are going to ask me how I know.”

“I was going to ask you how much you know,” said Lin Hao, “which is a different question and it is the one I actually came down for.”

## Four Men And One House And Nine Pounds

“There are four of them,” said Emil Brack.

He turned the slate round and did not write on it, which he does not do.

“About four hundred pieces of pencil paper. Every one of them is against one of four men, and the four men work for one house, and the house has an office above a shop off Cordwain and about nine letters go out of it in a month.”

“Four hundred.”

“Four hundred pieces of paper and not four hundred people and not four hundred claims, and I have said that distinction to all of them this month and only one of them heard it.”

He put his thumb on the edge of the slate. “The four men are easy. One signs for the works and stands at a yard gate for about four days in every month. One takes a cart and does not give a name and has not given a name in about nine years.”

“One goes round about six sets of stairs with a rent book and has never once been in a court that had a stair to it. And one sits in an office above a shop and writes the letters, and every letter that any of the three has ever had is in his hand.”

“And what is on the paper.”

“Nine pounds,” said Brack. “Nine pounds on every one of them, and the same nine pounds, and it has been the same nine pounds since the ninth month of last year, and I have had nine figures a morning for about four months in front of all of them, and every one of them can add.”

He squared the book again.

“That is the whole of what I know and it is correct and I would not be telling you if it were not.”

## What He Wants Not Printed

He put the chalk down, turned round, and took the end of the wall with his coat off the back of it.

“Now. If it is printed.”

“Yes.”

“If it is printed anywhere — on a page, on a notice, at a counter, in your own book in your own hand — about forty people will say it came from you.”

“Why forty.”

“Because you are the only person in this city anybody has heard of who finds things out and does not get paid for finding them out, and because a man in a white plate with no figure cut into it is a thing that about nine hundred people can read all the way down and one of them will say it to about nine others before the week is out,” said Brack.

“And then the rest of them will hear inside about a month, because they all go to the same counters.”

“And then.”

“And then about four hundred people will stop writing in the bottom two inches of their own piece of paper,” said Emil Brack. “Not because of you. Because of what a claim is when everybody knows where it came from.”

He said it without any weight at all, the way a man reads a figure off a slate.

“A claim a person made in their own kitchen with their own pencil is theirs. They made it up and they can put it down and they can pick it up again.”

“And the moment everybody in this city knows a claim came out of a building with no door onto a street, then every one of those four hundred people stops and waits to be told. And they will wait about four years, because that is how long a thing takes to become a thing this city does anything about, and there is not one of them who has four years of waiting left in them.”

He picked the chalk up and turned it over twice.

“So that is what I want, and I have said it once and I am not going to say it again. If it is printed, they stop writing. If it is not printed, they go on writing what they believe, and they are right to, and not one of them will ever be able to show anybody that they are right, and that is the whole of what any of them has got.”

## Nobody Says It Came From Him

“That is the best thing anybody has said to me in about four years,” said Lin Hao.

“Do not do that.”

“I am not thanking you.”

“You are looking at a slate as though there were going to be something on it.” Brack turned it back round. “There is not going to be anything on it and there is not going to be anything in the twelfth book, and that is the whole of what I have asked for, and I asked for it about eleven seconds after you came down the lane.”

“I have not asked for a figure and I am not going to be paid for this, and I have thought about being paid for it for about nine seconds on the way down here and I decided against it.”

“Why against it.”

“Because a man who pays a broker for a fact has a fact a broker found for him,” said Emil Brack. “And I have not got a building. If you print it, it came from a lane, and a lane is not a building, and about forty people will look at a lane and decide it came from a man, because that is what people do with a man.”

He put the slate on end and got the twelfth book squared.

“You have it now and I cannot tell you what you are going to do with it and neither can you, and that is the correct condition for a man in your position to be holding four men in, and I am going back to my nine figures.”

*Two thousand six hundred and forty-two days. Less two thousand six hundred and thirty-five, the twenty-sixth of May of the eighth year, is seven, and a man who has never been thanked in nine years gave away the answer to a question that has been open for nine weeks and asked for nothing at all.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is sixty-three days, which is two months and three days after them, and there has not been one person in this city for one day of them who is able to check any one of them, and there has never been.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is two hundred and forty-three days, which is eight months and three days after them.*

## Four Doors He Cannot Go Into

He went back up off Cordwain with four names in his head that he would not write down and could not have taken out of his head if he had tried.

There were four places he could have put it and he went past all four in about two hundred yards.

Not the second column. Three lines in it and three lines in it at the thirtieth of June and a row may be added to and may not be taken from, and the fifth field takes a person out of the mouth of the person it is about.

Not the Register. He could have entered it in a clerk's hand with his name under it, and a clerk who may only enter what she is given would have entered it exactly and could not have improved one word of it, and it would have gone onto a page at four o'clock on the thirtieth of June and out to about two thousand counters.

And it would have said that four men who work for one house are the men in the bottom two inches of every one of those pieces of pencil paper in this city, in his own hand, with his name at the foot of it.

Not a note down four flights. She had refused him five times in four years and the sixth was at about a quarter to five on the twelfth of May, and what she would say to four names is that a list of people is not an index and that a column is not a list.

And not the woman who cannot read, who has not been near that table since the eighth of December and did not say she was coming, and about whom nobody in this city may ask a single question.

So he stood on a landing at about half past two in the afternoon with the whole of what he had come down the lane for, and there was not one place in this city it could be put where all of them could get at it and no person in it could be got at.

Nobody in Ostara is going to say it came from a lane. He had been about to spend four years making it impossible for anybody to say where anything came from, and a man on a wall off Cordwain had just made sure that the one thing he was about to do could not be traced to him either.

That was not a defeat. Emil Brack had been right about every fact for nine years and had asked for nothing and was not going to be thanked and had put a stone on a slate in a lane where four hundred people a week watch it go up.

And a claim that everybody knows came from a building stops being a claim and becomes a request, and there is no form anywhere in this city that answers a request.
