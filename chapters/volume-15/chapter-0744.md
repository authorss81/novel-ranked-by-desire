# The Column Opened

Two thousand eight hundred and one was the twelfth of November and the nineteenth is day two thousand eight hundred and eight, and two thousand eight hundred and eight less two thousand eight hundred and one is seven, and nineteen less twelve is seven by the month count.

## Three Lines And A Heading

The second column has been on the quarterly sheet since before this office existed and it has had a heading on it that nobody in the building has ever been able to explain, and the heading is two words and it says **carried by**.

That is all it has ever said. It is printed on the form once a year along with everything else and it is the same on all four of the sheets that go out, and a heading cannot be altered after it has been printed, and nobody has ever asked for it to be.

Under it there are three lines.

The first of the three is the line from the twelfth of September of the seventh year. It carries a date and a number and four words and a man’s name, and under the name a sentence in a clerk’s hand saying that a drawer is not to be opened.

The other two are older than this office. They are in the same hand as each other and it is not a clerk’s hand and it is not his. One of them names a place. The other names a person who is not alive and whose name is in no other book in that building.

He had looked at that column perhaps forty times in nine years. He had never once read down it.

## What A Column Of Carriers Is For

He stood at the table under the window on the morning of the nineteenth and read the three lines the way a person reads an instrument he has been keeping for somebody else, from the top, with the ruler along the edge.

“A name on that page,” he said, “is not a description of anybody.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut.

“It is not what somebody said about a person and it is not what was done to a person.”

“It is not,” said Wenna Raut. “And you are about to say the next part yourself and I would rather you got to it than had it handed to you, because you have had a bad week and I have watched you get things handed to you by people who wanted you to have them.”

He said the next part.

“It is the name of whoever carried the thing.” He put two fingers on the heading and did not move them. “That is what *carried by* means. It is not a column of what was received. Every one of the three lines has a name on it and not one of them has a description on it, and the two that are older than this office have names on them the way a cart has a name on it.”

## The Two That Are Older Than This Office

“I asked you in the first year what those two lines carry.”

“You did, and I said I had been given the sheet and the two lines were on it when it came and I was not going to improve one word of anything, including a question,” said Wenna Raut.

“That was the correct answer then and it is still the correct answer and I have not come back about it in eight years because it was the correct answer.”

“You have come back about it now.”

“I have come back about it because I have been asked a question in a lift four days ago that I could not answer, and the answer is on that page and has been on it since before the building was put up,” he said.

“A rota is a record of a whole person’s week. It is the only record I know of that has the off days in it, and the off days are the whole of what a person does that nobody is looking at, and this column has been holding names of people who carried things for about nine years and it has never once held anything about what they did with the time.”

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

“That is a sentence,” said Wenna Raut, “and it is not an entry, and I am not going to enter it, and I am not going to say it is wrong either, and I would thank you not to look at me like a person waiting to be asked whether a column may be opened.”

## What The Register Has And The Column Does Not

“There is a fifth field in the Register,” she said, “and I put it beside the fourth one in May on my own account, because I was afraid a clerk would come along and put a person in it the wrong way round.”

“The fifth field is about what was done to a person.”

“The fifth field is about what was done to a person, and it is filled out of the mouth of the person it is about, and it has been empty for six months and it is not going to be filled in by me,” said Wenna Raut.

“That is the Register. The second column is not the Register. The second column does not take a person described. The second column takes a name, under a heading that says carried by, and that has been its shape since before I came here, and if a name goes into it, it goes in as the name of the person who carried something, and nothing else about that person goes in with it.”

“So there is one field in this building that will take a person and it is not the one we built.”

“There have been three in nine years,” said Wenna Raut, “and you built two of them and I have not been wrong about either one yet, and I would like the third to be the old one, because the old one has never once been wrong about anybody.”

## The Only Name He Has

He thought about it for the rest of the morning with the sheet squared in front of him and the drawer shut in the wall on his left and the bundle on the table beside it with twenty-six things on it that nothing is going to be done with.

A column of carriers. A page that goes out four times a year to about two thousand counters, and about nine people a week who read to the bottom of it standing up. A name at the head of a line under two words that mean carried by.

About four hundred people carried a piece of pencil paper up four flights of a stair in a building off a street two streets wide and put it on a third table, and then carried four hundred and nine pieces of rag paper four flights up another stair to a counter off a third court, and every one of those four hundred journeys was made by the same woman, and there is no page in this city with her name at the head of anything.

She is the only name he has.

Not the only name in the column — there are three up there and two of them are older than the office — but the only name he could enter tomorrow morning without going out of the building to get it, and the only name that is not his own, and the only name in this city attached to carrying every single one of about four hundred claims into the only place in Ostara that can put a claim true against a day.

He understood, at about half past eleven, standing at a table with a ruler against it, that he had been thinking about this for four days and that he had known four days ago what the answer was going to be, and that everything between the fifteenth of November and this morning had been him arranging to arrive at it.

And that he had arranged it, and that arranging it was the thing he does, and that there is no instrument in this city which can be aimed at a person except a column with a name at the head of it, and that he has spent nine years refusing to use one on anybody and has spent four days working out how to use one on her without it looking like that.

He was not going to be forgiven for it and he was not asking to be. He was not going to tell her first either, and he turned that over twice on the morning of the nineteenth and it came out the same way both times, because if he told her first then she would have to agree to be named or refuse, and a refusal in this city is a thing a person carries round for the rest of their life, and he had no business handing her one.

He did not write it down. No paper in this city will take a line saying that a man intends to do a thing, and there has not been one since the first year, and he was not going to invent one on the nineteenth of November.

*Two thousand eight hundred and eight days. Less two thousand eight hundred and one, the twelfth of November of the eighth year, is seven, and a man read the second column from the top for the first time in nine years and found that all three lines in it were names.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is two hundred and twenty-nine days, which is seven months and nineteen days after them, and the thirty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last forty-nine of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is four hundred and nine days, which is thirteen months and nineteen days after them, and a copy carrying four sentences and no receipt has been older than that afternoon for every one of them.*

## The Heading Is Two Words And It Does Not Open

At about half past two he asked her one question, which was not her question of the morning, and which she answered anyway because he was allowed to ask the office anything at all.

“If a name went in it tomorrow, what would be the heading over it.”

“It is printed on the form,” said Wenna Raut. “It says carried by. It has said carried by since the first form this office ever sent out and it will say it on the next one, and you cannot take a heading off a printed form, and neither can I, and there is no office in this city that changes a heading on a form after it has been printed.”

“Right,” he said.

He went down four flights at about half past four with the folded docket he no longer carried against his ribs out of habit and stood about nine feet back from a rail in a passage off Cordwain while a man who runs a desk under a window read the foot of a page out loud to a man with a cap in his hands, without being asked, and paid nothing for it, and stopped at the striking the way he always stops.

The second column was nine feet above the four words and about nine feet below the head of the page, and about nine people a week stood in that passage and read all of it.

Three lines. Three names. And two words at the top of it that had been printed before this office existed and could not be taken off the form now that anybody knew what they meant.
