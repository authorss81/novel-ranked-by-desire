# A Name In The Second Column

Two thousand eight hundred and fifteen was the twenty-sixth of November and the third of December is day two thousand eight hundred and twenty-two, and two thousand eight hundred and twenty-two less two thousand eight hundred and fifteen is seven. November runs to its thirtieth and there is no thirty-first. The twenty-sixth to the thirtieth is four, the first of December is one, and the third is two more, and four and one and two is seven.

## Ten In The Morning With The Sheet Out

The sheet for the quarter-end had been kept at the back of the room in a flat file since the middle of November, because a sheet is not squared until about half past two on the day it goes out and a sheet that is squared early goes crooked in the fold.

He took it out at about ten and squared it against the edge of the table himself and wrote the date at the top of the second column in his own hand, which he has not done since the sixteenth of August, and which is not what a clerk does and is allowed of him because he is the only person that office may ask.

Then he said it out loud, slowly, in the order it was to stand, because Wenna Raut may only enter what she is given and cannot improve one word of it and will not begin until she has been given all of it.

“Under the date. A name. The name is Perla Quist, and she is alive, and she is not an officer of this city and she does not work in this building and she has never in six years been sent to do anything.”

“Go on.”

“Under the name, in your hand, say: carried every sheet of pencil paper brought to the third table in a laundry off a street two streets wide, up four flights, to this counter, and carried them on; and say that the papers themselves are not in this building and are not in hers; and say that the index of what those sheets carried on the day each was made is nine sheets under a third table and may not be cited.”

“And the number.”

“There is no number in it,” he said. “There is a number against the other three and there is no number against this one and there is no figure of any kind in this column.”

Wenna Raut wrote it in about four minutes and read it all back in the order it stood, and she did not improve one word, and her voice did not change on any part of it.

## The One Question Of The Morning

She squared the sheet to the edge of the table.

“I had a question this morning at about nine,” she said, “and I have been carrying it down to the table, and I am going to spend it now and then I have not got another one until tomorrow.”

“Ask it.”

“What goes in the line under the name that you have told me is about carrying and is not about the person.”

“Nothing.”

“Then it is a line with a name and no description and a sentence underneath it and no number,” said Wenna Raut. “Which means anybody who reads it is going to take the name for the whole of it, because there is nothing else on the line to take.”

“Yes.”

“That is the correct entry and I am going to enter it,” said Wenna Raut, “and I want it recorded in this room, out loud, by you, that I said that to you and you said yes, and that I entered it anyway, because in about four years somebody is going to read that line and work out that a clerk saw the whole of it and wrote it down.”

He said it. She entered it.

There was no argument in that room at any point and there has never been an argument in that room, and the two of them had both known that since the first year and neither of them had ever minded until about eleven o’clock on the third of December of the eighth year.

## What It Closes

The second column is a column of carriers and it had been a column of carriers since before the building this office sits in was built, and for nine years it had also been the only place in any book in this city where nothing whatever about a person had ever been entered.

That is now over.

He could not put a piece of paper in it, because the heading says carried by and paper is not carried by anybody in the shape the column takes. He could not put a claim in it, for the same reason. He could not put a street, a date or an account in it.
There were exactly two things in nine sheets and nine years that had ever fitted under that heading, and both of them were a person, and until this morning one of them was dead and the other one was himself.

The place in this office where paper is received and nothing is said about anybody has just been closed by the only man in Ostara entitled to close it, and it was closed by him in the direction he has spent four years insisting he would never move in.

## What It Costs, Said Out Loud By Him And Not By Her

He said it to the table, at about twenty past eleven, with Wenna Raut at the far end of it, because it had to be said in a room and she was the only other person who could hear it and she may not be asked whether she agrees.

“About four hundred people carried a piece of paper up those stairs and put it on that table, and every one of them thought they were dealing with a laundry and a rule about umbrellas and about who mends what, and none of them knew her name and none of them needed to.”

“Yes.”

“From this afternoon there is a name under a heading on a sheet that goes out four times a year to about two thousand counters, and the name is the reason they trusted the index, and they will be told it by somebody who is paid nothing to tell them, and they will believe it because it is printed.”

“And the man who printed it cannot open the drawer that holds what they wrote, and he is the only person in Ostara who may ask this office any question in the world.”

“Yes,” said Wenna Raut. “And none of it will be in this building when she finds out, because it goes out at four on the thirtieth and this building has no door onto a street and she will hear about it at a counter or in a passage or from a woman selling something, and by then it will have been true for about a month.”

He had not thought about that part and she had, and she said it in the level voice she uses at a counter.

“And there is one more thing and then I am going to stop saying it,” she said.
“This office has never in nine years put a person in a book it does not have to. She worked that out about four years before I did and she has never mentioned it. And a line went into that column this morning with a name on it and nothing else, and it is the first time, and the man who put it there is the same man in both sentences, and one of those two sentences is going to cost him.”

He said, “Yes,” and that was the whole of the exchange, and neither of them thanked the other for anything and neither of them expected to and neither of them has ever done.

## Twenty-Seven Days

He did not go up four flights that afternoon or the next morning or on any day after it, and he did not send anybody, because he may ask this office anything and he may not send anybody and there is no form in this city on which he could send anybody if he wanted to.

At about half past five he stood at the table with the drawer shut in the wall on his left and the bundle beside it with twenty-six things on it, and the sheet squared under the window with a date at the top of the second column and a name under the date and a sentence in a clerk’s hand under the name and his own name under that.

It does not go anywhere until four o’clock on the thirtieth of December.

*Two thousand eight hundred and twenty-two days. Less two thousand eight hundred and fifteen, the twenty-sixth of November of the eighth year, is seven, and a name went into the second column of a quarterly sheet in the office’s hand with a man’s name under it, and the woman it is about was not asked.*

*Less two thousand five hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of March of the eighth year, is two hundred and forty-three days, which is eight months and three days after them, and the thirty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last sixty-three of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is four hundred and twenty-three days, which is fourteen months and three days after them, and a copy carrying four sentences and no receipt has been older than that afternoon for every one of them.*

## What Is On The Table For Twenty-Seven Days

The line was correct.

That is the part he could not get past for the rest of that week, and it is the part that will be true about every decision this office has ever made that was any use, and it is the reason he was eventually going to have to say out loud, to somebody, that being right is not the same as being forgiven.

*carried by* — and a date, and a name, and a sentence in a clerk’s hand that says a woman carried about four hundred pieces of pencil paper up four flights of a stair and that the papers are not in this building, and a man’s name at the foot of it, and no number anywhere on the line.

There is not one error in it. There is not one word in it that he would take out if he could take anything out, and he cannot take anything out, because a row may be added to and may not be taken from and a line under a printed heading on a quarterly sheet is exactly the same instrument as a row in a bound Register.

For twenty-seven days the only copy of that sentence in the whole of Ostara is a sheet lying face up in a room with no door onto a street, and there is nobody in this city who is entitled to be shown it, and a woman who has known for six years exactly what she is for is four flights down a street and about nine minutes away from a lift, and she does not know it is there.
