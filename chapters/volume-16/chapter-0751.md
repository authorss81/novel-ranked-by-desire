# Nobody Came Up The Stair

Two thousand eight hundred and forty-nine was the thirtieth of December and the first of February is day two thousand eight hundred and eighty, and two thousand eight hundred and eighty less two thousand eight hundred and forty-nine is thirty-one, and thirty-one days is the whole of the gap between the last working day of last year and the first of this one, because a month runs to its thirtieth and there is no thirty-first in this city.

## The First Morning Of A Year With Nothing Due In It

The drawer in the wall on the left of that table was shut at half past three on the fourteenth of October two years ago under an instruction with his own name under it, and it is shut now, and there is nothing anywhere in this city on which a drawer may be opened again once a man’s name is under the instruction that shut it, including by the man.

The bundle is on the table beside the drawer with twenty-six things on it. The twenty-sixth of them is a piece of rag paper about the size of a copy of the foot of a printed page with nothing on it of any kind, and it went onto that table at about ten past ten on the twenty-eighth of October of last year and has been looked at about four times since and has never once had a pencil mark in it.

There is no requirement on anybody to open any of the twenty-six. That is not a rule anybody has written down; it is the way the table works, the same way a key board works.

It is there because the table stands in a room where things are entered, and a bundle is a thing that is not entered, and it has stood there through four quarter-ends and eleven sheets and two drawers and one instruction. In nine years not one clerk has ever been asked to look inside it, and not one of them has ever wanted to.

He has understood about four times in nine years that a bundle is an instrument, and that the price of an instrument is a floor, and that he has not been able to bring himself to pay it, and that the twenty-sixth thing on it is the proof and has been empty since the fourteenth of October.

Nothing has come through the front door of that office since the twenty-eighth of December. Nothing is due until the thirtieth of March. There is a woman at a counter in it during the day who gets one question a morning and had not spent this one, and there is a bound Register open under the window at about four hundred and thirty rows, of which one has an empty space in the middle of it where a clerk’s pen was and was not.

That row is the twenty-first of October of the seventh year. It is the only empty field in nine years of that book. A row may be added to and may not be taken from, and a field may be written at the moment it is being written and never afterwards, and he is the only person in Ostara who may ask this office any question in the world and he is not permitted to ask it about her.

## Thirty Times Ninety-Six

Yusuf Adeyemi came in at about a quarter to eleven and did not take his coat off, which is what he does, and stood at the end of the table with one hand on the back of a chair he was not going to sit in.

“Two thousand eight hundred and eighty,” he said. “It is thirty times ninety-six.”

“And nothing left over.”

“Nothing left over,” said Yusuf Adeyemi. “It is the third time this office has printed a figure that comes out exact. The first was two thousand one hundred and sixty, on the first of February of the seventh year, and that is thirty times seventy-two. The second was two thousand five hundred and twenty, on the first of February of last year, and that is thirty times eighty-four. Three in nine years, and I have never once been asked for one of them.”

“Not one person has ever asked you for any of the three.”

“Not one person has ever asked me for any of the three, including the first,” said Yusuf Adeyemi, “and I have held this thing for six years under a title that cannot be produced, and I want that said back to me in this room this morning in the ordinary way, which is that you have said it and I have heard it.”

He said it back to him.

“And the other thing,” said Yusuf Adeyemi, “is the thirty-second sheet. It has been face up on that rail since four o’clock on the thirtieth of December. Thirty-one days.”

“Thirty-one.”

“A sheet goes up every ninety days, and every one of the last ninety has gone up at four on the last working day of a quarter, and not one of them has ever stood on that rail for a day without something else happening in this room.”

He took his hand off the chair.

“In thirty-one days this office has put nothing on a page. Not a row, not a line, not a figure. A page goes out at four on the thirtieth of March and it will be the thirty-third of them, and the last thing on it was written by me in December and the thing before that by the two of you.”

## Four Lines In A Column And Nothing Going Into Them

The sheet for the quarter-end is in a flat file at the back of the room and does not come out until about half past two on the thirtieth of March, and it is not out.

The figure at the head of it will stand where it has stood since the thirtieth of March of the fifth year, and there is no permission for it to move and there never has been, and how many sheets and how many quarters it has stood still across is not a thing this morning is for.

Below the head there are about four hundred and nineteen lines with a date against every one of them and an hour against the date and no figure against the hour and no name along the line.

Below that is the second column, with four lines in it under two printed words that say **carried by**. He has read down that column once in nine years, on the nineteenth of November of last year, and it has four lines in it this morning and it will have four lines in it on the thirtieth of March, and there is no fifth one coming.

The reason there is no fifth one coming is a woman who is four streets away and two floors above a bench, who has said no to him five times about nine different things, who put the price of her own name to his face in about nine seconds, and who has not been asked about anything since.

## About Half Past Five, At The Foot Of The Hill

That corner is busier at half past five than at any other hour of the day.

About two hundred people a day stop at it. About ninety read the board. The four inches at the foot of that wall are read by about nine people in a week, and not one person who stops at the board ever goes down to them.

The board is carrying a month’s form with two ruled squares at the foot of it and nothing whatever in either. One is for the figure of the thing at the address. One is for the standing of it.

Neither has held anything on any day since the eighth of October of the first year, when a clerk was given a sheet and a form and nothing to go on and typed two words into one of them because the box was there and the words were there. There is nothing in this city for asking anybody what a box is for.

And the four inches have had nothing in them since the thirtieth of December.

That is what he went out for, four times in four weeks, getting himself down low enough against a kerb with his back to a drain and his hands in his coat, in the hour before he starts.

On the fourth of this month he found a piece of pencil paper with two words on it and nothing under them. On the eleventh there was one. On the eighteenth and on the twenty-fifth there was not one.

And the last mark he could find anywhere under a striking in this city belonged to a man in a laundry who had written nine words about a thing that happened to somebody else in a doorway, and it was eleven weeks old.

The four inches are the only place in Ostara where a person who has had something done to them can put it down in their own hand. About four hundred people have used them. He has never once been able to ask any of them a question about it, and no instrument in this city could, and he cannot think of one, and he has been trying for five weeks.

## What He Has Been Living Inside Since The Third Of December

On the third of December he put a line in a column with a woman’s name at the head of it and his own name under it, and at about four minutes past four on the thirtieth a man behind a counter under a window in a passage off Cordwain read it out loud to eleven people, in the voice he reads everything in, because that is what a man behind a counter does with what is on the page.

He is paid nothing for it and never has been, and there is nobody alive he could name who has thanked him for it.

A man with a cap told a woman. The woman told a man at the mouth of a lane before five.

Lin Hao stood nine feet back with his hands in his pockets and let it happen. For about a week before that he had wanted one thing in this city, a single thing a person could not carry to somebody else, and in about four minutes he watched it go to two people.

There was no instrument in Ostara that could have stopped it and there never will be.

It is in about four kitchens now and it will be in about nine by the end of this month, and the man who put those two figures into the air first has been paid nothing for saying them on any of the four times he has said them, and he has been right every time.

## The Number He Cannot Get

What he is actually afraid of on the first morning of a year that divides by nothing is a number.

About nine kitchens is a count of kitchens. There is no list of kitchens anywhere in this city. There is no list of addresses in any book in that building, and no list anywhere else either, and there is no instrument on which this office may ask a person what she did with a piece of paper she carried up four flights to a bench.

He has four addresses he could name. He has no way to be in any of them, and no reason to suppose he would be let in if he got to one, and the one woman in Ostara who could tell him which four they are has said no five times and is not going to be asked a sixth.

He comes in at eleven at night and goes out at half past five, four nights and three off, on a rota the size of his own hand that carries four names and that he asked for.

*Two thousand eight hundred and eighty days. Less two thousand eight hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December of the eighth year, is thirty-one, and nothing has come through the front door of that office since the twenty-eighth of December and a figure came out exact for the third time in nine years.*

*Less two thousand eight hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December of the eighth year, is thirty-one, and the thirty-second sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirty-one of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is four hundred and sixty days, which is fifteen months and ten days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
