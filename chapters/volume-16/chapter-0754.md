# The Field About What Was Done

Two thousand eight hundred and ninety-four was the fifteenth of February and the twenty-first is day two thousand nine hundred, and two thousand nine hundred less two thousand eight hundred and ninety-four is six, and twenty-one less fifteen is six by the month count.

## The Fifth Field, Said Out Loud At A Table

“The fifth field is the one about what a person had done to them,” said Wenna Raut. “Not the one about who turned up with the paper, which is what half the people who have ever heard of it assume, and there is not an officer in a division building in this city I would rather be wrong in front of.”

She said it at the table rather than at the counter because there were two of them in the room and the other one needed to hear it.

“The words go in it from the person it is about, in that person’s own mouth, and not from whoever happens to be standing here. Nine years, and it has stood there the whole time like a fifth door in a wall with four in it.”

“Who says so.”

“I say so, and I said it at this counter on the twelfth of May and you had it out of me in about four seconds then, and the whole reason I am saying it again this morning is that I have begun to think somebody in a division might come to that counter inside four years and put a person in the wrong one, and I would like it to be in this room that it was named.”

“You named it at a counter.”

“I named it at a counter and it was in a clerk’s mouth and it went in a clerk’s hand nowhere,” said Wenna Raut, “which is exactly why I am saying it at a table.”

## The Sheet Out Early, And Nine Months Of An Instruction Nobody Has Read

He had taken the sheet for the quarter-end out of the flat file himself on the morning of the twentieth and squared it to the edge of the table, and it would be squared again on the thirtieth at about half past two because a sheet that has been squared early goes crooked in the fold, and Wenna Raut had let him do it and had not said anything about it.

It lay under the window with the head at the top of it and about four hundred and nineteen lines under the head and four lines in the second column under two printed words that say **carried by**, and the four sentences at the foot, and six lines under them with the fourth of the six struck from the first letter to the last, and a name under the striking, and two inches of nothing at the bottom of it.

It does not go anywhere until four o’clock on the thirtieth. He had got it out early to read the foot of it, and he had read the foot of it about nine times in five days, and the foot of it had not changed since the thirtieth of December.

The instruction of the tenth of June is nine months old.

Every piece of paper received through the front door of that office is to be entered as received, with a date against it, and with a description of the person who brought it, written in the words that person used, whether or not it is true, whether or not it is complete, and whether or not anybody is able to check it.

That is what it says.

Here is what it has been doing.

It has been producing rows.

About four hundred and nineteen accounts in that book before a clerk’s hand and eleven since the thirtieth of March, and every one of the four hundred and thirty has a day against it and an hour against the day and nothing whatever against the hour, because the fourth field is the hour, and the description was never going to go into the fourth field, and there is no other box on the form for a person.

So for nine months this office has been obeying an instruction about persons by producing a receipt.

He had written the instruction in about nine seconds on a June morning and had not read it since. Wenna Raut had read it back in about nine seconds and entered it in about four minutes. And between that morning and this one the office has asked about four hundred and thirty times what a piece of paper was, and not once what happened to the person who brought it in.

“And in the middle of all four hundred and thirty there is one row that did produce a person,” he said. “The twenty-first of October, and a space in the middle of it, and she used no words at all.”

“That is the only row in that book that has ever managed it,” said Wenna Raut, “and it managed it by having nothing to put in it, which is not the same thing as being right.”

## The Sentence That Is Not About A Sheet

He said it out loud then, because he had been carrying it up two floors and down two floors for thirteen days and it was going to come out of him or it was going to come out of him in a month.

“On the eighth of February a woman came up four flights to a bench in a laundry off a street two streets wide. She was handed a piece of office paper with a date at the top of it and four words on the back in a clerk’s hand. Perla Quist read the four words to her. And then, about four seconds later, at that bench, out of her own mouth, in about nine seconds, she said a sentence.”

He gave it to the room exactly as she had given it, in her order, without improving a word of it.

“They said it didn’t happen.”

“That is not about a person,” said Wenna Raut.

“It is exactly about a person. It is about whether a thing that was done to her happened, and it is the first sentence anybody has given this office in nine years that is about her at all, and I have carried it up two floors for thirteen days and I have not put it anywhere.”

“You cannot put it anywhere. That is what I am telling you. There is a field for it — the fifth, the one I have just named at this table — and the field is in this room, and the sentence is four streets and four floors away, and the only thing that will ever join them up is a person standing at a counter being asked something.”

“Which you may not ask her.”

“Which this office may not ask her,” said Wenna Raut, “and which it could not ask her if it wanted to, and there is a woman four streets away who has refused five times to have her name at the head of anything and who is right five times, and neither of those facts is going to change because you have been carrying a sentence up and down two floors for thirteen days.”

## The Two Books On Two Floors

He had it before eleven and he had it wrong at ten.

There is a field for what was done to a person.
There is a sentence about a person.

They are in two different books in two different buildings about four hundred yards apart, and one of them has never been filled and the other has never been written down, and there is no instrument in Ostara that will ever put one against the other except a person answering a question at a counter.

And he may not ask her anything. He is the only man in this city who may ask this office any question in the world, and the whole of what he is permitted with that is the office.

“There is a sentence about a woman in a book two streets away and a field about that same woman in a book in this room, and neither one of them can be reached from the other by anything a person is allowed to do. And the sentence that would go in the fifth field is hers,” he said.

“The fifth field is in this room and she is not going to be asked about it by anybody, ever. And I have spent a morning finding out that the one thing in this building that was built for her has been sitting here empty for nine years with me on the wrong side of the room from it.”

Wenna Raut squared the Register to the edge of the table and did not improve anything he had said, including the parts that were wrong about her, because a clerk enters what she is given.

## Her One Question Of The Morning

“I have had this since about eight o’clock,” she said, “and I have been holding it because I wanted the fifth field out of your mouth first, and I have got it, and I am spending it now, and I have not got another one until tomorrow.”

“All right.”

“May this office enter a sentence that a person said out loud at a counter.”

He stood at the end of the table for about nine seconds.

“No,” he said. “There are four instructions in that book and every one of them is an instruction about a piece of paper, and there is nothing anywhere in this city on which this office may write down what a person said to it, and I cannot enter her sentence and neither can you and neither can the man who signs the keys out every seventh day.”

“That is not an answer,” said Wenna Raut.

“It is the only one there is.”

“No, it is not,” said Wenna Raut, “it is the shape of an answer, and I have been given the shape of one in this room about four times in nine years and I have learned to hear it. You have told me that you may not. You have not told me what happens if somebody else may.”

He did not have an answer to that and she had one question a morning and she had spent it and she squared the book again.

“I know,” he said.

*Two thousand nine hundred days. Less two thousand eight hundred and ninety-four, the fifteenth of February of this year, is six, and a clerk said out loud at a table what the fifth field is for and has never been filled, and the sentence that would fill it was given at a bench four flights up on the eighth and has not been written down anywhere.*

*Less two thousand eight hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December of the eighth year, is fifty-one, and the thirty-second sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-one of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is four hundred and eighty days, which is sixteen months exactly after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
