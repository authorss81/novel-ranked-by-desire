# Nobody Can Be Asked

Three thousand and fifty-five was the twenty-sixth of July and the second of August is day three thousand and sixty-one, and three thousand and sixty-one less three thousand and fifty-five is six, and two out of the thirtieth, which is the last working day of that month, is the second of August.

## Ten To Twelve On The Top Landing

There is no water on the top landing of a branch office off a third court and there has not been any since before he came to it, and he carries it up four floors on the word of a man who left that building in the second year.

He stands on that landing for about nine minutes most nights before half past eleven and it is the only surface in this city he is permitted to deface with a thought.

He had got it by the twenty-eighth of July and he had been carrying it about five days and it was five sentences long and he had never once said it out loud to a person, and on the second of August he put it on the back of his own right hand in pencil because the left one has seven things on it already.

It is the shortest argument anybody has made in this city in nine years and it is a list and the list is the argument.

## How He Got To Five Sentences

It started on the twenty-seventh of April with a name he did not say out loud and put back in a coat pocket on the second landing of a branch office.

It went into a corridor in May, when four men in four different rooms said they had been told the same thing by a person none of them had seen before or since and there is no name anywhere in any of the four sentences.

It went into a laundry in June, when a woman with a pencil said no out loud for the sixth time and did not thank him for not asking and told him he was early.

It went into a lane in July, when a man with a slate said there was nowhere left to put a thing he wanted unprinted and then told him that four men had asked him about a building and that he had said no four times and it had not worked once.

And it went into a room off a third court in July, where a clerk told him that about four hundred people had been written down in public and in their own words for what they said out loud, and that there was no way at all in Ostara to give any of it back.

Five sentences in three months, out of five refusals, and not one of the five was against him.

## One

This office may not ask a person anything.

Not about a piece of paper, and not about where she takes it, and not about what she has done with it since. There is a rule about that and there is also the arrangement, and the arrangement came first and has held for nine years and about four hundred people have trusted this building because not one person in it ever asked them anything.

The rule could be broken by a man. The rule has been breakable since the eighth of October of the first year.

## Two

The index may not be sent.

Nine sheets under a third table four flights up a street two streets wide, about two hundred lines under a heading a woman ruled on her own paper in the eighth year, a copy of a counterman’s nine-year ledger in a pigeonhole, and a fourth column with a heading on it since the twenty-first of February before last.

It may not be cited in any division in this city by anybody, ever, and it has never once been cited in six years.

It may not be sent to a counter either, because the day it is sent it becomes a document and the woman who keeps it is a woman who has been handed one, and about four hundred pieces of pencil paper are safe in this city for one reason only and that reason is her.

## Three

The boy may not be asked.

He has carried a board up a slope at the foot of a hill for seven years and put it against a kerbstone and squared it with the toe of his shoe and pushed it flat and gone back up without looking at it. There are two ruled squares at the foot of the month’s form and they have been empty since the eighth of October of the first year and he has not looked at them in seven years.

Not one person in this city has ever asked him a question about any part of that, and there is a reason, and the reason is that about two hundred people a day go past that kerb and about ninety read the board and there has not been one in all that time who wants to know what a box is for.

He is not asked about the boxes. He is not asked about the form. He is not asked by anybody about anything at all, and that is not care and it is not protection. It is that there is no question and no one to ask it.

## Four

The woman with the short-handled broom may not be asked.

She has worked that corner every morning of his life and she is not named in any book this office holds and she is not named in his mouth either, for the third running volume, and there is nothing on which she could be identified if he wanted to identify her.

She sweeps to the kerb and back up again about ten past seven. She has never once mentioned what she has seen done down there, and she has not been asked, and she is not going to be, and she has not asked him anything either.

That is the whole of nine years between two people who have been at the same corner every single morning.

## Five

The woman who cannot read is never going to be asked.

She has come up four flights to that bench eleven times in eleven months and asked each time whether what happened to her happened, and been told no each time, and she has been carrying a piece of office paper with four words on the back of it for about a year and has never once been able to read it and has never once asked anybody to read it out to her.

There is not one form anywhere in this city on which this office may ask a person what she has done with a piece of paper. And there is nothing on any of them that would let it ask her what she would like to know either, and nothing that would let it ask her nothing and write down that it asked her nothing.

She is not named. She is not traced. She is not asked anything by anybody, anywhere, in any year of her life.

And there are about four hundred pieces of pencil paper in this city that are only safe because the woman who carried each one of them is a woman with nothing at the head of anything. That is the fifth sentence underneath the fifth one and it is the reason the whole list holds.

About four hundred and thirty of those pieces went up four flights to a bench and came down them again over six years, and every one of them went down because it came down, and not one of them was entered anywhere and not one of them was sent anywhere and not one person in this city except a woman with a pencil has ever known that a piece of paper was in that room at all.

One of them is folded in half in the inside of a coat on the left.

## What The List Comes To

He stood on the top landing and read the five of them back off his own hand in about nine seconds.

Not one person on that list is anybody’s enemy.

The woman with the pencil has said no six times and been right six times. The clerk may only enter what she is given, and has been right about that every morning for nine years. The boy is right about the board. The woman with the short-handled broom is right about the corner.

The woman who cannot read is right about the eleven months, and about the window, and about everything she has said out loud. And the only person in this city who has ever written down what she said wrote it on a sheet in a laundry and not in a book.

Every one of those five is right.

That is not the difficulty. The difficulty is that a list of people who are right is a list, and he has spent nine years learning what a list does to the person whose name is on it.

And there is a sixth line on that hand and he wrote it last, at about a quarter to twelve, and it is the only one of the six that is about him.

I am not on that list. I am the only person in Ostara entitled to put one question to that office, and I have used it twice in nine years, and about four hundred people in this city have carried something to a counter or a bench or a kerb for nine years and not one of them has ever been able to put a third question to anybody at all.

He stood on the landing and looked at the six for about four seconds and then he did the thing he does when a thing in his hand will not go down anywhere, which is to say it out loud to a room with nothing in it.

“Nine years.”

Four floors of washrooms over him and a stairwell and a bin room and about nine hundred gallons of water carried up four flights by one man for six years, and a landing with nothing on it, and the sound of a building that has not stopped.

“Nine years and five sentences and every one of the five belongs to somebody else, and there are about four hundred people in this city carrying a piece of paper who could be told what this office holds about them in about nine seconds, and not one of them is going to be asked anything by anybody, and the reason is five refusals and a man who cleans washrooms.”

He went down the four floors and did the third floor, which is what he does.

*Three thousand and sixty-one days. Less three thousand and fifty-five, the twenty-sixth of July of this year, is six, and a man wrote five sentences on the back of his own right hand at about ten to twelve on a landing with no water on it, and every person in every one of the five is right, and none of them is anybody’s enemy.*

*Less three thousand and twenty-nine, the thirtieth of June of this year, is thirty-two, and the thirty-fourth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirty-two of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is six hundred and forty-one days, which is twenty-one months and eleven days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
