# The Thirty-Fifth Sheet

Three thousand and one hundred and thirteen was the twenty-fourth of September and the thirtieth is day three thousand and one hundred and nineteen, and three thousand and one hundred and nineteen less three thousand and one hundred and thirteen is six, and thirty less twenty-four is six by the month count, and it is the last working day of that month and of the quarter.

## About Half Past Two, And A Sheet Squared Twice

The thirty-fifth sheet went onto the table under the window at about half past two and she squared it against the wood in about three seconds, and then she squared it again about four minutes later, which is what she does at every quarter-end since the thirtieth of March and which neither of them has mentioned.

About four hundred and nineteen lines stand under the head of it, each with a date against it and an hour against the date and no figure and no name.

Under those, the second column, with four lines in it under two printed words that say **carried by**.

Under that, four sentences at the foot.

Under those, six lines in this office’s hand with the fourth of them struck from the first letter to the last and a man’s name set under the striking, and two inches of nothing below that.

And under the four sentences and above the striking and in no column anywhere on that sheet, four lines that are new since the first of February, and a man’s name under each of them.

## The Figure At The Head Of It

Eight hundred and fifty-two.

It has held that value since the thirtieth of March of the fifth year and has not shifted once since, and not one person in this city has the standing to shift it, and there never has been one.

This is the count of sheets. It is unmoved across nineteen sheets, counting the nineteenth onward as the nineteen that have carried that number since it was put there, and this afternoon's is the thirty-fifth.

And it is unmoved across eighteen quarters, which is the count of quarters, and it is a different count and it is also right, because a quarter here is a quarter-end later than that thirtieth of March and one has fallen every ninety days since. Three thousand and one hundred and nineteen less one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine is one thousand six hundred and twenty, and that is eighteen times ninety exactly.

The two figures disagree by one, and a reader at a rail is going to be handed both of them, so the page has to say which one it is doing.

## Four Lines, And Three Of Them Are Instructions

The first is the thirtieth of March. When a person says a thing at that counter, this office is to write down what that person said, in the words that person used, with a date against it and an hour against the date, on the standing sheet, and not in the second column.

The second is the tenth of June. When a person brings a paper to that counter, this office is to tell that person, in that room, what this office holds about them, and nothing else, and not to improve it.

The third is nine words in a man’s own pencil that a man said out loud at that counter on the nineteenth of July in front of about nine people, and it is a line and it is not an instruction, and it is on that page because a clerk may only enter what she is given.

The fourth is the sixteenth of August. Whatever a person says in answer to what this office has told them is to be entered in the same row, under what they were told, in the words that person used, with a date against it and an hour against it, and a row may not be taken from.

Three of those four are decisions this office made about itself and one of them is a record of a man, and the order they stand in is the order they went in.

The first two went out on the sheet of the thirtieth of June. The third and the fourth have never been on a page that went anywhere.

## The Flat File, And Taking It Out Himself

He had it out of the flat file at about ten past nine on the morning of the thirtieth and he carried it himself, and not one person in this city is entitled to carry it and not one person has ever offered to.

The flat file is a long shallow drawer in a room off a third court and it holds thirty-five sheets in nine years and nothing else, and each one of them is the same length and about eleven feet when it is open, and the top two hundred of the lines on any of them are the same forty lines, because no person has ever gone up further.

He put it on the table under the window at about a quarter to three and did not square it, because that is her job and has been for four quarter-ends, and she squared it at half past two and then again four minutes later.

He had it in his hand for about nine minutes that afternoon and he did not read it and he did not look at the foot of it, and both of those things were on purpose, because a clerk squares a sheet twice at about half past two and a man who has been putting one of them into a book for nine years does not read it first.

## About Four Of The Eleven

He knew three of them.

A woman who has been in eleven times in eleven months and never once asked to be told anything, who comes up four flights and says a sentence and goes down again. A man who was given his sentence by somebody else at a kitchen table before he came. And a boy of about seventeen who comes with his mother and stands at the rail and does not go in.

None of the three can read it. He has known that about two of them for eleven weeks and about the third one since the fourteenth of August, and the only reason he knows any of it is that a clerk reads it out loud to them in a corridor about four feet wide.

They stood at that rail on the last working day of the quarter and listened to the whole of the bottom of a page, which is the only time in the year that happens.

## At About Ten Minutes To Five

There is a place in that passage where you can see the rail, the desk and the window behind it and not be in the way of anybody at it, and he has stood in it four times in nine years, and he stood in it at about ten minutes to five this afternoon.

Eleven people were at the rail.

Not four. Eleven, which is what stands there at this hour on the last working day of a quarter, and which has been what stands there four times a year since the eighth of October of the first year.

The man behind the desk did the thing he does. He began at the top of the foot of that sheet and worked down it, and he went over the striking without slowing, and he read the four lines of the column as though each of them were an address, because that is what is written under those two words.

Then he came to the four new lines and he read all four of them, in the order they stand, and he gave the July one the same weight as the other three, because it is nine words on a page and a man behind a counter reads what is on the page.

## The Two That Have Never Been On A Page That Went Out

The ninth sentence of the foot of it is the one about a building off a third court keeping the stone at the foot of a wall, and it has never been read out loud in that passage by anybody before this afternoon.

The tenth is about nine feet higher up the same sheet and it is the denial, and it has been read about four hundred times.

He read both. He read the nine words in a man's own hand in the voice he reads a page in, and he read the denial straight after it without a pause, and the passage gave back about half of the first and most of the second.

## What One Of The Eleven Said

A man on the end of the rail said, “That building is on the third court.”

“It is not on the rail,” said the counterman. “It is on the page. I read what is on the page.”

“He said a building.”

“He read a building and a stone and a wall off a page, and then he read the other line, and both of them are on the page and both of them are in the same hand and I could not tell you one of them from the other except that one of them is about nine feet above the other.”

“Read the second one again.”

The counterman read the denial again and gave it to all eleven, and about four people at that rail did not stop arguing about the first one while he did it.

## What The Man Under The Window Said Afterwards

They went back up the lane at about five minutes to five and the counterman came out from under the window and squared the sheet against the edge of the rail with his thumb, which he does every time.

“Four lines have come off that page since the year began,” he said. “I have read all four of them this afternoon and I could not tell you one thing about any of them, and I am not going to be told, and I am not going to be asked.”

“I am not going to ask.”

“I know you are not,” said the counterman. “You have asked me two questions in nine years and one of them was in August and you carried it five days for that one.”

He put his thumb along the edge of the rail.

“And I read the one about a building before the one that says it is not connected to anything, because that is the order they stand in, and a man who reads a page reads it the way it stands.” He looked down the passage. “And two of the eleven have gone up that lane saying the first one. I did not choose which one they kept and I was not permitted to choose and I would not have chosen.”

*Three thousand and one hundred and nineteen days. Less three thousand and one hundred and thirteen, the twenty-fourth of September of this year, is six, and the thirty-fifth sheet was squared at about half past two and squared again about four minutes later and went down two floors and stood face up on a rail at four, and a man who is paid nothing read the whole of the bottom of it to eleven people including two lines that had never been on a page that went out.*

*Less three thousand and twenty-nine, the thirtieth of June of this year, is ninety, and the thirty-fourth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last ninety of them and came down off it at four this afternoon, and the thirty-fifth went up in its place and stands until the thirtieth of December.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is six hundred and ninety-nine days, which is twenty-three months and nine days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
