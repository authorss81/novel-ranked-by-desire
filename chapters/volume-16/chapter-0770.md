# Read Back At The Counter

Three thousand and two was the third of June and the tenth is day three thousand and nine, and three thousand and nine less three thousand and two is seven, and ten less three is seven by the month count.

## What He Had By The Ninth

He did not write it down.

That is the first thing about the ninth of June that is not true of every other decision he has ever made in this building, and he has made about nine hundred of them, and not one of the nine hundred has been carried in his head for two days and then said out loud in an empty passage to a man who is paid nothing before it went into a book.

It came out of the nineteenth of May and out of nothing else.

Two rows in two books about four hundred yards apart, nine days apart, both in a clerk’s hand, both with a date and an hour against them. One of them true. One of them about forty words of a thing that had never happened, said out loud in a corridor four feet wide in front of about nine people, and entered in the second woman’s own words, and correct to be entered.

Neither of them can be taken from. Neither of them can be improved. And there is not one form anywhere in this city on which either of those two women can be shown what a book says about her.

That had been true since the ninth of May. It had been true before the ninth of May as well, except that before the ninth of May there was nothing in any book about either of them at all.

## A Man Behind A Counter Under A Window

He went down at about half past four on the ninth, before it was entered, because he wanted a man who is paid nothing to hear a sentence from a person and not off a piece of paper.

The counter is deal and it is under a window in a passage off Cordwain, and the man who runs it has been there nine years, and about nine people a week stop at the rail at the end of it, and he has never once been asked what he thinks about what is on the page in front of him.

“I have got something for you and it is not on the paper yet,” he said. “It goes on the thirtieth of June at four, and you are going to read the bottom of that page out loud to whoever is standing there, because that is what you do.”

“That is what I do,” said the counterman. “Go on.”

“You are going to read one more line than you read at the end of March. You are not going to know what it means. I am not allowed to tell you, and there is not one other person in this city entitled to tell you, and in about four years there will not be one either.”

The counterman looked at the desk for about four seconds.

“I have been reading the bottom of that page to about nine people a week for nine years,” he said, “and there has never once been anything on it I could not read out, and I have never once been told what any of it means, and I have not asked.”

“I am telling you that you shall not be told.”

“Then that is the arrangement, and it is the same as the other four, and I will read it.” He put his thumb on the edge of the desk. “The only thing I want is to hear it out loud once before it is on the paper. There is not one person in that passage at four o’clock on the thirtieth of June and not one person in there now. You can say it now if you have got it.”

## The Sentence He Said In An Empty Passage

He said it.

*From the tenth of June of this year, when a person brings a paper to that counter, this office is to tell that person, in that room, what this office holds about them, and nothing else, and not to improve it.*

He said it in the ordinary voice, and the passage took it and gave about half of it back.

“That is the whole of it,” said the counterman.

“That is the whole of it.”

“Then I can read that,” said the counterman, “because I have read the other four lines at the bottom of that page about nine hundred times and none of them ever had a date in them like that one has, and a date is the only thing on a page I have ever known for certain what it is.”

He went back up afterwards, and the long stair in that building is the wrong one for a chest that has been spent since the fourth year, and he took it anyway, because he had about nine minutes to be at the counter on the third of the three and he was going to use them going up.

## What It Closes

Nine years.

About four hundred and thirty rows in a bound Register in which there was one empty field, and about four hundred and nineteen lines on a sheet that goes out four times a year to about two thousand counters, and a drawer shut under an instruction with his own name under it, and a column with four lines in it, and twenty-six things on a bundle that nothing is going to be done with.

And a page that a man who is paid nothing reads out loud to about nine people a week so that a claim waits to be told.

All of it rested on one thing, and the one thing was not a rule.

A person could not be told what any of it said.

Not one person in this city had ever had to be protected from that, because not one person had ever been hurt by it. There has never been a single citizen of Ostara who has known whether there was anything written about them or not, because the answer has always been that there is not going to be anything written about anybody in a building with no door onto a street.

From today there is going to be, and somebody is going to be told it, out loud, in a room, by a clerk, in words they used.

And there will be about nine of those a week for as long as this building stands, and not one of them can be asked to take it back.

## He Gives It To Her Before She Has Read It

He did not read it back to her first and he did not hold it in his own hand for four minutes while he worked out what it cost.

He put it on the table under the window face down, with the date at the top, and he said, “That is the tenth of June. Read it back to me and tell me whether I have got it right, and then it is yours and it is not coming off the page.”

There was a minute in that room in which he could have stopped and put his hand on the sheet and held it there while he said the four sentences that would have let her refuse it before it was entered.

He did not do that. That is the whole of what he did on the tenth of June and it is the first thing he has ever put into that office that he did not price first.

She read it back standing up, which she does not do.

Not one word of it came back different from the way it went in. She took about eleven seconds over it and then laid the pen down across the book on top of the last thing she had entered and left it there, and stood looking at the shelf of the room as though the shelf were going to tell her something.

“Now say the rest of it,” said Lin Hao, “and say it now, and I have earned the right to hear it.”

“You have earned nothing of the sort,” said Wenna Raut. “You have handed me an instruction you did not price, on a morning you knew what day it was, in a room where you have spent nine years making sure I was never handed anything that had not been through your own hands first. Say something about that first.”

He did not say anything about it.

## What It Costs, Said By The Clerk

“From the thirtieth of March I have been the hand and the mouth,” said Wenna Raut. “From the tenth of June I am a person who says things about people out loud, and I do not know whether they are true, and I will know by about the end of the summer because that is how long it takes for about nine of them to come back in with a different version.”

“That is not the whole of it.”

“No. Here is the whole of it and I have had it since about four o’clock yesterday.” She squared the book to the edge of the table. “For nine years I have been a person who writes down what she is given and cannot be wrong. That has been the best position anybody in this building has ever held, and I built it out of about four hundred and thirty rows of other people’s words, and it is the reason this room has never once had to apologise to anybody.”

“From today you say them.”

“From today I say them, out loud, to their faces, in corridors about four feet wide with nine people in them, about nine times a week, for as long as this building stands. And I will be wrong about some of them. Every wrong one goes on a page that goes to about two thousand counters four times a year and cannot be improved and cannot be taken from, and I will be the mouth of it.”

She picked the pen up.

“Put your hand down. I am going to write it on the sheet and it is the second line this office has added to the foot of one of its own pages in nine years, and it will be on a rail in that passage at four o’clock on the thirtieth of June, and a man who does not know what it means will read it to about eleven people.”

“I am not going to be able to take it back.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut. “Neither am I, and that is the difference between the two of us and it has taken you nine years to hear it.”

## The Second Line In The Office’s Hand

She took her time over it, which she does not, and the date went at the top of the line and his name at the foot of it, and then she read the whole of it back in the order it stood on the page, including the date.

And then she squared the sheet to the edge of the table and left it there.

The second line stood under the first one, under the four sentences and above the striking and in no column anywhere on it, and about nine feet below the first there is a set of six lines with the fourth of them struck from the first letter to the last and a name set under the striking, and below that the paper stops and there are two inches of nothing.

Twenty days and it goes out.

It has not gone anywhere yet. A page goes out four times a year at four o’clock on the last working day of a quarter, and this one goes out at four o’clock on the thirtieth of June, and a clerk will carry it down two floors and put it face up on a rail.

A man will read the whole of the bottom of it to whoever is standing there, and one of the things he will read is a sentence that neither of them is going to be allowed to explain.

*Three thousand and nine days. Less three thousand and two, the third of June of this year, is seven, and a man said a sentence out loud in an empty passage to a man who is paid nothing and did not improve it, and a clerk entered it in this office’s hand with a man’s name under it, and it is the second new line at the foot of a page, and there is nothing in this city on which it may be taken back.*

*Less two thousand nine hundred and thirty-nine, the thirtieth of March of this year, is seventy, and the thirty-third sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy of them, and the thirty-fourth goes up at four on the thirtieth of June and does not come down until the thirtieth of September.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is five hundred and eighty-nine days, which is nineteen months and nineteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
