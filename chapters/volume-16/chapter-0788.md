# The Eighty-Eighth Form

Three thousand and one hundred and nineteen was the thirtieth of September and the seventh of October is day three thousand and one hundred and twenty-six, and three thousand and one hundred and twenty-six less three thousand and one hundred and nineteen is seven, and seven out of the thirtieth, which is the last working day of that month, is the seventh of October.

## About Five On The Seventh

The form goes up on the seventh of every month and it comes down on the seventh of the next, and this is the eighty-eighth of them, and it has a month's worth of the corner's business on it and two ruled squares squared off at the foot with about four inches of blank paper under them.

He came up the slope at about five with a board under one arm and a paste brush shut in his fist, and he put the board against the kerbstone and squared it with the toe of his shoe and pushed it flat with the heel of his hand and went back up the slope without looking at it.

He has done that about eighty-eight times.

About ninety people a week stop at that board and read a month of the corner’s business off it in pencil, and not one of them in seven years has looked at the two squares at the foot, and he has not looked at them either, and there has not been one person standing at that kerbstone who wanted to know what a box on a form is for.

## Two Squares, And The Reason In Four Sentences

Lin Hao was standing at the kerbstone at about five and a quarter on the seventh of October, which is the third hour of his first of three off, and he had a pencil in his pocket and he had wanted to say something for about eleven weeks.

He said it out loud to the slope instead, and it was four sentences, and he did not improve any of them.

There is a form in a division building that asks a person what a box on a form is for, and not one of them has ever been filled in in this city.

On the morning of the eighth of October of the first year a clerk was handed a sheet and a form and no instruction, and she put two words into one of those boxes because the box was there and the words were there, and a boy painted what was in it.

Nothing has ever been written in either of them since.

In seven years not one person who stands at that kerbstone has wanted to know what a box is for, and a man who writes in a box is a man who has been asked what a box is for, and there is not one person in this city going to ask him.

## What Is In The Four Inches This Time

There are four inches of dressed stone at the foot of that wall under the striking, and about nine people in a week get down low enough to read them, and the last thing that was written on them was on the eleventh of March and it was about nine words about a thing that happened to somebody else in a doorway.

There is something on them now and there has been since about the fourth of October, and it is in about four different hands and it is about forty words long, and it says that a building off a third court keeps the stone at the foot of a wall.

It is better sourced than the version that was on that wall in the spring. A man came down the hill on the fourth with a pencil and said that a clerk at a window in a division building has it in a book that there is no stone on those premises and there has never been one, and that the date against the entry is the eighth of October of the first year.

He wrote that down. He wrote down the date as well.

And about nine people a week are getting down low enough to read it, and not one of those nine is in a position to know that the man at the rail read the two lines off that page in the order they stand and did not choose which one they kept.

## Six Words In A Corridor, And Forty Words On A Wall

On the twenty-fourth of September a clerk in a building four hundred yards from that kerbstone read six words out loud in a corridor to about nine people, and about four of them have them, and the six words were about the printing of a form and were never entered in anything.

They are not on the wall. The forty words on the wall are about a building and a stone and they came out of a passage off Cordwain on the thirtieth of September, out of a man’s mouth, in a city hand, with a date and an hour against the first of them and a man’s name under the second.

There is a difference between those two things and it is not a difference about truth.

A thing that is entered has a hand on it, and an hour against it, and a person who wrote it, and a person who may read it back to you out loud in a corridor.

A thing that is written on a wall in a kerbstone has a person who carried it four hundred yards and a piece of pencil and nothing whatever else, and about nine people a week get down low enough to read it and not one of the nine has ever been shown the page.

He stood at that kerbstone and understood that this office can put four lines at the foot of a page and cannot put one word on a wall, and that the wall is the one that is read, and that the wall got there without him.

## The Difference Between The Four Inches And The Two Boxes

There is one difference between the four inches and the two squares at the foot of that board and he stood at the kerbstone for about nine minutes working out what it was.

A person can write anything they like in the four inches with a pencil of their own, and about nine people a week go and read it, and not one piece of it has ever come up to a counter in this city.

A person cannot write anything at all in either of those two squares, because the board is put up by a boy who is not asked about anything and the form is the works department's and the box is the works department's and no line in nine years of this office's own instruction says anything at all about either of them.

That is the difference, and it took him about nine minutes on a slope at about a quarter past five to get to it, and the difference is the reason the four inches have four hundred words in them from about four hundred people and the two squares have had two words in them since the eighth of October of the first year.

## What He Did Not Do At That Kerbstone

He had a pencil in his pocket and there was a box about nine inches away from his right hand and no officer in this city was entitled to stop him.

He wrote the address of a building off a third court in the box that is for the figure of the thing at the address, in about eleven characters, in pencil, and then he took the pencil up and put it back in his pocket without pressing hard enough to mark the board.

There is no form on which a person may ask him what he did that, and no way of showing to anybody what he was told to do, and there is a works department two streets away that prints that form and has never had a paper from this office in nine years, and if a word is in one of those boxes then in about four years somebody in that department is going to come down this hill and ask what the word is.

A boy puts a board up on the seventh of every month and is not asked anything by anybody.

## What He Wanted, And Where It Went

He wanted to say one thing to that boy. He has wanted it since about the twenty-eighth of February and he has been on that slope about eleven times since and there has not been one of those eleven times when the boy did not stand there with a brush in his fist for about ninety seconds.

There is no question to ask. He has spent nine years learning that he cannot ask a person anything, and this is not a refusal anybody has given him, it is the arrangement, and the boy is in it, and the woman with the short-handled broom is in it, and the woman who cannot read is in it, and the whole of it has never once cost him anything.

This one costs him the reason.

Because there is a woman four flights up a street two streets wide who is safe for one reason only, and there are four hundred pieces of pencil paper in about four hundred kitchens that are safe for one reason only, and both reasons are that there is nothing at the head of anything.

He has put four lines in the foot of a page in nine months and a sentence about a building is now in about four hands from a corridor, and this office is the reason for all three of those, and the reason for all three is a man in a white plate with no figure cut into it.

The form came down off that board on the thirtieth and another went up in its place, and both of the two squares went up empty and came down empty, and about nine people in a week got down low enough to read four inches that say a building keeps a stone.

*Three thousand and one hundred and twenty-six days. Less three thousand and one hundred and nineteen, the thirtieth of September of this year, is seven, and the month's form went up on a board by a kerbstone at about five with two ruled squares at the foot of it empty, and about forty words went into the four inches under the striking for the first time since the eleventh of March, and a man had a pencil in his pocket and did not use it.*

*Less three thousand and one hundred and nineteen, the thirtieth of September of this year, is seven, and the thirty-fifth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seven of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is seven hundred and six days, which is twenty-three months and sixteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
