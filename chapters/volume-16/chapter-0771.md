# A Claim With Two Days On It

Three thousand and nine was the tenth of June and the seventeenth is day three thousand and sixteen, and three thousand and sixteen less three thousand and nine is seven, and seventeen less ten is seven by the month count.

## Two Days On One Piece Of Pencil Paper

He came up the stair at about a quarter to eleven with a piece of pencil paper in his hand and there were two days on it and both of them were in his own writing.

The first was underneath a line, and the line was underneath nothing, and the first was the day he went down to the bottom of a wall at the foot of a hill and put pencil against dressed stone.

The second was at the top, in the same hand, and there was a ruled stamp across the top of it that had come out of a division building about nine feet deep, and the second was the day a clerk at a window did something to a copy of the thing.

“It is the same thing,” the man said. “It is a room with a door on a court and there is a name against it and it came out of a division building, and those are two days and they are eleven weeks apart and they are both about the same room.”

Wenna Raut did not take the paper off him.

## What A Clerk Is Entitled To Ask

She gets one question of the morning and she had not spent it, and she spent it here and she said what she was spending it on before she spent it, which is what she does when a question is going to cost something.

“Whose hand is the second day in.”

“Mine,” said the man. “I wrote it down from what the window told me. There was a man in a queue who wrote both of them on a copy and I took the number off the copy because the copy is what I had.”

That is the whole of what was said about it and it took about nine seconds and both of the days are in his own hand and one of the two is a day somebody handed him.

## What The Second Day Came From

He said it because she asked, and she asked because a counter in this city is a thing that knows where a thing came from, and this is the part of it that takes about nine seconds and never varies.

He wrote the first day in a lair off Cordwain in about October of last year. He wrote it at the bottom of his own paper and he put a day against it because a person puts a day against a thing they have done.

He took the paper up four flights to a bench off a street two streets wide because that is what about four hundred people have done with a paper in this city, and the woman at that bench carried it on.

Then in May he took a copy of it to a window in a division building because a man in a queue had told him in a kitchen that the whole of it came out of a division and that a division has a day on it.

The clerk at that window looked at it for about nine seconds and stamped the foot of it and wrote the day of the stamp beside the day he had already written, and said that the day there was the day the division had it, and did not say what else that day was.

“And I put that one underneath yours because it was the day a building answered me,” said the man. “That is why it is underneath. It is not a better day. It is the day somebody at a window wrote on my paper.”

## A Woman With A Pencil Told Him In June

He had it before he came up the stair, because a woman in a lair four streets away had said it to him in June and had said it twice and had not wanted to be thanked for it.

A woman keeps an index up four flights of a stair two streets wide, and it may not be cited by anybody in any division in this city, and it has never once been cited in six years, and she has said no to him six times about nine different things and has been right six times.

About nine people a month go up that stair with a piece of pencil paper and carry it on again. On the ninth of June this man was the eighth of those about nine, and she told him in about nine seconds that the second day was not his day, and she told him why, and she wrote nothing down.

She had never seen the page. She has never in her life seen the page and she has never been sent anything by anybody, and that is the reason she is able to say a thing like that out loud to a stranger about nine times a month and be right about nine times a month.

There are four places in this city where a person can be told something about a piece of paper, and one of them is a bench with a pencil on it that is not an instrument and cannot be cited, and a woman who keeps it has been sitting there for six years saying *that is not your day* to strangers in about nine seconds.

## Three Days, And Not One Column That Says Which Is Which

There are three days in this city and they are not the same object and a man cannot tell them apart by looking at the number.

The day the thing happened. The day a man made a copy of it at his own table. And the day a clerk at a window in a division building did a thing to that copy.

And there is a fourth that has only existed since the eighth of October of the first year, which is the day a piece of paper came through a door in a building with no door onto a street and was written into a row with a date against it and an hour against the date and nothing whatever after the hour.

For nine years that fourth day was the only one this office held, and a man could keep it on his paper without it ever competing with the other three, because the other three did not come here.

They come here now, and they have been coming here since the thirtieth of March, and about nine people a week stand at that counter with a day they cannot get rid of.

She entered his paper as received, with the date at the top of the row and the hour against the date and nothing whatever after the hour.

“It is the seventeenth of June,” she said. “It is not the ninth. It is not the day you went down to the wall and it is not the day the window stamped it. Those two are yours and this one is mine, and there are three of them and I have entered one.”

“Then write the other two.”

## Two Days Entered Under The Four Sentences

He said it in the ordinary voice and about nine people were in that corridor and it went down.

The instruction of the thirtieth of March says that when a person says a thing at that counter this office writes it down in the words that person used, with a date against it and an hour against the date, on the standing sheet and not in the second column.

So there are now two lines under the four sentences on the sheet that goes out at four o’clock on the thirtieth of September, in this office’s hand, with a date and an hour against them, and neither of the two lines says anything about a person.

It goes out in thirteen days and about two thousand counters hold it.

“What you are carrying out of this room,” said Wenna Raut, “is two dates in a stranger’s handwriting under four sentences about this city. It is not a claim. It is not about a room. It is the only row in nine years of that book that is about a day and about nothing else, and there is no field on any form in this city that will tell me which of those two is the day and which is the making of it.”

“I know which one is which.”

“You know which one you were told,” said Wenna Raut. “That is a different sentence and you have been carrying it up a stair to find out whether anybody here would agree with you, and I have written down what you said, and I could not have written one word less of it, and the page will carry it in thirteen days.”

## Where He Is Going With Two Days

He did not go up a third court with it, because the third court is where this office is, and there are four flights in it and a door at the top of them.

He went down to a window off that court where a clerk has been sitting for about five long years and has never once been wrong about what a counter is.

There is a magistrate’s counter in that building and the clerk at it is entitled to exactly one question a day about a thing and not two, and he reads the papers where they lay, and he did not let the man sit down.

The man put two dates on the wood in front of him, one under the other, and he said that they were about the same room and that one of them was the day the room did it and the other one was the day a copy was made, and he asked which of the two was the kind of day a person can act on.

The clerk read both papers where they lay, which is what a counter is for, and then he slid the second one back a full two inches across the wood towards the man who had carried it in.

“The day here is the day you wrote the copy,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Then it is not the day of the room.”

“No.”

The man put his thumb on the edge of the counter and said the thing he had come down four flights to say, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and there were about four people behind him in that corridor.

“Then it is not the room either,” said the clerk at the window, “and there is not one question left today, and you have used none of it on the thing you came for, and I am going to ask you about the first one.”

*Three thousand and sixteen days. Less three thousand and nine, the tenth of June of this year, is seven, and a man carried two days on one piece of pencil paper up a stair, and a clerk entered one of them and wrote the other two under the four sentences in the man’s own words, and he has gone down four flights to find out which kind of day a number is.*

*Less two thousand nine hundred and thirty-nine, the thirtieth of March of this year, is seventy-seven, and the thirty-third sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-seven of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is five hundred and ninety-six days, which is nineteen months and twenty-six days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
