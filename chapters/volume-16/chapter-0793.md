## Two Rooms Above A Bakery

## What Binds Without A Paper

“You made a promise once,” said Lin Hao, when the bread was gone and the kettle was on.

Petra did not look up from the stove. “I have made a good many. Which one.”

“The one you have been inside for eleven years and cannot produce,” said Lin Hao. “The one with nothing written behind it.”

She turned round then. She had a pale band of skin at her left wrist where a cord had been worn years back and a small dark mole under her right ear, and she had had both since before he knew her and neither had ever been written down anywhere.

“Who told you about that,” she said.

“You did,” said Lin Hao. “About six years back. On a night shift. You said you were bound and you did not know by whom, and then you never said it again, and I have never asked you for it since.”

Petra leaned back against the blanket chest and folded her arms, not to keep him off but to keep herself warm through a dress gone thin at the shoulders.

“A promise binds without a paper,” she said. “That is the whole of it. No hand signs it and no hand files it and no clerk reads it back to you in a corridor. You say it to a person and it holds your body from that hour.”

“How is it discharged,” said Lin Hao.

“The same way,” said Petra. “Without a paper. You say to the person you said it to that it is done, and it is done, and not one other person in Ostara can do it for you.”

“What if you cannot find the person,” said Lin Hao.

“Then it stands,” said Petra. “Eleven years. Twenty. Until one of you is dead and even then not quite, because a promise is not a debt and death does not close it the way a book closes a row.”

## The Only Person Who Can Witness One

Lin Hao pulled one of the cold chairs out and turned it round and straddled it with his arms along the back, because his chest had been spent since the fourth year and standing too long in cold rooms still cost him breath he did not like to waste in front of anyone.

“Can anyone witness it,” he said.

Petra shook her head. “Only a person you have to be able to talk to. That is the rule of it, though I was never taught it by anyone. A stranger cannot witness a promise because a stranger cannot be spoken to the way the person it binds has to be spoken to. A clerk cannot enter it because she was not spoken to at all. A man at a rail cannot read it because it was never on any page.”

She came away from the chest and stood in front of the stove with the rug under her feet and the cold light from the shutter slats across her shoulders.

“I have tried,” she said. “About four years back I went to a woman who keeps an index and asked her to write it down for me, and she said a sheet is not a person and sent me home.”

“About two years back I asked a woman in a ward to take a share of it out of me, and she said what is taken from a body is not itemised and the person it is taken from is never told. I left before she could ask me anything more.”

Lin Hao felt anger come up in him, not at her but at the shape of it, at an instrument that could hold a woman for eleven years and leave her nowhere to put it down.

“Do you remember who made it,” he said.

“No,” said Petra. “I remember the month. I remember the light in the room. I do not remember the face, and I do not remember the words, only that I said yes and meant it, and that I have been meaning it ever since.”

She laughed once, short and without any meanness in it.

“That is the ordinary condition of every instrument in Ostara and not a thing that was done to me,” she said. “Every claim in that office is a sentence somebody said with nothing behind it. Every line on that sheet is somebody mouth with a date against it. Mine is only older and has no date, which makes it cleaner than most.”

## What She Wanted That Evening

The kettle boiled and she made tea in two thick cups and put sugar in his without asking, the way she had done for years, and they drank it standing up because the chairs were still cold.

## What Cannot Be Discharged In Front Of Anyone Else

Afterward they lay under the wool cover with the cold light at the edges of the shutter and her head on his unspent shoulder and his hand in her hair, which she allowed because she had asked for everything that evening and he had done nothing she had not asked for.
