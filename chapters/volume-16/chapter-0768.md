# A Woman Wrote It Down

Two thousand nine hundred and eighty-eight was the nineteenth of May and the twenty-sixth is day two thousand nine hundred and ninety-five, and two thousand nine hundred and ninety-five less two thousand nine hundred and eighty-eight is seven, and twenty-six less nineteen is seven by the month count.

## A Man Who Came Up With A Sheet And Said Something

He came up the four flights in the middle of May with a piece of pencil paper in his hand and put it on the third table the way about four hundred people have put one there, and he did not ask for it to be carried up a stair to anywhere.

Then he said a sentence out loud at that bench, which had not happened in that room in nine years, and it was about eleven words long.

“The bottom two inches went up on the eighteenth and there was nothing on them.”

“You went down.”

“I went down on the fourth of this month and on the eleventh and on the eighteenth,” he said. “Nothing on any of them. There has been nothing on them since before the new year and the last thing on them was the eleventh of March and that was a man in a laundry writing nine words about a doorway, and that is the last thing anybody in this city has written down about themselves.”

He put the pencil paper on the bench and squared it against the wood.

“I am not the only one who has noticed and I am not going to be the one who says it to a man behind a window. I am saying it to a woman with a pencil.”

## The Fourth Column

Perla Quist had the fourth sheet out and it has had a heading on it since the twenty-first of February of the eighth year, and the heading says what a page carried on the day the page was made.

That sheet has about two hundred lines in it, and nothing else in Ostara can set a claim against the day a thing happened, and it may not be cited in any division in this city, and it has never once been cited, and she has said so to no person at all.

Every one of those two hundred lines is a copy. A man comes up four flights with a copy of what the counterman read out of the foot of a page, and she takes it and writes the day on it and puts it under her heading. About the same number come into that room on their own, and she copies them herself, in the room, from the rail, with the book in her hand and about nine people standing behind her.

A copy of a page is not a claim about a day. It is the same words on a different piece of paper in a different building, and anybody who wanted to check one of them would have to go and stand at a rail and ask a man who is paid nothing whether he said it, and he does not remember, because he has read the bottom of the same page to about nine people a week for nine years.

That is why the column may not be cited. Not because she will not say so. Because there is nothing in it that could be checked by anybody.

There is one other thing on the wall of that room and it has been there since the seventh year, and it is a copy of a counterman’s nine-year ledger in a pigeonhole, and every line in that copy is one of the same two hundred lines with the date added, and it exists because about four people in this city asked her at about the same time what would happen to the day on a line if the page went somewhere she could not see.

She made the copy. She has never shown it to anybody. It is the only thing in that room that is a record of a record, and it is the reason about four hundred people still trust her, and she has never once been thanked for it.

## The First Thing In It That Is Not A Copy

The eleven words at that bench went into that sheet in her own hand on her own paper and they were not a copy of anything.

He looked at them.

“That is the first thing that has gone in that column in two years that is not a copy of the foot of a page,” he said. “It is a thing a man said out loud at that bench and there is no page anywhere in this city with it on.”

“No.”

“Then it is the only true line in that sheet that is not somebody else’s handwriting.”

“It is the only line in that sheet that anybody could check, if anybody could come and ask me what day I wrote it on and who said it to me,” said Perla Quist. “And about four hundred people in this city would be able to do that, and not one of them has ever asked me a question about anything, and that is not modesty. That is the arrangement and it has held for six years.”

## She Would Not Rule A Heading

“You have a heading over it.”

“I have a heading over a copy of a page,” said Perla Quist. “I have had that heading on my own paper since the twenty-first of February of the eighth year and I ruled it myself and I have never put a second heading over anything in this room and I am not going to start on the twenty-sixth of May.”

“There is going to be more of it.”

“There is going to be more of it by about the thirtieth of this month, and I have known that for about three weeks.” She squared the ruler against the bench. “The count of it is the same two numbers it is every month, because there is one afternoon it was said out loud on and a man with a slate is right about it, and about nine people come up those stairs a month for exactly that reason.”

“Give me the sheet.”

“No.”

“You did not have to say that so fast.”

“I have been asked for things in that room five times since the seventh year and I have said no five times and I am not going to do it in the ordinary way this morning,” said Perla Quist. “I am going to say something else first, and I am going to say it out loud before you ask me about it, because you were going to ask me about it inside four minutes and I would rather you had it in the ordinary way.”

## The Reason, Out Loud, Before Anybody Asked

“A heading is a thing somebody can ask about,” said Perla Quist. “That is all a heading is. It is a thing a person can put in front of another person and say *what is under this*. And a thing somebody can ask about is a thing somebody can ask about twice, and after the second time it is a thing somebody can be asked to stop.”

She did not pick the pencil up and she had been holding it for about a minute.

“There are about nine sheets under this table and about two hundred lines under a heading I ruled myself, and a copy of a man’s nine-year ledger in a pigeonhole, and there is not one instrument in Ostara that can reach any of it, and that is the only reason it has never been taken off me.”

“If you put a heading over the other kind, then in about four years somebody in a division is going to write the word of my heading on a form,” said Perla Quist. “And the form is going to come to me. And on the day that form comes to me I am a woman with two headings instead of a woman with a room.”

“There are about four hundred pieces of pencil paper in this city that are only safe because the woman who carried them is a woman with nothing at the head of anything.”

She put the pencil down across the bench.

“No,” she said. “That is the sixth time and you have not asked me for anything, and I am counting it, because you have come up that stair four times in seven weeks and stood at the end of that bench for nine minutes each time, and I would rather tell you now than in four years.”

## Four Flights Down, With Nothing

He went down four flights with nothing in his hands, which was the third time in seven weeks, and stopped on the second landing for about four minutes.

Somebody in a laundry four streets away has written down, in her own hand, on her own paper, in a column that may not be cited, that a man got down to the bottom two inches of a wall on the eighteenth of May and found nothing on them, and that the last thing on them was the eleventh of March.

That is the thing he has been standing at a kerb on his own time for six weeks trying to establish, and it has not been established, and it has been established, and both of those are true.

It was established by a stranger at a bench in about eleven words. It is in a room four flights up that no officer of this city may enter, in a book that cannot be cited, under no heading at all. The man who said it will never know it was written down. The woman who wrote it will never read it out to anybody.

He did not ask her for the sheet and she did not offer it and neither of them remarked on it.

There is not one form anywhere in this city on which a man of rank one who cleans washrooms may ask a woman with a pencil for a piece of paper she has written herself, and there has not been one since the seventh year, and she has said no five times and once more on the twenty-sixth of May, and he is not going to make it six.

*Two thousand nine hundred and ninety-five days. Less two thousand nine hundred and eighty-eight, the nineteenth of May of this year, is seven, and a man said eleven words at a bench about the bottom two inches of a wall and a woman wrote them in a column that may not be cited and refused to rule a heading over them, out loud, before anybody asked her.*

*Less two thousand nine hundred and thirty-nine, the thirtieth of March of this year, is fifty-six, and the thirty-third sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-six of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is five hundred and seventy-five days, which is nineteen months and five days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
