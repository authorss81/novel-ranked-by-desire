# The Denial He Cannot Take Back

Three thousand and one hundred and thirty-three was the fourteenth of October and the twentieth is day three thousand and one hundred and thirty-nine, and three thousand and one hundred and thirty-nine less three thousand and one hundred and thirty-three is six, and twenty less fourteen is six by the month count.

## The Whole Of It, Before He Had Written Anything Down

He had it by the sixteenth and he carried it for four days, and it is the fourth instruction this office has made since the first of February and the fifth line in this office’s hand at the foot of one of its own pages, and the July line and the August line are both already on the sheet that went to the rail at four o’clock on the thirtieth of September.

The other four are about a person. What a person says at that counter is written down in the words that person used. A person may be told what this office holds about them. A person may answer what this office told them.

This one is about a denial, and a denial is not a person, and that is the whole difficulty with it and it took him four days to get to the bottom of it and about nine minutes to write down.

On the sixteenth of August of the eighth year this office entered, in its own hand, under the four sentences and above the striking, a sentence saying that no page of this office and no stone in this city is connected to any other, and that a resemblance is not a figure.

It has been face up on a rail in a passage off Cordwain four times a year since, and a man who is paid nothing reads the whole of the bottom of that page to about nine people a week, and he cannot be asked to read less of it.

It has never once been read as a denial. It has been read as nine words about a building and a stone, because that is what it names, and that is the only kind of sentence anybody repeats.

## What It Closes

It closes the life of a denial as a formality.

A formality is a sentence that is on a page and is true and is read out loud about nine times a week to people who take it home, and every one of them remembers the two things it names and not the word that joins them, and in about four months about four people a week in this city will be able to give that sentence to somebody who was not at that rail.

He has known that since the nineteenth of July. He has had four days to do something about it and there is exactly one instrument in this city that can put anything in front of about nine people a week at that rail, and it is the foot of a page, and the only way to use it is to make the denial a line of its own that somebody has to say.

And the only person who says the foot of that page is a man who is paid nothing, who cannot be asked to read less of it, who has never been told what any of it means, and who is going to be asked.

## What It Costs, Said By The Person Who Will Have To Say It

He said it out loud to a clerk at about ten past nine on the morning of the twentieth and he did not price it first, because he has been priced at by better people than him in this room twice and has not forgiven either of them.

Wenna Raut did not pick the pen up.

“Read it to me once,” she said, “and then I am going to say what it costs, and then you are going to give it to me and not hold it.”

He read it to her once.

“It costs me nine times a week, for ever,” said the counterman’s sentence, which he had gone down and taken out of that man’s own mouth at about half past seven that morning, standing at that counter, and had written in his own hand on a piece of pencil paper and had not improved one word of it.

“That is what the man will say,” said Lin Hao, “and he will say it to me, and I am going to put it in the instruction in his words and not in mine, because he is the one who will be standing at that rail.”

## The Fifth Line In The Office's Hand

*From the twentieth of October of this year, the sentence entered on the sixteenth of August of this year is to be read out at that rail every morning to whoever is standing there, by whoever is standing there.*

*Lin Hao.*

She read it back standing up, which she does, in the order it stood on the page, with the date.

And then she did the thing she has done twice, and did not do the third time, and she put the pen across the book.

“No,” she said.

“You said no on the twenty-ninth of April and on the nineteenth of July.”

“I said no on the twenty-ninth of April and on the nineteenth of July about things you wanted to enter,” said Wenna Raut. “This is not that. This is the fourth instruction this office has made in its own hand since the first of February and the fifth line at the foot of that page and three of the other four are about a person and this one is not.”

She squared the book to the edge of the table with both hands.

“And it is the first of the four that makes a man say something he has not been told, out loud, to strangers, every morning, for ever, and it is going to be the same man every morning and there is nothing in this city that will put anybody else at that counter.”

## The Thing He Is Not Going To Say

“There is a part of it you have not asked me about,” said Lin Hao, “and I am going to say it before you do.”

“Say it.”

“By whoever is standing there.” He put two fingers on the cover of the book. “It is not him. That is the whole of the design and it is the only part of it I have thought about for four days, and he is not going to be the only one who says it.”

He did not say the rest of it.

What the rest of it was is that about nine people a week stand at that rail and about four of them have been there more than once, and that in about four months one of those four is going to say that sentence out loud in a city hand to people he does not know, and he has not been told what it means, because not one person in this city is entitled to tell him and there never will be one.

He had decided that in four days and he was not going to say it in that room, because a clerk in that room has said out loud what this office holds about people since the tenth of June, and he was not going to hand her one more sentence with a man's name under it on the same morning.

## What She Says It Costs, In About Nine Seconds

“A claim in a city hand,” said Wenna Raut, “spoken out loud in a passage, about nine times a week, for ever.”

She stopped there.

“That is what it costs the man who is paid nothing and it is the whole of it,” she said, “and I am going to add about nine words of my own because I am the mouth of this office and I have been since the tenth of June, and then I am going to write your line.”

She looked at the shelf as though the shelf were going to tell her something.

“He does not know what it is denying. He has never been told. And in about four years there is going to be a man stand at that rail and ask him what that sentence is denying, and he is going to have to say the words out loud and he will not be able to tell the man he does not know.”

She picked the pen up.

“Give it to me.”

## It Goes Out On The Thirtieth Of December

He gave it to her and she wrote it in about four minutes with the date at the top of the line and his name at the foot of it, and she did not improve one word of it.

It is the fifth line in this office’s hand at the foot of one of its own pages. It stands under the four sentences and above the striking and in no column anywhere on that sheet, and about nine feet below it there is a denial with a man’s name under it, and the page stops two inches below the striking.

It goes out at four o’clock on the thirtieth of December, which is seventy-one days from this morning, to about two thousand counters.

He went down to the passage off Cordwain at about half past ten in the morning on the twentieth of October and there was nothing at the rail, because a page only goes up four times a year, and the counterman was under the window with the shutter down.

He told him. He said it in about nine seconds and he did not improve one word of the man’s sentence, and he did not tell him what it was for.

Then he asked him what he was going to say to the people who would ask him, and the counterman looked at the deal of the counter for about four seconds.

“I shall say that is what it says,” he said. “I have said that once before in nine years, in June, to a woman, and I have not got a better one and I am not going to look for one.”

*Three thousand and one hundred and thirty-nine days. Less three thousand and one hundred and thirty-three, the fourteenth of October of this year, is six, and a denial on that page is to be read out at a rail every morning by whoever is standing there, and a man who is paid nothing said in nine words what it costs him.*

*Less three thousand and one hundred and nineteen, the thirtieth of September of this year, is twenty, and the thirty-fifth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is seven hundred and nineteen days, which is twenty-three months and twenty-nine days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
