# Two Books And A Stair

Two thousand nine hundred and ninety-five was the twenty-sixth of May and the third of June is day three thousand and two, and three thousand and two less two thousand nine hundred and ninety-five is seven, and three less twenty-six is seven by the month count.

## About Four Hundred Yards And Four Flights

There are two books in this city about four hundred yards apart and there is one man alive who has read both.

One of them is a bound Register in a room off a third court, open under a window with a ruler squared along the edge of it and a clerk at the far side of the table. It had about four hundred and thirty rows in it on the first of February and there are about four hundred and seventy in it now, and the difference is pieces of paper that came through a door, and it is a count of rows and not a count of anybody.

The other one is about nine sheets under a third table in a back room off a street two streets wide, with about two hundred lines under a heading a woman ruled on her own paper in the eighth year, a copy of a counterman’s nine-year ledger in a pigeonhole, and a fourth sheet with a heading on it since the twenty-first of February before last.

And about four hundred and thirty pieces of pencil paper went up those four flights and came down them again.

That is a count of pieces of pencil paper. It is not a count of claims and it is not a count of people, and about four hundred is what it has always been about, and about four hundred and thirty is what the paper adds up to, and no person has ever put those two figures next to each other, because no person has ever been in a position to.

## Four Hundred Yards, And What Is In Between

He had walked it about nine times in four years and it takes about eleven minutes at the pace he walks, and there is a market end at the corner of it that is not open on the third of a month and a run of washrooms on the other side of the road with the door propped.

A street that is two streets wide is the reason there has never been a door onto a street at the end of it. A door onto a street is what turns a room with a counter in it into a counter in a street, and a counter in a street is a thing anybody may walk into, and about four hundred people walked into this one between the eighth of October of the first year and the twenty-eighth of May of the ninth.

And the four hundred yards between the two books are the entire reason those two books have never been joined, and there is not one instrument anywhere in Ostara that will carry a thing across the four hundred yards, and he has gone over that ground about nine times in four years and has never once worked out a way to do it that would not cost about four hundred people their only reason for trusting anything.

## About Four Hundred People

The other number is about four hundred people, and he has never seen one of them and never will and could not pick one out of a crowd in a street.

Some of them wrote in the bottom two inches of a wall at the foot of a hill in pencil, in their own hands, in nine years, about four hundred times.

Some of them carried a piece of pencil paper up four flights to a third table in a laundry and had it carried on and went down the stairs again and did not know the name of the woman who carried it, and that was the arrangement, and about four hundred people decided to trust it on the strength of not knowing.

About nine of them in a week get down low enough to read those four inches. About nine people in a week come up those four flights. Not one person in this city is doing both, and there is nothing in this city that would let anybody find out which nine is which.

## No Instrument In Ostara

There is not one instrument in Ostara that will ever put the two of them side by side.

Not in this city and not in any division building and not at a rail in a passage and not on a form.

One of those books takes a piece of paper and turns it into a row with a date against it and an hour against the date and nothing whatever after the hour. The other takes a piece of paper and turns it into a claim true against a day, and it may not be cited by anybody, ever, in any room, and it has never been cited once in six years.

There is a fifth field in one of them and it had nothing in it on the first of February. It has had one line in it since the twelfth of May, and the line is about a window in the third month of last year, and Wenna Raut said out loud in that room what the field was before she wrote a word of it.

And there is a woman in this city who has said the only sentence anybody has ever said to that office about herself, and it is on a sheet in a laundry and not in any book, and it was written down by a woman with a pencil on the fifth of May and read back five times and not headed, and she was not sent and she is not going to be.

And the only way those three things ever get joined up is a person standing at a counter being asked something, and this office may not ask a person anything.

## Four Flights, On The Third Of June

He went up them at about half past eleven on the third of June with nothing in his hands, which was the fourth time in eight weeks, and the street was two streets wide and the stair was narrow enough that two people going the other way have to agree about it.

There were nine sheets under that third table with about two hundred lines under a heading, and about four hundred and thirty pieces of pencil paper that had gone up and come down, and a fourth sheet with about two hundred copies of the foot of a page in it and eleven words at the end of it that were not a copy of anything.

And about four hundred and seventy rows in a book about four hundred yards away with a date and an hour against every one of them, and one field with one line in it out of all the fields in nine years.

He stood at the end of that bench for about nine minutes and did the arithmetic he had been not doing for six weeks.

There is not one person in this city who appears in both of those books, and the only way to find out whether about four hundred and seventy rows and about four hundred people are the same four hundred is to go through about four hundred pieces of pencil paper one at a time and write down who carried each of them and where it went when it came down.

And not one person may be sent to do that. And this office may not ask. And a woman four streets away has said no five times about anything at all and once more on the twenty-sixth of May, and he is not going to make it six.

## The Reason He Gave To A Stairwell

He did not go through them.

He went down one flight and stopped on the second landing and said the reason out loud to a stairwell, which is what he does when there is a thing in his hand he cannot put down anywhere.

“I am the only man in this city who knows that both of those books exist,” he said. “And if I go through about four hundred pieces of pencil paper then I am a man with a list of about four hundred addresses, and a man with a list of about four hundred addresses is a man who has been to about four hundred doors.”

“That is the arrangement. That is the whole of it, and it has been the arrangement for nine years, and it is the only thing that is holding. The only reason it is holding is that a man who keeps two books in this city has never walked out of this building with anything in his hand about anybody, and there is not one division form in Ostara that could be used on me if I did, and I would do it anyway, which is exactly why it works.”

He stood on the second landing for another four minutes.

He had got as far as about forty on the twenty-ninth of May and he had the method and it worked, and the method is that you take a piece of pencil paper and you match the day that was written on it against a day in that book, and where two days agree you have found one person out of about four hundred.

That is the only instrument in this city that can put a claim true against a day against a row, and it works, and it has never been done.

“And in about four years about forty people will know that a man has been in a laundry counting things, and about four hundred will hear it and not one of the four hundred will be told what it was for.”

He went down the rest of the stairs and did the second floor and then the third and then the stairwell and the bin room, and his chest has been spent since the fourth year and he had to stop twice.

And he did not go up them again.

*Three thousand and two days. Less two thousand nine hundred and ninety-five, the twenty-sixth of May of this year, is seven, and a man stood at the end of a bench for about nine minutes over about four hundred and seventy rows and about four hundred people and did not go through the four hundred pieces of pencil paper, and gave the reason for it out loud to a stairwell, and has not gone up those four flights again.*

*Less two thousand nine hundred and thirty-nine, the thirtieth of March of this year, is sixty-three, and the thirty-third sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last sixty-three of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is five hundred and eighty-two days, which is nineteen months and twelve days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
