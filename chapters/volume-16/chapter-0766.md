# The Register Takes A Description

Two thousand nine hundred and seventy-four was the fifth of May and the twelfth is day two thousand nine hundred and eighty-one, and two thousand nine hundred and eighty-one less two thousand nine hundred and seventy-four is seven, and twelve less five is seven by the month count.

## The Same Woman, And A Third Time Up The Stair

She came through the front door on the ninth with a piece of pencil paper in her hand, and the door came off its latch behind her, and Wenna Raut entered it as received, with the date against it and the hour against the date and nothing whatever against the hour.

That is what happens to about four hundred and thirty rows in that book and that is what happened to that one, and it is the same counter and the same voice and the same eleven words she had asked on the fourteenth of April, which are in the book in her own hand under the four sentences with a date and an hour against them.

She had asked *then what do I do*, and the office had not answered her, because there is not one thing in it that answers anything, and because a row may be added to and may not be taken from.

On the ninth she said two things out loud at that counter and both of them went onto the standing sheet, because the instruction of the thirtieth of March says this office writes down what a person says at that counter in the words that person used.

On the twelfth of May she came up the stair a third time and stood at the counter for about four minutes and said a third thing, and the third thing was not about a piece of paper at all.

## What The Field Is, And What It Is Not

Wenna Raut had it in her hand before the woman got to the counter and she said it out loud in about nine seconds, in a room, with a man standing at the end of the table.

She said it because she said it at that counter on the twelfth of May of the seventh year and at a table in February, and both times she said it so that a clerk could not come along afterwards and put a person in the wrong place.

“The seventh year was the first one, and I said it because I was afraid of a clerk arriving in about four years with a hand on that volume,” she said. “I am saying it now because of a different thing and I have had a week to work out what the different thing is and I have not got it, and I have said it anyway, and that is the whole of what I have to offer this morning.”

“Name it anyway.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut. “That is the part I have not got, and I do not enter what I have not got.”

“The fifth field is about what was done to a person,” she said. “It is not the field about who brought the paper in. That is the fourth field, and the fourth field is the hour, and the hour went in on the ninth in a clerk’s hand and I am not moving one word of it and it may not be moved.”

She put the pen down and squared the book to the edge of the table and did not pick the pen up again.

“The fifth field is filled out of the mouth of the person it is about, in that person’s own words, and not from whoever brought anything, and not from me. I am not permitted to improve one word of it and neither is anybody else in this city.”

“Will you say that again.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut. “Once. It is said.”

## What It Has Been Doing For Nine Months

“You have been asking me for the wrong thing since March,” she said. “Not this morning. Since the June before last. That instruction says a description of the person in that person’s own words, and I have been putting a receipt in the only box there is, every day of those nine months, because that is the box.”

“Then it has been obeyed.”

“It has been obeyed for nine months and it has produced about four hundred and thirty rows with a date and an hour on them and nothing whatever after the hour, and I did the correct thing every morning of those nine months with a sentence that could not be read.” She turned the book a quarter. “A clerk who is given the wrong field enters the wrong field correctly for nine years and nothing anywhere in this city tells her.”

“You are telling me.”

“I am telling a room, and there is a man in it, and that is two people, and it is more than I have told.”

## Entered In The Words She Used

“You are going to write it in what I say.”

“I am going to write it in what you say. You have not said it yet and I cannot write what has not been said and I am not going to prompt you, because a clerk who puts a sentence into somebody’s mouth is worse than any other thing that has ever happened in this room.”

The woman stood at that counter for about four seconds with her fingers on the front of it, the way a person leans on a thing they are about to let go of.

“They told me at a window in the third month of last year that nothing had happened to me,” she said. “It did happen. I have not slept since. I am not asking you for anything at all. I am telling you what happened to me, and you are the first place I have said it out loud in about nine months, and if it goes in the wrong box you tell me now and not afterwards.”

“It is in the right box,” said Wenna Raut.

Then she wrote it.

She wrote it in about forty seconds in the words that were used, with the date at the top of the field and the hour against the date, and she did not change a word of it and she did not put a stop in that had not been said.

She did not add anything about the window, or about the building, or about which of about nine counters in this city it was, because not one word of that had been said to her.

## She Watched Her Do It

The woman watched the whole of it with her chin about four inches above the line of words, the way a person watches a hand, and she did not move once while it was being written.

Forty seconds is a long time when a woman is reading her own handwriting going into a book in a building with no door onto a street, and about nine people were standing in the corridor behind her for the whole of it, and not one of them could see the page.

“It says what I said.”

“It says what you said.”

“It does not say what happened. It says what I told you happened.”

“That is the field,” said Wenna Raut. “It is what was done to a person, in that person’s own mouth. There is no room in it for what actually happened, because I have not been told what actually happened, and I am not permitted to go and find out, and you are not obliged to tell me one further thing in your life.”

“It will still be there.”

“It will still be there after you and after me and after this building has been taken down and put up again.” She squared the book. “There is not one person in this city with the standing to touch it, and I am the person who wrote it and I could not take it back if I wanted to and I will not be able to improve it in about four years when somebody reads it and thinks I should have written it differently.”

The woman put her hands in her sleeves.

“Can I take it out.”

“No.”

“Can I add to it.”

“Yes,” said Wenna Raut. “Any time. In your own words, at that counter, with a date and an hour against it. A row may be added to and may not be taken from, and that is not my rule and it is not this office’s rule and it is the shape of every book in this city.”

## One Person, One Sentence, And A Clerk Who Is The Mouth Of It

A woman told a clerk at about twenty past eleven in the morning that a man at a window in the third month of last year had told her nothing had happened to her, and that it had happened, and that she has not slept since.

And the clerk is now the only other person in Ostara who knows it.

He stood at the end of that table for the whole of it with his hands in his coat pockets and understood about four things, and he understood the fourth of them last.

About four hundred and thirty people have handed that clerk a piece of paper. Nine years. Not one of them has ever been in the fifth field.

There are five instructions standing in that book in one hand. Four of them are about a piece of paper and one of them shuts a drawer, and every one of the five has been obeyed every day of nine years and every one of them produced a receipt.

And the fifth field was the right one the whole time, and it was never filled because there had never been anybody in that room who was willing to stand at that counter and say out loud, in words, that a thing had been done to her, and that she was not asking for anything.

*Two thousand nine hundred and eighty-one days. Less two thousand nine hundred and seventy-four, the fifth of May of this year, is seven, and a clerk said out loud in that room what the fifth field is and what it is not and that whatever goes in it is entered for ever, and a woman said a sentence about a window in the third month of last year, and it went in in her own words, and it is the first thing in that field in nine years.*

*Less two thousand nine hundred and thirty-nine, the thirtieth of March of this year, is forty-two, and the thirty-third sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last forty-two of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is five hundred and sixty-one days, which is eighteen months and twenty-one days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
