# A Page And A Stone

Three thousand and forty-eight was the nineteenth of July and the twenty-sixth is day three thousand and fifty-five, and three thousand and fifty-five less three thousand and forty-eight is seven, and twenty-six less nineteen is seven by the month count.

## A Window Off A Third Court

The magistrate’s counter is a window with a clerk behind it and about five long years in that chair, and he has never once been wrong about what a counter is, and he is entitled to exactly one question a day about a thing and not two.

He reads the papers where they lay. He does not write them down and he does not take them away, and everything he has ever learned has come back to him out of a book he is entitled to open and not out of a person.

The man who said the second denial at a counter off that same court on the nineteenth was in the passage behind Lin Hao at about twenty past ten on the morning of the twenty-sixth, holding about nine words in his own pencil, and he had come down to get it stopped and had been told there and then that there was nothing in this city that could stop a sentence a man has heard out loud.

They put the pencil paper on the wood and the clerk read it where it lay.

There were about four people behind them in that passage and they had come out of a building with no door onto a street and into one with a window on a court, which is the whole of what anybody in Ostara can do about a sentence in about nine seconds.

## The Rule Of That Window

“What can you do with a piece of paper at this counter.”

“I can read it,” said the clerk. “I cannot keep it, I cannot copy it, I cannot take it up four flights and ask anybody what day it means, and I cannot send it anywhere. I can ask one question a day about a thing and there is not a second one, and I use it when I know what I am going to do with the answer.”

“I am asking whether there is a building off a third court that keeps the stone at the foot of a wall.”

“That is a question about an address,” said the clerk, “and I can tell you whether an address is in my book and what is in the entry, and I cannot tell you whether it keeps anything, because this court does not keep anything and neither does anybody else in it. That is not modesty. It is the size of a book.”

He put his fingers on the wood for about a second and drew them back, which is a thing that clerk has done about four times in five years.

“Ask me about the address and not about the wall.”

## The One Question Of The Morning

“Ask him about the address,” said Lin Hao.

The clerk looked at the pencil paper for about four seconds.

“I have not spent today’s.”

“You have used it on nothing. It is a quarter past ten.”

“It is a quarter past ten and the day has not started,” said the clerk. “I use it when I know what the answer is going to be for. Ask me that again at two o’clock.”

He went into the back at about four minutes to eleven and was gone for about eleven minutes.

## What Came Back On A Slip

He came back with a slip of his own paper, face down, and turned it round on the wood and put his thumb on the top line of it and read it out in the voice he reads everything in.

The address is in a book in this building. It has been in that book since the eighth of October of the first year and it has not moved in any of the years since, and it is on a ground floor passage four feet wide, with a door at the back of it with a bar across the door.

Above it: four floors. There is nothing in that entry about what is on any of the four and the clerk did not go and find out, because that is not what the book is for.

There is no stone on that premises. There has never been a stone on that premises. There is about four hundred ounces of wax in a pan in the corner of the ground floor and a shelf of iron-strapped boxes on a wall, and the clerk at the window said that last part twice because he had looked at it twice.

The date against the entry is the eighth of October of the first year.

Lin Hao looked at the date for about four seconds.

That is the morning a clerk was handed a sheet and a form and no instruction at a counter off that court, and typed two words into one of two ruled squares because the box was there and the words were there.

## What It Is Worth To The Person It Is About

The man in the passage took the slip and read it standing up, about nine words about a building and about wax and about boxes, and then he looked at the date at the top of it.

“So it is not a stone.”

“There is no stone on that premises,” said the clerk at the window. “That is not a matter of opinion. It is an entry and it has been an entry since the first year and I have read it to you where it lies.”

“Then somebody is carrying the wrong thing.”

“Somebody is carrying a thing he heard at a rail and got inside out,” said the clerk, “and it is not my job to know which of the two halves he got wrong, because he has not asked me which half. He has asked me whether there is a stone, and there is not, and that is the whole of what a counter can give a person.”

He put his thumb on the slip again.

“The date is not a reason,” he said. “Every address in this building has a day against it and about four hundred of them were entered on the day this court was opened, because that is the day the book began. A day is a day. It does not mean anything else and I am not going to be asked to make it mean something else.”

## Two Sentences That Do Not Help Anybody

A question with a day on it is the only thing anybody in this city has ever been able to act on.

A building is not a person.

Neither of those two sentences helps anybody at all.

They are both true and there is not one person in Ostara who can join them up into a third one.

A woman has been carrying a page for about a year and it was true when she could not read it. A man has been carrying nine words for sixteen weeks and they were his own pencil. A building has a day on it, and the day is real, and a building cannot be harmed by what people believe about it, and cannot be told it was wrong, and cannot be paid.

About nine people a week go down to the bottom of that wall. About nine a month come up four flights. About nine a week stop at that rail. None of those three numbers is a count of people and all three of them are about the same four hundred people.

There is no instrument anywhere in this city on which this office may put a fact into the hands of a person who has been given a different one.

The man in the passage put the slip back on the wood and the clerk took it and turned it over and put it in a drawer with about nine hundred others in it, and the drawer was locked behind him, and that is what a counter is for.

## What The Man Is Going To Do With It

The man in the passage stood there with his own pencil paper in his hand and looked at the two of them for about nine seconds.

“I am going to take it back down to the kerb,” he said. “That is what I do with anything I cannot answer and there is nothing else in this city to do with it.”

“You are going to write that a building keeps the stone.”

“No,” said the man. “I am going to write what a man told me at a window, because a man at a window read it out of a book that has been in this building since the first year, and that is a better thing to have carried down than a thing I heard at a rail.”

He said that in the ordinary way and he was not being clever with it, and it was not going to make anybody’s position any better.

“It is still the wrong sentence,” said Lin Hao.

“It is a different sentence with a better source,” said the man, “and in about four years there will be two versions of it on that wall and they will not agree with each other, and I will be dead or I will be sitting in a chair, and either way I will not be the one asked about it.”

He went up the stairs with the pencil paper folded in two and did not stop on any of them.

Lin Hao walked back up the third court with the whole of it and he had it by about eleven and it was a fact about a building and it fits in on a page.

And the clerk at that window had said the true thing about it twice, and neither of the two was the thing that would have helped, which was that the man was going to go down to the bottom of a wall and write a sentence about a building, and that the sentence would be better sourced than the one it replaced, and that this office is not permitted to ask him not to.

Lin Hao walked back up the third court with the whole of it and he had it by about eleven and it was a fact about a building and it fits in on a page.

*Three thousand and fifty-five days. Less three thousand and forty-eight, the nineteenth of July of this year, is seven, and a clerk at a window spent the one question of his morning and came back with a fact about a building, and the building has no stone on it and has had a day against it since the eighth of October of the first year.*

*Less three thousand and twenty-nine, the thirtieth of June of this year, is twenty-six, and the thirty-fourth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty-six of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is six hundred and thirty-five days, which is twenty-one months and five days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
