# The Counterman Asks

Three thousand and eighty-two was the twenty-third of August and the twenty-ninth is day three thousand and eighty-eight, and three thousand and eighty-eight less three thousand and eighty-two is six, and twenty-nine less twenty-three is six by the month count.

## A Man Who Is Paid Nothing

He did not answer the question.

He put his thumb down on the deal of the counter the way he does with the edge of a rail and looked at the shutter for about four seconds, and then he said, “I do not know.”

“That is not an answer either.”

“It is the answer there is,” said the counterman. “I have been under this window nine years and I read the bottom of that page out loud to about nine people a week and I have never once known what any of it is for, and I have not asked, and I am not going to start now.”

“I am not asking you what it is for.”

“Then what are you asking me.”

“Whether a line under a sentence is a sentence,” said Lin Hao. “Because you read the four sentences, and then you read what is under them, and there are two of them now and there were none in March, and I do not know which part of that page they belong to and neither does anybody else in this city.”

## The Second Question In Nine Years

The counterman put his cup down on the desk with the handle still turned away from him.

“Not one person has ever asked me that,” he said. “You asked me a thing in December and I said no and that was the first question anybody put to me in nine years and you carried it eighty-four days before you asked it. This is the second one anybody has put to me and it is the first one anybody has asked me because they wanted the answer.”

“That is a hard thing to be owed.”

“It is not owed to me,” said the counterman. “It is nine years of me not being asked, and you have just come and been the second, and it has taken you four seconds, and I have been standing at this window for nine years waiting for a question that was never going to arrive.”

He turned the cup a quarter on its own base without moving the handle.

“Now I am going to ask you one, and you have not got a right to refuse it, because I have been reading a page out loud for nine years on somebody else’s account and not one person has ever once told me what is being added to it.”

## What He Asked

“Is the line at the foot of that page part of the four sentences.”

Lin Hao did not answer for about four seconds.

“No,” he said. “It is not part of them, and it is not under them, and it is not one of the six lines below, and it is not one of the four in the column, and it is not the two inches of nothing at the bottom of it. It stands between the four sentences and the six lines and it is in this office’s hand and there is a man’s name under it and it was entered there by a clerk who was given it.”

“Then what is it.”

“That is the whole of what I can tell you and I have told you all of it.”

“You have not told me what I asked.”

“Not one person in this city is entitled to tell you,” said Lin Hao. “There never has been.”

He put his fingers on the deal of the counter and left them there.

“I am the only person in Ostara who may put one question to that office and I have put two in nine years and neither of them was that one, and I cannot put it now.”

He stopped there and did not improve it.

## A Man Behind A Counter Reads What Is On The Page

“That is a strange answer to give a man who is about to say it out loud.”

“It is the true one.”

“I did not say it was not,” said the counterman. “I have been reading that page to about nine people a week for nine years and every one of those nine has gone away with two things: what it said, and what it was for. And in nine years not one person has come back and asked me what it was for except about four, and they asked it wrong, because they asked me to leave something out.”

“I have not asked you to leave anything out.”

“No,” said the counterman. “You have come down here and asked me whether a line is a sentence, which is the first sensible question anybody has put to me since the eighth of October of the first year, and I have answered it as well as I can, and now I am going to tell you what I do with it.”

He took his thumb off the deal of the counter.

“I am going to read it. Because it is on the page, and a man behind a counter reads what is on the page, and I have never once in nine years put my hand over a line of it because I did not know what it meant, and I am not going to start now.”

## The Question He Could Have Put And Did Not

He had two of them in nine years and had a third one in his mouth about four seconds after he came in, and he kept it there and it came out later in a different form.

The third one was the obvious one. He could have stood at that counter and put the single question this office can be asked and asked it of a man who is paid nothing instead of a room.

*What is that page for.*

He would have got the same answer he got in December, in the same number of seconds, and it would have been no, and there would not be one instruction in this city afterwards that anybody could put in front of anybody.

What he had actually been asked was a smaller one and it was not addressed to him. Where do the four sentences stop.

Nothing in Ostara will take that down in writing.

The answer to it exists in one place and one place only, and it is a hand and a pen and a clerk in a room off a third court, and it would have to be a line in this office’s hand at the foot of a page. There are four of those already, under the four sentences and above the striking, and not one person in this city has ever written the fifth for anything except what they wanted done.

## The Sheet He Was Given And Did Not Take

He took a piece of pencil paper out of the inside of his coat while the man was talking, and he wrote the whole of the bottom of the page on it in about four minutes, in his own hand: four sentences, the column, the six lines, the striking and the name under it, and then the four lines of this office that had gone out on the thirtieth of June and the two that had not.

He put it on the deal of the counter face up.

“You do not have to say anything about any of it,” he said. “It is only so that you can see what is going to be under the four sentences before the thirtieth of September, in case anybody at that rail asks you and you cannot remember.”

The counterman did not touch it.

He looked at it for about nine seconds. He can read.

Then he put his other hand on the edge of the wood beside it, not on it, and left it there.

“No,” he said.

“It is yours. You can burn it.”

“No, I am not going to take it,” said the counterman, “and I am going to tell you why, and it is the only reason I have ever refused anything in nine years in this room.”

## Why He Would Not Take It

“If I have that in my hand at four o’clock on the last working day of a quarter, then I am reading the bottom of that page off a piece of paper,” said the counterman. “And every word I have ever said out loud in that passage came off the page with my eyes on it, and about nine people a week have believed that the man reading it was reading the page and not reading a man.”

He pushed the pencil paper back across the deal with two fingers.

“I do not know what is on it that is not on the page. You know that. That is the whole of your question answered twice. And if I take it, then from the thirtieth of September there is a piece of pencil paper in this room with your hand on it, and the next man under this window will be reading it off that, and none of the nine will ever know.”

Lin Hao picked the paper up and put it back in the inside of his coat.

## What He Said About The Fourth Of Those Lines

“That is the right answer,” he said, “and I am not going to thank you for it, and I am going to say the rest of it anyway.”

“Go on.”

“The four lines at the foot of that page have gone out twice. The first two went out on the thirtieth of June and the other two go out together on the thirtieth of September, and one of the two is not an instruction, it is a sentence a man said out loud at a counter, in his own words, in about nine seconds, in front of about nine people.”

“And the other one.”

“The other one is a denial,” said Lin Hao. “It says that no page of this office and no stone in this city is connected to any other, and that a resemblance is not a figure.”

He watched the man’s face.

“You are going to read a sentence about a building and a stone, and then an answer to it, and then a denial, and they are all going to come out of your mouth in about four minutes in a passage four feet wide, and every one of the about nine people standing at that rail is going to hear all three.”

“That is what a page is.”

“That is a page,” said Lin Hao. “And there is not one instruction in this city that lets you stop reading at the end of any of the four, and there never has been, and you have never asked for one, and I have just told you that you could have one and you have refused it.”

The counterman picked his cup up and put it down again without drinking from it.

“Then I shall read all of it,” he said, “and I shall read it in the order it stands, and I shall not stop at the striking, and anybody who wants to know what any of it is for can come and stand at that rail and hear it, because that is the only place in this city it comes out of my mouth at.”

*Three thousand and eighty-eight days. Less three thousand and eighty-two, the twenty-third of August of this year, is six, and a man who is paid nothing was asked a question and refused a sheet and said out loud that he will read whatever is at the foot of that page in the order it stands, and nothing written in this city lets him read less of it.*

*Less three thousand and twenty-nine, the thirtieth of June of this year, is fifty-nine, and the thirty-fourth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-nine of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is six hundred and sixty-eight days, which is twenty-two months and eight days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
