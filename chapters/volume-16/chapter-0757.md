# The First Week Ends

Two thousand nine hundred and thirteen was the fourth of March and the eleventh is day two thousand nine hundred and twenty, and two thousand nine hundred and twenty less two thousand nine hundred and thirteen is seven, and eleven less four is seven by the month count.

## A Question He Knows The Answer To Before He Gets There

He had it by nine on the morning of the ninth and had carried it round three days without asking it, which is three days longer than he carried the instruction of the tenth of June, and the reason he carried that one for about an hour was that he had not understood it yet.

There are four reasons why this office cannot learn one thing about one woman, and he had them in order, and every one of them is the shape of a reason and none of them is about her.

No person has ever been sent by this office to any other person, in nine years. Not in December when a name went into a column, and not in February when a woman came up four flights to a bench.

And there is a form in a division building two streets from the laundry that carries a person from a counter to a door and back again with a date against it. It belongs to that division and not to this room, and in nine years not one sheet has gone between the two of them.

Nine sheets under a third table may not be sent to anybody and may not be cited.
The boy may not be asked.
The woman with the short-handled broom may not be asked.
And this office may not put a question to a person about what she has done with a piece of paper.

That is the whole of it and it took him nine years to arrive at it and it is four sentences long, and he had not been arguing with any of them for about four months, and he was going to go up four flights that afternoon and ask one question anyway.

He wanted to.

That is the honest part and he wrote it out at about ten to twelve the night before, in pencil, on the back of his own left hand, which is the only surface in this city he is allowed to deface with a thought: *I wanted to, and there was no reason, and wanting is not a reason.*

## What He Did With The Morning Instead

He did the thing he does when there is a question in his hand and no answer to it, which is that he put it in front of the only other person in the building and did not give it to her.

“You have got a question,” said Wenna Raut, without looking up, at about half past nine on the ninth. “You have had it since the fourth and you are carrying it about and you are going to say it out loud in about nine seconds and it is going to be about that woman, and I am telling you now that the answer is the same one it was in May and in December and that I am not going to spend anything on it.”

He did not say it out loud in nine seconds. He said it in about four minutes and it took longer than that because he began at the first of August.

“And I am not asking you to change any part of it,” he said. “I am telling you that I have worked out how to get a question to a woman in four streets without asking her a single thing about herself, and it has taken me three days, and it is a form in a division building and that division has never had a paper from this office in nine years, and I have not written to them and I am not going to.”

“You are going to go and try it in about eleven seconds,” said Wenna Raut, “and it is going to be a letter and it is going to be refused in about nine days because it has no person on it, and after that you will have sent something and you will not be able to un-send it.”

“That is the whole of it.”

“That is the whole of it,” said Wenna Raut, “and I am not going to say anything else about it, and you have not asked me a question and I have not spent anything.”

He wrote nothing, sent nothing, and did the floors of the third floor that he had not been asked to do.

Then he stood on the top landing for about nine minutes with the arithmetic on the back of his own hand, where he keeps everything, and the landing had no water on it then and has not had any since.

## Four Paragraphs On A Stair

He made the decision on the second landing of his own building at about half past two in the afternoon, with his shift not started for eight and a half hours and nothing to do with the time.

He had been three days building it and it had four parts and every part of it was somebody else’s refusal.

A woman who keeps an index is a person who can be sent. A division two streets away has never had a paper from this office. A form that could carry a person from a counter to a door is not this office’s to fill in.

And the whole of it comes back to a woman who came up four flights on the eighth of this month, was handed four words about a sheet of paper, and went down them again.

There was no part of it he could cut and no part of it he was permitted to keep, and the man who cleaned washrooms, who is the only person in Ostara entitled to put a single question to this office, was not entitled to put one about a person.

He put it behind him at the second landing and went and did the third floor instead, which is what he does.

## Nine Minutes At A Bench

She had the ninth sheet out and the pencil lying across the bench and she was doing what she does, which is going down a column of about two hundred lines a day at a rate of about nine, and she did not put the pencil down, which is what she does when she is about to enter something.

He stood at the end of the bench for about nine minutes and said one thing, and it was not the thing he had come up four flights to say.

“You are right and I am not going to ask you a sixth time and I am not going to send anybody.”

Perla Quist did not look up.

“No,” she said. “You are not.”

“That is the sixth time and you have given me the reason four times and it has never once been about me or about this office, and I am not going to improve it or repeat it back to you or thank you for it.”

“I have been up four flights with a question in my hand since the second landing and I have put it down, and I am telling you I have put it down. In a few years somebody is going to work out that this office came up that stair carrying something and went down it empty, and I would rather the sentence describing that were mine than be found written by you.”

“That is the right sentence,” said Perla Quist, “and I am not going to thank you for saying it, because thanking a man for not asking is the same sentence as thanking a man for letting you be damaged, and you worked that out in December and you did not work it out by being thanked.”

She turned the page and went down three lines.

“You may go down the four flights. And you may come up again when there is a thing to bring and not a thing to take, and there is usually a thing to bring, and you have never once come up with a thing to take and got one.”

## What Came Back Down The Stair

There are four addresses this office does not have, and one of them is a woman who has come to a counter off a third court eleven times in eleven months.

He came down two landings and thought about that, because a man with a spare week thinks about the shape of his own incompetence and he had got four of them stacked and was carrying all four down a stairwell in a branch building.

Three of the four were about a woman who keeps an index and did not want to be found, and one was about a woman who cannot read and could not be reached by any road he was allowed to walk. He had not got them into an order in three days and he did not get them into an order that week.

What he had got, by the time he was at the bottom of the stair, was that there was a fifth address he had never once thought of counting, which was the one at the foot of the hill with a board by a kerb on it, and that he had no way of being near it either, and that about nine people a week got down low enough to read it.

On the first landing he heard the front door of the room off the third court open behind him, up two floors, and he heard the sound travel down the stairwell and arrive at the bottom before he did.

He stood on the landing and listened to it.

It was not a person. There was no footstep and there was no voice on the other side of it and no clerk said anything to anybody, and it was the sound of a door in a building with no door onto a street opening because somebody was pushing a piece of paper under it.

Eleven pieces of paper in eight days. Again.

He had spent the afternoon going up four flights to give something away, and while he was on the stair the door had come off its latch for the eleventh time in eight days, and there was a clerk in that room who gets one question a morning and had not spent it, and about four hundred and thirty rows in the Register and one of them with a space in the middle of it, and nothing whatever was happening in there that he had done.

*Two thousand nine hundred and twenty days. Less two thousand nine hundred and thirteen, the fourth of March of this year, is seven, and this office sent nobody to anybody, and a man went up four flights and came down without asking one thing, and the front door came off its latch behind him.*

*Less two thousand eight hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December of the eighth year, is seventy-one, and the thirty-second sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-one of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is five hundred days, which is sixteen months and twenty days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
