# The Bundle Is Twenty-Six

Three thousand and one hundred and one was the twelfth of September and the eighteenth is day three thousand and one hundred and seven, and three thousand and one hundred and seven less three thousand and one hundred and one is six, and eighteen less twelve is six by the month count.

## Twenty-Six Things On A Table Beside A Shut Drawer

He was in that room at about ten in the morning on the eighteenth, which is the second of his three off, and there was nothing in the world for him to do with a hole in a week except go and stand next to a table.

The bundle is twenty-six things on a table in a room with no door onto a street, and it has been twenty-six things for about four years, and nothing is ever going to be taken off it and nothing is ever going to be put on it.

The twenty-sixth of them is a piece of office paper about the size of a quarter of a page and it is empty.

It was empty when it came out of the drawer in the wall on the left of that table and it is empty now. He carried it out in the inside of his coat in the seventh year and put it on the table in about nine seconds, and a clerk watched him do it and did not write one word of it down, because she may only enter what she is given.

The drawer it came out of has been shut since the twenty-first of October of the seventh year under an instruction with his own name under it, and the arrangement is that it is never opened again by anybody, and that includes him.

## What A Bundle Is

A bundle is an instrument and every instrument in this city has a floor under it, which is the number below which the thing does not go and does not answer.

A drawer has a floor. A book has a floor. An account has one, and he has not been able to move one in either direction since the first of October of the fourth year and is not going to, and the figure under it is not printed anywhere in this office and is not going to be printed by him in a story he tells anybody.

The bundle has one too, and the floor of the bundle is a person.

That is the whole of what it is for. It is not a store of things. It is the place where this office keeps the people it cannot ask anything, and there are twenty-six of them, and they are twenty-six separate reasons, and the bundle does not know that and neither does the room.

He had understood that about four years ago and had understood it again this morning on a second of his three off with a sheet in the inside of his coat that said four names and four dates and two printed words on the head of a column.

## The Wanting, In One Paragraph

He wanted to put it on the table.

Four hundred and seventy rows in a bound Register two floors down, and a drawer that cannot be opened, and a bundle of twenty-six things that nothing is going to be taken off, and a copy of the only copy of the second column in the world in the inside of a coat against a man's chest, on a table about nine feet away.

That is four places. He has wanted, in the last four days, to put one thing in a second place, because that is what a person does when a thing is the only one of its kind, and because he lost a drawer and a bundle is what a drawer is for.

He wanted it badly enough that he stood at the end of that table for about eleven minutes without doing anything at all, which is longer than he has stood at anything since the spring.

## The Reason Is Not A Rule

There was never a rule about the bundle.

There is no instruction in the bound Register that says what may be put on that table and no instruction that says what may not, and a person could not show to have been told what to do about it, and if he had put a twenty-seventh thing on that table at about eleven o'clock in the morning no officer in this city would have been entitled to stop him.

He could have done it. That is the part of it that has been standing at the end of that table for eleven minutes.

The reason he did not do it is that a woman four flights up a street two streets wide has spent six years being safe because nothing in this city has her name at the head of it, and he knows in about four seconds what happens to a woman when there is, and it happened to about four hundred people this year and about nine of them a month stopped, and the price is charged to none of them.

And a second thing, which is smaller and which he is not going to say out loud to a room with nothing in it: a bundle with twenty-six things on it holds nothing anybody has handed over.

The moment it holds a thing he wrote by asking, it is a list of what he has asked for. He has spent nine years learning what a list does to the person whose name is on it, and there is a list of five on the back of his right hand and a list of six under it.

## What He Did Instead

He took the sheet out of the inside of his coat at about a quarter to twelve and stood it on the edge of the table for about four seconds with his finger on it.

Then he picked it up and put it back in the inside of his coat on the left, where there was nothing in it.

That is where the twenty-sixth thing had been for about two years, between the third of the fourth month of the seventh year and the afternoon of the seventh year, in a pocket against his left side, folded once, with nothing written on it at all, and it went from that pocket onto that table in about nine seconds in front of a clerk, and the clerk wrote nothing down.

So the inside of that coat is the only place in this city where a piece of paper has been kept twice.

And that is not an instrument and it is not a floor and it cannot be entered in anything, and it does not know that it is the last of anything, and it has no heading on it, and a woman with a pencil has said in this city that the moment a heading goes over a thing it becomes a thing somebody can ask about twice.

## The Nine Words On The Back Of His Hand, And What He Did To Them

On the ninth of August he wrote nine words in pencil on the back of his own right hand and did not improve them and did not say them to anybody.

She keeps no list. Going would be making one.

He had been up four flights on the fifth of September and up four flights again on the twelfth, and he went over it on the second landing of his own building at about eleven in the morning with a sheet in his coat, and he accounted for it the way a man accounts for anything he has done that he is not going to be praised for.

He did not go to ask.

He went to put one question, and the question was about the paper, and she said yes to it in about nine seconds and it was the first thing anybody had asked her in six years that she could answer by writing something down, and she has not written a line of it on the ninth sheet and has not put a heading over it and has not put one word of it in the pigeonhole.

Going made nothing. Not one person was sent. She asked him for nothing and she has asked him for nothing in six years and she has told no person in this city what is in that drawer.

And he is going to have to go up those stairs again, because there is a thing in the inside of his coat with two printed words at the head of it that mean something in a works department and nothing at all in this one, and there are three people in this city who could tell him which, and he has asked none of them and is going to ask one.

That is not making a list. That is the other thing, and the other thing has a name on it in about eleven degrees.

## The Twenty-Sixth Thing Is Still Empty

He counted them because he counts them about once a year and it was that kind of morning.

Twenty-six, and he could not have told anybody what the other twenty-five were. Not one of them has a name on it in any book this city holds, and that is not protection and it is not modesty. There is nothing on which he could write them down and nothing on which anybody could ask him for them, and a man who could not tell you what is on his own table has not got a list.

The drawer was shut. The bundle was twenty-six. The column had four lines in it and nothing had gone into it since the third of December of the eighth year and nothing was going to go into it this month, because a clerk may only enter what she is given and there is no form on which this office could take a thing off a person who was not a person who came to the counter.

He got his coat off the back of the door and put it on, and the sheet was in the left side of it and went up against him when he moved.

He has never once in nine years walked out of that building with anything in his hand about anybody, and that is the whole of what has been holding. He broke it at about a quarter to twelve on the eighteenth of September with a piece of pencil paper that he did not write and cannot take back, and he did it on the second of his three off with not one person watching, which is the cheapest possible way to do it and he did it anyway.

Then he went down two floors and did the third floor, which is what he does.

*Three thousand and one hundred and seven days. Less three thousand and one hundred and one, the twelfth of September of this year, is six, and a man stood at the end of a table with twenty-six things on it for about eleven minutes and put nothing on it and took nothing off it, and carried the only copy of the second column out of that building in the inside of his coat, which is the first thing that has left it in a hand in nine years.*

*Less three thousand and twenty-nine, the thirtieth of June of this year, is seventy-eight, and the thirty-fourth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-eight of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is six hundred and eighty-seven days, which is twenty-two months and twenty-seven days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
