# Nobody Is Sent

Two thousand nine hundred and sixty was the twenty-first of April and the twenty-eighth is day two thousand nine hundred and sixty-seven, and two thousand nine hundred and sixty-seven less two thousand nine hundred and sixty is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven by the month count.

## A Name He Was Allowed To Use

On the second landing of his own building on the morning of the twenty-seventh he had a name in his hand that would have closed the whole of this in about nine days.

There is a woman in a division building who is allowed to ask any person anything at all. She is not this office. This office has never sent anybody anywhere in nine years and would not be able to prove that it had, and she has never been sent anything by anybody, and there is not one form in this city on which she could be shown to have been told what to do by a man who cleans washrooms.

He could have said that name to her once, in a corridor, in about four seconds, and there is no form involved at any point of that, and no clerk, and no letter, and no record, which is exactly why it works and exactly why it cannot be undone.

The mechanics of it were in his head by the evening of the twenty-fourth. A woman who is allowed to ask anybody anything, a corridor about nine feet wide, a sentence of four or five words, and a woman who comes up four flights once a month whether anybody has asked her to or not.

He had it worked out in nine days and the nine days were the twenty-fourth to the second, and on the second of May he understood that the nine days was how long it would take her to ask and about nine more before anybody knew about it at all.

She would have asked the woman who cannot read. Not about the sheet and not about the four words and not about the eleven months. She would have asked her whether she wanted to know.

And the woman who cannot read would have found out.

That is the whole of what the name does and it took him four days to work out and about forty seconds to be sure of, and then he carried it down to about seven in the morning and put it away and went up four flights instead.

## Up Four Flights In The Middle Of The Morning

He went up at about eleven in the morning on the second of his three days off, which is a thing he had not done in six years, and the stair was full of light from a landing window at the second floor and had about eleven people on it in about eleven minutes.

He had been up that stair four times in seven years and behind a girl carrying linen three times out of those four.

He had a sheet in the inside of his coat. He took it out on the second landing, looked at the front of it, which had a date at the top and four ruled squares and nothing whatever in any of them, and put it back.

It was not a letter. There is no letter in this city that goes from a man to a woman and cannot be refused at a counter by a clerk with a rule.

It was the only piece of paper in his possession carrying a date that no person had ever asked him for, and he had put it in his coat on the twenty-fourth, and he took it out on the twenty-eventh and put it back, and that is the whole of what he did with it.

## Three Holes In A Week, And Two Questions Used In Nine Years

He has four nights and three off on a rota the size of his own hand that carries four names, and he asked for that shift in the sixth year and there was nothing in this city on which a man of rank one could ask for one, and it was granted by a man who found the arrangement convenient and has not thought about it since.

Three of those off are holes in a week, and everybody who works nights knows exactly what is in them, and not one person in this city has ever been able to write a single one of the three down.

The first of the three he sleeps. The second of the three until about two he does not know what he is doing and has never been able to account for. And the third of the three he comes down here and stands at a table in a building with no door onto a street and is the only person in Ostara entitled to ask that office one question in the world.

That question has been used twice in nine years. Once in December about a page, and once on the twenty-first of April in a sluice at the end of a ward about a woman asleep in the third bay.

He had never once spent the second of the three walking up four flights to a laundry in the middle of the morning, and he did it on the twenty-eighth of April, and the reason he did it is about nine paragraphs below this one.

## Four Flights, And Nothing Asked

The back room smelled of boiled soap and damp linen and the steam came under the door from the washroom front. The ruler was squared against the edge of the bench with the paint gone through to the wood under about four inches of it.

Perla Quist had the ninth sheet out and about two hundred empty lines under a heading she ruled on her own paper in the eighth year of it, and the ruler squared against the edge of the bench, and the pencil held the way a person holds a thing they are about to put down and use again in a minute.

He stood at the end of that bench for about nine minutes and did not ask her a single thing, and she did not look up once, and she went down three lines in that time.

Then she said, without stopping, “About nine came up those stairs in March and about nine came up them in April.”

“How many brought a sheet.”

“About nine brought a sheet,” said Perla Quist. “In April two of the nine brought nothing at all, and stood in the room for a while, and went down again.”

“That is the first time in six years anybody has come up four flights to this room with nothing in their hands.”

“That is the first time in six years,” said Perla Quist, “and I have been here every single one of them, and I have been thinking about it since the eighth of April and I have not told anybody, and I am telling you because you will work out something wrong about it inside a day and I would rather you had it right.”

## The Temptation, In One Paragraph

He wanted to say the name.

Not on the third of the three, and not in a corridor in a division building, which is where he had first worked out that he could say it.

He wanted to say it at that bench, to that woman, in about nine seconds, on the twenty-eighth of April.

Because she is the only person in this city who has ever been right five times about this, and she would have known in about four seconds whether it was a good idea, and she would have told him, and he would have done it, and about four hundred people in this city would have been safer by the middle of June.

He did not say it.

## The Refusal, In One Paragraph

The reason is not a rule.

There has never been a rule. This office may not ask a person what she has done with a piece of paper, and it may not, and he may not, because he is not this office, because he is a man on a night shift in a white plate with no figure cut into it, and he could stand in that room and say anything at all to that woman and there is not one officer of this city entitled to stop him or to hear about it afterwards.

Nine years of a rule that is not a rule, kept by two people who agreed with each other and never once said so out loud, and he is the only one of them alive who could walk through it this afternoon, and about four hundred people in this city would be safer tonight if he did.

He did not say the name, and he could have, and there is no instrument anywhere in this city that will ever record that he did not.

## What She Said While He Was Going Down

“There is a thing you are not asking me,” said Perla Quist.

“No.”

“You have come up four flights twice in three weeks with nothing in your hands and you have stood at the end of that bench for nine minutes on both occasions, and I am not going to ask you what it is, and I am not going to thank you for not asking, and I am not going to ask you for a sixth time about anything at all.”

“That is the sixth time.”

“That was the fifth,” said Perla Quist. “You are early.”

She turned the page and went down three more lines and did not look up again.

He went down four flights with nothing in his hands for the second time in three weeks, and the woman at the bench did not know that anything had been refused, and nobody in this city is told about the two of them in a laundry, and there is not one person he could say it to.

And about nine people a month are coming up those four flights now with nothing in their hands at all, and about nine a month are standing in a corridor four feet wide with a piece of paper they have carried about four hundred yards, and neither of those rooms is an instrument and neither of them can be asked a question, and the only reason either of them still exists is that neither one is a counter.

*Two thousand nine hundred and sixty-seven days. Less two thousand nine hundred and sixty, the twenty-first of April of this year, is seven, and a name that would have closed this in nine days went up four flights in a man’s mouth and did not get out of it, and a woman at a bench told him that two people had come up those stairs in April with nothing in their hands.*

*Less two thousand nine hundred and thirty-nine, the thirtieth of March of this year, is twenty-eight, and the thirty-third sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty-eight of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is five hundred and forty-seven days, which is eighteen months and seven days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
