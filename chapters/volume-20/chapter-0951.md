# The Wrong Word First

Four thousand two hundred and eighty-nine was the thirtieth of December and the twentieth of this one is day four thousand three hundred and nine, and four thousand three hundred and nine less four thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is twenty, and that is the second winter of the card on that door.

## The Card In The Light It Is Read In

He went up the four flights about twenty minutes early, because it takes a card about four minutes to look at properly and he did not want to be doing it while somebody was on the stair.

The card stood about a sixteenth of an inch off the door on three nails that have been the same three nails since the first year, and the holes round them were a quarter of an inch wider than the nails were and about nine months older than the nails themselves.

The first line was sound. It was a street and it had been a street for two hundred years and it would outlive him.

The second line had gone at both top corners. About the depth of a coat button, at each end, where the ink had lifted out of the fibres and the fibres had gone pale and stayed pale. The fourth word and the fifth word stood further apart than they had in November, and a person standing in front of that card would have to decide for himself that they were two words.

The third line was the hours, and it was the only one of the three that anybody actually comes up four flights for.

There is no name on it. There has never been a name on it.

## Nothing Has Changed

Nothing had changed, and that was the answer, and it took the whole morning to find out that an answer can be twenty days long.

The two carriers came up at a quarter to ten with the strap between them and the book went on the table where it goes, and the tick of the back board started about four seconds after it was set down, and it does that about every four seconds and has done since the second morning of the autumn.

They came all morning and they came the way they always come, and neither of the two people in that room was in the habit of adding them up.

Four of them came about a piece of paper and had what they came for inside about six minutes each. One came about a person who would not come, and the row went in with a date at the head of it and an hour against the date, and nothing under the hour, and he said the price of it out loud with the door open before she went down four flights because that is what that table is for.

One came about a division form and was told what the form wanted, which is not what he came about, and he took it.

And one stood on the third landing for a while and then went down again without coming to the door, which happens often enough and is not a finding.

## Ten Past Ten, At The End Of The Table

The man was standing at the end of that table at about ten past ten with his hat in both hands and he had clearly been standing there long enough for the clerk to have finished what she was doing.

"I have not come up here to ask you anything," he said.

"That is allowed."

"I have read that card twice from the bottom of your stair," he said. "On the day it went up and again this morning, about a quarter of an hour each time, standing on the bottom step where the light is not on it yet. And I can read it. I have been at counters for about nine years and I can read a notice with a soft corner on it."

"Then what has brought you up four flights."

"The end of the second line has gone," said the man. "There are two words at the end of it standing apart with a gap in between them that is not a space, and I have been trying for a week to convince myself it is a space, and I cannot, because the fourth word and the fifth word have not got a space between them and there is one there. I want it whole."

He said the last part as though he were asking for something small.

## What Cannot Be Done About It

Lin Hao stood on the other side of that table with his thumb on the edge of it and worked through the whole of it in about nine seconds, the way a person works through something they have already worked through.

He could not write it down.

Not because of any rule anybody had made. Because a piece of paper with those three lines on it would be a copy, and a copy is a second door with the same three lines on it in a hand that is not his, and there are about four of those already in this city and every one of them is wrong somewhere and not one of them is this office's.

He could not correct it. Correcting the man's copy was making the man's copy true, and making it true meant writing it, and writing it meant the office had started putting its own hand to a third copy.

He could not tell the man it was the second line that had gone soft and leave it there, because a man who has been told that a card is soft will tell the next man that the card is worn, and worn is a reason to ask somebody else.

And he could not hand the man a thing to carry.

A man who has been given three lines in a hand can lose the hand and keep the three lines, and a man who has been given three lines in his own hearing can lose neither, and the difference between those two men is the whole of what this office has left.

"What I can do is say it," he said.

## Twice, And The Second Time From The Second Step

He said it once at that table, slowly, in the ordinary voice, and the man stood still and did not take anything out of his coat, which was the thing Lin Hao had been afraid of.

Then he walked the man out to the second step and stood on it with his back to the open door and said it again the way he says it at half past four when the light has gone off the street and the last people of the day are standing at the bottom of that stair.

"It is the same sentence," said the man.

"It is the same sentence said from the place it has to be heard from," said Lin Hao. "If you take that card off that door tomorrow the ink is what it is. I cannot change that with my voice and I would not if I could, because a card that lasts a year is better than a card that lasts a week, and a card that gets rubbed out is a card somebody remembers wrongly."

"I have never thought about a card lasting."

"Neither had I, until the thirtieth of December."

"I want to say the whole of it back to you," said the man, "not because I do not believe you. Because I have been wrong about a notice before and it cost me a morning and about four hours of somebody else's time, and the wrongness was never in the notice. It was in that I did not go and stand in front of the thing."

"That is the correct use of a mouth," said Lin Hao, "and it is a bad thing to have found out about a card."

## The Hour She Would Have Had To Do It Alone

The man said thank him at the top of the stair, and he meant it, and Lin Hao told him there was nothing to be thanked for, which is what that table says to somebody about four times a fortnight, and this morning it was said there by a man who has never once been inside that room.

Wenna Raut had her pen down and had not written one word.

"Give me about four seconds," she said, "and then tell me what you want me to do with this."

"You write nothing."

"I know I write nothing. I want to hear you say the reason in the ordinary voice while I am sitting here with a closed book, because in about four years somebody is going to ask me what a man in a white plate said at that table on the twentieth of January about three lines on a door."

He said the reason.

"They are not a question and not an answer," he said. "They came up a stair in a man's head and they have not got a date and an hour on them and they never will, and that book cannot take them and would not know what to do with them if it could, and a row under an hour that says a man asked me to read a card out loud is a sentence that pretends to be about a piece of paper and is not."

"Then the cost is this," she said, getting up to go out and then not going out. "In about four years there is going to be a thing that happened in that room and it is going to be you standing on a step in a doorway, and there is nothing whatever on this table that anybody can put in front of anybody."

"Yes."

"Go and stand on the step then," said Wenna Raut, "and I will put the hour against the day in my own head, because I am going to be here for it."

"You cannot keep a count of that stair in your head any more, you told me that in October."

"I am not keeping a count of that stair," she said. "I am keeping one hour in one day, and there is a difference, and if you ask me the difference I will tell you and then I will not be able to do it again."

## Down Four Flights And A Counter Under A Window

He went down at about twenty to four and out onto the street, and about two hundred yards along it and one street off, in a passage with a counter under a window and a rail and about nine people standing in it, a man was saying his three lines out loud.

He had a small coin in his hand and he was turning it over between two fingers while he said them, and he said them well. He said the street. He said the door at the head of four flights off a third court. He said the hours.

And then he said a fourth thing, which was not on the card, which had never been said to him by anybody, and which was entirely true.

Lin Hao stood at the other side of that passage about four seconds and worked out that the four things had gone into that man's mouth as one sentence, and that it was going to go into about nine more mouths that afternoon, and that there was no longer any way to tell which part of it was his.

Four thousand three hundred and nine days. Less four thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is twenty, and a man came up four flights on the twentieth of this month because the end of the second line had gone soft, and was given the whole of it twice in one morning, and in a passage about two hundred yards off, a man with a coin in his hand was saying it with one more clause in it.

*Less four thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is twenty, and the forty-eighth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand eight hundred and eighty-nine days, which are sixty-two months and twenty-nine days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
