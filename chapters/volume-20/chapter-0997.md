# Three Of Them On A Stair

Four thousand six hundred and twenty-two was the third of this one and the tenth of this one is day four thousand six hundred and twenty-nine, and four thousand six hundred and twenty-nine less four thousand six hundred and twenty-two, the third of this one, is seven, and ten less three is seven by the month count.

## On The Landing With The Door Open

It is colder on that landing in December than it is anywhere else in that building, because the door stands open from a quarter to ten and the stair is four flights and there is no fire on four flights.

He came up at about ten with the card in his hand at his side rather than in his pocket, because he had been standing on that landing four days ago looking at the end of the second line, and he wanted to look at it again on a working morning with the door open and people on the stair.

The other carrier was at the top with the strap coiled at his feet. The carrier with the bad back was two landings down. Wenna Raut was at the end of the table inside with her hands in front of her and no pen near them.

He said the three lines out loud on the landing at about a quarter past ten, in the ordinary voice, with the door behind him and the stair going down away from him.

## The Thing She Has Been Holding Since The Twenty-Ninth Of June

The carrier with the bad back had her hand in the small of her back and she came up the last two steps slowly and did not stop at the top.

"I have been holding something since the twenty-ninth of June," she said, "and I have held it through every working day since then because I am not allowed to say it to anybody."

"Say it."

"You stood in that court at the bottom of those stairs on the twenty-ninth of June with the door open behind you and you told a court full of people standing there that a man may set up beside you at that stair tomorrow and charge them, and that there is no version of that afternoon in which you are the man who stops him."

"I remember saying it."

"You said it to me first, in June, and I told you that you had no first use left," said the carrier with the bad back, "and you said you were not going to invent a better sentence than mine, and you have not, and that is the correct thing to have said and it is still the correct thing to say."

She shifted the coil at her feet with her heel.

"And then you spent five months giving the saying of those three lines away, and every one of those months you have been standing at the bottom of that stair, and a man has been standing about thirty feet from you with a price, and I have carried that strap up four flights past him twice a working morning since the summer and I have said good morning to him both times."

## What He Cannot Do About It

"You know I cannot stop him."

"I know that," she said. "I worked that out on the landing in June and I have not needed anybody to tell me since and I am not asking you for it now."

"Then what are you asking me for."

"Nothing. I am telling you what I have been holding." She put her hand on the rail. "I have been holding it since June because in June you gave away the only thing this office had that kept that stair honest, and you gave it away in front of me, and I said thank you for it on a landing at the time and I should not have."

"You were not supposed to say anything."

"He is not the problem either, and I want that said on this landing before anybody says it anywhere else. That man has never lied to me about what that stair is worth, he charges less than it is worth, and he has never in his life been up those four flights."

"I am not supposed to say anything and I said thank you and I have been carrying that since June," said the carrier with the bad back. "A person who gets thanked for a thing thinks about it. That is the whole of what I have learned about mouths in nine years and I have learned it about you and not about any stranger."

## The Three Of Them On The Landing

The other carrier got up off the step.

"There are three of us in that room," he said, "and there are about four people at the bottom of that stair most mornings saying those three lines to each other for nothing, and there is a man thirty feet off with a price, and none of us can stop him and none of us should. I have said all that and I want it recorded that I said it and that I am not against it."

"Then what."

"Then there are three voices on that stair where there was one, and not one of the three of us knows which one a stranger is going to get," he said, "and you are standing at the top of it now saying them, which you were not doing a week ago, and I do not know what happens when the three of us and about four people at the bottom all answer at once and none of us is the one they came up for."

Wenna Raut said, from inside the room: "Say the thing that is actually the problem."

"That I cannot read a row."

"That you cannot read a row," she said. "That is the whole of it. All three of you can give the same stranger a different sentence, and nothing in this city can say which of the three he would have got in March."

## What He Said About It

He stood on that landing with his back to the open door and the cold coming off the stair, and he said the thing he had come up there to say, and he said it to the three of them and to the stair.

"The card at the top of this door has gone soft at two corners and I cannot read the end of the second line of it from a foot away. I am not going to take it down this month. I am going to say those three lines from the top of this stair in the mornings and from the bottom of it in the dark in the evenings, and you three are going to carry on answering whoever comes up."

"And if the three of us ever want to stop answering we are going to have to be three people who all agree on the same morning, and not one of us is going to be able to prove afterwards which of us it was."

"Say the cost of it."

"From today the answer a stranger gets at that door does not depend on which of us happens to be standing at the table, and I have been selling that as the whole of the thing for five months. It is not the whole of the thing."

"What is."

"That from today I cannot be the person a stranger is asking for even when they come up for me, and I will not find that out unless somebody tells me, and none of the three of us is going to."

"That is the first thing you have said out loud in five months that you could not have said in March," said the carrier with the bad back.

"I know. I have been saying the other ones since March because they were free."

## At Half Past Five She Goes Down Past Him

He came down the four flights at half past five with the card end up under his arm and the strap in his hand because he had carried it down the last two flights himself, and the other carrier had the top end down the rest of the way.

The man was at the bottom of that court.

He was not doing anything. He was standing about thirty feet off with his hands behind his back in the way a man stands who has been waiting a while, and he had the whole of that court to himself, and he had been there long enough to have a coat buttoned up.

The carrier with the bad back went past him at about half past five with the strap over her shoulder and said good morning, and he said good morning back, and neither of them said one word about it.

Lin Hao was in the arch and he could hear the whole of it.

## What She Said From The Second Landing

He said the three lines at twenty to five to an empty court from the bottom of his own stair, because that is where he has said them since November, and the court was empty and the arch was empty and the whole of that court had been going on without him for about four minutes already.

She came back up about half of the stair and put her head round the turn.

"You cannot stop him," she said. "So here is the second half of what I have been holding since the twenty-ninth of June and I am saying it in December and not in June."

"Go on."

"I am not going to be able to carry that strap up past that man for another winter," said the carrier with the bad back. "Not because he has done anything. Because I am the one who carries it, and I will be the one who says good morning, and there is going to be a morning when he does not answer me."

Four thousand six hundred and twenty-nine days. Less four thousand six hundred and twenty-two, the third of this one, is seven, and ten less three is seven, and a woman who had been holding one sentence since the twenty-ninth of June said it out loud on a landing in December, and the second half of it is that she is going to stop being able to carry a strap up four flights past a man at the bottom of her own stair.

*Less four thousand five hundred and fifty-nine, the thirtieth of September, is seventy, and the fifty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of December.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand two hundred and nine days, which are seventy-three months and nineteen days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
