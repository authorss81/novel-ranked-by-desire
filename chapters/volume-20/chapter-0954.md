# The Coin In His Hand

Four thousand three hundred and twenty-three was the fourth of this one and the eleventh of this one is day four thousand three hundred and thirty, and four thousand three hundred and thirty less four thousand three hundred and twenty-three is seven, and eleven less four is seven by the month count.

## A Yard Off Cordwain With Four Carts In It

He went down at half past two and along about four hundred yards and one street off, into a carters' yard where four carts stand under a tarpaulin and the gate is open to the street whether anybody is in it or not.

There were two women talking at the tailboard of the second cart with a sheet of oilcloth over their heads, and they were not arguing, and they were not waiting for anything. They were talking the way people talk in a yard when it is raining and they have four minutes.

One of them was saying: "and he says he will take anybody, and he knows the way, and it is not that much."

"How much is it."

"Enough to hire a cart for an hour and not enough to hire one for two."

He stood at the gate for about nine seconds and then went in, because the alternative was to stand in a street listening through a gate, and he had done that in a passage in February and had let it happen and had been wrong to let it happen.

## He Said It Before She Had Finished

The woman turned round. She looked at the plate in his coat and then at his face for about four seconds longer than most people do, and then she carried on with what she was saying, which he understood completely and did not enjoy.

"You are the one off the four flights," she said.

"I am."

"Then listen, because I have got four minutes and it is about you whether you like it or not." She hitched the oilcloth. "There is a man who will walk anybody up four flights of stairs to that door for a small amount of money, and he does it in the street and he does not come up to this office and he does not pretend to be anything else. That is the whole of it and I am not here to complain about anything."

"I am not going to send anybody to him."

She stopped.

"I have not finished."

"I do not send anybody," said Lin Hao. "I did not send anybody in August when a woman was walked up those stairs by a man she had brought herself, and I am not going to send anybody now, and I would have said that to you before you finished that sentence if I had understood it any faster, and I am telling you that I have said it and that you did not hear it."

"You are allowed to tell me it now."

"I am allowed to tell you it now and I am telling you it now," he said.

## The Man Who Knows The Way And Has Not Been Up It

"He is not a fraud," said the other woman.

"I did not say he was."

"You had your face."

"I had my face because I have been up four flights for nine years and a man who will take my stairs for money is a thing I have not had to think about since the thirtieth of December." He put his hands in his coat. "Tell me about him."

"He has been doing it since about the turn of the year. He stands in the street by the market end and he asks people if they want the stairs at the end of the third court, and he says what it costs before he says where it is, which I liked."

"Which you liked."

"He said the amount first," she said. "There is a man on the corner by the market who tells you the address free and you find out afterwards that it is a walk. That one told me the money first and then the address, and he did not lie about either of them, and I want you to understand that I have been going round this city for nine years and not one of them does that."

"He does not live up them."

"He has not been up them," she said. "You can tell by asking him, and I asked him, and he said no, he knows the way because he has walked up them carrying a crate."

## Four Flights Of Air Between A Door And The City

"Which means he has been up them," said Lin Hao.

"He has been up them carrying a crate for a man who sells fish and he says he has not been up them to that door, and both of those are true, and he did not try to make them the same."

Lin Hao stood in the wet yard and worked out what he had.

There were four flights of air between that door and the city, and that had always been the arrangement, and he had spent nine years thinking of it as a wall. It was not a wall. It was a walk, and a walk is a thing a person does, and anybody who lives near the bottom of a walk knows how to do it.

A man had worked that out in about nine days and had set a price on it, and the price was small, and he had not lied to a soul.

The address was still free. That was the thing he kept coming back to in that wet yard and could not get past. The man in the street was not selling the address. The address was on a door in the open and it had been on a door in the open for seven weeks and would be there on the thirtieth of March whether anybody liked it or not.

What was being sold was the four flights. And four flights is not information. Four flights is a man's knees.

"How does he know how long it takes," he said.

"He walks them himself first," said the other woman. "Every time. He walks up and he comes down again and then he waits, and then he walks the person up with them, so it takes about twice what it should take and he does not care."

"And he charges for that."

"He charges for that," she said. "That is the part I would not have believed in February."

"He has been up a few times," said the other woman. "Not many. He says he does not do it every day because there is not a market for it every day."

"And everybody he has walked has got there."

"Every one of them got there," she said. "That is the part that has me."

## She Had Come Up Four Flights To Ask Whether It Was Normal

"You have been up there," said Lin Hao.

"I was up there on the second of this month with a paper about a landlord who will not answer me, and I got an answer in about six minutes, and it cost me the whole morning, because I have a place at the end of that yard and the morning is what pays for the week." She got the oilcloth tighter.

"And I have a second paper. And there is a man in the street who will walk me up and be paid for it, and I have got enough in my apron for that and not enough for the morning."

"So you have not paid it."

"No."

"Why not."

The woman looked at him for about four seconds.

"Because you are the man who took the stairs away," she said. "Not the stairs. The morning. Somebody told me in a lane that you gave that address to a man who was paid nothing to give it away, and that was generous, and I believed it, because that is what generosity looks like."

"And now there is a man in the street who will walk me up for money and I have got a piece of paper in my coat, and I came up four flights this morning to ask a person whether that was a normal thing to do."

"You came up four flights to ask me."

"Yes."

"That cost you the morning."

"Yes," she said. "And I would like to have got something for it."

## What He Went Back Down For

He told her what she had come for, in about nine seconds, in the ordinary voice, with the yard gate open behind him.

Nobody will ever be charged for being told where that door is, he said. The address is free. It has been free since the thirtieth of December and it is going to be free, and he was not going to tell her not to use the man.

She said, "That is the first time anybody has told me not to use the man."

"Then take the morning," said Lin Hao, "and walk up at ten past ten when the light is still on the card, and it will cost you a morning every time you do it, and that morning is yours and I cannot get it back for you."

"And the man in the street."

"He is not a fraud and he is not lying to anybody and he has not been up those stairs to that door in his life," said Lin Hao. "I am not going to find him and I am not going to have anybody sent, and in about four months somebody in this city is going to decide that is the sort of thing this office should put a stop to, and it will not be me, because I have got nothing that reaches a man standing in a street."

He went back up the four flights at about ten to four with about forty minutes of the card's hours left and stood at the top of them and found that he had been given a piece of information and had nothing whatever to do with it.

That was new, and it took him the length of one flight to understand that it was new. In nine years there had not been a single thing anybody could tell him about that door that he had an instrument for. A wrong hour could be traced to a piece of paper. A wrong hour said out loud could not be traced to anything, and the fourth clause of the twenty-seventh had been the proof of it.

A man with a small coin in his hand was the proof of it twice over, because a coin is the one instrument in this city that survives being handed on.

And the worst of it was that the man was right. A thing that costs a person four flights twice over is worth something, and a man who walks up and comes down again first and then walks the person up with them is charging for exactly what he does and not one grain more than that.

Nothing in this city can tell a person they ought to do it for nothing, because the person doing it is not this office and cannot be reached by this office.

The other woman said one more thing to his back as he went.

"It is not much," she said. "What he charges. I want you to know that it is not much, because you will hear it somewhere and I would rather you heard the smallness of it from me."

Four thousand three hundred and thirty days. Less four thousand three hundred and twenty-three, the fourth of this one, is seven, and a woman in a yard off Cordwain said that a man will walk a stranger up four flights of a third court for an amount a person can carry in an apron, and he has never been up them himself, and every person he has walked has got there.

*Less four thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is forty-one, and the forty-eighth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last forty-one of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand nine hundred and ten days, which are sixty-three months and twenty days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
