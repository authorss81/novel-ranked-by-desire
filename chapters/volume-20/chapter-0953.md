## The Sentence With A Hole In It

## The Only Record Nobody Has Produced

"Your file has three months of nothing on it," she said. About two hundred sheets went through it in three months and not one had anything to do with you."

"That is the first thing anybody has ever been able to say to me."

"It is the second thing. The first was about your chest." She looked at him properly for the first time. "Now the second one. That office at the top of your stair is the only record in this city that has never been produced. Not refused. Never produced. There is no request for it anywhere, no schedule, no index and no copy, and I have been an auditor for about nine years and have never had it on one."

"You could have asked for it."

"So could nine hundred people," said Ines Duarte. "Asking is a form, and that office has no form and no head and no sign and no name on the door, and a form sent to a door with three lines on it comes back unopened and burns."

"What are you going to do about it."

## Three Mouths And Not His

"I have read your three lines in three other mouths," she said. "In a hall in November, in a lane, and in a market at the end of this month, from three people who are not you and who are all slightly wrong in a different place."

"The interesting part is that I have never once asked you for them, in nine years, and you have never once offered, and neither of those has been a decision. I want you to hear that from a person who is paid to produce."

"Why have you never asked."

"Because I have never once been in a room with you and a piece of paper both of us can see. That has been available to me since about the fourth year."

## What She Would Have To Have

He said, "Say what you would have to have."

"To audit you," she said, "I would have to have the three lines from your own mouth, on a day I chose, in a room I chose, and written them down myself. Then I would have something to check a hundred and fifty wrong copies against."

"Which you cannot have."

"Which I cannot have without taking them off you. And here is the part I have thought about for about three months and not said out loud. An auditor who wants the thing she cannot produce does not go home. She goes and gets it from whoever is holding it."

"There is one person holding it and he is standing in front of me with his coat on," said Ines Duarte, "and he gave it away for nothing on the thirtieth of December and would give it again tomorrow."

"I would say them."

"You would say them to a woman who has a pencil," said Ines Duarte, "and afterwards there would be a copy in this city with my hand on it, and it would be the first one anybody could call the true one, and in about four years a stranger would come up four flights with it and ask you to confirm it."

"So it is the same six things."

"It is, and I have got about four of them for a living," she said.

## Afterwards, In The Bad Light

She took herself down onto the edge of the table and put her boots back on one at a time, and her hands were red at the first two knuckles of the right one from a pencil she has been holding for three months.

"Right. The rest of it, standing up, and then you can go."

## The Corner Of The Second Sheet

She squared the two hundred sheets back into their pile, then went through them for the second one, squared that as well, turned its corner about two inches and let it down, and it stood a finger's width off the pile in the curve where it had been.

"Do not say anything about that," she said. "I have done it four times in nine years and this is the fifth room."

He said nothing about it.

"It is a hall that has nothing in it and it is the best I have, and I will put it back the way I found it, and if you ever did tell then it would be because you read something in a pile of paper that I had no business moving."

She wiped the pencil dust off the corner with her thumb and left a grey thumbprint about the size of a nail on the top sheet, which was the only mark she made in that room and she made it on purpose.

## The Question She Handed Back To Him

At the door she stopped with her hand on the frame.

"Answer me one and I will answer you one," she said. "Why did you let a man in a passage read that address out loud in February when you could have stopped him, and why did you do it again four seconds after you had decided not to."

"Go and find out who has added a fourth clause to those three lines," said Ines Duarte. "It has been going round Cordwain since about the twenty-third, and I have heard it in three rooms and every room has written it down as one sentence."

"That is not a service being sold yet. It is a service being used," said Ines Duarte. "Somebody is going to want to be paid for it, and when they do there will be a price, and it will not be a fraud and the man will be right."
