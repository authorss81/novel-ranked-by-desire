# Not Said Again

Four thousand six hundred and forty-three was the twenty-fourth of this one and the thirtieth of this one is day four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, and four thousand six hundred and forty-nine less four thousand six hundred and forty-three, the twenty-fourth of this one, is six, and thirty less twenty-four is six by the month count.

## The Last Working Morning Of The Year

The book came up at a quarter to ten on the carriers' strap and the two of them took it up between them and set it down on that table the way it has been set down every working morning for nine years, which is square on a square, and not one person in that building has ever been able to say why. The three of them were in the room before it was.

There was a woman with a question about a floor of a house at about eleven and a man with a piece of paper he could not read at about one and a woman at about two who had brought something of her own up four flights and was told, by the carrier with the bad back, in the ordinary voice, that what this office makes of a piece of paper she brought is ours and not hers.

None of them asked for him.

He said the three lines out loud on the top landing at a quarter past ten, in the ordinary voice, with the door open behind him, because the end of the second line of that card is soft at the top left corner and he has been going up there to read it since the third of December.

Then he went down the four flights carrying it with the wire round the buckle twice, and took it along that road and put it down where it goes.

## At The Counter Under The Window At About Four

He went down and round and along that road to the passage off Cordwain at about half past three, because three times in twelve months that counter turns a sheet round on its rail and lets a stranger in the street read the figure at the top of it, and he has missed none of the three in nine years.

There were people in that passage who had never seen the man behind the counter and would not see him again. At about four he took the fifty-first off that rail and put the fifty-second on the same two nails, carrying no new line, and turned it round so the figure faced the room, because he has always done that.

"Four thousand six hundred and forty-nine less one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine is three thousand one hundred and fifty, which is thirty-five times ninety. Thirty-six sheets and thirty-five quarters, differing by one, and the figure standing at the head of that sheet is eight hundred and fifty-two, and it has stood at that figure through thirty-six of those sheets and thirty-five of those quarters."

A woman near the front wrote something down. A man beside her asked her what it was. She told him it was not that. He said he had stood behind that counter for nine years and it was a figure and not a thing, and she put her pencil down and said so was a thing to her, and he said it was and went back to his work.

Lin Hao stood well back against the wall of that passage and let the man get on with it, on the grounds that a man who leans over another man's work has not understood what the work is for.

## At Ten Past Four, At The Bottom Of His Own Stair

The light went off the wall above his own door at about ten past four for the season and the court went the colour it goes, and there were about nine people in it, and he went and took his own second step from the bottom, with the arch at his shoulder on one side and a wall a man's height on the other.

He said the three lines out loud one last time.

He gave them the whole of it: the street, and the door at the head of four flights off a third court, and the hours, which run from a quarter to ten to half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour. He said them at the length and at the pause and in the voice.

There was a woman at the back with a shawl over her head who had come up that arch in the cold at twenty to five, and a man with a bundle about thirty feet off with nothing to sell, and two people he did not know who had come up to see what was happening at the bottom of a stair that has a card on the door.

They had not been sent for and they had not been told anything. They had come up an arch because there have been people at that court saying three lines to each other since before the twenty-eighth of October, and a person who has been sent by somebody else turns up to look at that.

## What It Costs, Said Out Loud

"I am going to say what this costs and then I am going to stop talking, and then the three of you are going to carry on up there without me, and that is the arrangement and I have not got a say in it."

He looked at the woman at the back.

"About four people a fortnight in this city cannot read that card. That is not a guess, that is what the card is for and it has always been what the card is for, and it is the only reason three lines were ever nailed to a door instead of nothing."

"This office will not build them a second one, and it will not put a lamp under that arch, and it will not stand a person here in the dark on a rota, and the reason is that every one of those is a copy and four of them are a list, and a list of who cannot read is a list of people."

"They will go on coming at twenty to five and they will go on standing in the dark under an arch."

"And the wrong copies keep going," he said. "They are going now, at this moment, out of this court, from my own mouth, and there are four in the afternoon on about half of them and there is a version with the second court in it and there is a version where the door is at the top of three flights, and none of those were invented by anybody. They were said by people who were being kind."

"And I will never know how many," said Lin Hao. "Nothing in this city can count them and it is not going to start, and I am the last man in it who would try, and I have spent nine years not writing down the name of a single person and I am not going to start in December."

## What He Says Next

"I am not going to say them again."

He said it in the ordinary voice, at the bottom of his own stair, in the dark, at about nine minutes to five on the last working evening of the year, to about nine people who were standing there and to the three who were four flights above him with a door open between.

"He is not going to say them again."

He put his hands down by his sides.

"I built that on the fourteenth of December of the twelfth year and it took me one winter and it is the only thing I have ever built. It was a voice instead of a number. A number can be read at four feet in the dark by anybody with a lamp, and a mouth cannot, and I proved that to myself on the twenty-sixth of November with a woman who asked me to say it again and could not hear the second time either."

"I am the only copy of the right version of those three lines in this city that anybody could check a stranger against. Not the only one who knows them. About four people at the bottom of this stair say them correctly to each other and a woman at a market says them and a woman at the end of a Row has been saying them since before I did."

"A man cannot be produced by somebody who does not already trust him. I worked that out about nine weeks ago from a woman in a chapel who was entirely right about it, and I have been getting it wrong in a different direction every day since."

## The Court Does Whatever The Court Does

That is the whole of what he said and he said it at the length and it took about four minutes, and then he stopped, and not one person asked him anything, which he had not expected and had not prepared for.

Then the court did whatever the court does.

The man with the bundle said the three lines out loud to the woman with the shawl over her head, to see whether she had them, and he had the street and he had the door at the head of four flights.

She took them back off him one at a time, and the hours she had were four in the afternoon, which has been fastened onto those three lines since the last week of January by somebody who was being kind about a light going off a street. He gave her the right ones again straight away and she got all three of them.

Then the four feet of pavement went on without him, the way it had been going on since about the twenty-eighth of October, and one of them turned to another one and gave them the street and the door and the hours, and the hours were not the ones on the card, and Lin Hao stood on his own step and let it happen, and it is not a thing that can be called back.

Four thousand six hundred and forty-nine days. Less four thousand six hundred and forty-three, the twenty-fourth of this one, is six, and thirty less twenty-four is six, and a man said three lines out loud at the bottom of his own stair for the last time and then said what it costs, and the fifty-second sheet went up on the same two nails at four that afternoon carrying no new line, and about four feet of pavement at the bottom of that court went on without him.

*Less four thousand five hundred and fifty-nine, the thirtieth of September, is ninety, and the fifty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last ninety of them, and at about four this afternoon it came down and the fifty-second went up on the same two nails carrying no new line.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand two hundred and twenty-nine days, which are seventy-four months and nine days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
