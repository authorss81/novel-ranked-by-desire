# The Only Copy

The seventh of this one and the fourteenth of this one is day four thousand four hundred and eighty-three, and four thousand four hundred and eighty-three less four thousand four hundred and seventy-six, the seventh of this one, is seven, and fourteen less seven is seven by the month count.

## Going Back Down The Same Road

He went back down that road on the second working day of the fourth week with nothing in his hands, having been eleven minutes inside that door three weeks before and having been outside it every working morning since, and the wide door was open in daylight again and the man at the bench knew him by his walk.

"I am not taking the tenth," said Lin Hao.

The binder did not stop work and did not look up.

"I know you are not," he said. "I have had a bureau book in on the fourth and I have had two men asking after my ninth and there is nothing on that board for the tenth and there has not been since about noon on the thirtieth of last month, because I took it down myself."

"Then it is gone."

"It has gone the way a date goes."

He put the shaving down. "I cannot hold a date. There is no holding a date. A man who holds a date is a man telling a bench that it is empty when it is not, and I have two other people who would lose their work because of it."

"You can have the tenth of the month after or you can have the twelfth, and if you do not take one of them today then both of them are gone by this evening and you will not have either."

"I will tell you now as well: my next clear run after the twelfth is about five weeks past that, and I am telling you now so that you cannot say afterwards that I kept it from you."

## Saying The Reason Out Loud To A Tradesman

"I know why," said Lin Hao. "I am going to say it anyway because you are the only person in this city who has to carry it and you should have it from me in my own words rather than work it out."

The binder leaned on the bench with both forearms and let him.

"A cart in daylight going four hundred yards from a rail to a binder's bench in the middle of a working morning is the only copy of nine years of other people's answers going past streets with a strap round it and no cover on it. Every person who sees it comes and looks."

"Some of them go and stand where it went and wait for it coming back. And then there is a crowd outside a building four hundred yards off, on a working morning, watching a door, and that is a thing that did not exist this morning and would never go away."

"That is a real reason," said the binder. "It is the reason I would have given, and I have heard it from about four men in thirty-one years and none of them came back."

"There is a second half and it is worse."

"There usually is, with you people."

"There is a rule in the office four flights above that stair," said Lin Hao, "that the book is never upstairs overnight. It has been in force since the twenty-fifth of October of the year before last. I have watched it for nine years and it has never once been broken, including on the mornings when I could not manage the stair and had to be late."

"And that is in your way."

"It is not in my way at all, and I have only just noticed that," said Lin Hao. "That rule is about a night. It is about the stair and the dark and a book of other people's answers lying in an empty room four floors up with a door that a stranger could try. It says nothing whatever about a day."

"Whoever wrote it was thinking about nights, and I have been carrying it round my head for nine years as though it were about the book, and it is about the building, and the building is not going anywhere."

The binder picked his shaving back up.

"Then you have been guarding a door against a thing that walks out of it in daylight," he said, "and I have been in this trade thirty-one years and I have never once heard a man put it that plainly."

## The Thing That Has Not Happened In Nine Years

"Take the twelfth," said Lin Hao.

The binder wrote it on the board on the end of the bench and turned the board round so he could see it, and it was the twelfth of the month after, and it was four weeks off.

"That is all the arranging there is," said Lin Hao.

"That is all the arranging there is. Now go and think about what you have just done, because you did not decide not to take the tenth. You decided it on the thirtieth of last month at about four in a cellar with four people in it."

"And then you went up four flights and wrote nothing down, and then you booked a cart, and then you told me a street on the seventh with the door shut that you were not coming, and you have sat on it for a fortnight being a man who is going to take a date."

The binder did not look up. "I have had men cancel on the day and I have had men cancel a fortnight before, and both of those are ordinary. I have never had one sit on it for a fortnight. What have you been doing?"

"Working," said Lin Hao.

"You have been standing in that room being asked what is happening and not saying, which is worse than saying, because it is the other half of saying."

The binder put the board down. "There is one person on this earth who knows what that book is and it is not the woman who keeps your sentences. That is not a complaint. It is an arrangement."

"But it is an arrangement with one leg, and this morning the leg you have just taken away is the only one holding up a date."

## Down The Road With Five Weeks

He came out of that room at about a quarter past two and did not get as far as the turning before he had to stand still for a minute, which was not his chest, or not only his chest.

He had gone down that road on the seventeenth of the month before last with two quotes. He had gone down it on the thirtieth with a head said in a cellar and a date taken and a cart hired. He had gone past it on the seventh with the door shut.

And on this afternoon he had gone down it to un-take a date he had taken three weeks earlier, and had paid for the privilege of changing his mind in four weeks, and had not paid in money, and had paid in a promise.

He had made a thing come due in November by saying it out loud in a frost on a stair, and the thing had come due in June on an ordinary working morning with the light on the wall, and he was not keeping it today, and he was not keeping it for the reason he had made it, and the reason had not gone anywhere in seven months and was standing in a room off a market with its hand on a board.

That was the part he could not get round. He had expected to want a better reason. He had gone into that room with sentences ready for having changed his mind on a rational basis and every one of them had been a version of the same one, and he had come out with the reason exactly where he had brought it in, because the reason was still true and a true reason does not improve with age.

He got as far as the turning and stood in the street with the light on him and said out loud, to an empty pavement, in daylight, on a working afternoon, the thing that had been sitting in him since the seventh.

"Not one person in that building knows."

Then he said it again, because the first time it had come out as a question and the second time it came out as a fact.

## Looking For A Rule About A Day

At about five he was at the bottom of his own stair with his hands empty, which is the only way he ever comes down it, and he stood there with his back against the wall about the height of a man and made himself go through the twelve.

An answer goes in writing. Both question and answer go in the words used. Nothing is taken from a person, and every written answer is carried to the person who asked by a hand whose taking of it is written down. There is the fold with the flap gummed shut for a paper the asker brought up themselves, and there is the reading aloud in that room once with the person it is about in it, and a clerk who does not read a row back from memory anywhere.

He went through the rest of them standing in the light at the bottom of his own stair, and he could not find a single one of them that had anything to say about nine days, or about a day, or about a street.

Not one of the twelve has ever been about the record leaving anywhere. Every one of them is about the room, or about the stair, or about a person, or about what a person is not to have done to them. The twelve were written by people who were thinking about the building, and the building has not moved in nine years, and he has been carrying them about as though they were a fence.

The fence was never around the book. It was around the door.

He had a binder, and a cart, and a date, and an argument with a woman he had not had yet, and a nine-day hole coming, and not one person in the four flights above that stair knew a single thing about any of it, and the reason was that he had a rule about who is in a room, and the rule was right, and he had used it to keep the news out of a building he had personally decided to break.

Four thousand four hundred and eighty-three days. Less four thousand four hundred and seventy-six, the seventh of this one, is seven, and fourteen less seven is seven, and a man gave a date back to a binder in a room off a market and walked out four weeks poorer with the same reason in his mouth that he had brought in.

*Less four thousand four hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is fourteen, and the fiftieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fourteen of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of September.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand and sixty-three days, which are sixty-eight months and twenty-three days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
