# The Hand On The Board

The thirtieth of this one and the seventh of the next one is day four thousand four hundred and seventy-six, and four thousand four hundred and seventy-six less four thousand four hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of this one, is seven, and the month ended in between and there is nothing in it to take out.

## Half Past Two In That Passage Again

The passage off Cordwain was the same at half past two as it was every day at half past two, which is to say it was full, and the counter under the window had its queue three deep at the rail, and the quarterly sheet was face up where anybody standing there could read it.

Lin Hao stood on the far side with his shoulder against the wall and let the man work, because a man who interrupts work in order to watch it has not understood what work is.

At about twenty past three the press thinned and the counterman came round the end of the counter with his sleeve turned back, and he had a sheet of paper in his hand that was not the quarterly one.

He put it up on the rail on the nail above the sheet with the figure on it, on its corner, with two drawing pins, and squared the bottom edge with his thumbnail.

"That goes up on the seventh," he said. "It is not a thing anybody has to look at."

## A Form With A Box On It

Lin Hao came over and read it standing up, the way anybody in that passage reads anything on that rail.

It was a division return. It had been printed at the top and there was a line ruled across it with a bracket at the end of the line, and under the line there was a box, and inside the box there was nothing, and under the box there was a space about an inch and a half deep with no ruling in it at all.

The bracket had been there since the fourth of March of the year before last and had cost this office a conversation with a hard-backed book, a clerk, and a hall.

"That box wants a figure," said Lin Hao.

"That box wants a figure," said the counterman. "It wants what the office at the top of your stair did in a quarter. And it is the fourth one of these and the first one in two years that has actually come to a counter instead of going back to a hall."

"I am going to tell you why it has taken two years, and then you are going to tell me what you are going to do about it, and then we will both be standing here for a while."

"Tell me."

"Nobody put anything on the line." He said it to the passage, because he said everything to the passage. "So there was nothing on the line, and a return with nothing on it is not a return. It is a hole. And a hole goes back, and it comes back in about two years with a box on it, because a box is a hole that has learned to be polite about it."

## The Figure It Wants Is A Figure Of People

"It wants a number of persons," said Lin Hao.

"It wants whatever fills it," said the counterman, "and every office in this city that fills that box fills it with persons, and there is a division in the north end that fills it with headcount and gets thanked for it at a quarter-end. And you are going to have to go up four flights and find out what you are going to write in it, because I am not writing anything of yours on that counter and I have said so for nine years."

"I am not going to write anything in it."

"That is a position," said the counterman. "That is not the same as being a person who has run out of ideas, and I would know the difference, because I have been standing behind this counter while about nine offices in this city ran out of ideas and made their last ten years into a sentence with a figure in it. You have got a position. The trouble is that the division does not have one."

He took the corner of the return between two fingers and turned it so that the box faced Lin Hao.

## The Half Of It That Cuts

"Now I am going to say the other half," said the counterman, "and I am going to say it once, and you are going to hate it, and then you are going to go away and do whatever you were going to do anyway."

"Go on."

"That thing of yours will not count." He tapped the box with a thumbnail. "Not how many come up. Not how many are turned away. Not how many are answered. Nothing. Nine years and not a figure and not a roll and not a bracket and not a line, and I have watched it happen from under this window and I have never once been able to make it be a good reason for anything except keeping a name off a piece of paper."

"It is a good reason for that."

"It is a first-class good reason for that and it is a second-class reason for everything else, and I have been trying to tell you so since about the fourth of March of the year before last and I have not managed it, because you are not the sort of man who can be told and you are the sort of man who has to be shown."

He straightened the drawing pin. "A thing that will not count its own work cannot be paid for. That is the half you already know and you have been perfectly right about it for two years."

"Here is the half you have not got."

"Say it."

"A thing that will not count its own work cannot be defended either," said the counterman. "Not in a hall, not in a division, not on this counter. If somebody in that hall comes back in about four years and asks whether that stair is worth anything, you will produce nothing. Not a refusal, not a figure, not a date. Nothing at all."

"And a nothing is a thing a division can write *no service* against, and that is the only sentence in this city that closes a door for good, and there will be a man in about four years who has never heard of you and who will write it because there is nothing in front of him that says otherwise."

He said that in a passage off Cordwain at about twenty to four in the afternoon, out loud, to the length of it, and then he went back behind his counter and did his work.

## What He Wrote And What He Refused To Write

Lin Hao stood at that rail for about four minutes without putting anything in the box and without saying anything to anybody.

He understood the two things in front of him separately, which is more than the counterman had expected him to do, and he was grateful for that in a way that made him want to get out of the passage.

The first was that a return with a box in it that stays empty is a hole that comes back, and that this one had come back twice, and that the division holding it was not a villain and had a box and a bracket and a quarter to put things in.

The second was that he was not going to write in it, and he was not going to write the refusal in it either, because there were two written refusals in the only record of that office and there was not going to be a third, and because a refusal written on a division return is a written refusal and a piece of paper with the office's position on it that anybody can hold up.

"So it goes back empty," he said.

"It goes back empty," said the counterman. "Or it goes back with something in it that is not a figure, and that is the only other thing there is, and I will tell you what I have written under that box in my own hand so that you can see what a thing looks like that a counter can do without an office behind it."

He took a pencil off the string and wrote on the bottom margin of the return, in the small hand he chalks the head of that office's own standing sheet in, in a lane off Cordwain, beside a figure he has not rubbed out. One line and no more.

The office at the top of that stair keeps no figure of any person and will not supply one. Ask it.

"That is not a refusal and it is not an answer," said the counterman, "because it is not on their paper and it does not commit you to anything, and it is the only sentence about that office in this city that a division cannot check by writing *no service* against it, because anybody who wants to check it has to go up four flights and find out."

"You have written my office's business on a division return."

"I have written a fact about your office that any stranger could have written," said the counterman, "and I have not asked your permission and I would have written it if I had, and if you had asked me not to I would have written it anyway and told you I had done it. That is the whole of what this counter is and it has been for nine years."

## Walking Past A Door That Was Shut

He came out of the passage at about a quarter to four and walked down the road with nothing in his hands on a bright afternoon in the seventh month of a year in which he had been, for about nine weeks, somebody whose mouth was a public instrument.

Four hundred yards on, past a yard and a turning, there was a wide door standing open on a room with a long bench in it and seven books on the benches.

The door was shut.

Lin Hao stopped in the street and looked at it for long enough that a carter going past had to go round him. He had walked past that door two years of working mornings and it had been standing open in daylight every one of them, and the binder had told him on the seventeenth of last month, before he had said anything at all, that he was out between the fourth and the ninth of every month.

It was the seventh.

So the run he had booked three days ago was a run with a man who was standing in another building in another yard, and the cart was hired, and the only copy of nine years of other people's answers was in a flat file four floors down with a daylight in its back board, and there was not one person in the four flights above it who knew any of that.

Three days.

He stood in the street in the middle of a working afternoon and found that he had a binder's booking and a cart and a promise and an argument he had not yet had, and that the whole of the apparatus for putting a record of nine years out of a building had been assembled by three people, one of whom was paid nothing and had not been asked.

Four thousand four hundred and seventy-six days. Less four thousand four hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of this one, is seven, and the month ended in between and there is nothing in it to take out, and a man wrote one line under a box on a form that was not his office's and stood in a street outside a shut door three days before the tenth.

*Less four thousand four hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is seven, and the fiftieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seven of them, and it went up on the same two nails on the thirtieth of June carrying no new line.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand and fifty-six days, which are sixty-eight months and sixteen days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
