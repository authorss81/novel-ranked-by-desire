# Brack Prices A Mouth

Four thousand three hundred and thirty-seven was the eighteenth of this one and the twenty-fifth of this one is day four thousand three hundred and forty-four, and four thousand three hundred and forty-four less four thousand three hundred and thirty-seven is seven, and twenty-five less eighteen is seven by the month count.

## Nine Stools And A Slate And Twelve Books

The lane off Cordwain is about nine feet wide and has nine stools in it, and the stools belong to a man called Emil Brack who does nine districts and keeps the day on a slate and has twelve books behind him that not a clerk has been permitted to touch since the second year.

He had it in about a week before Lin Hao came down, which was faster than nine days and slower than a man who was inventing.

"Sit," said Brack. "You have come to ask me what I have got and I am going to tell you and then you are going to tell me I have it wrong, and I want to get the telling part out of the way."

"Have you written it anywhere."

"No," said Brack.

"Have you written the figure anywhere."

"No," said Brack, "and I am going to have to keep not doing that for about four more days, so ask me again then."

## The Figure He Has Got

"You have got a figure for the man in the street."

"I have got a figure for a man who takes money for four flights of stair in a third court," said Brack. "That is a different man and I would be careful about the difference, because the man in the street is not what that lane is talking about. The lane is talking about what it costs to be got to a door."

"How much have you got."

"About four times what a woman in a yard off Cordwain told a man in a white plate on the eleventh of this month."

Lin Hao did not say anything.

"You did not tell me her number and you have not told me that there is a number," said Brack, "and I have had four times something for about a week, which is a guess with a shape to it, and I want it on the wall before anybody puts it there properly."

"You are four times out."

"I am four times out," said Emil Brack, "and I know I am, and I have had a week to work out why and I have got it exactly wrong, and I would like you to tell me in what direction."

## What He Is Pricing And What He Thinks He Is Pricing

"You have priced a man," said Lin Hao. "You have priced the man who does the walking, and the walking is worth about what you have written, and probably a bit under it, because he walks up and comes down again before he takes anybody, which doubles it."

"Then why am I out by four."

"Because you have priced what a person in this city will pay to have four flights taken off them," said Lin Hao. "And that is not what is happening. That is what happens when a thing has been happening for long enough that people have believed it for a while."

"Believed what."

"That they cannot do it themselves."

## What The Lane Has Actually Said

"Say the rest of that," said Brack. "Because if it is what I think it is then I have had that sentence in a lane for a week and I have not written it down because I did not trust it."

"You have had it in a lane for a week."

"I have had it in a lane for a week," said Emil Brack, "and what the lane has actually got, in about nine mouths, is not that. The lane has got that there is a man who will take you up four flights, and that he does not go in, and that the man in the white plate knows about it and has not stopped him. That is the sentence in this lane and it is the wrong one."

"That is worse."

"That is much worse," said Brack, "and it is much worse because it is true, and because it puts you at the bottom of it, and because about four of the nine people saying it have never met you and would not know your face."

"I know."

"You did not know that on the eleventh of this month."

"No," said Lin Hao. "I did not know that on the eleventh of this month and I have been going around a city for a fortnight believing that the fourth clause was a mistake."

Brack put the chalk down on the rail and stood looking at the middle of his own lane for about four seconds.

"That is a good piece of work," he said. "That is a better piece of work than anything on my slate this month, and I am not writing it down, because a thing I write down about four flights becomes a rate in about a fortnight and then it is a rate somebody can be put on."

## The Figure That Is Not Rubbed Out

The slate on the wall behind those nine stools has carried two things in it since the autumn and there are two things in it now.

On the left, in small chalk, is a figure that has been on that slate since before the card went up, which is about forty people who will not have to be told where a thing came from, out by more than half a quarter, and it is not rubbed out.

On the right, in another man's small chalk, in four-inch letters that he did not ask to be made smaller, is the head of that office's standing sheet, which went onto that slate in the autumn and has not moved since and is not going to be argued with.

There is a third figure in Emil Brack's head, out by more than half a quarter again, with no unit he can write after it, and it is not on the slate and he has said for a year that it will not be this year.

"You have got three figures in this lane," said Lin Hao, looking at the wall.

"I have got two figures on that slate and one in my head and they are about three different subjects and they have been standing next to each other since the autumn and you are the only man alive who has ever asked me to separate them."

"I am not asking you to separate them."

## The Figure He Does Not Own

"I am not going to correct yours," he said. "I want that said in this lane and in the ordinary voice so that anybody standing at either end of it hears it."

"That is the first thing you have ever not done in this lane."

"It is because I do not own it," said Lin Hao. "A figure on that slate that I put there belongs to me and it is wrong in about four days, and every person who takes it off this wall believes it came out of a room with a door at the head of four flights. Yours came out of about nine years of a man watching a street, and I have been in it twice and I could not have got it."

"So it stands."

"It stands wrong and in the expensive direction, and it stands wrong because a lane prices whatever is being asked about, and the asking changed about a week ago and the lane has not noticed yet." He put his thumb on the rail. "There is a third thing about it and I would rather it came out of my own mouth here than out of a wall in a week."

"Go on."

"About forty people will not have to be told where it came from, and that number is not going to move this year or next," said Lin Hao, "and it is still true, and it is true in this lane as much as it is anywhere, and I am not going to argue about it here because arguing about it in a lane is how a figure gets a subject put on it."

## What He Asked For Instead

"Ask me," said Brack.

"Has anybody in this lane been told those three lines by somebody who was not me."

Brack looked at him for a long moment and then went along that lane and shut the street door and came back and put the chalk in his pocket, which is a thing he has done twice in nine years and both times because a conversation had stopped being about a figure.

"Three names," he said. "A woman at the end of a market floor who says them out loud to people who cannot read a clock. A woman at a stop where the service stopped calling, who says them to whoever is standing there. And a man who delivers bread in the third district and has been saying them at a door for about a month."

"Three names, said out loud, not written."

"I am not writing them," said Emil Brack. "I will not say them twice and I will not say them in that order again, and I have not had a list and I am not going to make one, because a list of people who say a thing for nothing is a list of people, and I keep lists of nine districts and not of this."

"There are two more."

"There are two more," said Brack. "And I am not going to say them to you today, and I will tell you why, and it is not to be clever."

## Why He Would Not Say The Last Two

"Because the first three of them are doing it in the open where anybody can stand and ask them, and the last two are not," said Brack. "One works a floor where about nine hundred people a day go past her and cannot stop her, and the other stands on a platform in the dark at about six in the morning where about nine people can hear her and not one of them can catch her."

"And when I have said to them what is being said about them."

"Then you will say two sentences to two women in two rooms, and neither of those women will be on a wall and neither of them will be in this lane, and if you put it in this lane you will have made a price for two women who have never asked for one."

He took the chalk out of his pocket and put it on the rail and did not write on the slate.

"The man in the street," he said. "Are you going to say anything to him?"

"I will not go and find him and nothing will be sent."

"That is the second time somebody has told me that about a thing in this city."

"It is the first time for me," said Lin Hao, "and I have said it twice this month, and both times to somebody who did not ask me to say it."

Four thousand three hundred and forty-four days. Less four thousand three hundred and thirty-seven, the eighteenth of this one, is seven, and a lane off Cordwain had a figure for four flights of a third court that was wrong by about four times and in the direction that makes a service expensive, and a man who prices things for a living said it did not go on the slate because a rate in a lane becomes a thing people can be put on.

*Less four thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is fifty-five, and the forty-eighth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-five of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand nine hundred and twenty-four days, which are sixty-four months and four days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
