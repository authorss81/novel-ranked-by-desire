# The Woman Refused Under It

Four thousand five hundred and thirty-nine was the tenth of this one and the seventeenth of this one is day four thousand five hundred and forty-six, and four thousand five hundred and forty-six less four thousand five hundred and thirty-nine, the tenth of this one, is seven, and seventeen less ten is seven by the month count.

## Saying It Out Loud On The Seventh Day

He told her at about eleven, with the door open and people in the room, which is how he says things and which she had told him in July was the cruellest place any of it could be said.

"A woman came up those four flights on the tenth with a piece of paper of her own and asked what we told her about it, and the carrier with the bad back told her she cannot have that back, and I was standing at the end of that table and I let it happen."

Wenna Raut put her pen down on the line and squared it against the edge of the table.

"And you have had that since a quarter past eleven on the tenth."

"Seven days."

"Seven days." She said it the way she says the words that were used. "I am not going to ask you why, because I have been in this room nine years and I know exactly why, and it is that you needed the only other person in this building to keep telling you things, and you got what you wanted."

"Yes."

"Go on."

"There is no authority behind it," said Lin Hao. "It is not one of the twelve. I went through all twelve in my head on that morning and it is not one of them and it has no date and there is no form anywhere in this city for making one or for undoing one, and it was enforced on a stranger at about a quarter past eleven by a person who believes in it, in the same voice, and neither of us could have stopped it."

## She Came Back Up On The Seventeenth

She came back at about half past eleven on the seventeenth of the month with the same sheet in her hand and the same date on it and the bracket at the end of the line, and she was not angry.

That was the first thing Lin Hao noticed and it was the thing he could not get past for the rest of the day. She came up four flights into a room she had been turned out of seven days before, and she came in past Wenna Raut without stopping, and she put the sheet on the table, and she said thank you for seeing me.

"I am sorry about the tenth," said Wenna Raut. "That was not mine to do and I have been told about it this morning for the first time."

"I know it was not yours."

## The Two Answers

"What did you tell me on the fifteenth of August?" Lin Hao asked.

He asked it and heard himself ask it, and put his hand up between them, and then took it back down again at once because of what it was.

"No," he said. "I am not going to do that."

"I have not said anything."

"I was about to ask you to tell me what this office told you on the fifteenth of August so that I could put it beside what it is telling you now." He put both hands on the back of a chair.

"That is not a question. That is an instruction to build a second version of you, and you would have built it out of your own memory of this office, and it would have been my memory in your mouth."

The woman looked at him for a while.

"You are the man that stands at the bottom of the stair," she said.

"I am."

"Then you have not asked me anything at all about nine years of this office, have you."

"No."

## What He Said With The Door Open

"Then I will tell you what it is not worth," said the woman. "It is not worth the difference, there is no difference to find, and I did not come up here to find one. You told me in August that what I brought up was yours to read and not mine to ask about, and on the tenth of this month that a woman with a strap cannot hand it back. Both of those were said to me like this office was being careful with me."

"It was. And I have been in this quarter for eleven years and I have not had anybody careful with me about anything."

She put two fingers on the sheet.

"I came here in June and somebody told me what a paper is for, and I went away and used it, and it did what I needed it to do. I came here in August and somebody told me it again and I have thought about that every day since."

"I came up on the tenth and I was refused and I went down that stair angry for about forty minutes, and then I got to my own door and found out that the whole of it was that I did not want to be told a second time. I wanted to be told it again. That is all that refusal was."

## She Took It Back Apart

"So here is what I am not going to do," she said. "I am not going to go down that stair and start keeping what you tell me. I have tried about two hundred versions of keeping things in my life and I am extremely good at it and it has never once helped anybody, least of all me."

"I am going to go down those four flights and I am going to forget what this office says, and that is going to be fine. What this office says does not stay true past about a week, and I have relied on it for eleven years and it has been fine for eleven years."

Wenna Raut said, "It is not fine, it is your business."

"It is my business," the woman agreed, "and I have just handed it back to myself, and I did that on the tenth with a piece of paper in my hand, and I did not lose one hour's sleep over it. I think you all had better look at that."

Then she said the thing that finished him.

"And you are all wrong about what saved me. You keep thinking it was that somebody wrote it down and you could check it, and there is nothing written down, and I could not check it, and I have not thought about it since August."

"What saved me was that when I came up those four flights somebody stood in that room and answered me out loud, with their own mouth, to my face, while I was standing there. That is the whole thing. That is what I have been telling my sister for eleven years and she does not believe me."

## Asking The Only Question He Can Answer

The room went quiet for a moment.

Wenna Raut looked at the table. The carrier with the bad back was three flights down with a strap that had nothing on it. Lin Hao understood that the woman had just taken the argument he had lived inside for nine years and put it down on the table between them, and that he was not going to get it back, and that the only honest thing he could do about that was to say so in front of a room.

"Can I do one thing," he said.

"You can do the thing you do."

"Not for you. For everybody." He turned round to the room, which was not empty, which it never is. "I am going to say three lines out loud and I want the woman to take them, because she has never got them and she is the one person in this room who has never had a card."

So he said the street, and the door at the head of four flights, and the hours between a quarter to ten and half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and he said them the way he says them at the foot of his own stair, which is the way he has said them since November.

The woman wrote nothing down. She said them back to him once, all of them, and she got all of them.

"That is me," he said, "and I am not going to be at the bottom of that stair on a working morning. So that is what I have to say to you about what I have just given you, and you should have it from me and not work it out on a landing."

"I should," said the woman, "and I have not got a piece of paper with it on, and I would not know what to do with one."

## Half Past Two On The Landing

She went down at about two and he went down with her as far as the second landing, which is further than he has gone down with anybody in nine years.

"I am going to forget it," she said again. "And you are going to stand at the bottom of that stair and say it out loud, and a great many people are going to go up those steps behind me, and not one of them is going to have anything at all to show anybody afterwards. You have thought about that. Have you thought about whether that is worse than what I have just told you about myself."

"No," said Lin Hao.

"No," said the woman. "Well. Neither have I, and I have got the better of it, and that is all I came up for."

Four thousand five hundred and forty-six days. Less four thousand five hundred and thirty-nine, the tenth of this one, is seven, and seventeen less ten is seven, and a woman who had been refused under a rule with no date on it came back up four flights seven days later not angry at all, and told a room of strangers that what had saved her for eleven years was not a record but a person answering out loud to her face while she was standing there.

*Less four thousand four hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is seventy-seven, and the fiftieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-seven of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of September.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand one hundred and twenty-six days, which are seventy months and twenty-six days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
