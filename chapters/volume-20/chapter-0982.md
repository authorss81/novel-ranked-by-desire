# Twelve Practices In Two Heads

Four thousand five hundred and eighteen was the nineteenth of this one and the twenty-sixth of this one is day four thousand five hundred and twenty-five, and four thousand five hundred and twenty-five less four thousand five hundred and eighteen, the nineteenth of this one, is seven, and twenty-six less nineteen is seven by the month count.

## Trying It Out Loud At Eleven

He tried it at about eleven in the morning, in the room, with the door open and somebody at the far end of the table.

He got as far as saying that an answer goes in writing and that both the question and the answer go in the words that were used, and the woman at the end of the table looked up off her page and he stopped in the middle of the sentence.

She did not say anything. She went back to her page. He stood there for about four seconds with the rest of it in his mouth and then he walked out onto the landing and stood in the light with his hands on the rail.

That was the answer and it took him a quarter of an hour to accept it. Nothing in this room could be said out loud that was not said to whoever happened to be standing nearest the door, and what he had wanted to do at eleven in the morning was find out whether he could say the twelve out loud at all.

## What Happens When A Stranger Asks

"Wenna Raut."

"What."

"A stranger comes up those four flights and asks what the rules of this office are."

"Then they go away again," she said, "and about half of them come back a fortnight later asking something else, because they have worked out by then that we do not put things on paper."

"That is not what I asked."

"I know what you asked." She kept her eyes on the page. "The answer is that they are not written down. There is no page anywhere in this building or in this city that says what any of them is."

"They are what three of us do, and they are written on nobody, and in about four years there will be a man in a hall who will ask a division to say what this office does, and the division will write down whatever you say to them, and that will be what this office does."

"That is the thing at the counter again."

"That is the thing at the counter, and I am not arguing with it, I am telling you the position." She put her pen down. "If you write the twelve on a sheet then in about two months that sheet is a copy of them, and in about a year somebody will find a thing in it that is wrong, and then you will have to decide whether to correct it, and correcting it makes a second sheet."

"I have watched that happen to a document in another room in this building and I am not doing it here."

## Half Past Five And An Empty Room

They did it at half past five when the room was empty and the light off the landing had gone off the wall and gone down behind the houses.

The carrier with the bad back came up at twenty past five with her coat still on and stood at the end of the table with a hand in the small of her back, and Lin Hao had asked her once, on a landing, and had not asked her twice.

"Say them out loud," he said. "Both of you. All the way through, and when one of them is in doubt say that it is in doubt."

"That is going to take a while."

"It is going to take about an hour and it is the first time anybody has ever asked either of you for it."

## The Rows Are Not The Rule

They got a good way into it before he understood what he was listening to.

The carrier put a practice. Wenna Raut put the same one a different way. He put it a third way, and he was the one who had written them down, and the two of them had been living in the middle of his version for nine years without ever once putting it beside it.

"Stop," he said. "Go back. Nothing is taken from a person. Say it as you say it."

"Nothing is taken from a person off them," said the carrier with the bad back. "You are not taking the paper off them. You are not taking the thing off them. There is a difference and I have always understood there to be a difference and I do not suppose anybody has ever explained it to me."

"There is no difference."

"There is a difference to the person it happens to," said Wenna Raut, "and I have been the person it happens to, and she is right and you are wrong, and both of those sentences are about an answer that is read out in this room with the subject in it, and you know which one we are talking about."

She was right and he knew which one she was talking about and it was not that one.

Then he asked the question he had come up four flights to ask.

"Is any of them in the book."

Neither of them said anything for a moment.

"Define in," said Wenna Raut.

"Any of the twelve. Is a page of this office's own record anywhere that says what any of these is."

"No," she said.

"None of them?"

"Not one," said Wenna Raut. "Not one of them is in that book and it never has been and I have never once been able to make that feel right. Every one of them is a thing a person does. And the book has a great many rows in it, and every one of those rows is one of these things having been done, and I have spent nine years reading rows and calling them the rules, and a row is not a rule."

## The Hardest Half Of It

"The book was so that you did not have to trust me," said Wenna Raut.

She said it looking at the pen, not at him, and then she left about four seconds before she said the rest of it, which was longer than four seconds and which she had clearly had since the seventh.

"That is the whole of the nine years," she said. "Every one of them. You put a date and an hour against what I said and you carried it down four flights every evening yourself, and you never once handed me anything and you never once asked me to read a page back."

"And you told yourself it was about the record, and it was about me, and I was good enough at it, and you never found out that you had made a decision about me nine years ago because you never had to."

Lin Hao stood at the other side of that table with his hands on the wood and did not have a sentence, and understood that she was not expecting him to and had said it in front of a third person on purpose.

## The One Neither Of Them Could Date

They went on to the end and it took longer than he expected, and about halfway through the last part the carrier with the bad back said something that stopped the room.

"Nothing goes back down those four flights."

"Say it again."

"A person comes up here with a paper of their own and asks what we told them about it, and we do not give it back to them. Nothing goes back down those four flights." She said it the way she says the three lines at the bottom of a stair. "I have done it since I started carrying and I could not tell you when I started and you can ask me as many times as you like and the answer is that I do not know."

"What is the date it started."

"I do not know."

"What was the standing sheet saying in the year before that."

"I do not know, and neither do you, and you were not here." The carrier with the bad back took her hand out of her back and put it on the table. "I got it off the stair. That is where I got it."

"You put that book on my shoulder twice a day for two years and it went up and it came down, and not one thing in it ever went back the way it came, and I decided somewhere in that about paper, and it is not one of yours."

## What They Agreed On Out Loud

"Write it down," said Lin Hao.

"No," said Wenna Raut.

"Then we have no way of stopping it."

"We have exactly the way we have always had it," she said, "which is that three people know it and one of them is standing here."

He looked at the two of them for a while and then said the thing out loud with the door open on a dark landing, because it was the only instrument he had and he had learned in June that it can be spent once.

"Nothing goes on paper. Not tonight, not in the morning, and not on a sheet of our own if one of us thinks of it in the middle of the night. Whoever is asked gets the words used, whatever person happens to be standing there, and if the three of us have been saying one of these three different ways for nine years then the one they get this week is the one the person standing there has been living by."

"That is not a practice," said Wenna Raut.

"It is not written down anywhere at all," he said. "You said that to me on the fifth of August and it has not stopped being true since."

The carrier with the bad back put her coat straight and looked at the door.

"Then it stands," she said. "And I want the three of you to understand what you have just agreed to, because it is not nothing. There is a woman coming up those four flights inside a week with a paper of her own, and she is going to ask me what we told her about it, and I am going to do it again."

"And I am not going to be asked twice and I am not going to be argued with," said the carrier with the bad back, "and after that I am going to do it the same way the next time."

"And if one of us says no," said Lin Hao.

"Then you will be doing something you have never done in nine years," said the carrier with the bad back, "and I will watch you do it, and neither of us will have anything to write down about it afterwards."

Four thousand five hundred and twenty-five days. Less four thousand five hundred and eighteen, the nineteenth of this one, is seven, and twenty-six less nineteen is seven, and three people said the whole of what nine years of an office had been standing on out loud in an empty room at half past five, and found out that the only part of it with no date on it belonged to a woman with a bad back who had got it off a stair.

*Less four thousand four hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is fifty-six, and the fiftieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-six of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of September.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand and one hundred and five days, which are seventy months and five days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
