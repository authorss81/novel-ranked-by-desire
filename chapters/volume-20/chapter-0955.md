# The Ones Who Cannot Pay

Four thousand three hundred and thirty was the eleventh of this one and the eighteenth of this one is day four thousand three hundred and thirty-seven, and four thousand three hundred and thirty-seven less four thousand three hundred and thirty is seven, and eighteen less eleven is seven by the month count.

## A Woman With Nothing In Her Hands Came Up The Four Flights

She came up at about ten past eleven with nothing in her hands at all, which is the mark of a person who has been told something rather than sent with a paper.

She had been saying the three lines out loud for nothing since before he noticed that she was saying them, and she did it at the end of a market floor where people cannot read a clock on a wall, and she had told him so once, in a lane, and he had asked her about the weather.

"Sit down," he said.

"I will not sit down, I have a floor at two and I have been up here since a quarter to ten in my own head." She put her weight on the edge of that table. "There is a man in a street who will take anybody up four flights of a third court, and he names his price before he names the door."

"How much."

"Say it."

## She Does The Arithmetic In Her Head And Gets It Right

"That is what it is."

"I want to hear you say it, because I have said it out loud twice this week to two different people and I have got it wrong once and I want to know whether I have got it wrong."

He said it out loud.

Sanna Ruhl looked at the floor of that room for about four seconds and worked it against something she had already in her head.

"That is not much," she said. "I know what I said at the market and I know what he said in the street and I want you to understand that I have stood on a floor for nine years saying a figure out loud to about nine people every hour, and I could not tell you to within a quarter of what it is worth at that end."

"I got this one right first time because he said it out loud in front of a man who was not interested in it."

"You asked him."

"I asked him," she said. "He told me. And then he walked up those stairs and came down again and then walked an old man up with him, and it took him about twice what it should have taken, and the old man got there."

## The Ones It Hurts Are Not A Category

"The amount is not the finding," he said.

"It is the finding to the person who has the amount."

"It is the finding to the people who have to find it, and that is what has been wrong with my thinking since the eleventh." He turned round and looked at the stair. "The people that a price like that hurts are not a category. It is a few particular people, and I know them, and they are not people who would come up here and tell me."

"How do you know them."

"Because they come about a paper, and about a landlord who will not answer them, and about a man who will not come, and they have come up four flights in a morning on foot because four flights in a morning is free and four flights twice in a morning is most of a day."

He stopped there.

"That is not a list, and I am not going to make it one," he said. "I am telling you that I know them and I am not going to say out loud how I know them, because the moment there is a way of saying that out loud there is a place to write it down."

## Nobody Left A Word

"They have stopped coming," said Sanna Ruhl.

He did not ask how she knew and she did not say.

"You can tell at a market end," she said. "You do not need a book for it. You watch the same four people for a month and then they are not there any more and there is nothing at the end of it. There was no row with anybody and nobody was rude and not one person was refused. They just stopped, and they did not tell you, because there was nothing to tell you about."

He had known it and had been hoping that somebody would come up and say it, and now a woman who worked a market floor had said it before eleven in the morning and he had nothing to put in front of her.

He had been up four flights twice that week, and the second time he had had to stand on the half landing between the second and third floors for about a minute, and the warm part of that climb for nine years has been the half landing with nothing in it to look out of.

Four flights is free. Four flights twice in a morning is most of a day, and his body had put its own price on it without anybody asking it to, and he had been down those stairs on the eleventh of this month at half past two feeling exactly what a person with a landlord who will not answer her was feeling about a second paper in her coat.

"Do not do it," she said. "Do not ask me who."

"I was not going to ask you who."

"You were standing there with your mouth half open and you were going to, and if you had I would have told you and then it would be a thing you knew and not a thing you had said out loud, and you have been very careful about that since the thirtieth of December." She came off the table.

"I am going back down four flights. I have cost myself the morning and I did it to be in a room where somebody said a number out loud, and I am not sorry, and I want you to know that I am not sorry so that you do not try to give me anything for it."

## What He Said Out Loud With The Door Open

He said it with the door open, standing, in the ordinary voice, while the book was on the table and the two carriers were in that passage with the strap.

"I want it said with the door open that this office will never charge anybody for being told where that door is. Nothing is to be sold for it. That has been the arrangement since the thirtieth of December and it goes on for as long as there is a card on that door, and not one person in this city is going to be asked for three lines on a piece of stiff grey card."

"And that is all of it."

"That is all of it," he said, "and I have wanted to say it out loud for about four days."

Wenna Raut said nothing and the carrier with the bad back said nothing, and both of them had heard it, and neither of them wrote anything down, and it was the first time in nine years that he had said a thing out loud in that room and known before he said it that it would not do anything at all.

## The Thing It Was Aimed At Was The Wrong Thing

He worked out why at about ten past twelve, standing on the second landing with his back against the wall.

The man in the street was not selling the telling.

That door has been standing in the open for seven weeks and the man has never touched it and has never had it in his hands. What the man was charging for was the walking. He walked up and came down again and walked the person up with him, and that was his labour and his knees.

Labour cannot be stopped from being sold by anything in this city, and nine years of paper has never once had anything to do with a man's legs.

So he had stood in a room with the door open and said a true sentence at a problem that had moved.

And he had been glad about it. That was the part he could not get round for about a minute. He had been glad for about four seconds, and it had not been gladness about anybody in the passage. It had been gladness about being the man who said it, which was the thing he had wanted for nine years, and it had arrived the way a thing arrives when you have said the sentence out loud and found that it cost nothing.

Nobody in that room had needed it. The people it was for were standing on market floors being walked up by a man who had walked them himself first.

That was the difference between a sentence that is free and a thing that is free, and he had not known there was a difference until the eleventh of this month, and it was going to have to be said out loud somewhere with the door open too, except that he could not work out what the sentence was or who it was for.

## What The Clerk Said About Being Glad

"You were glad," said Wenna Raut.

"I was glad for about four seconds and you have no business knowing that."

"I have been at that table for nine years and I have watched you spend that four seconds about four times," she said. "It is the only part of the work that gives you anything and it is the part you can least afford."

"Then say the rest of it."

"The rest of it is that you have been saying that sentence since the thirtieth of December because saying it is free, and it has stopped being free, and you have not noticed the change because a man saying it out loud to a room does not feel like a man setting a price."

## The Lane Off Cordwain

At about half past one the carrier with the bad back came in from the passage and put the strap down on the chair and said the thing she had come up to say.

"They are talking about it in Brack's lane."

"All of them."

"Not all of them. One, and he has had it about a week and he has not written it anywhere, and you are going to go down there and he is going to be right about most of it and wrong about the thing that matters, and I want that written on the wall before you go." She rolled her shoulder the way she does. "He has got a figure for the man. And he has not got it right."

"How wrong."

"Wrong by about four times, and in the direction that makes it expensive, and he does not think it is expensive, he thinks it is fair." She stopped. "And there is a woman at the market end he has been pricing without ever speaking to, and a woman at a stop where the service stopped calling, and he has priced them both the same, and they have never met."

Four thousand three hundred and thirty-seven days. Less four thousand three hundred and thirty, the eleventh of this one, is seven, and a woman who works a market floor came up four flights in her own morning to hear a figure said out loud, and a man went down those same flights at about half past one with the price of it wrong in a lane by about four times and in the direction that makes it expensive.

*Less four thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is forty-eight, and the forty-eighth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last forty-eight of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand nine hundred and seventeen days, which are sixty-three months and twenty-seven days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
