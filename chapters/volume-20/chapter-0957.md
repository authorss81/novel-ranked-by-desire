# The Second Time Round

Four thousand three hundred and forty-four was the twenty-fifth of last month and the second of this one is day four thousand three hundred and fifty-one, and four thousand three hundred and fifty-one less four thousand three hundred and forty-four is seven, and thirty less twenty-five is five, and five with two taken out of the next month is seven by the month count with the crossing named as itself.

## She Came Up At About Eleven With Nothing In Her Hands

She was standing on the third landing at about eleven with her coat buttoned to the neck and she had not come up to the door when he opened it, and she had the particular stillness of a person who has spent a morning deciding to come.

"You have not come up here about a question of your own," he said.

"No."

"Then say what you have come about and I will tell you before you sit down whether it is going to be a row."

Her hands stayed in her pockets.

"Somebody in my building has told me that next time there is a matter at that office I can pay a man to take me up," she said. "Not you. A man in a street. He knows the way and it is not much and he has done it for other people."

"Who told you."

"A woman who lives two floors under me and who has never been up those stairs in her life and knows all about them." She took her hands out of her pockets and put them back. "I have come up four flights this morning to tell you that, and that is all, and I would like to be allowed to say it and go down again."

"Say it."

"I have said it."

## What She Came Up There To Ask And Did Not Ask

"There is going to be a second question," he said, "and I can see it standing behind the first one, and I am not going to let you get to it on four flights of your own morning."

"I was not going to get to it."

"You came up here to find out whether you are allowed to use a man in a street."

"I came up here," she said, "because I have been sitting in my kitchen since the fourth of this month with a thing going round in my head and I cannot get it to sit down, and it is not about a man in a street."

"Then say what it is about."

She was quiet for about four seconds on that landing.

"In October you gave me a thing," she said. "You did not give me a thing. You refused me a thing and you were kind about it and you said a price out loud before you said no, and it was the right thing to do and I want to say that first, before the rest of it."

"It was the only thing there was to do."

"It was the only thing there was to do, and I went home and I have been saying since the twenty-fourth of October that a man in a white plate gave me a doorway in which to refuse an answer about my own husband, and I have said that to about four people." Her voice did not change at all. "I have no idea whether that is true."

## What Changed And What She Is Carrying

"What is not true about it."

"The giving," she said. "It was a rule. It has been a rule since the eighteenth of August and it is written in your own voice on a wall four hundred yards off, and I read it, and I brought my husband up four flights because of it, and he said no in a doorway because of it. And that has been the best thing that has happened in my house for about four months."

She stopped, and he waited.

"Now there is a price on the stairs," she said. "And a man in a street will walk anybody up them for a small amount, and he is not lying to anybody. And I have gone from telling four people that a rule did a thing for me to sitting in my kitchen not knowing whether a rule did a thing for me or whether a set of stairs did a thing for me because somebody was paid to help me with them."

## The Part Of It He Had To Sit Down For

Lin Hao went down onto the third step, which he has not done on that stair in about four years and which took the bottom out of the argument for about nine seconds.

"Go on with it," she said. "The rest of it."

"There is no rest of it. You have said the whole of it and you have said it better than I have understood it in a fortnight." He looked up at her from the step. "Say the sentence you actually came up here with, which is not about a man in a street."

"I came up here because a woman two floors under me told me I could pay somebody next time, and I felt something come up in me on a stair landing, and I have been carrying it since the fourth," she said. "I felt glad."

"Glad."

"I felt glad that I could pay for it," she said. "And I do not know what that is. I have not got a paper about a landlord this month and I may not have one at all."

"What I have got is that somebody in my building has told me that a thing which used to cost me a morning now costs me something smaller, and I was glad on a landing about eleven o'clock at night, and I have not told anybody in that building and I have told you."

## Nine Seconds In The Ordinary Voice

He said it in about nine seconds, in the ordinary voice, standing in the open doorway with the light behind him and the stair behind her.

Not one person in this city is going to be asked a penny for being told where that door is, he said. That has not changed since the thirtieth of December and it is not going to change because a man has started walking people up for a small amount.

The rule about your husband has not changed and it was not made for you and you are not its subject. You came up those stairs in October because you would not have come up them otherwise, and your husband came up because you made him, and a man in a doorway said no, and that was correct and it was ordinary and it was not a gift.

"I have watched about nine institutions give people things and I have watched about nine people keep them as gifts for the rest of their lives," he said, "and I would rather you had a rule you can lean on than a gift you have to be grateful for."

He stopped.

"That is nine seconds and about forty words and it is the whole of it," he said. "It is not a new rule. It is not a change. It is what has been there since the eighteenth of August."

## Nothing Goes In That Book

She said, "Will you write this down."

"No, and I am not going to explain that either."

"Somebody will."

"Somebody will," said Lin Hao, "and I will not correct them, and I will not tell you which way they have got it wrong, and I am not going to promise you that it will not go into the mouths of about nine people in your street this week. That is the worst thing about it and it is not the answer to your question."

"Then what is the answer to my question."

"There is no question."

"There is a question," she said. "I came up four flights with it. I have not asked it, and I am not going to."

"That is because a question which comes up those stairs with a date and an hour against it goes in a book on the table behind you, and I have not got a date and I have not got an hour, and I am not going to stand here and manufacture the two so that you can write something down and feel better about it."

Wenna Raut had her pen down.

"I am not entering this," she said.

"You are not entering anything and you did not ask to," said Lin Hao, "and that is the correct thing to do with a visit, and it is also the reason I am saying the cost of this with the door open, which is that in about four years there is going to be a woman in a hall who says that the office at the top of that stair gave a thing away free on the second of March and there is nothing in that room either way."

## What It Costs And Who Pays It

"Name it," said Wenna Raut.

"It is this," he said. "I have a door that anybody may find and a stair that anybody may walk, and from the second of this month there is a price on the walking, and the people that price is going to bite are people like the one standing on this landing, who have come up four flights in a morning because four flights in a morning is free."

"And you cannot put it back to what it was in October."

"I cannot put it back to what it was in October because I do not know who took it out of October." He looked at the stair. "And I will not have anybody sent."

## What She Said On The Way Down

She went down two steps before she turned round, the way people do on a stair when they have said the hard part and have one more thing and are ashamed of the one more thing.

"The man in my building," she said, "told me the man in the street would not tell anybody he had been paid."

"I would not have expected him to."

"He did not say that. He said nothing at all about it, because he had not been told there was anything to say." She got her hand on the rail. "That is what I wanted to come up and say and I have been carrying it for four days, and it is that the price has not made anybody worse than they were. It has only made the thing visible."

Then she went the rest of the way down and he stood in the doorway until he heard the yard gate.

The carrier with the bad back was at the turn with the strap over her shoulder and she had clearly been standing there for a while.

"You stood on that landing for about four minutes with a woman and said nothing in the middle of it," she said. "I can tell that off a stair without writing it down."

"That is not a count of anything."

"It is a noise," said the carrier with the bad back. "I have been listening to that stair for nine years and I know the sound of a conversation going somewhere and the sound of one that is not, and you have had two of the second kind this morning and one of the first kind before them."

"What did I sound like."

"You sounded like a man who has been told a thing and cannot do anything with it," she said, "which is a sound I have had off that stair about four times a year for nine years, and it has never once been followed by anything, and I have never asked you about it and I am not going to start."

She went up past him and put the strap on the table and that was the end of it.

He went back to that table and said the cost out loud with the door open before he had done it once, which is the practice, and Wenna Raut wrote nothing, and it was the second time in a fortnight that the room had held a thing with a date and an hour against it and a person in it and nothing anywhere else.

Four thousand three hundred and fifty-one days. Less four thousand three hundred and forty-four, the twenty-fifth of last month, is seven, and thirty less twenty-five is five, and five with two taken out of the next month is seven, and a woman came up four flights in her own morning to tell him she did not know whether a rule or a stair had done something for her in October, and he answered the woman and not the street, and nothing went in that book.

*Less four thousand two hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is sixty-two, and the forty-eighth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last sixty-two of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand nine hundred and thirty-one days, which are sixty-four months and eleven days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
