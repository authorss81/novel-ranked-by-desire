# The Figure He Will Not Have

Four thousand six hundred and twenty-nine was the tenth of this one and the seventeenth of this one is day four thousand six hundred and thirty-six, and four thousand six hundred and thirty-six less four thousand six hundred and twenty-nine, the tenth of this one, is seven, and seventeen less ten is seven by the month count.

## The Same Question Three Times

He put the same question to the three of them in that room on the seventeenth of December at about eleven, with the door open, and the question was one he could not ask a stranger and could not write down anywhere.

What is a person who comes up those four flights worth.

The clerk answered in about nine seconds and it took her four sentences.

She said: a person who comes up those four flights is worth the answer and the hour and somebody saying it to their face while they are standing there. It is worth nothing at all if the same person asks twice. It was worth a good deal more on the seventeenth of September to a woman refused her own paper on the tenth than it is to me this morning. Put the seventeenth in the box.

The man who carries the strap answered in nine words and said afterward that they were all of the words he had, and that he had never read a row to anybody in his life and was not going to start guessing at what a row is worth.

The carrier with the bad back did not answer at all.

## Why She Would Not Answer

"I am not answering that, and you are going to get my reason in the length of a breath, and then you are to leave me alone about it until the spring."

"Go on."

"Because a person who comes up those four flights is worth what you give them, and I carry the strap up and down it twice a working morning and I have watched you carry that book down four flights and about four hundred yards for two years, and I am the only one of the three of us who has watched both halves of the whole of it."

She put her hand in the small of her back and took her time.

"What you give them is the answer and the hour and somebody saying it out loud to their face. I cannot put a figure on that, and I have carried the strap past about a thousand of them and not one of them asked me what it was worth, because they were on the stair and not in a market."

## The Arithmetic Of It

He said it out loud in that room because it had to be said out loud.

Somebody in a hall has asked, at some point, what this office does. There is a box on a form for it. In October that box wanted a figure of persons and this office put nothing in it, and the counterman wrote his own sentence under it on his own paper and it was true.

But a figure of comers divided by a figure of answers is a figure of service, and every hall in this city wants that figure, and this office cannot produce it and never could.

"You have been refusing that figure on principle since March," said Wenna Raut.

"I have been refusing it on the count since October," he said. "I am not refusing it now. I am refusing it on the arithmetic."

## The Sentence He Did Not Have In November

He had been asked the same question of himself in that room on the fourteenth of October and he had said no to it, and the reason he had given was about names, and the reason he had given was that the day this office starts writing names it stops being this office.

In November that was the whole of his answer and he had thought it was the strong one.

In December he had the other one and it was better, because it was not about anybody's character.

"A number would be wrong by whatever the answer is worth," he said. "And there is nothing in this city that can check it. The only three things in this city that could check it are the three people standing in this room, and each of them is the thing being counted."

He looked at the door standing open behind him.

"Take a morning in October. Somebody comes up those four flights and the three of us give her three different sentences and every one of them is right. Now put a figure of comers in and a figure of answers in and divide."

He put his thumb and forefinger about an inch apart on the end of that table, which is a thing he has never done in nine years and which the clerk noticed and said nothing about.

"What you get is an average across three offices that do not exist. The average is wrong by exactly the width of the gap between the three sentences. And the gap between the three sentences is the service, so the figure you put in the box is wrong by the service."

## A Figure Of Answers And Not Of People

Before he said any of it he made himself say the other half out loud, because there is a figure that this office is allowed and one that it is not, and he wanted the distinction in the room before the sentence went into the street.

There are about nine answers a day in that room and he has said that number out loud in that room with the door open and has refused to merge it with rows or questions or people or lines or sheets. That figure is a count of answers and it is true and he would say it again.

There is not a figure of persons. That is refused, and it was refused in October for a reason about names, and the reason about names is not the reason he is refusing it now.

"Both of those are refusals of a figure," said the clerk. "One of them has been standing nine years and the other of them has been standing three months and I would like to know which is the load-bearing one."

"Neither," said Lin Hao. "The one about names is why we are all right. This one is why the number we are refusing would not have been the number they wanted anyway."

## Said Where A Stranger Can Hear It

He said it out loud afterwards, at the foot of his own stair, at about half past twelve, with the door of that room standing open behind him.

There was a man in the arch.

He had come up the court and stopped outside the door because a man in a white plate was standing at the bottom of four flights saying things, and he had been standing there long enough to have heard the whole of it, and Lin Hao did not stop in the middle of it and did not go back inside.

The man in the arch waited until the end and then said: "You have not got a number for that."

"No."

"Then I will write no number," said the man. "I have come about a sheet for my brother's business and I have got a fistful of halls to ask before him, and this is the first one that has told me in advance that I am not going to get anything, and I am going to go back down those stairs now and cross your office off my list."

He said it cheerfully. He went down the arch with the paper still in his hand and did not go up four flights at all, and he did not need to, and he had told the man standing there in advance, which had not been done to him in nine years of asking halls.

"That was the correct answer," said Wenna Raut from the landing above. "And you have now got it in a street instead of in a room, and those are not the same thing and you know they are not."

## What He Had Done

He stood at the bottom of his own stair and took stock of what had just happened, and the taking of stock took about nine seconds and the whole of it was this: he had said the first sentence this office has ever said out loud in a street that was not the three lines.

It was a refusal of a number. It was said at the length of a court, in daylight, within nine feet of a stranger, and it went out of that court in a stranger's mouth, and it was the only true thing this office has said in that arch since March and it had nothing whatever to do with the door at the top of it.

Wenna Raut came out to the landing at about one and looked down the stair at him.

"You have just started something," she said.

"I have just said a thing."

"You have just said a thing in a street, and there is now a man walking down an arch with it, and in about four years somebody is going to use it, and you will not be able to stand in that arch again and say nothing."

He cannot take it back, and he cannot check it, and in about four years a hall will ask a man what this office is worth and a person will say that a man in a white plate told a man in an arch in December that he had not got a number for it.

It will be in three versions by then and he will have no way of finding out which one started it, and there is a woman in this city who has argued for twenty years that that is exactly what a sentence is worth and no more, and he conceded the whole of it to her on a stone floor in September.

Four thousand six hundred and thirty-six days. Less four thousand six hundred and twenty-nine, the tenth of this one, is seven, and seventeen less ten is seven, and a man said a number out loud where a stranger could hear it at the bottom of his own stair, and the stranger carried it down an arch in his mouth and went away glad, and nothing in this city can hear what he said afterwards.

*Less four thousand five hundred and fifty-nine, the thirtieth of September, is seventy-seven, and the fifty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-seven of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of December.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand two hundred and sixteen days, which are seventy-three months and twenty-six days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
