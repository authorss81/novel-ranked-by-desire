# Out Of The Building

Four thousand five hundred and four was the fifth of this one and the twelfth of this one is day four thousand five hundred and eleven, and four thousand five hundred and eleven less four thousand five hundred and four, the fifth of this one, is seven, and twelve less five is seven by the month count.

## The Last Ordinary Morning In That Room

The six lines were read out at a quarter to ten because that is the order they are read in, and the room got on with the day, and people came up the four flights and got what they had come for, and Lin Hao stood at the end of the table with his hands behind him and watched a book lie on a table for the last time before it was nine days old.

Wenna Raut had the practice sheet out beside the standing sheet and had not asked for it and had brought it out anyway, and she left it where she could both of them see it from the door. The bundle was on the side table where it is.

Not one person said anything about the book all day, because there was nothing to say about a book that was going to a binder, and the room went on the way it goes.

## Half Past Five And A Cart In The Yard

He came down the four flights at half past five in the ordinary way with the book under his arm.

He had been thinking about that walk for a fortnight and he had got it wrong in every direction. It is not four flights and about four hundred yards with a wire round the buckle twice. It is four flights and about four hundred yards with a book on a cart and a strap round the book, and the strap is the carriers' strap, and the wire is for a man's own hands and a man whose own hands are not on it.

The cart was standing in the yard with its shafts down and a board across the tailboard, and the man standing beside it had been paid already and had been told to take it four hundred yards and stop at a wide door in daylight.

The two carriers came down with him. Neither of them had said anything about it since the twenty-first and the twenty-eighth of last month, and the carrier with the bad back was the one who had asked, and she had put a rule of his own back at him on that landing and had not let him thank her for it, and so the four of them stood in a yard at half past five with a book and a cart and did not do anything for about nine seconds.

"Strap it," said the carrier with the bad back.

## His Name On Somebody Else's Paper

The strap went round the book and the buckle went through and the other carrier took the slack, and the book went onto the boards of the cart and sat on them the way it sat on the table, which is to say square, because for nine years it had been put down square by somebody.

The man at the tailboard had a slip on a board. It had the day on it and the number of days on it, and under those a line that wanted the name of whoever was answerable for the load, and the man put his thumb on the board and turned it round.

"There is nobody's name on that all day," he said, "every book I have carried this year and not one of them had a name."

Lin Hao took the pencil off the string.

He wrote his name on the tailboard of a hired cart, in his own hand, on paper that was not his office's paper, which had not been put in a file or entered in a book or read out in a room or corrected by anybody in nine years and had never once been done and was going out of that building in a man's pocket before the light went.

That was the whole of the ink. His own name on a stranger's paper, with the day on it and the number of days on it, and on the practice sheet on the desk there was a line in her hand dated the twenty-third of May, about somebody else's mouth.

The man folded the slip into his coat.

## What It Costs, Said In A Street

He stood in the street beside the cart with no coat round the book and his own coat on his back, which is what he had said he would do in a frost on a stair in November, and he said the cost out loud where the yard was and the street was and the light was.

"I am saying this in a street and not in that room," he said, "because a thing said in that room is about a person and a thing said in a street is about this, and there are three people here and all three of them are part of it."

"Then say it," said the carrier with the bad back.

"Nine days with no register at all," said Lin Hao. "Not a closed one. There is a table in that room and nothing on it and a woman at it who will answer out of her own head and will not be wrong and cannot be checked."

"About nine answers a day for nine days will be refused or given out of somebody's memory instead, and there is nothing anywhere in this city that will ever be able to tell you afterwards which of them was which."

"And the only copy of nine years of other people's answers is going out of this building on a cart in the street under a man who does not know what is in it."

"That is what it costs."

"That is what it costs and I have known every part of it since the seventeenth of the month before last and I have said every part of it out loud to a person before now, and I am saying the whole of it at once, here, tonight, because in nine days I will not be able to say it to anybody who can write it down."

The two carriers and Wenna Raut stood in the street with him. Not one of them said a word, and he understood as he stood there that this was going to be the shape of the nine days, and that the shape of it was three people in a room saying nothing out loud.

## Standing In The Street With It

He walked beside it. It was about ten past six and the light was still on the wall above his own door and would be gone by the end of September, and he walked on the left of the cart with his hands in his pockets and no coat round the book.

A cart with a strap across it goes four hundred yards fast. He found himself hurrying at the end of it, and then not hurrying, and then standing in the road while the man took the shafts up and got it moving.

At the wide door the man stopped and knocked once with the side of his fist and stood there.

The binder came out of the brightness of that room into a street that was going dark, and looked at the cart, and then at the book, and then at the man, and then at Lin Hao.

"Other people's answers," said Lin Hao.

"Right." The binder went to the tailboard and undid the strap himself, and the carrier with the bad back took the end of it because there were two of them who could. The book came off the boards in one movement and went in over the threshold, and he watched four inches of daylight come out of the bottom edge of its back board and go into a room with four doors open.

"On the bench then," said the binder, "out of the light, and I want that door shut until I have looked at the edge of it, and I want to be left alone with it for a quarter of an hour."

Then the cart stood at the kerb with nothing on it at all, and the boards had the dust of nine years of other people's answers on them in a grey rectangle, and the strap lay coiled on the tailboard where Lin Hao had watched a stranger fold a slip into his coat.

That was the whole of it. It took about a minute and it was the first time in nine years that anything had happened in the middle of that trade at a quarter to seven in the evening, and three people watched it and one of them understood what it was.

## What Could Not Be Un-Been

He walked back in the dark with nothing in his hands, which is not the way he comes down that stair, and is the only way he has ever come down it with nothing in his hands, and he understood on about the second hundred yards that nothing about it was reversible, and not because of the nine days.

A sheet in a file can be brought back and no person need ever know it went. A book in a flat file can be taken out of a building and put back and the building does not know. And a thing which has been outside is a thing other people have seen, and nothing in this city or any other can put a thing back the way it was before a stranger looked at it in daylight.

## What The Cart Man Said On His Own Account

He had about two hundred yards still to go and then the woman came out of the doorway.

She was on the same side of the street as the binder's and she came out because a cart was standing at a kerb with its shafts up, and she stopped the man with a hand on the rail of it and asked him, quite pleasantly, where he was taking it.

"I am not taking it anywhere," said the cart man. "It went four hundred yards, to a wide door, to a man at a bench. That was the whole of my instructions and I have been paid for them."

"Then what was in it."

"Paper. It had a strap on it like the ones the offices use, and I did not open it, and I would not have opened it if I had been asked to."

The woman said, "Well, if it is one of those four-flights ones, I shall be having a look at it, because my sister has been trying to find that door for a month," and she went back in and shut the door.

Lin Hao was standing in the dark about ten feet off with his hands in his pockets and he heard the whole of it, and he could not follow it and could not stop it and could not write it down.

It had happened on the first night, on the delivery, about an hour after the book had gone indoors, to a woman at a doorway who would be at the bottom of a third court in the morning to find out whether it was true.

She was going to go and find that door in the morning on the strength of a thing a man had said for money and had not known what was in it. He wanted to say something and said nothing, and the rest of it belonged to a man with a bench and was nine days away, and by morning it would be loose about this city without him.

## Twenty To Seven

He got to his own court at about twenty to seven and the stair was dark from the second landing up and the door was open at the top with a lamp in that room at an hour it had not been lit at in nine years, because there was nothing in it to work on.

A woman was standing at a bare table with her coat on and her hands empty.

"That is the thing I have been talking about since the twenty-first of last month," said Wenna Raut, "and you did not tell me you had changed the day."

Four thousand five hundred and eleven days. Less four thousand five hundred and four, the fifth of this one, is seven, and twelve less five is seven, and a man wrote his name on the tailboard of a hired cart and put the only copy of nine years of other people's answers on it in the dark and it was outside.

*Less four thousand four hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is forty-two, and the fiftieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last forty-two of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of September.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand and ninety-one days, which are sixty-nine months and twenty-one days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
