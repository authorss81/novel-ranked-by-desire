# The Nine Days Held

Four thousand five hundred and sixty-six was the seventh of this one and the fourteenth of this one is day four thousand five hundred and seventy-three, and four thousand five hundred and seventy-three less four thousand five hundred and sixty-six, the seventh of this one, is seven, and fourteen less seven is seven by the month count.

## Coming Up With A Number

He came up at a quarter to ten on the fourteenth with the number ready, and he had had it ready since the thirtieth of September and had been carrying it about for a fortnight without putting it in a room.

That is the whole of how that office works and there is nothing to be proud of in it. A fact does not get itself into a room. Somebody has to walk up four flights with it and say it out loud, and he had been putting it off for fourteen days because every time he got to the bottom of his own stair he understood again that the number was of answers and that the number he wanted was of people.

The day he brought it up was the day the other one became impossible to refuse.

## The Two Halves Of The Counter

"It has been on me for a fortnight," he said, "and it is this. Nine days without that book in that room. About nine answers a day, refused or given out of a person's memory, and I am not going to merge that number with anything else, and I want it said in this room with the door open before the day is out."

Wenna Raut let it stand for about four seconds, which was the most she had ever given a figure of anything.

"Say the second half," she said.

"There is not a second half."

"There is always a second half and you have known that since the seventh of July." She put her pen down. "Nine days is a count of days and anybody can check it against a calendar. Nine answers a day is a count of answers and nobody can check it against anything, and you know that, because you refused to check her against anything on the seventeenth of September and she told you what had saved her and it was not a figure."

## The Nine Days

"They held," said Lin Hao.

That was all it took, and he had known for a month that it would be all it took, and he had put off saying it for a fortnight because a man who has arranged to lose the only record he has spent a morning finding out that nothing happened does not get to say so lightly.

"They held. Nobody found out. Nobody came back angry. There was no morning in the middle of that where a person came up those four flights and looked at the room and understood that anything was different, and I have been waiting since the twelfth of August for one of those mornings and it did not come, and that is the finding and I am going to say it out loud before either of you says it."

"Lin Hao," said Wenna Raut.

"Say it or do not say it. It is the same either way now."

She said it.

"That office was running on me," she said. "It was never running on that book. The book was so that you did not have to trust me and I was good at it and you were never once wrong to make that decision, and neither of us has said that out loud in nine years."

## What The Number Is Not

"And I am not saying the figure of people," he said.

"You have said that once before and I know what it sounds like and so does everybody in this room."

"I know what it sounds like," said Lin Hao. "That is not why. In July I would have given you the reason about a number being better than a name and it would have been the true reason and it would have been a use of the true reason, and I am not doing it."

"Then give me the reason."

He put both hands on the back of a chair.

"Because on the tenth of September a woman was refused out of a rule that has never been written down by anybody, and it was enforced correctly, and it made her happy for eleven years' worth of a morning, and I could not stop it because there was nothing behind it to appeal to. That is a belief with no number behind it, and it is the mildest version of this there is."

He looked at the door and at the four flights of stair behind it. "And I have spent nine years refusing to write names down, and I was right to, and if there is ever a figure of persons for that room then the only way anybody can get one is to start writing names. The day this office starts writing names it is not this office any more."

"That is the best sentence anybody has said at that table in nine years," said Wenna Raut, "and I am not going to say that twice, and you are not going to thank me for it."

## The Days That Were Not Anybody's

"And there is a second figure and it is a smaller one and it is worse," he said.

"Go on."

"That book went out on the twelfth of August. A man said nine days and he was wrong or he was not, and either way it came back on the thirtieth of September." He said it slowly because he had said it to himself about forty times. "That is forty-eight days out. Nine of them were the ones I arranged and costed. The other thirty-nine were not anybody's arrangement and nobody has a figure for what happened in them and nobody is going to."

"Because there is no date on it," said Wenna Raut.

"Because there is no date on it anywhere in the world except in my head, and I got it in a room with four doors open, and I did not send anybody, and I said on the fifth of July that a thing said once in a court belongs to the court and I was right about that and it is the same fault and not a different one."

She nodded at that and did not soften it, which was the correct response and the reason he had said it out loud.

## Where The Anger Went

He had come up that stair that morning with a number and an argument and he went down it at about one with neither of them, and he found out on the second landing that the anger had gone somewhere after all.

It had gone into the counterman's sentence, which was the only thing anybody had said to him in about four months that had been aimed at the exact place where he was standing.

*A thing that will not count its own work cannot be paid for and cannot be defended either.*

He had spent the whole of July treating that as a thing about a division return and a box on a form. It was not about a form at all. It was about this room. This room could not be defended, and he had known it since the seventh of July, and he had gone on running it anyway, and there was not one sheet in this building on which he could put the whole of that down.

Which brought him back to the sentence he had written out on the twenty-fourth of September in front of two people, that a practice of this office is a thing one of three people does out loud in that room. Three people. He had chosen that number because it was the number of people who happened to be standing in the room, and he had not thought once, on the day he said it, about whether there would be a fourth.

That was the anger and it had nowhere to go, because the only person it was about was a man who is paid nothing behind a counter under a window, and there was nothing to be done to him and nothing owed by him.

## Three Of Them And A Door

At half past three he was back on the landing and the other carrier was there with the strap rolled up and nothing on it.

"You are back early," the other carrier said.

"I am."

"That is not a complaint. It is not mine to make and I am not going to." He shifted the coil with his heel. "Are you going to keep coming up in the mornings?"

"Yes."

"Not to do anything."

"Not to do anything," said Lin Hao. "That is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement since the twenty-fourth of March."

The other carrier stood at the top of that stair for a while and then said the thing that had been standing in that room since the twelfth of August and that not one of the three of them had been willing to be the first to say.

"There is going to be a morning," he said, "when somebody comes up those four flights and asks a question, and all three of us in this room know the answer, and the answer is going to be three different sentences."

"Go on."

"Then somebody is going to have to stop being the answer."

## The Woman With The Bad Back On The Same Landing

The carrier with the bad back was at the top of that stair at about ten past four with the strap rolled up and nothing on the other end of it, and she had been there most of the afternoon.

"You have been up and down my stair four times today," she said.

"I have."

"You were not thinking about the strap."

"I was not thinking about the strap," he said, "and I want to say the other thing to you before I say it to the two of them tomorrow, because you have been the only person in this building who has asked me anything about it."

"Say it."

"There is going to be a morning when somebody comes up those four flights and asks a question, and all three of us know the answer, and the answer is going to be three different sentences," said Lin Hao. "And I have been standing on this landing for about a fortnight working out what to do about that, and I have got it, and it is not a good one, and I would rather tell you than the clerk."

"Tell me."

"You are going to keep doing the thing about paper," he said, "and neither of the two of us is going to say anything about it, ever, in that room. That is what I have got. And if either of us ever stops then the whole arrangement has been a way of three people keeping quiet in the same room for nine years, and that is true, and I am saying it to you on a stair and not to her, and you know why."

The carrier with the bad back stood with the coil of the strap at her feet and did not say anything for long enough that he thought he had got it wrong.

"You have been telling me that since the tenth," she said.

"Then somebody has to."

"Go on and say the rest of it tomorrow with the door open, because I am not the only one who has to hear it and you know that as well as I do."

She shifted the coil with her heel. "And say it early. There is a person coming up those four flights tomorrow who wants a thing a stranger cannot give her, and it is going to fall on one of the three of you and not on me, because I am not answering anybody. I am only carrying."

Four thousand five hundred and seventy-three days. Less four thousand five hundred and sixty-six, the seventh of this one, is seven, and fourteen less seven is seven, and a man said nine days out loud in a room with the door open and found out that they had held, and that this office had been running on a woman since before he came to it, and that the only anger left in him was for a man behind a counter who could not be helped and was not owed anything.

*Less four thousand five hundred and fifty-nine, the thirtieth of September, is fourteen, and the fifty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fourteen of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of December.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand one hundred and fifty-three days, which are seventy-one months and twenty-three days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
