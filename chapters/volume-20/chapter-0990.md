# Three People At The Door

Four thousand five hundred and seventy-three was the fourteenth of this one and the twenty-first of this one is day four thousand five hundred and eighty, and four thousand five hundred and eighty less four thousand five hundred and seventy-three, the fourteenth of this one, is seven, and twenty-one less fourteen is seven by the month count.

## Saying It Before He Had Done It Once

He did it on the twenty-first, at about a quarter to ten, with the door open on the landing and a room behind him, and he did it before he had done it once, which was the only order available to him.

"From this morning the three of us answer," he said. "In this room, in our own words, whoever of us is standing here when the person comes up those four flights. I am going to be at the bottom of that stair from a quarter to ten to half past five as I have been since the twenty-fourth of March, and if anybody wants me they can come and get me and I will come up. That is the whole of it."

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds and the door was open.

## The Clerk's Objection, In Her Own Words

"That is not a practice and it is not going to become one," said Wenna Raut.

"I know."

"Say the rest of it out loud, because you have just handed a thing to two people and the rest of it is what it costs and you always say the cost first."

"There is no form for it," she said. "Not in this building and not in this city. In about four years there will be a rule about who answers at the top of that stair, and there will be a hall somewhere that has never heard of this office and a man in it who will ask what this office does, and somebody will tell him there are three of you."

"And about four years after that a woman will come to that hall with a piece of paper and ask which of the three was right on a morning in October, and not one person will be able to say, and not one person will be able to say who decided it either."

"Then that is what it is."

"That is what it is," said Wenna Raut, "and I am saying it in front of the room so that you have it and so that I have said it, and you are not to tell me afterwards that I did not warn you, because I am warning you now and I will do it once."

## The Carrier's Objection, In His Own Words

The other carrier stood at the top of that stair with the strap rolled up at his feet for about a minute before he said anything.

"I have carried it up and down for two years and I have never read a row to anybody in my life," he said. "I did not know that until about four minutes ago and I want it said in this room that I did not know it."

"It is not a question."

"It is a question to me and I am the one who is being put at the top of a table."

He did not move. "I cannot tell you what a piece of paper in that book means. I have carried it past the table about nine thousand times. And if somebody comes up those four flights tomorrow with something in their hand and asks a question about it, then the person standing here will be me, and I will not be able to do it, and whoever is in the room will know that I cannot do it, including the person who came up the stair."

"Yes," said Lin Hao.

"Then say it out loud to them anyway."

## What It Costs, Said Before It Was Done

"I am going to say what it costs and then I am going to stop talking and you can all get on with the morning," he said.

"About nine answers a day will now be given by people who have not been in this room for years. One of you has never opened that book and does not intend to. One of you has carried a strap and has never read a row. And the whole of nine years of argument about the words used is now three people's argument and not mine."

He put his hands down by his sides.

"That is the cost and there is a second half of it, which is that in about nine days one of you will say something to a stranger in this room that I would not have said, and it will be better than what I would have said, and I will not be able to hear it and neither will they, and that is the arrangement."

"Say the last part of it," said Wenna Raut.

"You cannot take it back," he said. "I said this in this room and you all heard it and there are people on that landing and about half of them heard it, and I have spent a year telling a city that a mouth is the only instrument it has got, and I am not going to be a man who says a thing like that and then gets to be embarrassed by it in a fortnight. It is said. That is what a mouth is."

## What The Room Did About It

The room went on doing what it does, because a room does.

The carrier with the bad back went down at about ten with the strap over her shoulder, and stopped on the second landing the way she sometimes does, and came back up and said one thing at the door.

"You did not put it on anything."

"No."

"Then it is yours and not ours, and that is better and it is worse, and I did not want to go down those stairs this morning without asking you one thing." She had her hand in the small of her back. "When somebody up there says something to a stranger that I would not have said, will you come up?"

"Not unless somebody asks me."

"That is not the same as no."

"No," said Lin Hao. "It is the same as what you said on the landing in June, and I have not found a better sentence for it since and I am not going to invent one now."

He put his shoulder against the frame of the door. "If nobody asks me then I am a man at the bottom of a stair. I have been one since the twenty-fourth of March. The only thing that has changed is that I am not the one they climb for."

She went down.

The other carrier stayed and rolled the strap up into a coil, and then unrolled it again, and then rolled it up, which he had never seen him do.

"You will find out in about nine days that I cannot do it," he said.

"You will find out in about nine days that you can do most of it," said Lin Hao, "and that the part you cannot do is the part I was doing, and that is the correct arrangement and I am not going to soften it for you now."

Four thousand five hundred and eighty days.

## At Half Past Five In The Dark

He came down the four flights at half past five with his hands empty and the wire round the buckle twisted twice in his pocket doing nothing for anybody, and there was no tick in the strap and there had not been for three weeks, and he got to the bottom of his own stair at about twenty to five with the light gone off the wall above his own door for the season.

He said the three lines out loud to his own court.

There was nobody in it. He said them anyway, in the voice, at the length, standing on his own second step from the bottom with the arch on one side of him and the low wall on the other.

He had said them in that place on and off since November. This was the first time he had said them as a man who was not going to be the answer at the top of that door, and there was nobody in the court to hear the difference, which was the last thing he was going to get to keep.

Then two people came up the arch and went past him into the court, and they were talking, and one of them said the three lines out loud to the other one to see whether she had them right.

She did not have them right. She had the street and she had the door at the head of four flights and she had the hours as four in the afternoon, which is what the woman with the basket had in the middle of September, and it has been welded onto those three lines by somebody being kind since the twenty-third of January, and nobody knows whose it is.

Lin Hao did not stop them and did not correct them and did not say the right hours out loud from his own second step, which is the one thing he has been doing every working evening since the fourteenth of May and which would have taken about four seconds and fixed it for both of them.

He let them go up the four flights together saying it wrong.

That is what he gave away and he gave it away in a mouth on the twenty-first of October, in a room with the door open, in front of the two people it now belonged to, and there is not one instrument in this city that can call a mouth back.

Four thousand five hundred and eighty days. Less four thousand five hundred and seventy-three, the fourteenth of this one, is seven, and twenty-one less fourteen is seven, and a man stood at the top of four flights and told a clerk and a carrier that the three of them answer now, and he said the cost of it before he had done it once, and at twenty to five that evening two strangers said the three lines wrong at the foot of his own stair and he let them.

*Less four thousand five hundred and fifty-nine, the thirtieth of September, is twenty-one, and the fifty-first sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty-one of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of December.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand one hundred and sixty days, which are seventy-two months and no days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
