# A Back That Knows The Weight

Four thousand four hundred and ninety was the twenty-first of this one and the twenty-eighth of this one is day four thousand four hundred and ninety-seven, and four thousand four hundred and ninety-seven less four thousand four hundred and ninety, the twenty-first of this one, is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven by the month count.

## On The Landing With The Strap Rolled Up

The carrier with the bad back was on the landing at about a quarter to ten with the strap rolled up in a coil at her feet, which is where she puts it every morning for about nine minutes before there is anything in it, and he had asked her twice about things on that landing and had got nothing useful either time.

"There is a thing to tell you and I have had it since the thirtieth of last month," he said.

"You have had it since the thirtieth of last month and it is the twenty-eighth," said the carrier with the bad back, "and I have watched you have it."

The book was on the table with the door open and there were people in the room. He said it to her there, at a volume that carried about four feet and no further, and she listened to the whole of it with her hand on the strap.

"You are going out," she said. "In a cart."

"On the twelfth of the month after, in the morning, nine days."

She put one hand on the strap and one hand in the small of her back in the way she had been doing since the winter of the year before last, and looked at him, and did not say the obvious thing for about four seconds.

"You did not ask me," she said.

"No."

"You have never once asked me anything about that book and I have carried it up those four flights two years and five days in seven," said the carrier with the bad back, "and I have asked you about the strap twice and you have never once asked me where it was going. So I am going to ask it now and you are going to answer it, and then we will both find out what you do with the answer."

"Where is it going."

"To a room off the market with four doors open, on a bench, for nine days," said Lin Hao, "under a cart in the street on the way, and a man on the cart who does not know what is in it."

## Where Is It Going

"That is the right answer," she said, "and I have been standing here since the thirtieth of last month working out whether you would lie to me about it, and you have not, and I want you to know that I noticed."

She sat down on the second step, which she had not done in the two years he had known her, and the bad back was in the hand in the small of her back and not in the way she sat.

"Right," she said. "Here is what I have got, and it is not an argument, it is a back."

"Go on."

"A book on a cart has no shoulder under it."

She said it the way she said the words of the three lines on the foot of that stair, exactly and slowly. "I have had that strap round that book two years. It goes across me here and it has a mark on it on that side and the mark has been in the same place on my coat since about the second year."

"And the book knows where the strap goes without either of us deciding it. And in nine days it will be on a bench under four doors in daylight, and for about nine days I will be carrying a strap with nothing on the end of it and my back will not know what to do with my own arms."

"That is the worst thing I have heard about it and it is not a reason."

"It is not a reason and it is the thing I was going to say first, which is how you know it is true," said the carrier with the bad back. "And the reason is this, and it is about four seconds long."

"You are sending nine years of other people's answers out in a cart with a man who does not know what is in it, and a man who does not know what is in it knows exactly where it is. That is the whole of it."

"You are going to have to trust a person with a strap who cannot tell the difference between your book and a crate."

## The Thing She Knew On The Second Morning Of November

"You knew," said Lin Hao. "On the second morning. In the frost. You knew on the second morning and you never said so to a person."

"I knew on the second morning," said the carrier with the bad back, "because I put it down on that table and I did not let go of the strap, which is not a thing I do. And I had been listening to it since the sixth month of the year before last and I had not put a word on it anywhere."

"How long."

"Two years and more," she said. "I could tell you how long in days and I am not going to, because the day I say it out loud it is a figure, and a figure about that book is a thing that gets said at a counter, and you taught me that in March and I have not forgotten it and I am not going to forget it inside nine years on the strength of a conversation."

That was the first time she had ever put a rule of his back at him and she put it without any weight on it at all, and he had to stand still on a landing for a moment.

"So you have had it for two years and no person has ever asked you for it."

"You have never asked me for it."

"I am asking you for it now."

"Do not," said the carrier with the bad back. "If you ask me for it now then I have given it, and if I have given it then it is a thing you have got, and you will do something with it. You do not want it. You want it because it is the only one of its kind in this city."

"I have known you since the fourth year and I have watched you collect about nine things out of this room that you never used and I am not going to be the tenth."

## Not Thanking Her For It

"I am not going to thank you," said Lin Hao.

"I know you are not going to thank me and I would have been insulted if you had, because you said the reason to a woman at a market end in June and I heard about it from a carter," said the carrier with the bad back. "Go on, you have a face on you."

"The twelfth of the month after, in the morning," he said. "A room off the market, four hundred yards past a yard and a turning, a wide door, daylight, and a man at a bench who will be told the book is yours if you say the word."

"If the cart is late, or if it comes back with the strap cut, or if it comes back and the book is not right, you take it back to that room yourself and you say the book is mine and you say nothing else at all."

"You have not asked me anything and you have given me the whole of it," she said.

"You are the only one in this building who can be sent. If I send the other carrier then there is a stranger in that room in a cart and a stranger in that room at the bench, and neither of them knows anything, and I would be doing what I have spent the whole of this year arguing against."

"You would be doing what the man in the street does."

"I would be doing what the man in the street does and I would be doing it with the only copy in the city," said Lin Hao, "and I would rather a woman who has carried it two years took it back than a man who has never opened it. That is the whole of the reasoning and it is not good reasoning and it is the reasoning I have."

The carrier with the bad back got up off the step in two stages, with a hand on the rail, and looked at him for a while.

"You have never asked me for anything in two years," she said, "and you have just given me an errand for the worst morning of my working life, and you have not thanked me for either of those things, and I am going to do it."

## What Happens To A Tick That Is Not In The Room

The book came down the four flights at half past five that afternoon in the ordinary way, in the dark, with the wire round the buckle twisted twice, and he carried it the four hundred yards with the back board ticking against his ribs about every four seconds.

The carrier with the bad back was at the bottom of the yard when he came out of the gate, which she had not been in nine years, and she did not say anything about the cart and she did not touch the book.

She stood and listened to it.

"That has been going since I was in this building," she said.

"It has."

"You have carried it down four flights and about four hundred yards every working evening for two years with that in it," she said, "and it is going to be on a cart for about nine minutes of the nine days, and after that it is going to be on a bench in a room with four doors open in daylight, and it is going to be louder there than it has ever been, because there is no stair and no coat and no stone to take it off against."

Lin Hao stood in a yard in the dark and listened to it with her and it ticked.

"What happens to it when it is in a room with nothing else in it," she said.

"I do not know," said Lin Hao.

"That is the first thing in nine years you have said to me that you do not know," said the carrier with the bad back, "and I have carried this strap up and down those four flights two years and I am telling you that I am the only person in that building who can hear it, and if you have taken it out of my hands for nine days then for nine days there is not one person in this city who can hear it at all."

Four thousand four hundred and ninety-seven days. Less four thousand four hundred and ninety, the twenty-first of this one, is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven, and a woman who has carried a strap up four flights two years told a man where his book was going and then told him what would be lost when it left her hands.

*Less four thousand four hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is twenty-eight, and the fiftieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty-eight of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of September.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand and seventy-seven days, which are sixty-nine months and seven days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
