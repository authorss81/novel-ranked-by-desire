# A Rule That Was Never Made

Four thousand five hundred and thirty-two was the third of this one and the tenth of this one is day four thousand five hundred and thirty-nine, and four thousand five hundred and thirty-nine less four thousand five hundred and thirty-two, the third of this one, is seven, and ten less three is seven by the month count.

## On The Landing With Nothing On The Strap

The carrier with the bad back was still coming up at a quarter to ten every working morning with the strap wound into a coil at her feet, and there was nothing on the other end of it, and she had not said she would stop and he had not asked her to.

He got there at about ten and stood at the top of the stair with the coil of the strap on the boards between them.

"I am going to ask you a thing and you are going to say no, and I want the no now while there is nobody on the landing."

"Ask it."

"The rule about paper. Stop doing it."

"No," said the carrier with the bad back, and she did not even take a second about it.

## Why It Is Not Hers To Stop

"It is not mine to stop," she said. "That is the whole of it and it is why you cannot have it. If it were mine I would give it to you this afternoon and you would put it in that room in your own words and we would both be able to look at it in about four years."

"Then where is it from."

"Off the stair." She put her hand in the small of her back and left it there. "You put that book on my shoulder every working morning for two years and I carried it up, and a man carried it down in the evening, and not one thing that ever went up those four flights came back down them, and I decided somewhere in the middle of that about paper, and I have been right about it every time."

"Have you."

"Every single time a person has come up here with a sheet in their hand and wanted to know what we made of it, I have sent them down without it, and not one of them has come back up angry."

She shifted the coil of the strap with her heel. "They come back up with nothing, and they say nothing happened, and they go away. Do you know what that is? That is the correct answer to that person. What we make of a piece of paper they brought in is ours and not theirs, and if we hand it back they will be holding a thing belonging to an office instead of holding a thing belonging to them."

Lin Hao found that he had no answer to that and he had expected to have about four.

"There is a woman coming up those four flights this morning," he said.

"There is always a woman coming up those four flights this morning," said the carrier with the bad back, "and I have never once been wrong and I am not going to start."

## A Quarter Past Eleven

The woman had a sheet in her hand and a date on the sheet and a bracket at the end of a line, which is what people bring, and she came up the four flights at about a quarter past eleven and stood in the doorway of that room and said that she had been in in the middle of August and she wanted to know what this office had told her about it.

Wenna Raut put her pen down.

"You were answered on the fifteenth of August," she said. "Do you have the date on that?"

"I have the date. I want to know what you said."

The carrier with the bad back was on the landing with the coil of the strap at her feet and she came into the room while it was happening, which Lin Hao understood afterwards was the whole design of it and had been the whole design of it since the twenty-sixth of last month.

"I cannot give you that back," said the carrier with the bad back. "You have your paper and I do not hand paper back."

## Watching It Happen

It was not loud. That was the thing he had to sit with afterwards.

She said it in the ordinary voice, at the ordinary volume, the way she says the three lines at the bottom of a stair, and she did not raise it and she did not explain where it came from and she did not look at him or at Wenna Raut while she said it. The woman at the door stood there for about four seconds, and then said *I see*, and went back down the four flights.

She went down them slowly, one hand on the rail and no hurry at all, the way people go down a stair they have decided about. At the bottom she turned the corner toward the yard and did not look back up, and he stood in the room and listened to her go and understood that in about four minutes she would be out in the street, and that by the afternoon this office would be a thing a woman had been told a thing about on a stair.

He did not say anything.

He had it ready. He had the sentence ready and it was a true sentence and it was the sentence he had been saying out loud at the bottom of that stair since November, and if he had said it then the carrier with the bad back would have been overruled by a man in a corridor and the whole of the arrangement that has held that stair together since March would have gone out of the room on a cart.

So he stood at the end of that table and let a woman who had been turned away under a rule with no date on it go down four flights, and he did it on purpose.

Then he went through the twelve in his head, one at a time, in the order they were made, with a date against every one of them, hunting for the one she had just enforced.

It was not one of them.

He did it twice. The third time he got as far as the one about an answer being read out once in that room and not read again with different people in it, because that is the nearest shape to what she had done, and it is not the same shape at all.

That one is about a person who is the subject of an answer. The carrier's is about a person's own handwriting. He had no date for hers, because there was none, and after that he had no date for it in any sense of the word.

## What Happens If You Are Wrong

He waited until the door had shut at the bottom and then he went out onto the landing.

"What happens if you are wrong."

The carrier with the bad back was rolling the strap up into a coil again and she did it while she answered him.

"Then a woman goes down that stair believing this office does not keep anything," she said. "And she will be believing a thing that is nearly true, because we do not keep that and we cannot produce it and we will not say a figure. She will be wrong by about the size of one sheet of paper and she will never know it and neither will we, and that is a great deal better than the other way round."

"Who is the other way round."

"That is the whole of what you have been doing since March," said the carrier with the bad back, "and you have been doing it with a door and a card and your mouth, and it has been better than mine, and you have never once had to hand anything to anybody. You are going to find out that that is not because you are a better man than me. It is because you have got a thing to stand at the bottom of and I have got a strap."

He stood on the landing and did not have one word for that, and it went into him in about four seconds and stayed there.

## What He Did Not Do Before He Went Down

At about two he was back at the end of that table and Wenna Raut was working and he had not said one word to her about any of it, and he understood that he was not going to, and that he knew what he was doing, and that he was going to do it anyway.

If he told her then the carrier with the bad back would find out inside a day. And if the carrier with the bad back found out that she had been reported up, then the next thing she was sure of would not be said to either of them again, and she was the only one of the three of them who had a practice with no date on it and a reason for it that worked.

So he kept it for himself for seven days, and he told himself for those seven days that he was keeping a thing for a woman who had earned it, and on the seventh day a woman came up those four flights with a piece of paper in her hand who was not angry about anything at all.

## Half Past Five With Nothing In His Hands

He came down the four flights at half past five with his hands empty for every working evening since the middle of August and the wire round the buckle twisted twice in his pocket doing nothing at all for anybody.

At the bottom of his own stair in the last of the light he said the three lines out loud to the court.

A woman going through the arch with a basket stopped and said them back to him to see whether she had them right. She had them right except for the hours, which she had as four in the afternoon, and he told her the light goes off that wall at about ten past four so that the last hour of those hours is a door in the dark.

That he can do. He did it, and she said thank you, and she went through the arch with her basket. Then he stood in his own court for another four minutes trying to work out why one of those two things was the easiest thing he does and the other one was a rule with no date on it that a woman with a bad back got off a stair.

Four thousand five hundred and thirty-nine days. Less four thousand five hundred and thirty-two, the third of this one, is seven, and ten less three is seven, and a woman with a bad back refused a stranger under a rule that has never been written down anywhere by anybody, in the voice she uses for three lines at the foot of a stair, and a man stood at the end of a table and let it happen and said nothing to anybody about it for seven days.

*Less four thousand four hundred and sixty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is seventy, and the fiftieth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy of them, and it does not move until the thirtieth of September.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand one hundred and nineteen days, which are seventy months and nineteen days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
