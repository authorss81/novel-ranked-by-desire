# The Office Named In A Finding

Seven days had gone since a man went up two flights of a dark stair at ten past ten in the morning and came down them again without knocking. One thousand eight hundred and forty less one thousand eight hundred and thirty-three is seven, and eleven less four is seven by the month count, because the fourth of March is the fourth day of a month and a month is thirty days and has no thirty-first.

He came into the counter off a third court at about a quarter to eleven with nothing in his coat, because the clerk had sent a card on the seventh, and the card had one word on it, and the word was that the finding was held.

There is no place in this city for a magistrate’s clerk to put his own name, and he had not given one, and Lin Hao had not asked for it, and that had been correct and stayed correct.

## The Finding On The Table

The demand of the fourth of January lay face up on the table with the address of the room off a third court standing at the head of every line of it in a fair commercial hand that belonged to a landlord who had never been through a door of that building.

There were four lines on it. He did not want to read them and he read them.

*It changed what a person held. It happened on a day the person can name and in a place the person can name. Another person was there, or was given it, and that person can be named or described closely enough. And it is not on a page anywhere in this city.*

“That is the whole of your rule,” the clerk said. “Is it yours.”

“Yes.”

“Then it is not yours in here,” said the clerk. “It is the landlord’s, because he copied it out by hand off a board and he has sworn where he copied it from, and the place he copied it from has an address standing at the head of it, and that address is yours.”

He put his finger on the top line of the demand without touching the paper.

“A finding cannot use a rule and leave the rule where it came from. That is not a rule of this court and I could not tell you whose rule it is. It is how a paper works. You take a thing off a wall and you put it in a document and then the thing belongs to the document, and the document has to say whose it was, and when it says whose, that somebody is a party.”

“I am not a party,” said Lin Hao. “I am the only person an office may ask.”

“Then you are the man the office asks, and the office has been named in a finding for the first time in six years of it existing, and that happened on the seventh of March and I am sorry about the speed of it.” The clerk squared the sheet. “Read the top of the demand again. Do you see what is written at the head of it.”

“The address.”

“The address is not evidence of anything,” said the clerk. “The address is how this city finds a room with two chairs in it. It is not a signature and it is not a consent and it does not make anybody a party. What makes a party is a name, or a thing so exactly described that a man could not read it and get a different thing.”

He turned the demand about ninety degrees and put a pencil flat beside the four lines.

“So there are two parties on this and one of them is a landlord and one of them is a room.”

## The One Question

“You may ask me one,” said Lin Hao.

“You may,” said the clerk. “Not me. You may not. Nobody in this room may ask this office anything, because this office cannot be compelled and cannot sue and cannot be sued and holds no money, and I have known that about you since the seventh and it cost me a morning.”

“Then who is entitled to a question.”

“The finding is,” said the clerk. “A party that has been named for the first time is entitled to be asked one thing before the finding goes up, and the thing is asked by me, and it is put to whoever answers for the party, and I may not ask a second one. Not a better one. Not a narrower one. One, today, and then this file is shut to my questions for good.”

He did not have it written down. He had it in his head and Lin Hao could see him holding it.

“I have not asked it in four days,” said the clerk, “because on the seventh I could not work out who answers, and you turned up on the seventh, so I have been waiting since the seventh.”

“Ask it.”

The clerk took a breath in the way of a man who has had four days to get one sentence right and is not going to be praised for it afterwards.

“Who wrote those four lines.”

There was no noise in the room. The counter had a rail on it and the rail had been oiled in February.

Lin Hao stood in front of a table with a demand on it and understood that he had been waiting four days for a question he could not refuse and could not postpone and could not answer with anything but the truth, and that he would answer it, and that it had never once occurred to him in six years that this was how it would end.

“Say the name,” said the clerk, “and say it as you would say it to a room.”

“Lin Hao.”

“Rank.”

“Rank one. No figure cut into the plate in my coat.”

He wrote it. It took about four seconds and it was the shortest four seconds of his life, and the sound of a nib on a finding was the most ordinary sound in the building and it was the end of the only arrangement he had ever been able to rely on.

“I am obliged to record the answer exactly as it is given,” said the clerk. “That is the whole of my trade and I have never had a use for it.”

## What He Had Given Away

“You understand what you have just done,” said Lin Hao. “Say it back so that I know it is in a room with two people in it.”

“You have been the author of an unattributed rule for six years,” said the clerk, “and a rule with no author cannot be argued with, because there is nobody to argue it with, and an office with no name on its sentences is the strongest thing in this city and I have watched four landlords find that out in nine months.”

“Your sentences had no author this morning. They have one now. Mine says *Lin Hao, rank one*, and it is going up to a magistrate, and in about four months a man who lets six rooms off Lamplit Row is going to be handed a copy of it in a room with a table in it, and he is going to read your name and understand in about two seconds what it costs you.”

“I know what it costs.”

“No,” said the clerk. “You know what it costs you and you have not worked out what it costs me, because you have spent six years being the only person anybody is permitted to ask at an office, and there has been exactly one question in this city about anything you have written, and it has been sitting on my desk since the seventh of March, and it was mine to ask and not yours.”

He put the pencil down and squared the demand with the finding.

“Now the second part, and it is duller. A finding does not go up with a party in it and nothing in the file. The party returns a line. If the party returns nothing then the clerk writes *nothing returned* and it is not held against anybody, and that is the last thing that happens to it.”

“By when.”

“The twenty-sixth.”

“That is fifteen days.”

“It is fifteen days and there is no instrument on which a line comes back to this counter from a room with two chairs in it,” said the clerk.

“There is nothing on this desk with a box for it. I have looked. If you want the finding to go up with a line in it, the line has to be produced by somebody, and the only somebody your office may ask is the man standing in front of me, and he is not a party and does not have to produce anything.”

## A Rule With No Author

He wanted, for about four seconds, to be a man who had not been asked.

That was the actual thing and it turned up in him before he had decided on any emotion at all, and it was the identical shape to the want he had in a corridor in January, when a woman with a rank above his had asked him one question and spent it, and he had wanted the whole of that room back for about four seconds and then had not wanted it at all.

He wanted a finding with no name on it.

And the file on the table had a name on it, and a copy of the standing page was going into it, because a rule cannot go into a document without the page it came off, and the clerk had already had a copy of the twenty-first sheet made by a woman in the back who copies things without being asked whether the thing being copied is worth copying.

“You are going to copy the page in,” Lin Hao said.

“The page is the source of the rule,” said the clerk. “The source of a rule goes in the file with the rule. That is not me being careful. That is what a file is. And I will tell you what that means and then I will not tell you anything else, because I have used my one question and I have had it since the seventh and I am going to be careful with what is left, which is nothing.”

He turned the finding round and put it where Lin Hao could read it.

“It means that on any morning a magistrate takes this file up, he turns it over, and at the back of it there is a page with four sentences at the foot of it in the hand of an office, and the first of those four sentences has your name under it.”

“And the second of them says that where a Register has nothing it has not been given anything, and both of those are in front of a man who is going to decide a landlord’s figure in about four minutes.”

He shut the nib and put it in the tray and did not look up for about four seconds.

“You did not choose that. You chose a sentence. I am telling you what the sentence is now standing on, because nobody has ever told you and you have been the only person in this city who could be told.”

“That is the job,” said Lin Hao.

“That is the job,” said the clerk, “and there is not one person in this city who can give you anything for it.”

He squared the two papers together and tied them, and the string went round twice, and there was no ribbon on anything in that room and there never had been.

“A party returns a line by the twenty-sixth,” he said. “If a line comes back, it comes back to me and I put it in the file and nobody reads it but me. If it does not, I write four words and the finding goes up on the twenty-seventh.”

Lin Hao picked the bundle up off the table and it was heavier than a bundle of two sheets ought to be and he understood that the third thing in it was a page that went out four times a year at four o’clock, and that it was in a file now, and that a file is the one thing in this city that comes out when somebody opens it.

“Where is the address for the line,” he said.

“There is not one,” said the clerk. “There is no box. Take it to the room you came from and ask the woman at the counter there, because she may only enter what she is given and she cannot be asked to hurry, and if she has never been given a line in her life then that is the end of it and you may come back on the twenty-seventh and read what I wrote.”

He did not look up when Lin Hao went, which was the correct thing to do and was also, in that room, a kind of accuracy.

Lin Hao stood in the corridor off the third court for about a minute with a bundle in his hands and a rank on a piece of paper in a file behind a door.

He had spent six years making sure there was no such thing as a page anybody could hold him to. There was one now. It had his name on it and a second sentence under his name that said where a Register has nothing, it has not been given anything, and it was in a file, and it was going to be read back to somebody, and nobody had thanked him.

*One thousand eight hundred and forty days. Less one thousand eight hundred and thirty-three, the fourth of March, is seven. Eleven less four is seven by the month count, because the fourth of March is the fourth day of a month and a month is thirty days and has no thirty-first.*

*Less the fourth of January, one thousand seven hundred and seventy-three, is sixty-seven, and thirty and thirty is two months with seven days after it. The demand came into the room off a third court on the fourth with four lines on it in a fair commercial hand and two words in pencil on the back of it, and the twenty-sixth of March is in fifteen days and is day one thousand eight hundred and fifty-five, and eleven with fifteen is the twenty-sixth.*

*Less the thirtieth of December, one thousand seven hundred and sixty-nine, is seventy-one, and seventy-one is two months and eleven days, and the twentieth sheet went out at four on that day and the twenty-first goes out on the thirtieth of March at four.*
