# A Piece Of Paper Came Back

Seven days had gone since a clerk read four sentences at the foot of a page out loud to a man who was not permitted to answer any of them, and then wrote a minute of his own into a file and put it in front of a magistrate. One thousand eight hundred and ninety-five less one thousand eight hundred and eighty-eight is seven, and six less twenty-nine is seven by the month count, because the thirtieth is the last day of April and a month has thirty days.

He went down to the ground floor of the branch building at about eleven at night on the sixth of May, into a long corridor with a lamp on a stand at the end of it and three floors of washrooms overhead, and there were three women on the rota and the folded page on its nail behind him with four names on it.

Nadia Ferrous was at the far counter with a bundle in her arms. Petra Ilyin was at the middle one, reading, with a pencil lying where she put it down. Isolde Marchetti was at the end of the corridor at the counter that faces the wall and did not turn round, which is what she does.

He wanted the counter to be quiet.

That was the whole of the want, and he had had it since about ten o’clock, and it lasted about eleven minutes.

## Eleven O’Clock

The woman came in at about eleven minutes past eleven.

She had a wet coat on and a bundle of papers under her arm and she came up to a counter at eleven at night in a building with three floors of washrooms over it, which is not a counter anybody comes to for anything, and she put one sheet down on it and said *this wants taking* and went.

Nobody stopped her. There was nothing to stop her with. The counter is the last one open in this city at eleven at night and she had been told to take it somewhere.

Nadia Ferrous put her bundle down and came and looked at it.

“That is a demand,” she said.

“Is it.”

“It is the length of four lines with three things written on it — a figure, a date and a room — and that makes it a demand, and a demand is the only paper in this city a landlord is permitted to put before a magistrate.” She did not touch it. “That is the fourth of January’s with a different figure on it.”

“It is not the fourth of January’s.”

“No,” said Nadia Ferrous. “It is the same four lines and it is dated the ninth of April and the figure is not the same and the room is not the same and the row is not the same, and I have been reading a page a week for two years and I know what a demand looks like and this is a second one.”

He read it.

It was four lines long and it was the four lines and it was in a hand that was not the fair commercial hand of the fourth of January, and the figure was smaller and the date was the ninth of April and the room number was one, and there was no address at the head of it.

“What was the address on the January one.”

“The address of this office,” said Nadia Ferrous, “at the head of every line, because the landlord copied them off a board and the board has the address on it above the four lines in a notice that says what the notice says. This one has no notice at the top of it. This one is nothing but the four lines and three things written on them.”

## What A Landlord Does

“He copied them off a board,” said Lin Hao.

“He copied them off a board.”

“Then he is right to. He is a man who has let rooms and a tenant cannot produce anything, and he has read four lines off a board in a corridor, and four lines off a board in a corridor is the same as four lines off a wall, and there are about nine of those boards in this city and about thirty people a day walk past each of them and about two of the thirty stop.”

He put the sheet down where it had been. “He is right about the flat. I have not met him and he is right about the flat.”

“He does not know this office exists,” said Nadia Ferrous.

“No.”

“He does not know the office is at the head of the four lines, because he left the notice off, and there is nothing wrong with him, and about nine hundred people in this city can read those four lines and any one of the nine hundred can write them out for a landlord in about eleven seconds.”

“That is exactly what happened in the January and it took four months and a magistrate to find out who wrote them.” He looked at the sheet. “This one will take about nine months and it will end the same way and the man at the counter will never know why he won.”

Petra Ilyin turned a page and did not look up and said, without any weight on it at all: “He will know why he won if anybody ever puts it in front of him, and nobody is going to, because the page that would say it is not in this piece of paper.”

“No,” said Lin Hao. “It is not.”

## The Woman Who Carried It

The woman came back at about half past twelve.

She was sorry about it in a way that people are sorry about something they have been told to do, and she said that a man at a board off a different court had said that any paper carrying those four lines was to be taken to the address at the head of it, and that she had looked for the address for about forty minutes, and that there was not one.

“There is not one on this,” said Lin Hao.

“There is not one on it,” she said. “He said at the head of it. And I asked at the counter off the third court on my way and the woman there said there was no such address and would not say it twice to me.”

“Then I asked a man on a stair and he said the address was off a third court and that the counters off a third court shut at four, and I have been carrying this since eight and I have got to be at the other end of the city by two.”

“There is an address for this office,” said Lin Hao, “and it is on a page and not on that sheet, and it is at the head of every line of a page that goes out four times a year, and about two thousand people a day cannot walk past the foot of that page without reading it, and nobody has told that man any of it and there is no form on which he could be told.”

She looked at him for about two seconds.

“Then he has been told to take it somewhere it is not,” she said, “and I have taken it as far as I could and the last counter open in this city was yours,” and she said it without any apology in it at all, and she went.

## The Counter Until Four

Nobody at that counter shut for about three and a half hours after that, because there is nothing in this city to do with a demand at eleven at night except leave it where it was put, and a woman at a rent board had put it on a counter that opens at seven in the morning.

Nadia Ferrous came and stood at the end of it at about one and read it again from the top without touching it.

“There is nothing to serve it on,” she said.

“There is nothing to serve it on.”

“There was nothing to serve the January one on and a Bureau officer carried it up a stair by hand in January and wrote down that she had done it, and she is rank two and she is above you on paper, and she did it because a field officer may carry a thing herself if she is willing to write that she did.”

“I know what she did.”

“You cannot ask her to do it again,” said Nadia Ferrous. “She has said so herself, in writing, in her own hand, and she said it in the most careful sentence anybody in this city has ever put on a piece of paper. Once is a thing that happened. Twice is a woman walking a district on purpose to find a building with no street door in it, and she is not going to do that twice and she would be wrong to.”

He leaned on the counter with the bucket between his boots.

“I have been waiting since the seventh of February for somebody to ask me one question,” he said, “and it has now been handed to a clerk with no standing in front of a magistrate and a woman on a night shift who reads the foot of a page twice before she says anything about it, and neither of them is asking me, and I am not going to ask either of them.”

“You are going to ask me,” said Nadia Ferrous. “I have known that since about eleven o’clock and I have been waiting for it and I am not going to make it easy for you.”

“I was not going to.”

“No,” she said. “Here is the one. You told a whole city, at the bottom of a page that nobody can get past, that numbers get invented in this city on purpose. And you said the foot of that page is read by about four in a thousand and that nine people a week get to the end of one. It has been out at four four times.”

“Yes.”

“Then say it to me as a person who works nights and reads a page in about nine seconds because it is late and I am on a rota,” said Nadia Ferrous,

“because I have understood that sentence for about seven weeks and I understand the other one, and I am not going to say anything about either of them again, and I have never said anything about them to anybody including the man who wrote them, and I want you to know that I know exactly what I am doing by not saying it.”

“You are doing the right thing.”

“I know I am,” said Nadia Ferrous. “That is why I am not going to say it.”

She went back to her bundle and Petra Ilyin turned a page and Isolde Marchetti read on at the end of the corridor, and at about twenty past three he squared the demand where it had been put down so that it would not be trodden on, and the counter shut at four, and the sheet went into a drawer at the back of a branch office off a third court and it was still there in the morning.

*One thousand eight hundred and ninety-five days. Less one thousand eight hundred and eighty-eight, the twenty-ninth of April, is seven. Six less twenty-nine is seven by the month count, because the thirtieth is the last working day of a month and a month is thirty days.*

*Less the fourth of January, one thousand seven hundred and seventy-three, is one hundred and twenty-two, and one hundred and twenty-two is four months and two days. The first demand came into the room off a third court on the fourth. The second one came onto a branch counter off a third court at about eleven at night on this morning, dated the ninth of April, which is day one thousand eight hundred and sixty-eight, and there was no address at the head of it.*

*Less the thirteenth of November, one thousand seven hundred and twenty-two, is one hundred and seventy-three days, and the line went out at four on that day with his name under it in the office’s own hand.*
