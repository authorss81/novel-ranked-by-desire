# The First Creditor Comes Down

Eight days had gone since a woman of rank two had walked up a corridor at twenty past eleven at night and told him that about eleven thousand people had a week and that he was not going to be allowed into it. One thousand nine hundred and ten less one thousand nine hundred and two is eight, and twenty-one less thirteen is eight by the month count, a month being thirty days with no thirty-first in it.

The tray was not empty.

That was the first thing at about ten in the morning on the twenty-first of May and it had not been true since a March in an earlier year. He stood in the doorway of the room off a third court and looked at the tray for about four seconds before he went any further, because a tray that is not empty is the only thing this office has ever been able to do and he had arranged for six years that it would not do it.

The table had a bundle on it with string round it, and the string had been tied by a person who ties strings the same way every time, which is Wenna Raut, and the day book was open at the counter in front of her.

“You have four things,” she said, before he was within a yard of the table. “They came between the fourth of January and this morning, and I have entered all four, and I will say nothing else about them until you ask me for each one.”

“Four.”

“Four,” said Wenna Raut. “In order, or the one that is not a piece of paper first.”

## Four Things In A Bundle

“In order.”

He untied the string and laid them out, and three of the four he had known about.

The first was the demand of the fourth of January with the address of this room standing at the head of every line of it in a fair commercial hand.

The second was the finding of the eleventh of March with the clerk’s minute in it, back off a third court in a wrapper with a seal that had been broken and put back badly by somebody in a hurry.

The third was the demand of the ninth of April that came onto a branch counter at eleven at night with no address at the head of it, out of a drawer at the back of that counter after six weeks, because a drawer is not a place a thing can stay.

“The fourth,” he said.

Wenna Raut turned two pages of the day book round to face him without saying anything about it.

It was four lines long, dated the fifteenth of May, and it came from a counter off the fourth court.

*A person came to the counter at about eleven and asked whether this office would put something back on a board. I did not understand the question. I have read it four times and I do not understand it. The person did not come back and I have looked for the address and there is none.*

“The fifteenth,” said Lin Hao.

“Four days after a quarter went out,” said Wenna Raut. “That is the whole of it, and I entered all of it and improved nothing, and there is no form in this city on which this office is asked whether it would put something back on a board.”

“Did you tell the person anything.”

“I told the person to come back,” said Wenna Raut, “and the person did not come back. I would have told the truth if I had been asked. The clerk at that counter is permitted one question a day and she spent hers on a division return in the morning.”

Six days before this morning a person had walked into a building off a fourth court and asked this office whether it would put something back on a board. The office had sent her nowhere. There was no instrument on which it could have sent her anywhere.

“The fourth of January to the twenty-first of May is a hundred and thirty-seven days,” he said. “Three of these went in front of a magistrate or came off one. One is a question, and there is no form for the question, and it is the only one anybody asked us for.”

“Put it back in the tray.”

“It goes into the bundle on the thirtieth,” said Wenna Raut, “and the bundle goes into another room, and I do not know what happens to the bundle and neither does the Registrar, and I have said that once and I am not saying it again.”

## What He Wanted

He wanted the tray to be empty and it was not, and he had known since the eleventh of March that it was going to stop being empty because he had put his own name into a document, and he had gone on arranging the rest of his life around the expectation and had done nothing about it.

That was the want, and it lasted about eleven minutes. After that what he had was worse, because a full tray is a thing a man can be got to work on and a person is not.

## Down Cordwain

He went down Cordwain at about half past ten with nothing in his coat.

The twenty-first had been lying on that desk since the thirtieth of March and had gone into a bundle on the second of April, and the desk under the window had a ledger open on it with about thirty names in it, and the man who had run that desk for about nine years did not turn round.

“Same as ever,” he said.

“Not the same. It went into a bundle on the second of April and I have had a bare desk for seven weeks, and about a hundred people a day come past a bare desk and stand there a second and go.”

“That is the office’s business and not yours.”

“It is my desk.” He turned a page over. and did not look up, which is what he does.

“There is one further thing and then I go back to my thirty names,” he said, “and it is not about a sheet and there is no sheet.”

“Say it.”

“Somebody came down those stairs this morning.”

## Two Flights

The passage was about four feet wide and the stair went up out of the end of it in the dark, and there had been a second door on the landing with paint on it since before the man at the desk started. Lin Hao had been up it once, on the fourth of March, alone, at ten past ten in the morning. There had been nobody at the top of it and he had not knocked.

“How long ago.”

“About half past ten. She came down and did not come in here, went straight across the road, stood about eleven minutes, came back and stood about nine feet off where you are standing, and then went back up.”

“Which stairs.”

“Those stairs. About four people a month come down them and two come down for the water.” He looked up for the first time. “That was the first one in about two years who came down for nothing at all, stood in the cold for eleven minutes, and came back up.”

He shut the ledger about halfway and left his hand on it.

“She is big,” he said. “And she is not dressed for this morning and she came down anyway, and she was not going to the water because there is a water butt on that landing and I have watched her use it.”

“Then what did she want.”

“She wanted to know whether anybody comes down here in the afternoon,” said the man at the desk.

## The One She Asked

Lin Hao stood in a passage four feet wide and understood that a woman who cannot read had spent eleven minutes in the road, composed herself a question, put it to a man at a public desk, got an answer and gone back up.

“What did you tell her.”

“I told her the truth,” said the man at the desk. “I have never once asked anybody what a sheet was for and I am not starting now, at half past ten, with a person who has come down two flights in the cold.”

“A page comes down here four times a year and about two thousand people a day cannot get past the foot of it. There are four lines at the foot of it and about four people in every thousand who read that far. And under the second of the two lines a man added in a November there is a name.”

“Whose name.”

“A man’s,” said the man at the desk. “And I told her it is not the only man in the office, and I told her where he comes down and when, and I did not know I was doing the two of you a kindness.”

“And what did she say to that.”

“She said, that is not the same thing,” said the man at the desk. “She said it the way a person says a thing they worked out in the road. Then she said something else and I did not understand half of it, because she was going up the stairs while she was saying it.”

“Say it again as near as you can.”

“She asked, is that the man who owes me.”

He did not answer that and the man at the desk did not appear to be expecting an answer.

“No,” said the man at the desk, answering it himself. “It is not. It is a man who wrote a sentence about figures and about a Register having nothing.”

“I told her that too. And she said she knows it, and that it is eleven weeks old in a page and a day old in the road, and that a man who wrote a sentence about figures owes her six years of a street and not one grain of it.”

“And the rest of it.”

“And she said she came down to find out whether there is anybody in it.”

He turned a page over.

“And I told her no. There is nobody in it,” said the man at the desk. “There is a room with a table and two chairs, a woman at a counter, a man who comes down about half past four on the day a sheet goes out, and a drawer that has stood open and empty since the fourth of February.”

## Thirty Feet Of Passage

“And she went back up.”

“She said one more thing and it was the only one she said slowly,” said the man at the desk. “She said: then I am owed by nobody, and that is the fourth place. And she went up.”

The light came in under the sash and lay along the bare top of the desk, and the trams went under the ranks wall a street away and the floor of the passage felt it.

He had thirty feet of Cordwain and eleven minutes and a want that had been in him since the seventh of February. He wanted to say her name.

He had it, and it was on a page in a building with no street door, and he had carried it about for four years without once using it.

There was no instrument in this city on which he could say it to a man at a public desk, and saying it would have been the first thing he had done for a person in six years.

So he stood thirty feet off and said nothing at all to a man at a desk about a woman on a stair, and the man went back to his thirty names.

And the thing he understood, standing there, was the thing he had understood on the fourth of March at the top of two flights, and had not gone up again for.

She had come down a stair she had refused to come down for about two years, in the cold, on the eleventh of May, on the strength of a sentence a man had printed at the foot of a page in a November. She had got an answer. The answer was correct. It did not change one hour of her six years.

Nobody thanked her. Nothing at all came out of the two flights for her: no figure, no name on anything, no column to put a name in, and no answer to the one question she came down with.

She came down two flights and went back up two flights, and the Register of Unrecorded Merit stood on exactly the footing it had stood on that morning, and there is no form in Ostara on which any of that could be entered.

## The Note Of The Fifteenth

He came back up Cordwain at about ten to twelve with the counters clerk’s note of the fifteenth in his coat, and the question on it was one nobody could answer.

*A person came to the counter at about eleven and asked whether this office would put something back on a board.*

Six days before this morning. Six days after the quarter went out. And the person had gone into a building off a fourth court on the word of a man at a board, and had asked the only question anybody has ever wanted to ask of this office, and there was no form on which it could be answered and no clerk who could be asked to hurry and no counter anywhere in the city where the asking of it would reach anybody.

He put it on the table in front of Wenna Raut. She read it twice at the same speed, which is what she does, and put it in the bundle on top of the other three, and said nothing about it.

“Twenty-ninth of June,” he said.

“Six days after a quarter ends,” said Wenna Raut. “That is nine days. And the bundle goes on the thirtieth, and there is nothing on the desk in between, and whatever it is somebody wanted, it was wanted on the fifteenth and it is still wanted and nobody has been told.”

He stood in the room with a table and two chairs and a drawer open and empty since the fourth of February. The drawer had been the whole of what he had for six years and there was no longer any reason for it to be open.

“Put a mark on the bundle,” he said.

“I will not put a mark on a bundle,” said Wenna Raut. “A clerk enters what she is given.”

“That was not a request for a mark.”

“It was a request for a mark,” said Wenna Raut, “and I am not permitted to keep one, and if I had kept one the only person in this city who could read it is the man standing in front of me.”

*One thousand nine hundred and ten days. Less one thousand nine hundred and two, the thirteenth of May, is eight. Twenty-one less thirteen is eight by the month count.*

*Less the fourth of January, one thousand seven hundred and seventy-three, is one hundred and thirty-seven, and one hundred and thirty-seven is four months and seventeen days, and in those days three papers came to this office that went in front of a magistrate or came off one, and one came from a counter off a fourth court on the fifteenth and is a question this office cannot be asked.*

*Less the eleventh of July, which is day one thousand two hundred and forty, is six hundred and seventy days. The ninth line down has stood on a page going out at four every quarter for all of them, with an hour against every hour of it and no figure and no name, because a division was not permitted to ask her for one and did not ask her.*

*Less the second of May, one thousand eight hundred and ninety-one, is nineteen days. On that day an entry in a chained book in a room off a landing was written off, and this office is the reason it was written off, and this morning, the twenty-first of May, nobody in the world knows that.*
