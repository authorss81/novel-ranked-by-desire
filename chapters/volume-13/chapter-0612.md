# Nine Divisions Return A Line

Six days had gone since a magistrate’s clerk wrote a name into a finding and copied a page into a file with it. One thousand eight hundred and forty-six less one thousand eight hundred and forty is six, and seventeen less eleven is six by the month count.

He went into the division building at about half past ten in the morning with the finding bundle under his arm and four days to produce a line in it, and he had brought nothing to produce it with.

There are nine divisions and every one of them holds a round of districts, and between them they hold every counter a person in this city can walk up to and give something to, and there are about four hundred and nineteen accounts behind those nine counters that somebody entered once and nobody has ever been paid for.

## The Desk That Takes The Returns

Wren Kallo was at the returns desk on the ground floor with a tray and a stamp pad and two nails of her left hand gone into ink so far that the ink had stopped being a thing on a nail some months ago.

She read the card he put on the desk. She read it twice, at the same speed both times, which is what everybody in that building does with anything at all.

“A party returns a line,” she said. “You are not the party.”

“The office is the party and the office holds nothing.”

“The office holds nothing,” Wren Kallo agreed, “and that is the whole of what is wrong with you, and I have been saying it to four divisions a quarter for two years and nobody has ever brought me a finding about it until this morning.” She turned the card round and read the back of it, which was blank, because the back of a card is always blank. “You have brought me a finding about it.”

“You said four divisions.”

“Four divisions send me a return that has a figure on it and a name under the figure,” she said. “Five send me one that has nothing on it at all, not even a reason. Nine send me something. All nine have to send it by the twenty-first and I have to have all nine on this desk by the twenty-first, and I enter what I am given and I cannot improve it and I cannot be asked to hurry.”

“Nine divisions for one line.”

“One line per division,” said Wren Kallo. “Nine lines in a file. That is what a finding looks like when a party is an office instead of a person, and you have not had one before and there is no practice for it and I am doing it the way the form reads, which is the only way there is.”

She put a blank return on the tray in front of him and it had a heading and a space for a division and a ruled space about two inches deep and nothing else on it at all.

“Do not write on that,” she said. “I will not enter it if it is not in a division’s hand.”

## Eight Of Them

The first one came in at about eleven and it was the fourth division and it said that nothing had been given to the fourth division concerning the matter, which was in a return in her own ink with a signature under it, and Lin Hao read it twice and understood that a person had sat down and written four words and signed them.

The second was the first division and said the same thing in a different hand.

The third said the same thing and spelled *concerning* wrong.

The fifth, the sixth, the seventh and the eighth said the same thing, and by the time the eighth one was on the desk it had stopped being information and had become a fact about the weather.

Eight divisions had looked at a finding that named an office with no money and no staff and no fund and had written down that they had never been given anything about it.

None of them had written down why. A return has one ruled space and it is two inches deep, and a reason is not a thing a return has a place for, and every one of the nine officers of those nine divisions had done the correct thing in the only way the paper allowed, and the correct thing eight times over says nothing at all except that the offices have never been told.

“That is the whole of it, then,” said Lin Hao. “Eight blanks and a day to spare.”

“That is eight lines,” said Wren Kallo. “There are nine divisions and one of them is not here and one of them has been here since ten and has not gone to her counter.”

## The One Who Asked

Beatrix Halm came in at about half past one with her round bag over her shoulder and a pencil behind her ear and the good ear turned away from him, which is not a choice anybody makes and is only a thing that happens to people.

“Fourth division,” she said. “I have been at eleven counters since a quarter to eight and I have got a return for you and I have not put it on the desk.”

“Put it on the desk.”

“I will put it on the desk,” said Beatrix Halm, “when you tell me what the line is for.”

Wren Kallo did not look up. She had a tray and a stamp pad and two nails and she was not the person this was happening to.

“That is a fair question,” said Lin Hao. “It is the first one anybody has asked and I am going to answer it properly, so put your good ear this way.”

He turned his head and put himself at her left side, which is the side of her that works, and he did it without asking permission, which is what nine years of going into divisions has taught him to do and which she did not thank him for and did not need him not to do.

“A party named in a finding returns a line,” he said. “A line goes into the file. If there is no line the clerk writes four words and the finding goes up without one. Your office has never been given anything about this. That is the line.”

“That is a blank,” said Beatrix Halm.

“That is a line that says a blank.”

“No,” said Beatrix Halm. “That is where I have been for about eleven years and I would like somebody to say the rest of it to me, because I know exactly what a return is and I have never been told by anybody.”

She put the return on the corner of the desk and did not let go of it.

“There are about four hundred and nineteen accounts behind nine counters and I have entered every one of them in nine years,” she said, “and I enter what I am given and I cannot improve it and nobody can ask me to hurry.”

“About four hundred and eighteen of them are a person who came to my counter and gave me something and was never asked whether they wanted to give it, and I write them down and they go up and they come back as a line in a file, and nobody has ever read one of them back to the person who gave it to me.”

She turned her head so that she was looking at him properly and it cost her the whole of the good ear.

“So tell me what the line is for. Not what goes in it. What it is for.”

He could have said the fourth sentence of the foot of a page. He had it, and it had been out four times since the ninth of November and it would be out again in thirteen days.

He said it.

“Where the Register has nothing,” he said, “it has not been given anything.”

Beatrix Halm did not move for about four seconds.

“Say that again.”

“There is no reason for you to have it twice.”

“There is a reason,” she said, “and it is that I have been putting a blank in a return every day for nine years because there was nothing else to go in the space, and about four hundred people went away from my counter believing that the blank was an answer. I did not know it was. I want to hear it in the exact words you said it in before I put it on a piece of paper that goes to a magistrate.”

He said it again in the same words, and she turned her good ear at him the whole way through, and at the end of it she wrote it in the two-inch space with a pencil behind her ear and did not use her own hand to check it and did not improve a word of it.

## Nine

“That is going to a magistrate,” she said.

“Yes.”

“From me.”

“Yes.”

“Written by me, in a division, on a return, and it is your sentence.”

“It is the office’s sentence. I wrote it. The office printed it.”

“That is not the difference you think it is,” said Beatrix Halm, “and I am telling you the difference because you are the only person in this city who is going to be told it.”

“In thirteen days this goes out on a page with a figure at the head of it that has not moved in five quarters, and about two thousand people a day walk past the foot of it and cannot get past without reading it, and they have all of them decided it means that somebody somewhere is on top of this thing.”

“In thirteen days there will be nine copies of it in nine files on nine counters in nine buildings, and every one of those nine officers will have gone home thinking they have covered themselves, and that is what it does to a person, and it is not a sentence on a page.”

She squared the return on the corner of the desk and left it there.

“I am not being kind and I am not being cruel and I am going to say the thing I actually came down for. You have started a machine. I have been in this building for nine years and I have never seen it start and I do not know what it looks like when it does, and I am telling you that I think you have not either.”

“I have thought about it since November.”

“Not since the seventh of March,” said Beatrix Halm. “Nobody has thought about anything since the seventh of March. That is what a name in a finding does to a building.”

## Where The Record Is

Wren Kallo entered the ninth return at about four in the afternoon and it went into the tray with the other eight, and she read it twice at the same speed and stamped nothing, because nothing on a return is stamped.

“You have nine lines by the twentieth and not the twenty-first, because I will not be here on the twenty-first and I do not ask to be here on the twenty-first,” she said. “Take them to the counter off a third court and do not put them in a drawer, because there is no drawer, and if they are in a drawer on the twenty-second then a clerk writes four words.”

“I have no drawer,” said Lin Hao.

“You have a drawer under a table that has stood open and empty since the first of February,” said Wren Kallo. “I have been in that room twice. Do not put nine divisions in it.”

He took the nine returns and put them in a bundle and stood in the corridor of a division building for about four minutes.

Eight of them said that nine divisions had never been given anything about a landlord’s demand in a district off Lamplit Row, and one of them said, in a division officer’s own hand, in the words he had spoken in a corridor, that where a Register has nothing it has not been given anything.

And none of the nine was an answer. A finding wants one line about the thing in it, and there were nine lines about the counters of a city, and a file with nine blanks in it is a file that says the office has never been told, and about half of what this court does every morning is decide things on files that say nobody has been told.

He wanted a line that said something. He had wanted one since the seventh of March, and he had gone to nine divisions to try to get it, and what he had got was nine copies of the absence of it in nine different hands, and one of them had his sentence on it.

There is a thing about that which he understood as he stood in the corridor, and it was this.

Eight divisions had returned a blank because a blank is what a return has room for. One division had returned a sentence because a person in it had been asked, for the first time in nine years, what the space was for.

And there was exactly one other place in this city where a return of that kind existed, and it was not a counter, and it was not in the room off a third court, and it was two floors under a sales floor.

He had been to that room once in four years and he had gone up sixty-one steps to it, and there was a woman at the far table with a folder, and he had asked her about the account and she had told him things about a shape he could not hold, and he had never once asked her what she was doing there.

Nobody had. That was the thing that came at him at the bottom of the stairs of a division building on the seventeenth of March.

She had been asked five times about the account. She had never once been asked what the work was.

*One thousand eight hundred and forty-six days. Less one thousand eight hundred and forty, the eleventh of March, is six. Seventeen less eleven is six by the month count, and the third of March is the third day of a month.*

*Less the fourth of January, one thousand seven hundred and seventy-three, is seventy-three, and seventy-three is two months with thirteen days after them. The demand came into the room off a third court on the fourth, and a name went into a finding on the eleventh, and a line is due back at a counter off a third court by the twenty-sixth, which is day one thousand eight hundred and fifty-five, and there are nine days left of them, and nine days after this morning.*
