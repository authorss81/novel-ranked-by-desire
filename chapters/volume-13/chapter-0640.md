# The Figure Box Is Blank

Nine days had gone since a boy of sixteen at the foot of a wall had asked, for the first time in six years, what the two words at the bottom of it were for, and had been told, and had gone back to a paste brush. Two thousand and fifty-one was the twelfth of October and the twenty-first is day two thousand and sixty, and two thousand and sixty less two thousand and fifty-one is nine, and twenty-one less twelve is nine by the month count.

He went to the foot of the ranks wall at about ten in the morning on the twenty-first of October, and the boy was there with the month’s form still up on the board by the kerb with the two ruled boxes at the foot of it, because the form for a month goes up on the last working day of the month and that was nine days away.

He had something he had not had for six years, which was a want that was going to cost somebody else.

## The Left Box

“There are two boxes at the bottom of that,” he said.

“There are,” said the boy.

“The left one is for the figure of the thing at the address.”

“That is what the notice says.”

“Whose notice.”

“The one at the top, on the form,” said the boy. “It says a quarter and a Bureau and where it came from. It does not say what the boxes are for. I asked about that once, about two years back, and I was told there was a paper for it somewhere, and there is not one.”

He put the brush down on the kerb.

“You are going to tell me what the left one is for.”

“The left one is for the figure of the thing at the address,” said Lin Hao.

“Then what is the figure of the thing at the address,” said the boy. “Because I have been painting that form about forty times and I have never known, and there is a wall in front of me and about two hundred people a day stop at that corner and about two of them get to the bottom.”

## What Is The Figure Of A Wall

He stood about nine feet off a wall and worked it out at the speed of a man who has had nine days.

A wall is not a thing a figure can be taken of. A wall has courses in it and tiles and about four inches of stone under the lowest rank that a coat of paint will not hold on, and two words, and a form.

The only figure this office has ever had is at the head of a page, and it is eight hundred and fifty-two, and it is how many lines the page has got on it.

“Say that again,” said the boy.

“I cannot put it on your form.”

“Why not.”

“Because the left box is for the figure of the thing at the address,” he said. “The address on your form is the address of this office, and the thing at the address of this office is a page and not a wall.”

“Eight hundred and fifty-two is the number of lines on a page and it has not moved in six quarters. If I put it in that box then in about nine days it is on a wall in this city in the office’s hand, and about two hundred people a day will stop at it.”

“So it goes on a wall.”

“It goes on a wall, and in about nine days after that it is on about two hundred more walls, because that is what a figure does once it is in a hand anybody trusts, and about two thousand people a day cannot get past the foot of it.”

## The Boy’s Face

The boy looked at the north end for about four seconds.

“My father put a number on a board on a hill and the whole yard decided it meant something in about eleven minutes,” he said. “I heard about that. I was not there.”

“No.”

“So you are telling me that if you write a figure in that box then in about nine days it is a number about something else on about two hundred walls,” said the boy, “and you know that, because you watched it.”

“Yes.”

“Then it is not going in.”

“No,” said Lin Hao. “It is not going in, and I am going to leave it blank, and you are going to paint a blank box at the foot of a form on the twenty-eighth of this month and again on the thirtieth of November and again on every last working day for as long as there is a wall to put a form up on.”

The boy picked the brush up.

“That is the answer to the thing I asked you about,” he said, “and it is not the answer I wanted.”

“No.”

“You are going to be asked again.”

“Yes.”

“By somebody else. Not me. I have asked one question in six years, I got what I asked for, and I am not spending a second one inside a month.” He wiped the brush on his coat. “There is a woman comes past here most mornings who has about nine hundred people’s attention and she cannot write and she has got chalk on her, and she is going to ask you, and she is going to ask you this week.”

## What She Asked Instead

She asked him on the twenty-third of October, in front of about nine people, at about eleven in the morning, and she did not ask for a figure.

“You said eight hundred and fifty-two is how many lines,” she said.

“I did.”

“And then you let them put CLAIMS under it.”

“I did not let anybody do anything,” he said. “A man with a rag took a sentence down at about twenty past four and I stood nine feet off and did not stop him, and that is the whole of what I did.”

“Right,” said the woman of about sixty. “Then here is what I want and it is not a figure.”

“Say it.”

“I have been standing at the foot of that board for six weeks telling nine hundred people what a number means,” she said, “and I am a person who has told about four hundred of them a thing that is not true, because the number came off a rail on a morning and nobody said where it came from and nobody in this yard has ever asked.”

She had chalk dust on the front of her coat.

“I want to say in this yard, out loud, that I put a number on a board and it was a number of lines and I did not know that when I did it, and that about two hundred people have written it down and told other people, and that in about two hundred houses in this city there is a person who thinks there are eight hundred and fifty-two claims.”

“That would be true.”

“It would be true and it would be the fifth thing that has been true and it would not change one person’s rent,” said the woman of about sixty. “And I have worked out that I am not going to do it, and I want you to hear me not do it.”

“Why.”

“Because in about nine days that figure is the only thing I have got to stand in front of nine hundred people with,” she said, “and if I take it off them this morning then I am a woman on a step putting up something nobody in this yard has asked for, and I have been that once a month for about fifteen months and I am not doing it twice.”

He looked at her for about four seconds.

“You are not thanked,” he said. “You are given nothing. And in about nine days a page goes out at four with eight hundred and fifty-two at the head of it that has not moved, and about nine people a week will read it to the bottom.”

“I know how many read it to the bottom,” said the woman of about sixty. “I have read it to the bottom. It is the only thing I have ever read to the bottom of and I cannot write my own name.”

## The Figure That Was Available

There was a figure available to him. There has only ever been one and it has been available to him since the ninth of November.

He could have put eight hundred and fifty-two in the left box of a form at the foot of a wall in the office’s hand, and about two hundred people a day would have stopped at it, and about two of them would have got to the bottom.

Every one of the two would have gone and told about eleven people. In nine days there would be a number in the office’s hand at the bottom of a wall where two thousand people a day cannot walk past without reading it.

He had spent six years building one thing that a city could not check. It was a sentence and it was at the foot of a page four times a year.

A figure in the office’s hand at the bottom of a wall is not that. A figure in the office’s hand at the bottom of a wall is the only thing this city has ever obeyed.

He left it blank.

That is the whole of the decision and he made it in about four seconds standing nine feet off a wall on the twenty-first of October.

Nobody asked him for it. There is no instrument in Ostara that will put a figure in that box or take one out of it. The box has been empty since before this office had a printer and it is going to be empty on the thirtieth of this month and on the thirtieth of November and on the thirtieth of December.

## The Man At The Desk Under A Window

He went down Cordwain at about half past four in the afternoon.

“Seven,” said the man at the desk.

“Seven.”

“Seven of them the same and I have said that six times since the thirtieth of March and five of the six were strangers and one of them was a woman who could not read and stood with her thumb on the second column for about nine minutes.” The man did not turn round. “There is one more thing and then I will go back to my thirty names.”

“Say it.”

“There is a ledger on this desk,” said the man at the desk, “and it has about thirty names in it and it is the only thing I have ever kept. And since about the end of September I have started putting the dates people come in, because something changed in the middle of the year and I wanted to know what it was.”

“Anything in it.”

“Four things in it since the seventh of July,” said the man at the desk. “Four, and every one of them wants something off a page or a desk, and three of them are about the seventh of July and the fourth is not.”

He turned the ledger about nine degrees so that the top of it showed.

“There is a woman in here who wrote to the office about a figure,” he said. “She left a piece of paper at a counter off a third court on the twenty-eighth of September and it was never in a bundle, because I was standing here when she put it down and she put it down about two inches away from me.”

“You took it.”

“I took it and I have written the date on it in pencil because I have a pencil and a ledger and no form about anything.”

That is the whole of what this city has got at the end of ten months of the thing he wrote.

A sentence in the hand of an office on a page four times a year. A figure in about nine hundred mouths that means something else. A form on a wall with two boxes and nothing in either. And a man with a pencil and a ledger who has no standing and does not know it, and who has never once asked anybody what a sheet on a rail was for.

It is true. It has not been a comfort once in ten months. And there is not one instrument in Ostara behind any part of it.

*Two thousand and sixty days. Less two thousand and fifty-one, the twelfth of October, is nine. Twenty-one less twelve is nine by the month count.*

*Less the thirtieth of September, two thousand and thirty-nine, is twenty-one days, and that is three weeks. The twenty-third sheet went out at four on that morning with a figure at the head of it that has not moved in seven quarters and is not going to move on the twenty-fourth sheet either, which goes out at four on the thirtieth of December, day two thousand one hundred and twenty-nine, and that is two months and nine days from this afternoon.*

*Less the seventh of July, one thousand nine hundred and fifty-six, is one hundred and four days, which is three months with fourteen days after them, and on that morning about nine hundred people took a rule that could not be tested off their own board with a rag and a bucket over about eleven minutes, and the man who wrote it stood nine feet off and said nothing, and there have been one hundred and four days of it since.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is three hundred and forty-two days, which is eleven months with twelve days after them. It is the fourth of four sentences at the foot of a page. It is true. It was said out loud on the twelfth of October to a boy of sixteen at the foot of a wall and it is not a comfort and there is not one instrument in this city behind it.*
