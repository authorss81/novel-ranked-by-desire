# A Form From The Department Of Works

Seven days had gone since a keeper of a chained book told him on a landing that the only instrument in Ostara which could test the fourth of his four conditions was the man who had written it, and had then told him about a sum put out of a book that was not that book, four times, by somebody four floors up.

One thousand eight hundred and twenty less one thousand eight hundred and thirteen is seven, and twenty-one less fourteen is seven by the month count.

He came down to the wall at ten and found the boy with the paste brush already up the ladder, the month’s form under his arm and one boot hooked round the third rail.

He had one question and he had walked nine streets to ask it and he asked it from the kerb.

“Which wall gets taken down.”

The boy looked down at him from the ladder with the brush in his teeth and took about four seconds before he took it out.

“That is not one of the two things I am allowed to say.”

“It is one of the two things I am not allowed to say twice,” Lin Hao said. “You said in January that the place that decides which wall is done in a quarter is nine streets away. I have come to find out what the deciding looks like.”

“All right,” said the boy. “That one I can say. You come to the gate and you say which wall and they give you a piece of paper, and the paper has the wall on it and a date on it, and then you go and do what the paper says.”

“What does this one say.”

“This one says up,” said the boy. “It says up on the first working day of the month with two boxes at the foot and nothing in either of them, and it has said up on the first working day of the month for as long as I have been doing it.”

## Nine Streets

He was at the gate of the department of works at about half past eleven and he was in the queue behind a man with a barrow of broken paving, and it took him until about two in the afternoon, and the thing at the counter was a form.

There is a yard behind that gate and about thirty people were in it, and half of them were waiting on nothing at all and had been since the middle of the month. Two men were lifting flags out of a gutter. Nobody spoke to anybody. A woman came through with a hoop of iron over her arm and went past the counter without stopping and nobody looked up.

It is a piece of paper with a heading on it and a ruled line for the wall and a ruled line for the date, and one ruled box at the foot of it, and the clerk at that counter filled in two of the three and gave it to him without any interest whatever in him.

“The ranks wall,” Lin Hao said.

The clerk wrote it.

“The date.”

“Coming down for making good,” said the clerk. “Spring.”

He put the pen down and took the form out of the tray and turned it round and put it flat on the counter under the light so that Lin Hao could read it, which no clerk in four divisions and two rent boards and one magistrate’s counter had ever done for him in five years.

It said what clerks say. It said a wall, in a street, in a district, and a date in the spring, and the word *making good*, and one ruled box at the foot for the date the work was done, and it went to a schedule that he could not see and could not be told about and had no standing to ask for.

“Making good,” he said.

“Stone and pointing and a coat at the end,” said the clerk. “It is the ordinary list. There are about eleven of these a quarter and yours is on it because it has been on the list for years.”

“For years.”

“It has been on the list for years,” said the clerk, “and it is not an honour and it is not a punishment. A wall gets on a list when the mortar goes, and the mortar goes because of the weather, and there is no arguing with the weather and no form for arguing with the weather.”

“I am not arguing with the weather,” Lin Hao said. “I am asking what happens to the paint.”

“The paint gets painted,” said the clerk. “There is a boy on that wall every month and he puts the month’s form up at the bottom of it and there are two boxes on that form and he has never once had anything to put in either of them, and that is not my department and it is not his either.”

“It is nobody’s department.”

“It is nobody’s department and it has been for about six years,” said the clerk, “and if you have come to me about that then you have come to the one counter in this city where a person cannot even be entered on the list.”

## What He Wrote To The Department

He got it into the office’s hand by six in the afternoon and it took him four attempts.

The first attempt was a request, and a request is a thing that asks a person to do a thing, and he struck it out.

The second was an argument, and an argument is a thing you put in front of somebody who has to answer it, and there was not anybody in nine streets who had to answer anything.

The third was four lines long and it began *the wall carries two words in the hand of an office at an address* and ended *and the paint goes over them and does not take them and they have been there for six years*, and he read it in the room off a third court and it was true and it was the fourth true thing he had written this month.

The fourth one he did not strike out, and he got it into the office’s hand the same afternoon, and Wenna Raut entered it exactly as given and read it twice at the same speed and it went into the day book in about nine seconds.

It went into a day book on a shelf in a room with no street door, and that book goes into a bundle when the quarter is over and the bundle goes into another room, and the only thing this office ever puts in front of the public is one page four times a year.

And then he stopped, and he stopped in the middle of the room with his coat still on.

“Why,” said Adeyemi from the end of the table.

“Because you may only enter what you are given,” Lin Hao said. “And because I have just handed this office a question to put to a department of works, and nowhere in this city is there a piece of paper on which a question travels to a department of works.”

“There is one for a division and one for a magistrate’s clerk and one for a board, and every one of the three has a counter and a person behind it who is paid to give an answer. There is nothing at all on the other side of this. I have addressed a sentence to a piece of masonry.”

“Then say what you want.”

“I want two words repainted on a wall in the spring after the wall has been made good, in a shape, at a height, and I have no instrument to ask for it and the office cannot be compelled and cannot sue and cannot be sued, and there is no form on which anybody can be made to put two words back onto a wall that no longer exists.”

“You have written four lines about it,” said Adeyemi.

“I have written four lines about it and there is nothing in them that can be answered.”

He stood in the middle of that room and understood the whole of the thing at once and it was not a surprise and it was the worst morning he had had in six years.

He had spent a winter making two sentences legible to a city, and one of them had gone round nine corridors and a yard and a lane, and there is a wall in this city where two thousand people a day cannot get by it without reading, and the two words on it are at the height of a name on a plate.

The wall comes down in the spring.

There is no schedule anywhere in this city for putting two words back onto a wall that does not exist, and no instrument that can make anybody do it, and the office that paints that wall answers to a department nine streets away and not to the Register.

## Eleven At Night

He worked the branch floors and came down the corridor at eleven with the bucket, past a nail with the month’s rota on it and four names written out in a man’s hand, and past three women on a night. Petra Ilyin had her book open and her pencil still, and Isolde Marchetti was at the far end reading.

“Did you get anything,” said Nadia Ferrous.

“No.”

“What did they give you.”

“A form.”

She laughed at that, once, without any harm in it, and went down the washroom stair with her sleeves down.

He leaned on the counter with the bucket between his boots and turned the form round in his mind the way he has turned a page over four or five times in four years.

There was one ruled box at the foot of it, and it was for the date the work was done, and it was going to be filled in by a man with a ladder and a paste brush in about nine weeks, and he would fill it in the way he filled in the two boxes at the foot of the form that goes up the wall, which is to say that he would leave it empty and nobody would ever ask him what it was for.

He had been looking at two blank boxes at the foot of a wall for about five years.

And now he was holding a third one on a piece of paper from a department nine streets away, and it was blank before anybody had written in it, and it would be blank afterwards, and there was no instrument in Ostara that could make a hand go into it.

*The twenty-first of February, one thousand eight hundred and twenty days. Less one thousand eight hundred and thirteen, the fourteenth, is seven. Twenty-one less fourteen is seven by the month count.*

*Less the seventh of February, one thousand eight hundred and six, is fourteen. Less the thirtieth of January, one thousand seven hundred and ninety-nine, is twenty-one, and the month’s form went up on the thirtieth with two ruled boxes at the foot of it and nothing in either.*

*Less the fourth of January, one thousand seven hundred and seventy-three, is forty-seven. Less the thirtieth of December, one thousand seven hundred and sixty-nine, is fifty-one, and thirty with twenty-one is fifty-one by the month count.*
