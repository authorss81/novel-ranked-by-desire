# A Department That Answers Elsewhere

Five days had gone since a boy with a scraper had taken a hand’s width of six years of paint off the front of a wall in about two hours and had made a copy of two words on the back of a month’s form.

Two thousand one hundred and twenty-one was the twenty-second of December and the twenty-seventh is day two thousand one hundred and twenty-six, and two thousand one hundred and twenty-six less two thousand one hundred and twenty-one is five, and twenty-seven less twenty-two is five by the month count.

The department of works is nine streets from the foot of that wall and it is the building he queued for about two hours behind a barrow of broken paving in on the twenty-first of February, and there was a barrow of broken paving in the queue again, because it is the twenty-seventh of December and they are putting a hole in a street somewhere else in this city and the stone comes out in the winter.

He stood in it for about an hour and fifty minutes.

## The Counter

The man at the counter had been on it a long time and took the form off him and read the address on the top of it, which is the only field on it anybody in that building has ever read, and wrote a number on a slip about the size of a thumb-nail and gave him the slip.

“There you are.”

“What is it.”

“It is the number it went in under,” said the man at the counter. “It is a spike and a book and about nine hundred things on the spike and everything on the spike is a wall that a department has been told about at some point in the last thirty years. Yours is on the spike and it is a wall and the address is the wall and that is the whole of what the form says.”

He turned it about ninety degrees and read the address again to be sure.

“You have had this since February.”

“Yes.”

“Nine months.”

“Three hundred and six days.”

“Nine months and about six days,” said the man at the counter, “and I have had about nine hundred and forty of these and every one of them is a wall and every one of them says what is to be done to the wall in a line at the bottom and not one of them has ever said anything about what is on the wall. Not one. Not in thirty years.”

“There are two words on it.”

“There are two words on it and I have read the two words on about nine hundred and forty forms,” said the man at the counter, “and I have never once known what a form was for, because there is not a field on it for it.”

## The Question That Had Not Been Asked Before

He said, “What are they?”

It was out before he had decided to ask it and the man at the counter stopped with his hand on the spike and looked at him.

“Mine,” said Lin Hao. “The two words at the foot of that wall are in the hand of an office and the office’s address is on the form the boy paints every month, and there is nobody in this city who has ever asked me what they are, and you are the first person I have said that to in nine months and I have said it at a counter in a queue.”

The man at the counter came round the front of the counter.

“Say that again.”

“Two words at the foot of a wall nine streets from here, cut into four inches of stone, and they have been under sixty-two coats of paint since the eighth of October of a year nobody in this building says, and about two hundred people a day stop at that corner.”

“Read me the words.”

“No.”

“Why not.”

“Because I have read them to about four hundred people in a yard twice at the same speed with nothing improved, and the last time I did that about nine hundred people turned them into a count of something, and I am not going to be the reason a thing I said on open ground ends up in about two hundred houses meaning something else.”

“You think they are worth saying.”

“I think they are the only true sentence anybody has stood on in six years and I think nine people a week read them and about four in every thousand in this city have ever got to them, and those two figures have been the same every quarter and they are going to be the same on the twenty-fourth sheet, which is in three days.”

“And you think that is a good state for a city to be in.”

“I think it is the state a city is in when one office prints the only true sentence in it,” said Lin Hao, “and I think you are the ninth man in about nine years to ask me a question at this counter about anything other than a date, and I have not got a better answer than the true one and I am not going to invent one at you across a counter in a queue.”

“It is the twenty-seventh.”

“Then it is the twenty-seventh,” he said, “and I have had a figure in a hand all day and I am pleased to get one wrong.”

The man at the counter looked at him for about four seconds.

“That is the first time anybody has turned anything down at this counter that I have not been about to write it off in the book as refused,” he said. “I have written four hundred of those up since the spring and about nine hundred and forty things have gone in since I came, and about half of them were put in by men who wanted a date changed.”

He went back behind the counter.

“I cannot do anything with two words. There is not a column for two words on this counter, or on a schedule, or on a sheet, and there is not a person in this building who paints anything.”

“That is the half of it I came for.”

“That is the half of it you came for, and I am giving it to you now, and I am not going to charge you for it.”

“And the other half.”

“The other half is that you have been carrying that sheet in your coat for nine months,” said the man at the counter, “and you have not once read the bottom of it, and I have watched you take it out of your coat about four times since you came in and put it back every time. The bottom of it is the only part of it that has ever had anything on it for anybody.”

“That is the box for the date the work was done.”

“That is what I have been saying,” said the man at the counter. “Come back in a week and the box will be a different box, because the wall will be down and the date will have happened, and then you will have a piece of paper with a thing on it, and you will not have needed nine months of a queue to get it.”

“The other half.”

“The other half is the painting,” said the man at the counter. “The painting is not us. The wall is ours and the wall is going down in the spring for making good, and the painting is a Bureau contract and it goes on a schedule in a different building nine streets the other way.”

He left the book open on the spike. “The two offices have not sent a paper to each other in the nine years I have been on this counter, and there is nothing that makes them, and that is not a complaint. That is a schedule.”

## The Schedule

It was up in a corridor off the second floor and it was about nine hundred lines long and anybody could look at it, which is stated at the bottom of it in a notice that has been on the bottom of it since before the man at the counter came.

Every line has an address, and a line of work, and a box for the date the work was done.

The ranks wall was on it. He found it in about four minutes because he has been walking past the foot of it for six years and he knows what the address says and it says the address of the office that owns it, which is not this office.

The line of work said *take down to course and make good.*

The box for the date was empty, and every box on the nine hundred lines was empty except eleven, and those eleven had dates in them and the dates were all older than a wall being taken down.

“Who is doing the work.”

“The painting office,” said the man at the counter, who had come up the stair behind him because he had said he would. “Nine streets the other way. They will find out from this schedule, because this is the schedule and it is up on the last working day of every month and it comes down on the first, and that is how they have found out about every wall in this city for as long as anybody has been keeping it.”

“How long have they been finding out.”

“Since before I came,” said the man at the counter.

## Nine Streets The Other Way

The painting office is one room above a store and it has a counter in it and a boy of sixteen standing at a bench at the back of it, and the boy had been told that the works department had been.

“He has not been here since the twenty-second of December and he is not going to be here again until the spring,” said the man at the counter, who had come with him because a man cannot send a piece of paper nine streets on its own. “What do you want.”

“There are four inches of dressed stone at the foot of that wall.”

“There are four inches of dressed stone at the foot of about nine hundred walls,” said the man at the painting counter. “A wall is render up to the course and course below that. Making good is render. We do render. The stone at the bottom is the building’s and has been the building’s since it was put up and we have not painted a foot of dressed stone in my lifetime and neither has anybody in this room.”

“Not one person has ever asked you.”

“Not one person has ever asked me,” said the man at the painting counter, “because the form I get comes up from the works and it says an address and a line of work and a box for the date, and it has not got a field on it for what is at the foot of the thing, and I have been on this counter about eleven years and I have raised it twice and both times I was told it is a question and not a return.”

He turned the form round and read the address on it.

“That is yours.”

“That is the address of the office that owns the wall, which is not my office, and the two words at the foot of that wall are in my hand.”

“Then it is on your form and not on mine,” said the man at the painting counter, “and I cannot tell you what to do about it, and I would not if I could, because I have a bench and a boy and about four hundred walls and none of them have ever had a form with a question in it.”

## The Box At The Foot Of The Form

He got the form back on the walk and turned it over on the wall outside the store.

There is one ruled box at the foot of it and it is for the date the work was done.

Three hundred and six days.

He had carried a piece of paper in his coat for three hundred and six days because a clerk gave it to him in a queue in February.

There is a box on it for the day the wall comes down, and there is no way on earth to write a date that has not happened yet, and he had not seen that for nine months, because a man who is looking for a question to ask a department does not read the bottom of a form in his own coat.

He had been carrying the answer.

He stood outside a store on a cold street and understood the whole of nine months in about nine seconds, and then he folded it and put it back in his coat, because there was nothing else to do with it.

## What The Number Is For

The slip was about the size of a thumb-nail and it had a number on it and it was the first piece of paper with an instrument behind it that he had been given in about a year.

“You could ask for it back,” said the man at the counter from the works department, who had walked nine streets with him and had not been asked to.

“There is a line in the book and a date in the book and about nine hundred things on a spike. If a man came in with a number on a slip like that he would be told which wall it was and what was to be done to it and when it went in, and that is a thing that can be asked about by anybody who has got a slip.”

“That is not what it is for.”

“No,” said the man at the counter. “It is for us. You keep the slip and in thirty years somebody in a room that has never been opened will want to know what came in on the twenty-seventh of December in the ninth month and it will be in the book, and that is what a number is. It is not a way of getting anything. It is a way of being found.”

He turned and went back the nine streets without saying anything else about it, because he had a counter and it was nearly four.

## Three Days

At about four on the twenty-seventh of December he was at the foot of the wall again with about nine people at the corner and about four of them holding a sheet of paper.

The boy was not there and the board by the kerb had no form on it, because the form for a month goes up on the last working day of the month and the last working day of this month is in three days. The paint was about a week old on the render above the place where the paint had come off on the twenty-second, and the four inches of stone had about a week of weather on them and nothing else on them.

He stood about nine feet off with a slip the size of a thumb-nail in his hand and a form in his coat and did not know what either of them was for.

In three days a page would go out at four with a figure at the head of it that had stood for eight quarters and six lines at the foot of it with a hole in the middle of the five, and about nine people a week would get to the bottom of it.

And in the spring a man from a department would come to a wall nine streets from here with a form that says an address and a line of work and a box for the date the work was done, and there is not one field on it about the four inches of dressed stone at the foot of that wall, and the man with the form would not ask about it, because a question is a question and not a return, and he has raised it twice.

*Two thousand one hundred and twenty-six days. Less two thousand one hundred and twenty-one, the twenty-second of December, is five. Twenty-seven less twenty-two is five by the month count.*

*Less the twenty-first of February, one thousand eight hundred and twenty, is three hundred and six days, which is ten months with six days after them. A man queued for about two hours behind a barrow of broken paving in the eighth month of a year nobody here says and was handed a sheet about the size of a leases, with one ruled box at the foot of it for the date the work was done, and he carried it for all three hundred and six days and never once turned it over.*

*Less the thirtieth of September, two thousand and thirty-nine, is eighty-seven days, which is two months with twenty-seven days after them, and the schedule of the department of works has about nine hundred lines on it and eleven dates in boxes and the line for this wall says take down to course and make good, and the schedule is up on the last working day of every month, which is the thirtieth, which is in three days.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is four hundred and eight days, which is thirteen months with eighteen days after them, and the fourth of four conditions on the foot of a page went through on the twenty-third of November of this year and there is nothing written where it was, and a page goes out at four on the thirtieth of December in three days with the hole in it.*
