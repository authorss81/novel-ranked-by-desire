# The Man Entitled To Read It

Seven days had gone since a man with a slate read a woman a line out of his own memory at a wall end, and neither of them thanked the other and he has never found out what she did with it. One thousand eight hundred and eighty-eight less one thousand eight hundred and eighty-one is seven, and twenty-nine less twenty-two is seven by the month count.

The finding was listed to be read on the twenty-ninth of April and he went to the counter off a third court at about ten in the morning with no bucket, and the clerk was not at the table with the bundle on it, which was the first thing that was different.

He was at the back with a file open on a slope, and the bundle from the eleventh of March was not on the table, and there was a sheet of another colour on top of everything in the file.

“Sit down,” he said. “It is read back before it is taken up. You are the party and there has never been a party in this file before, so I am going to do it the way it is done.”

He wanted to be asked.

That was the want he had brought with him down a stair and across two courts, and it had been in him since the eleventh of March and it had got worse every week, and he had got as far as the door of the room off a third court at about ten past ten and had said it to himself in a corridor in words.

He was not asked.

He was read to.

## In Order

The clerk read the file the way a man reads a file, which is in order, and at the same speed, and he did not stop for anything.

“A demand of the fourth of January, four lines, a figure, a date and a room number. The four lines are the ones relied on by the landlord and he has sworn to the copy and the copy is off a board at a public desk.”

“The address at the head of the board is the address of the party named in this file. The landlord is right about the flat and about the tenant and about all three of the first lines and he has not been asked about any of it.”

“Objections,” he said.

“There are none from you, because you have none and I am not going to look for any.”

He turned a page.

“Answer of the party, returned on the twenty-sixth of March. The party holds nothing.”

“Is that what it says.”

“It is exactly what it says and it is nine lines and there is no more to it than that, and I have read it nine times because there are nine of them and they are all different in the hand and the same in the meaning.”

He turned another page.

“Returns of nine divisions of this city, the seventeenth of March. Eight of them are the same. The ninth has four words in it that are not in the other eight and I am going to read them because they are in the file and I have not got a discretion about the file.”

He read the nine and he read the ninth one and he read it the way it was written, which was in the middle of a quarter of an inch of a woman’s hand, and he did not look up.

## The Page At The Back

“Now the back,” said the clerk.

He took the sheet of another colour off the top of the file and squared it.

“This is a copy of a page printed four times a year by the party. It is at the back of the file because the four lines relied on in this demand were sworn to as copied off a page, and the page goes with them.”

“There is no date on this copy.”

“There is a date on the original and it is the thirtieth of March,” said the clerk, “and the copy is of this quarter’s, and I had it made on the seventh of March by a woman in the back who copies things without being asked whether the thing being copied is worth copying, and I am aware that that sentence is about me.”

He squared it the other way and looked at the foot of it.

“There are four lines at the foot of the copy in front of me and I am going to read them in order, because that is what a read-back is, and because you have not heard them read by anybody who is entitled to, and because a man who has read a thing nine hundred times to himself does not know what he sounds like.”

He read the first two.

They had been at the foot of that page since before Lin Hao came to the counter and they had no name under them and neither of them had ever been the subject of a question, and the clerk read them at the same speed he had read everything else and neither of them got the air that the other two got, and he did not explain them and he was not asked.

Then he read the third.

“A figure in Ostara may be a figure somebody made up on purpose. This standing sheet is printed by the office itself. Emil Brack.”

He did not say anything after it. He read the name the way a clerk reads a name.

“And the fourth,” he said. “Unrecorded — carried by: where the Register has nothing, it has not been given anything.”

He put the sheet down and squared it against the edge of the file and shut nothing, because files are not shut while they are being read back.

## The Thing He Was Not Allowed

“That is the whole of the file,” he said, “and there are three things I have not said and I am going to say all three of them now and then this is a read-back and not a conversation, and you may not answer any of them because you are not permitted a second question and I am not permitted a second question and that cuts both ways.”

“I am not going to answer any of them.”

“I know. That is the ordinary case and you are the first party in this court who has said so in advance.”

He put his thumb on the ninth return.

“One. The first three of the four lines are capable of being tried. This finding will be taken up this morning and I have no doubt about what it will do, and the landlord is right about the flat.”

“And the fourth.”

“The fourth is not capable of being tried by anybody in this building or in this city or on this earth, because to try it you must know every page in the city, and there is no such person, and the party named in this file is the only thing in Ostara that claims not to know what is on a page.” He did not look up.

“I have known that since the seventh of February. I have had seven weeks to find a way of writing a finding about a rule that nobody can try and I have not found one, and there is not one.”

“Two. The sentence about figures being made up on purpose is in this file in the office’s own hand with a man’s name under it, and it stands at the foot of the same page as the sentence about a blank, four inches away, in the same hand, and about two thousand people a day are obliged to read them as they go past.”

“That is not a question.”

“It is not a question and I am not permitted one and I am still going to say it, because a read-back is the one occasion in this room where a person who is entitled to speak gets to speak for as long as the file lasts.” He squared the file. “You cannot try the fourth line and you cannot try the sentence that says figures get made up.”

“They are both in here and neither of them can be tested and they are four inches apart in the same hand and I have to read both of them in order and out loud, and I have just done it, and I did it because the practice says to and because I am the man who reads it back and that is all I am.”

## The Minute

“Three,” said the clerk. “I am going to write a minute.”

Lin Hao looked up.

“A minute is not a finding and it is not a party and it is not a sentence of anybody’s,” said the clerk. “It is the only thing in this file that is mine. It goes in front of the magistrate with the finding and it is the only page in here that nobody has written but me, and I am entitled to one, and I have not had one in about two years, and I am going to use it on this.”

He took a sheet out of the drawer and squared it.

“It will say that the fourth of four lines relied on in this demand is a statement about the absence of a page. It will say that the party named as the source of the rule has produced nothing and cannot produce anything.”

“It will say that the first three lines are capable of being tried and this one is not, and that the finding proceeds on the first three. And it will say that the rule relied on is not capable of being tried by the court or by any party.”

“That is four sentences.”

“It is four sentences and it will go in in front of a man in about ten minutes and he will read it in about four, and there is no instrument in this city that will take it out of the file afterwards, including the office that printed the page.”

“You are entitled to it.”

“I am entitled to one question and I spent it on the eleventh of March and I have had nothing to do since,” said the clerk,

“and I am going to tell you exactly what you have just had done to you by a man who has been nothing to you, which is that your fourth line has been narrowed by a clerk out of the bottom of a stack of forms, and you did not consent to it and you cannot object to it and you are not the man who wrote it.”

He put the pen down.

“Read it back,” he said, “and then go and be somewhere else for an hour.”

“You said you were not permitted a second—”

“I am not asking you anything,” said the clerk. “I am reading a page back. Read it back.”

Lin Hao read the four sentences back at the same speed, because that is what a man does when he has been told to read four sentences back.

The last of the four was about a rule and not about a person at all, and it had taken the clerk seven weeks to think of and about four minutes to write, and it was going in front of a magistrate in about ten minutes, and there had not been one thing in this city Lin Hao could do about it since the eleventh of March.

He put the page down and said it was correct and went out, and the clerk did not look up, which was the correct thing to do and was also, in that room, a kind of accuracy.

*One thousand eight hundred and eighty-eight days. Less one thousand eight hundred and eighty-one, the twenty-second of April, is seven. Twenty-nine less twenty-two is seven by the month count.*

*Less the fourth of January, one thousand seven hundred and seventy-three, is one hundred and fifteen, and one hundred and fifteen is three months and twenty-five days. The demand came into the room off a third court on the fourth, a name went into a finding on the eleventh, nine divisions returned a line on the seventeenth, the line went to the counter on the twenty-sixth, the finding went up on the twenty-seventh, and the twenty-first sheet went out at four on the thirtieth of March.*

*Less the thirtieth of December, one thousand seven hundred and sixty-nine, is one hundred and nineteen days, and the twentieth sheet went out at four on that day and the thirteenth of November is day one thousand seven hundred and twenty-two, so the first of the four sentences at the foot of a page has been going out at four for one hundred and sixty-six days with a name under it.*
