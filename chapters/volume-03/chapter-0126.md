# Two Columns, One Head

The Office had asked for the case file on the twenty-second and had it by the twenty-sixth, and Tove Marlin brought it up the back stair of the Exchange building at twenty to eleven and put it on the counter under the certified run and squared the two of them against each other so that the tops of the sheets were level.

"You did not show me this yesterday," Lin Hao said.

"You did not ask for it yesterday." She put her hand flat on it. "I had it in a tube. It came out of a district file on Cobb Street at four o'clock yesterday and it has been read by three people, one of whom is a Field clerk who copied the date twice and one of whom is a reporting officer who wrote the word tolerance on the margin of the first one on the twenty-second and then went to a meeting."

The sheet was a single page. It had a head across the top in machine print and a column under the head, and the column had twenty-one entries in it.

The head said COUNTERPARTY.

Lin Hao looked at it for a while.

"The run says counterparty," he said. "And this says counterparty. Same word, same hand of machine, two documents, four years and four days apart."

"Same word," said Tove Marlin. "I have had it pointed out to me twice this morning by people who work in this building and did not know of each other, and both of them said it as though they had found something, and I have said to both of them that it is a heading and headings are cheap and that is the whole of it."

It was not the whole of it and she knew it, because she had squared the two sheets level on purpose at twenty to eleven in the morning with a clerk present.

"Six," said the clerk. "That is what the output has."

"Twenty-one," said Tove Marlin. "That is what the file has, on the same date, under the same word."

Thirty-four days. Five left in this month after today and twenty-nine in the next.

He did the subtraction standing at the counter with the plate queue audible two floors below through the socket. Six on one sheet. Twenty-one on the other. The word over both of them was the same word.

Fifteen.

"Fifteen movements on that date have nothing against them on either side of those two pieces of paper," said Tove Marlin, "and every one of the fifteen is somebody who was there. That is not a figure. That is five women and ten men, or it is a shift, or it is a queue, and I do not know which, and neither does the file, because the file says counterparty and stops."

"It does not stop," said Lin Hao. "It carries on for twenty-one."

"It carries on for twenty-one and it does not tell you why the machine stopped at six, and neither does the machine, because the machine is not on this floor and cannot be asked and will not be run in this room."

Lin Hao turned the case file round so that the margin faced up.

There was a hand of writing down the right-hand side of the certified run, in pencil, in a small slanted hand that was not Sarn's and that had gone over the same four words more than once.

Instrument out of tolerance.

Above it, a date. The ninth of the month the run was made, four years back.

And below the margin, running up the left-hand edge of the sheet where a sheet carries the marks of what has been done to it, four entries in four different hands in four different inks.

The first said the ninth, and under it a district summary number, and the word acknowledged.

The second said the fourth of the next month, and a quarterly reference, and the word carried.

The third said the second of the month after that, and the same quarterly reference, and the word carried.

The fourth said the second of the month four months after the run, and it was on a card rather than on the sheet, and the word on it was closed.

"They carried it four times," said Tove Marlin, reading them the way she read everything, which is exactly as written. "The ninth of the month it was run, into a district summary. The fourth of the month after, into the quarterly. The second of the month after that, into the quarterly again. And the second of the month four months after, when the file closed and the run went onto an index card with its six names on it and the margin went with it."

"Who reconciled it."

"Not one of them, because not one of them did the second thing." She squared the two sheets again. "The margin says the instrument was out of tolerance. It does not say which number was wrong, and the run was certified anyway, and then the whole packet went to the next place because the packet was a certified run and a certified run is supposed to go to the next place."

"Every one of those four offices was holding a run from the ninth and waiting for the next reading, and the next reading came on the twenty-first of some month and was in tolerance and was certified, and a piece of paper in tolerance is the end of everybody's problem."

"They never put two numbers side by side."

"They never had two numbers. That is the whole of the failure and it took four years to happen and it happened because the run went on without the file, and then the file went on without the run, and not one clerk in four years was ever sent to a room to stand between them." Tove Marlin took her hands off the paper. "I want to be exact about this, because it is going into a hall. It is not that four offices were careless."

"It is that four offices did exactly what they were each told to do, in order, on the day, and the failure was arithmetic, and arithmetic does not sit in anybody's column."

"Then it is my job," said Lin Hao.

"It is your job from this morning, and you have one question, because the hearing officer's rule lets a member put a question to the instrument before it is admitted, and a question has to be in writing and it has to go to the desk by the twenty-seventh." She put her hand on the certified run. "Ask why the output has six and the file has twenty-one."

"That is a question about the instrument and only the desk can answer it and it cannot be answered out of these two sheets, because those two sheets are about a man no one in this building has ever heard of."

He looked at the six names and the twenty-one entries and the pencil margin with four forwards on the edge of it.

"Is there a version where somebody is hurt," he said.

"There are fifteen entries with nothing against them," said Tove Marlin. "I have no idea whose they are and neither does anybody on this floor, and if there is a version where somebody is hurt then it is in a file in a district office and not in this room, and that is not a sentence I am going to put in a hall."


The Field house had a yard at the back and an inspection was not due and Runa Sato was sitting on the sill with her sleeves rolled past the elbow when he came round, and the cover note was on the sill beside her with a date on it.

The twenty-sixth. The day it was written for.

"It came back on the nineteenth," she said. "The cover. With the stamp on it and the demand under the stamp in a clerk's hand, and the demand is the twenty-sixth, and today is the twenty-sixth, and I read it on the nineteenth and I have had a week with it."

"What are you going to do."

"I am going to refuse it, and I have written the refusal, and it is in my own hand because a refusal in a serving officer's hand is the only kind that costs anything." She did not pick it up. "I will attest to six lines."

"I wrote six lines on the sixth of last month at a table in Bellhouse with you standing at the end of it, and I can produce the request, and I can name the source of every one of the six, and I will swear to all six in front of anybody they bring into a room."

"And I will not attest to the minute as a whole, because the minute as a whole is a claim that there are seven women in that house, and there are seven, and one of them is standing on a landing in a coat every day of her life because she will not be written down."

"And the minute as a whole asks me to swear to her without my having asked her, and I met her once, at a table, for eleven minutes, and she did not give me a sentence to swear to."

"Runa."

"Do not do that." She looked at him. "Do not come here and ask me what it costs, because I have costed it and it is the same cost as the first one and I know what I am buying. The first one bought me a hearing with a month on it. The second one buys me a hearing with a day on it, and a day is better, because a month is a thing an office chooses and a day is a thing I can count down to."

"And it is not your minute and it was never your minute, and you refused to complete it on the nineteenth in a clerk's day book, which is the correct refusal, and the demand went to me because you stood in front of it, and I want that entered on whatever page you keep."

"It is entered."

"Then here is the last of it and then you will go and clean your floors." She picked the sheet up and turned it round so he could read it. Six lines. A sentence about not attesting as a whole. Her name, and her post, and the twenty-sixth.

"There is no version of this where I sign seven," she said. "If they want seven they can come to Ferrier Street and ask the seventh herself, in person, in her own words, and she will say no, and then they will have asked her, and then it is a fact that they asked. I will not do for them the part where a person is never asked."

He read the refusal twice and it was four sentences and there was no softness in any of them and she was not asking him for anything at all, which he understood was the whole of what she had learned from him.

Thirty-four days, and a refusal in a serving officer's hand going up to a district office, and a hall on the twenty-ninth that would now read six lines and stop, in front of a hundred and forty seats and a rail and ninety standing places behind the back row, where a seventh name would have been.

And a woman in a hearing with a day on it instead of a month.
