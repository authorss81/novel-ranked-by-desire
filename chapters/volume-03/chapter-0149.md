# The Woman Who Is Not On The List

On the thirtieth the hall was in two papers and a street, and by the middle of the morning all three of them had found him.

The transcript had been typed by eight and was for sale over a counter on Cobb Street for a shilling. The item people were talking about was the last thing on the sheet: the member's own description, four sentences, in his own hand, saying that a member's account is a mass he cannot measure and cannot produce.

Underneath it, in the Office's flat hand, the thing that had actually happened. A member whose account remains undescribed, who did not touch a person in a public hall for four hours, and whose instrument produced an empty column.

Not one person in Ostara knew what to do with either sentence, and the city had decided to be interested in the second one for about eleven hours and then go back to being a city.

The other paper was in the Reading Square, because there is one board in this city that publishes everything. The repaint sheet from Thursday carried two lines that were not bands: the hour the sitting began and adjourned, and the finding, with a number against it that was not a score.

The street knew because two hundred and eleven people had been in a room.

At ten he cleaned the second floor with the queue going past, and Drant said nothing to him at the counter for the first time in four years, which took about nine seconds to notice and cost him most of the morning.

At four he went up the stairs above the chop-house and put the bucket down, and the landing light was on, and it had been on since the first of the month, and the woman sitting under it was in her coat.

Sun Meiling had a page of typing paper on her knees with four columns of figures on it in pencil, and she had been on that landing twenty-nine days and had come down twice, both times for the lavatory.

"Sit down. There is a chair on this landing and you have never once sat on it and neither have I, and we are going to do the whole of this on it."

He sat down with his back to the stairwell.

"You were good in that hall. I mean that as a shape and not as a compliment. You did three things in front of two hundred and eleven people and every one of them was at a table and none of them was to a person, and this morning Ostara has read a paragraph about it, and the paragraph is wrong in the exact direction that makes you a man who manufactures his own."

"That has been the Office's position since the twentieth of last month."

"It is going to be the position of every office that reads this file for the next four years, and you have made it very easy for them."

She put the pencil down on the step beside her.

"I am going to ask you for something. I have wanted it since about the third day I spent on this landing and I have not said it out loud to one person, including the woman who downstairs calls it a refusal when she talks about me."

"Ask."

"I want to be the person your description does not name."

The landing was very small, with a rail on one side and a broken spindle in it, and the light made their faces upward.

"Understand what I am asking for. There are seven women in this house and one of us is not named anywhere. Not in the roll, not in the transcript, not in the description, not in the four lines from the clinic, not in the minute, not in the forty-one names, and not on a list belonging to anybody who is anybody."

"You have been off paper since the first of the month."

"I did that before the hall and I would have done it after. A name on a document in this city is a handle. You put a name on paper and every office holding that paper can find the body attached to it.

"That is true of my name more than of anybody else's on that roll, because there is a research wing with a stamp on it and I work in it, and a person who works in it cannot be written down without the wing being written down."

"You are asking me to leave you off."

"I am asking to be the one who is left off, and I am going to say the rest of it now, because if I do not say it in the next two minutes you will decide that you have persuaded me, and you have not persuaded me of anything.

"You have not asked me one question about this. You have not asked me once. I decided this in about four seconds on the twenty-first and I have been sitting on it ever since, waiting to see whether you would ever notice."

"You are not going to give me the chance to stop you."

"I am going to give you the chance to have a position and I am not going to let you use it."

She turned round on the chair so that she was facing him and not the stair.

"And it is not modesty. I want that said in this room before anything else happens, because a woman who says she does not want to be named always gets read as a woman who is being careful. Modesty is what you say when you would like a thing and cannot have it. I would like the thing. I want it. I want the only room in this city with a door on it that a warrant cannot get through."

"That is not modesty either."

"That is not modesty either," said Sun Meiling. "That is what a research wing is."

She stood and put the landing light off with her hand and then put it on again, because she wanted the light, and he understood that she was about to say four things and wanted to see his face while she said them.

"First. I am not going to be kept by this house. Six women downstairs have kept a book with six lines a day since the twenty-first of last month, and since the eighth of this month every one of them has been named or describable in a document somebody in this city can ask for, and they did that for you.

"I am not going to be kept. I am not going to be on a plate, I am not going to be in a roll, and I am not going to be sworn to anything. I have said this on this landing to three people and not one of them heard it, and I am saying it to you on the thirtieth day of a month in which you have already told six women what you were going to do."

"The second is worse and it is the reason I am up here."

"Go on."

"Downstairs there is a woman who cannot keep a silence, a woman who prices things, a woman who keeps a day book with a pencil line in it, and a woman who writes down every name that comes through her car. Every one of them has spent four months building a wall out of paper around a man who does not need a wall.

"And on Thursday you stood at that table and told a clerk a true sentence in your own hand, and then you did three things to yourself in front of two hundred and eleven people. Not one person in this building could have stopped you. Not one person in that hall could have stopped you. Both of those facts were arranged by you."

"I told you first."

"You told us first," said Sun Meiling. "And that is the sentence I have been sitting on for two days, because I have gone back over it about forty times. It is the only sentence anybody has said to me in four months that actually changed what I believe about you, and it has changed it in the wrong direction."

She let that sit for about four seconds.

"A household that does not stop him is not a shield," she said. "It is an audience."

The landing was very quiet and the broken spindle moved about a quarter of an inch in the draught from the stairwell.

"I have been saying that for three months in the wrong words to people who could not act on it. It is not a slogan and it is not a complaint about a house. It is the only correct thing anybody in this building knows.

"It has been correct since the first of the month, and the six women downstairs have been wrong about it in public on Thursday, and they are not the ones who proved it wrong. He proved it wrong, by paying for it out of himself three times with a clerk watching and a certified machine on the table."

She let go of the chair.

"So I am closing it. The refusal is closed and I am closing it myself, and not one person in this house gets a vote. I am not going to be kept. I am not going to be on the roll. And I am going to be in the record with him, by not being in it. The six of you can hate that as much as you like."

"You cannot be in it by not being in it."

"I can. It is the only way anybody with my job has ever been in anything. When a thing is wrong and no office can prove where it came from, the whole record about it is what did not happen.

"I have spent eleven years finding the shape of things by looking at what is missing from them. This morning there is a description of that man in four books in this city and not one word of it in any of the four says Sun Meiling, and that is not a hole in the record. That is me, in it, for as long as the record stands."

She picked the pencil up off the step.

"And it is not a favour to you and it is not modesty. If it were a favour I would want something, and I do not want anything. I have not wanted anything from anybody since a man I worked with was taken out of a building I was standing in.

"I want to be the one who is not named. That is the whole of what I have. I have eleven thousand pages of other people's data and I have got this."

"The third thing, and I have been not saying it since the sixteenth. I did not get it from you and I want that understood before I say it."

She turned the page over on her knees. There were four columns of figures on the back of it as well.

"On the sixteenth you told six women at a table that there was one thing in that hall which might move and that it was you. That is the sentence. It is the only sentence I have been able to hear all week that had a shape to it, and I have been able to hear it in four places, and I did not get it in any of them from anybody who was in that room."

He did not move.

"I cannot tell you how I know it, and that is not coyness. If I told you, you would go and do something about the fact.

"The fact is that a sentence given to six people in a room in Ferrier Street on the sixteenth is now four people deep, three of the four have told another person, and the fourth is a builder on a bench in an Exchange building who has never in her life been able to help it."

"Wren."

"I am not going to say that name on this landing and neither are you." She turned the pencil over once.

"What I am going to give you is a shape, because the shape is the only thing I am actually good at. A sentence about a man who is going to move something on himself in public travels sideways. It goes to people who have had a cold minute and have started keeping track, and it does not travel through the people you told. It goes through the shop and the yard and the counter."

"How many people."

"I cannot give you a number, and that is not modesty either. I can give you the depth. It is four, and it is not going to be four by the end of next week, because a man who writes a thing down in his own hand makes every person who reads it decide to write their own down somewhere.

"You did that on Thursday at a table with a clerk, and it will cost you more in February than it cost you on Thursday."

"February."

"I have not got February." She said it flatly. "I have got a shape and it goes that way, and I have been wrong about things before, and not one person in this city has any way of finding out that I was wrong. That is the price of my gift and I have paid it for eleven years without anybody once asking me what it was."

She put the pencil behind her ear.

"Go on with it," said Sun Meiling. "You are going to keep the promise. You made it before you did it, and a promise made before the act is worth four times what it is worth after.

"I have watched you for four months and you have never once broken a thing you said out loud in a room where it could be written down. That is the whole of what I have about you, and it is not a small thing, and I am not going to dress it up."

The fourth thing took her about ten seconds.

"I want to be touched by a man who cannot describe me."

"You are aware of how that sounds."

"I am aware of how it sounds, I have thought about the wording of it for two days, and I have decided not to improve it." She put her hands in her lap.

"Every woman in this city who has wanted you has wanted the thing you can do. Every woman in this city has wanted to be written down next to you, because a man who is in the record is a man with a household, and a household is the only thing in Ostara that can be asked about somebody and come back with a person."

"Yes."

"And I do not want to be written down next to you. I want to be in a room with a man who cannot put me in a document, because there is no instrument in this city that could produce the sentence I am about to say about this afternoon, and I have had eleven years to make sure of it."

She looked at him.

"You are the only man in Ostara that an office cannot file. You are going to stay that way for the rest of your life, because of what you wrote on Thursday in your own hand, and I am going to spend an hour on a landing with you while that is true, and not one clerk in this city is ever going to find a page that says it happened."

"That is a want, and it is a good one, and it is not going to survive the month."

"It has survived twenty-nine days of a landing already," she said, "which is more than most things get in this house."

"Four terms," said Sun Meiling, "and I have never made four terms in my life, so I am going to read them off like a clerk, and you are going to let me, because it is the only skill I have got when I am frightened."

"All right."

"One. I am awake, I am not in anything, I have taken nothing, and I have not been inside a laboratory since Friday. Two. I will ask you twice, and the second time is a question. Three. You stop me with one word. Four. You tell me one thing about yourself that is not in a record, and then I will tell you one thing about myself that is not in a record, and neither of us knows what the other one is until we say it."

"That is Ines's fourth term."

"It is Ines's fourth term and she said it to you in her rooms on Thursday week, and you came out of that room and came up here and have not put it down." She said it without any weight. "I am not going to pretend I invented it. She asked to be the voice. I am asking to be the one the voice does not reach. That is the whole of the difference between us and it is enough to put us on different landings."

"Ask me," he said.

"I am not going to ask you in the words. I am going to tell you what I want first, and then you are going to answer it, and then I am going to ask you."

"Go on."

"I want you to take about an hour, put your hands on me, and be somewhere else for all of it." She said it in the flat voice she used for a column of figures. "Not to be careful. You have been careful with four women for five months, and every one of them has told you about it, and one of them is still telling you about it tonight.

"I do not want a careful man on this landing. I want the man who stood at a table for four hours and moved something in himself three times and let two hundred and eleven people watch him do it."

"I do not know how to do the other kind yet."

"Then do the kind you know, and be quick about it, and do not be careful."

She asked him. He answered.

She came off the chair and put her coat on the step and not down the stair, which meant that for an hour the only cloth of hers in this house lay two feet from where they were.

The landing is about five feet across, with a rail on one side and a window on the other that faces the backs of the buildings across the street, and there is a light on a cord. She pulled the cord and let it go about four times over the course of it, and neither of them mentioned it.

She took her clothes off in whatever order she found them, which was the skirt first because the button had gone, and she told him what she was doing at each stage in the flat voice she used for a column of figures, and she did not once tell him it was all right.

"Sun Meiling," he said.

"That is the first half of it." She was sitting on the step above him with her knees either side of his ribs and her hands on his face. "Say the whole thing or say nothing. I have been called the first half of it by four people in this city and I have stopped correcting them out loud and started correcting them in the head, and you have not got that far yet."

"Sun Meiling."

"That is better. Once more before the hour is up and it will be true."

She had his back against the stair rail and his legs out along the boards and she took him into her hand and started at the outside of it and worked inward, slowly, the way a woman takes a bearing off a point she is not going to arrive at today.

He got a hand on her knee and she left it there. He got both and she said, "Not yet, and do not hurry it, you have been careful for five months and I have watched you be careful, and it is the one thing in this house I cannot be patient about."

She let him get the second one in himself and then stopped him with two fingers flat on the head of it and told him what she was going to do and how long it was going to take, in the same voice she used for a column of figures, and he understood about a second in that the voice was not cold.

It was a woman with eleven thousand pages of other people's data in her head and no other way of saying a thing she meant.

She got him up onto his feet with her hands under his arms and had him hold the rail, and she stood where he could not get his back to anything at all, and she took him in her mouth with one hand flat on his hip and the other counting at the side of his neck, and she was exact and she was not gentle and she did not ask him once whether he could do it or how long it had been.

At one point she stopped entirely and made him tell her what he had felt at eleven o'clock that morning in a hall on Cobb Street, and he told her, and she said again and made him tell her twice more, and the third time she asked him what it had felt like from the inside of the room and not from inside his own chest, which was a different question and the only one she had wanted.

Then she sat him down on the floor with his back against the rail and came down onto the step above him and finished it there, with her hand in his hair and her weight on his thigh and no hurry anywhere in the last ten minutes of it, and she made him say her whole name once more before she let him have it, and he did.

She did not go near the fourth rib. She put an open hand flat against the side of him, held it there, and made him say the number out loud three times while her palm sat on it, and when it did not move she said, "Good. Tell me what it is like when nothing arrives," and he said he did not know, and she said, "Then say that instead," and he did.

Afterwards she sat with her back against the wall and her boots on the boards and took his wrist across her knee and put two fingers on it, and counted, and let him watch her do it.

"Anything," said Sun Meiling.

"Nothing. Nothing moved and nothing arrived. I did not spend anything on you and there was nothing to spend."

"I know." She turned his wrist over once and let go of it. "I read arrival the way other people read a face. It has never once been paid for and I am not going to start asking."

She sat up and put her blouse on and did not put the coat on.

"There is one day left in the month," she said. "On the first of next month Marguerite is going to read that visitors' book out loud at that table for the last time this month, and there will be a hundred and something in it, and the Office's sheet will still say eighty-nine."

"It will."

"And on the fifth of next month the Board repaints at nine, and your name is not on it, and it is not going to be on it in February. I have got a shape that says February and I am not going to tell you what it is, because I have been wrong eleven times and I am not going to hand you a number to be frightened of."

She stood up.

"Last thing, and then I am going to bed. It is the only one of the four that matters and I put it last because I wanted the other three out of the way first."

"Go on."

"I am going down to that table tomorrow night and say this out loud to the six of them, and one of them is going to argue and I do not care which. And then I am going to sit down at that table for the rest of my life and I am not going to take a pen."

"You have not sat at it since the twenty-seventh of last month."

"I have not sat at it since the twenty-seventh of last month and I am going to start again, and the first thing I say when I get there is that a household which does not stop him is not a shield, it is an audience, and that I said it about this house before he said it about himself in a hall.

"And that the six of them have known it since the sixth of this month and have been arguing about a price."

She picked the coat up off the step.

"It is not modesty and it is not a favour to him," said Sun Meiling. "I would like it written down somewhere that is not a document, and I am going to write it on the back of four columns of figures on a page of typing paper on a landing, and you are going to be the only person who ever reads it.

"That is the first arrangement in four months that has cost any of us nothing except me."

She went down the stairs.

At seven o'clock six women were at the table with the notebook open and the day's line not yet written, and he told them what had happened on the landing in about nine sentences and left out about six hundred words of it, and not one of the six asked him for the six hundred words, because all six of them had been asking him for the truth for four months and had learned what it cost.

The landing light was off.
