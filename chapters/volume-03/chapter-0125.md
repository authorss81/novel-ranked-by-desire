# The Old Run In The File

The twenty-fifth was a Thursday, and the Board repainted at nine whether or not there was anything new to say, and Lin Hao was not in the square to watch it and would not stand in that square again until the next time it happened.

He cleaned six floors before he came up the back stair of the Exchange building, the fourth floor last because the fourth floor is discharged and its washrooms are a week old and unused, and the queue at the plate socket had been twenty deep by nine and he had worked round it with the mop and taken the bucket through twice. It went into the book with the floors. The cause named on the page where it happened, and no number, because no count had turned.

The Certification desk's answer was on the file at half past ten with the desk's mark on it and a date, and Tove Marlin had a note from the hearing officer requiring the Office to produce it to the member and to answer his questions on it, and she had brought the note and the paper and herself, and she was sorry for the room in the way she was sorry for every room.

The clerk was the same one, with the ink on his cuffs and the tray with three instruments' papers in it. He put a single sheet on the counter face up and kept his hand flat on the bottom of it, which meant read and do not take.

"Certified run," he said. "The desk's own. That is what the hearing officer's rule asks for, and it is what the desk has got, and I am going to say before you read it that this is the best work this desk has ever certified and it is four years old and I have never been asked to produce it before."

Lin Hao read it.

It was one sheet of paper output with the Office's mark at the head and a certification line under it in a reporting officer's hand, dated, and the reporting officer's name was Ivo Sarn, and the certification line said the run was made against the tool's specification on the day named and that the output was complete to specification.

Below that the sheet had its two columns.

The left-hand column was headed subject and had one line in it, and the line was a plate number, four figures and a check letter. There was no name. A strip of paper had been pasted over the name line and burnished down flat, and it had been done by somebody who was good at it.

The right-hand column was headed counterparty and had six lines in it.

Six names, written in the same machine hand as the plate number, set in a column with the same leading, the way a thing comes out of a machine that is doing one job and doing it correctly.

He read them twice.

*Marta Vey. Ansell Verrow. Bethan Truscott. Iven Marchetti-Lowe. Dell Anspach. Willem Rasch.*

He had never heard of any of them.

Thirty-five days. Six left in this month after today and twenty-nine in the next.

"What are these."

"That is what came out of it," said the clerk. "That is the output. I am not permitted to tell you what it means and neither is anybody on this desk, and that is not modesty, it is that the desk certifies a form and the hall decides a meaning, and I have said that to you before and I am saying it again because it has come up twice since."

"And this is the run the Office is going to rely on."

"This is the run the Office is going to rely on. It was certified in the ordinary way, it is complete to specification, the subject column carries a subject and the counterparty column carries six counterparties, and there is a member of this Office in a hearing on the twenty-ninth who has been told that this desk's best work is what that machine produces of a man." He put his finger under the certification line. "You are reading a document, not a story."

"Not a hand has been laid on it and my own opinion of it is about two minutes old."

Lin Hao looked at the six names and then at the number in the left-hand column.

"That is not eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight," he said.

"It is not."

"Then it is not me, and the Office has certified a run on somebody else and is going to put it in front of a hall as evidence of what a machine does to me."

"That is exactly what the Office has certified and exactly what the hall will hear, and I am obliged to tell you that I put it in the tray myself on the twenty-second and that I have thought about it every day since." The clerk squared the sheet. "A certification wants a run and a builder's word. We had the builder's word on the eighteenth."

"The run is the part I had to go and find, and what I found was four years old and complete, and the alternative was a run we made ourselves this week against a specification, and a run we made ourselves is not the same kind of paper."

"What kind of paper is it."

"It is a run that has been certified and cannot be un-certified." He said it flatly. "A run we made last week is a run a member's own man could ask to have repeated in a room, with the subject present. This one cannot be repeated by anybody. The premises it was made in closed four years ago and the member it was made on is not available to this desk or to any other, and that is not a flaw in the paper. It is what makes it paper."

Lin Hao read the six names a third time and found that he had begun, without meaning to, to look for something in them.

There was nothing in them. That was the thing about the sheet. Twenty-five lines of typewritten space above it, a certification line, two columns, six names, and not one word about when any of those six people had been touched by anything, or by whom, or whether the man whose plate it was had ever seen their faces. A name in a counterparty column was a thing a machine had put in a line. It was not a story and it did not know it had happened.

He was aware of a second thing, and the second thing was uglier than the first.

In four years not one person in this building had asked what the six were doing on that date. Six names had come out of a machine, had been certified complete, and had gone into four documents in four quarters, and the only question anybody had ever put to them had been whether the output matched the specification, and it had, and so they had gone forward again.

Whatever had happened to those six people on that date had entered the city as six names in a column and had stayed six names in a column ever since, and there had never once been a question about them in a room with a human being in it.

"You said it has been carried forward," he said.

"Sarn's office carried it. It goes into the quarterly and the quarterly goes up. There is a line against it. That is what happens to a certified run; it does not sit still and it does not expire."

"Then it has been in the quarterly four times."

The clerk stopped with his hand on the edge of the tray.

"I did not say that."

"You said the quarterly."

"Four times is four quarters," said the clerk, "and a run is entered in the quarterly that follows it and in every quarterly after it for as long as the tool is on the bench, and this tool is on the bench and this run is certified, so yes. It is in four quarterlies and it is in all of them under the same six names." He looked at Lin Hao.

"That is a filing fact and I would rather you had it from me than worked it out in a corridor and came in here about it angry."

It was not anger that Lin Hao felt. He had had a month to get used to the shape of this city and what he felt now was something flatter and colder and much less comfortable than anger, which was the recognition of a machine working exactly as it was specified to work, in a city that had built four hundred thousand readings a year on the assumption that it did.

"The six names are the proof," he said. "That is what the Office is going to say. Six names out of a machine that meets its specification, on a subject who is not me, on a day four years back, and a hall full of people who have read a notice about me on a frame and have come to be shown that the machine works."

"The six names are a proof that the machine prints," said the clerk. "That is not the same claim and I am not going to help anybody make it one, and I will tell you what I will do instead, which is this."

"I will put the specification on the counter at half past ten tomorrow with the run, because that is what the desk has and the desk does not hide its own papers from a subject in a proceeding, and then you can put your question to it in writing and I will answer it as the officer who runs the instrument and not as a man who has read a document."

"You have never done that before."

"I have been on this desk seven years and there has never been a member in a proceeding with a run of the tool on the counter, and I do not know how this afternoon is going to go and I would rather it went with two papers on it than one."

"What I want is the file this came out of," he said.

Tove Marlin said, "I have already asked for it," from the doorway, and she had a second sheet under her arm that she had not shown him, and she came in and put it down on the counter beside the first one without saying anything else about it.

Thirty-five days, and the desk's best work in front of him, and a second sheet that the Office had brought and had not yet opened, and the whole of the difference between those two things was a question he had not thought to ask and a woman who had thought of it on the twenty-second.
