# Four Years And A Morning

Eleven at night until half past five, four nights and three off, and it was in his coat, and he was through the door of the branch at about ten to eleven on the night of the twenty-eighth of October with a bucket under his arm and nothing in the bucket.

Eighteen days from the morning of the tenth of October to this evening. There are twenty days in October after the tenth and this is two days short of all of them, and twenty less two is eighteen.

Rank one on a white plate with no figure cut into it anywhere.

## The Page On The Counter

The rota for the month came out on the twenty-seventh, and the man who keeps it wrote it out in front of him because that is the arrangement and has been since the November of the first year, when a man at rank one asked to be asked about his hours somewhere with a witness standing there.

The page is about the size of a folded hand. It has four names on it now.

His. Nadia Ferrous, who works the counter two nights a week and comes off at about three. Petra Ilyin, who comes in at about two after the laundry closes. And Isolde Marchetti, who comes in at about one from the bellhouse stage and reads at the far end of the corridor until about four, and has never once said what she is reading and is not going to be asked.

There is no band against any of them and no figure and no note about what any of them is owed. There is a name, a band, and a number of nights, and four names, and a man keeps it in a drawer and does not read it back to anybody.

## The Landing

There was a woman on the second landing at about twenty past eleven with a cloth and a bucket. She has been there every night since the eleventh of October of the third year.

He had carried a theory about her for four years, and the theory was that she did not ask him anything because there was nothing on the page in front of her that anybody could ask about, and he had never once tested it and had stopped expecting to.

"Water's off on the landing," she said.

"It is."

She came past him about forty minutes later with the bucket and stopped, which she has done perhaps twice in four years.

"You are not doing the third floor tonight," she said.

"No."

"I have watched you go up that stair four times a night for four years," she said, "and you have not done it once in a fortnight, and it is not my business and I am not going to make it my business. That is all I am going to say about it and I am going to go down this stair now."

## What He Did Instead

He did the first and the second and a bin room, and he went up the third at about four for the landing and came down again, and at about half past four he was on the street.

There are about nine thousand people a day under the ranks wall and not one of them reads it. The paint above the last tile is four weeks old and the two words are on the stone in the office's own hand at the height of a name on a plate, and a city cannot read them, and eleven thousand people go up a hill every week to a building that has never once claimed them.

Under the last tile, on the stone, a white tile with his name on it in chalk and nothing whatever under it.

Nobody has put it there. Nobody in this city has put it there. He has stood under it about two minutes at a time for four years and a woman with a short-handled broom has cleaned four inches of it every morning for about nine years and told him she has never seen anybody do it, and he did not ask her why and he has not asked anybody anything.


Emil Brack was at the wall end of the lane with the chalk at about a quarter to seven, and there are five stools and about a hundred and sixty people walk past them in a morning.

He looked up when Lin Hao came to the end and looked at him for about as long as it takes to read a figure, and neither of them said anything, and Brack went back to his stools and put the chalk in his coat.

He has offered every figure he has invented in two years, and the method, and about nine he decided off nothing at all, for one line in this office's hand on a public sheet once a quarter saying that he makes them up. He has never been answered. He is not a criminal and he is not diminished and he is right about every fact he says, including the ones that cost him.

The first morning in that lane was the third of October of year zero.

Thirty-six months of thirty days is a thousand and eighty days and that is three years, and a year is three hundred and sixty days, and the third of October of the fourth year is three hundred and sixty days after the third of October of the third year, and the twenty-eighth is twenty-five days after that.

A thousand and eighty and three hundred and sixty and twenty-five is a thousand four hundred and sixty-five days, and counting the first morning as the first is a thousand four hundred and sixty-six mornings.

It is a thousand three hundred and forty-seven days since the first of February of the first year, and the first morning in that lane came four months of thirty days before that, which is a hundred and twenty, and two days short of the first of February, which is a hundred and eighteen. A thousand three hundred and forty-seven and a hundred and eighteen is a thousand four hundred and sixty-five, and counting the first morning as the first is a thousand four hundred and sixty-six.

## What Is There That Was Not There

Four years ago this man cleaned washrooms at a branch office because he had asked for the shift, and there was an office with a drawer in it and a room with two chairs and a standing sheet that went out at four.

There is still a drawer. There is still a room with two chairs. The sheet still goes out at four and it still carries an address at the head of every line and about two hundred people a week in Cordwain read it, and the figure under the heading this quarter is eight hundred and forty-one and it is the same figure it has been since the first of July, because four hundred and nineteen people told nine divisions what they did and not one of them was a deed.

The office has no instrument. It has never had one. A clerk may enter what she is given and cannot improve it and cannot be asked to hurry, and the one man it is allowed to ask is a man at rank one on a night shift, and he has spent four years learning what that costs and he spent the last of it on the fifteenth of October and could not pay it and could not strike it and could not explain it in a footnote.

What is there that was not there is people.

Eighty-one of them went to about nine hundred steps in ninety days. Nine divisions. A hundred of the Church came down a hill in eleven days and nine of them brought something.

Forty of them went out for three days at the end of July to ninety-one doors and asked nothing and came back with nothing, and about nine hundred people in a yard worked out on their own that being useful is how you get to be owed nothing, and they were right about that and it was not the Church's doing.

There is a page with nine lines on it and no name against it and a woman two flights up off Cordwain who is the creditor of an account that belongs to nobody.

There is one line in a column that was ruled on the ninth of March of the third year and was empty for seven quarter-ends, and it goes out on a public desk on the thirtieth of December, which is fifteen days of October and thirty of November and thirty of December away.

There is a keeper two floors under a sales floor with six words in a chained book under her own name that she is going to take off a shelf on the first of January and read, and a name against them that was not there at ten past ten on the fifteenth.

There is a street four streets off the yard end that has been counted four times in thirty years and read once.

None of it is an institution. There is not one seal, one schedule, one fund, one hearing or one instrument anywhere in any of it, and every line of it is in somebody's own hand, and the whole of the machinery is a counter off a third court and a lamp on a stand and nine stairs.

## At Half Past Five

He wrote it up on the top step of the third flight at a quarter to five, with the bin room open behind him and the mop standing in the bucket and the branch counter four feet up on the corridor wall, and that counter says how many people went past the door of the Register office in twenty-one months, and nobody reads it, and it is emptied on the first of the year whether anybody read it or not.

*Twenty-eighth of October of the fourth year. A thousand three hundred and forty-seven days since the first of February of the first year. Eighteen days after the morning.*

*The office has no instrument, the slates are still in the lane, the wall still cannot be read by anybody in this city, the tile still has nothing under it, and the account is still one in seven emptied and not zero and belongs to nobody and cannot be drawn against.*

*And it is paid, and I am going to write down how it was paid, because in four years nobody is going to be able to check it and I would rather it were written by me than argued about.*

*I wrote the definition of an unrecorded deed for an office that had no instrument and no money and no column. Four lines, forty days, and it is narrow enough to take in one street and one loaf of bread and it leaves out about nine in ten of what anybody walks in with.*

*The office has no instrument. It has never had one and it is not going to have one, because I am the whole of it and I am a man with a bucket.*

*The office has no money and never had any.*

*The office had no column for seven quarter-ends, and it has one line in it now, and the line is me.*

*That is the whole of it and it took four years and it cost one house, one warden, one yard of about eleven thousand people, the fourth condition of about four hundred and nineteen people who never got a deed out of it, and one night under a tile that I did not spend, which was the only thing I had left that was mine.*

*There are four names on a page about the size of a folded hand and three floors of washrooms and a bin room and a woman on the second landing who has finally told me she has noticed something, and she is right, and she did not make it her business.*

*The second alternative did not happen. The second line on that wall did not get a threshold under it that a man with a chalk chose, and it is still on the stone in the office's hand at the height of a name on a plate.*

*The office's hand is my hand and there is not one other instrument in this city that could have put it there, and a city cannot read it.*

*And the man with the chalk is at the wall end of that lane at about a quarter to seven every morning and he is right about every fact he says and he has not been answered.*

*There is a woman in a lane at the end of it who pays a grain a head to be counted by a man who makes the figures up, and she has been right about that for two years and nobody has ever said so to her.*

*Eighteen days ago there was a morning that had been named for four years by a woman who has never been thanked for naming it, and there was a person in the room.*

*There is a person in the room.*

*There is a person in the room.*
