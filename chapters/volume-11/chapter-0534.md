# What The Church Did Not Bring

The thirtieth of December is the last working day of the quarter. A quarter in this city is three months of thirty days and there is no thirty-first in any of them, and the sheet went out at four with an address at the top of every line, and the figure on it was thirty-four.

It was thirty-four on the sheet of the thirtieth of September as well.

Ninety days. Three months of thirty days each. And in ninety days the Register of Unrecorded Merit entered nothing at all, which is the first time in three years that a whole quarter has gone by with the figure on the sheet standing exactly where it stood at the beginning of it.

## The Only Figure That Could Be Checked

Yusuf Adeyemi read the ratchet on the corridor wall on the morning of the thirtieth, at about ten, the way he has read it every week since a man at rank one asked him to, and he wrote it on the back of the sheet in a line of his own that goes nowhere.

The counter said one thousand and sixty-nine.

It said nine hundred and forty on the first of October at about nine minutes to five in the morning, which is the reading that is still chalked on the inside of his own notebook from the night before, and the difference between the two is a hundred and twenty-nine.

A hundred and twenty-nine people went past a door in ninety days. Three months of thirty days, and about forty-three a month, which is what that counter has done for twenty-one months, and forty-three times three is a hundred and twenty-nine.

A hundred and twenty-nine people came to a door in a quarter and about forty of them were let in and about nine of those nine sat down, and the book on the shelf behind the counter has not moved.

## What He Did With A Working Morning

He went in at about eight on the morning of the thirtieth, which is not his shift, and stood at the end of the corridor where the corridor turns and where a person who has come up a stair cannot see him until they are eleven feet away.

About four people came past the door before nine. Two of them halted at the board at the bottom of the hill and read it from about four feet back, and one of them came back up the stair afterwards.

The man was somewhere past thirty and he had a week's beard and a coat with the inside out and he stood about nine feet from Lin Hao with his hands in his pockets and did not come in.

"You have been standing there since eight," said Lin Hao.

"I have."

"What have you come for."

The man did not answer that for about eleven seconds.

"There is thirty-four on that sheet," he said. "Is that thirty-four people the City has got a thing about."

That is the sentence. He has had it in his head since about nine and he had walked up one street with it and down it again.

"No."

"Then what is it."

"Thirty-four unrecorded deeds. A thing somebody did to somebody else, on a day, with a second person in it, that is not written down anywhere in this city, and somebody has brought it in, and the office has entered it."

"The City has a page with thirty-four names on it and a number on it," said the man, "and it goes on a board at the bottom of a hill where anybody can read it, and you want me to believe it is a list of people who did something and forgot about it."

"Yes."

"That is not what it says on it."

"No," said Lin Hao. "It is what it looks like. Those are two different things and the second one is what about nine hundred people a week in Cordwain have been reading since the thirtieth of September."

He had it in his coat and he took it out and it was not a paper, it was not anything at all, and he had carried it as far as the stair.

"I sat with a man two nights in the fourteenth month of the second year," he said. "He was in a room off Cordwain and there were four of us in it the second night and I was not his family and I was not paid and I was not asked and his people did not know I was there until the morning."

"He died about four in the morning and I was the one holding him, and I have not told anybody, and I came up here twice on working mornings to put it in a book."

"Why did you not put it in a book."

The man looked at the end of the corridor.

"Because it says it is not on a page anywhere in this city," he said. "And I have been down here three times with it and every time I have got as far as that board and read the thirty-four and thought, they have got thirty-four people on a page already and I am the thirty-fifth."

"And I do not want to be on that page with those thirty-four, and then I have thought, that is daft, those are people somebody did something to and I am somebody who sat with a man who died, and then I have turned round and gone home."

He went down the stair.

That is what the sentence cost and it cost it in ninety days and it cost it to at least one man who had it in his coat and it will cost it to every man who ever stands at that board and counts to thirty-four and does not like being thirty-fifth.

## What The Clerk Said About It

Wenna Raut read the sheet twice at the same speed both times and put it in the folder.

"You have known this since the fourth of October," she said.

"Since the thirtieth of September. I wrote down on the second of July what was going to happen to it and I wrote it nine days before it came and I knew that it would and I did not stop it."

"You knew what it would do and not what it would cost."

"I knew it would be used."

"You knew it would be used against the office."

"I knew somebody would read a number against a name," said Lin Hao. "I wrote it at four in the morning on the thirtieth of June, in a column in my own notebook, and it is the correct reading and it came true."

The clerk squared the sheet.

"Then you have two sentences about this office in your notebook and one of them is right and one of them is about what people will think when they look at it, and the second one is the one that happened, and I entered it because I enter what I am given and I do not shorten it."

"Yes."

"I would not have made it shorter either," she said, "and I want that said out loud once in this corridor, because there are about four people in this building who will tell you that a clerk refused it and that is not what happened in this room."

## What The Church Did Not Bring

The hundred of the Church of the Quiet Number who came down the hill in eleven days in April had brought nine things, and nine of them had gone in.

On the thirtieth of September about forty more came down on the strength of the sheet of the thirtieth of April, and eleven of what they brought went in between the twenty-first and the twenty-ninth of September.

And then the sentence went out at four on the thirtieth, and in the first week of October about nine of them came to the counter and read it, which is what people do when they come to look at a thing and then stop coming, and they read the thirty-four and the two columns and the second one with nothing under it.

Agnete Lindqvist did not come to that counter in the whole of the last quarter. She did not write. She did not speak about it in the yard, and there is a yard, and about forty people come to it in an ordinary week, and she has been twenty years chaplain and she said in that yard in the eleventh month of the first year that if the Register ever got a column her own would fill it before the Bureau's, over about nine years.

Nine years, and eleven days.

He was on the second step at about seven o'clock in the morning of the second of January with the quarter's sheet folded once and the number Adeyemi had chalked on a card that week inside it.

The figure had not gone down. It had gone to nothing, which is worse, because a figure that goes down is a figure about a bad quarter and a figure that stands still while a hundred and twenty-nine people walk past a door is a figure about an office.

"Take it back," said Adeyemi.

"No."

"It cannot be un-entered by anybody including you. I have said that to you once and I am not going to say it again, because you have it in your own notebook in your own hand and you do not need me."

"I am not going to take it back."

"You printed that where this Register has nothing it has not been given anything, and four thousand people have spent eleven weeks with it, and what they have spent eleven weeks with is the discovery that the only thing this office can be read as is a list."

"Yes."

"And you are going to leave it there."

"I am going to leave it there," said Lin Hao. "If I take it back then the thirty-four is a count of things the City thinks are good, and about four thousand people will believe that, and Rosalind Keck's brother will be right about it for the rest of his life, and I will have taken back the only sentence in this building that a person could put in a kitchen and have the City agree with."

He put the sheet down flat on the step.

"And in exchange I get a counter that nobody comes to," he said. "That is the trade and I made it on the thirtieth of September and I would make it again and it has cost me one man with a deed in his coat and about a hundred and twenty-nine people who walked past a door in ninety days."

## Thirty-Four

*Thirtieth of December of the third year. Twelfth sheet. Entered: thirty-four, the same figure as the thirtieth of September, and ninety days of nothing.*

*Ninety days is three months of thirty days. A hundred and twenty-nine people came past the door in them, at forty-three a month, which is what that counter has done for twenty-one months, and not one thing came in.*

*Three columns now on the notebook page and the third one is the largest of them. Came and said nothing at all. Nine of them in ninety days, at the end of the corridor, eleven feet away from the turn, reading a board.*

*The sentence stays. The office is not a list. I have said the right thing in public about a number in a kitchen and it has cost me the counter, and the trade is the same trade it was on the thirtieth of September and I knew the price in July and I paid it in September.*

*What I want, and it is the first thing I have written down in four years that is a want and not a finding: I want one person through that door before the thirtieth of March.*

He read that last line back and it was the wrong thing to have written, and a figure put down after the fact is an account of the figure and not the figure, and a want is not a figure at all, and he left it where it was.
