# A Title He Did Not Ask For

He came down the corridor at about a quarter to ten on the thirtieth of October, twenty-two days after he had stood in the room with a bucket on the table, and Adeyemi was standing outside his own door with his coat off and a sheet under his arm, and neither of them went into the room.

"It shuts," said Adeyemi, "and it is warmer out here."

## The Joke, Said Once

"There is a joke in the name of that office and I am going to say it once."

"Say it."

"I am the Registrar of Unrecorded Merit," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "The first and only substantive act of my office to date is a man in a white plate telling me no in one sentence, and there are about two hundred people a week in Cordwain who could tell you what this office is for and every one of them would give you a different answer and not one of them would be wrong, because not one thing in this city is on anything."

"And if you can tell me which half of that is the funny one, I will buy the lunch."

Lin Hao thought about it for longer than Adeyemi expected.

"I do not know which half it is," he said.

"That is the correct answer and I have got eleven of them," said Adeyemi, "and I have never once given the joke to anybody, because the second time you give it to a person it stops being a joke and becomes a position, and I have read what happens to this city when somebody has a position."

## Where The Name Came From

"It was on the form before the office existed," he said.

"That is not an answer to anything."

"It is the only answer I have and I have looked for four months," said Adeyemi. "There is a sheet in the registry upstairs with a box on it and a heading above the box and the heading is that, and the heading was printed, and the office was made by a woman at a counter who was given the sheet and the form and an instruction, and an instruction is the only part of it that anybody in this building still has."

He turned the sheet under his arm round so that the top of it showed. The box was empty.

"Nobody in this building chose it. I have asked two people and one of them did not remember being asked and the other one remembered and said no, and that is the entire investigation."

## What He Forced In July

"You wrote the procedure," said Lin Hao.

"I wrote the wording of it," said Adeyemi. "Four hundred precedents in a drawer upstairs and about nine of them were any use and I took the useful parts of nine and threw the rest out, and the clerk who enters it did not change a word of it, which I insisted on in July and which was the only instruction I gave anybody that summer."

He put the sheet under his other arm.

## Whether One Is One

"There is one thing you are going to want to know before you say the rest of it, and I am going to say it in a corridor because it is not a thing for a room."

"Go on."

"The letter of the first of February says the office may put one question to one person. It does not say once. It does not say a year, and it does not say ever."

"You have read it."

"I have read it about nine times since the thirtieth of September and I have had it looked at by two people in this building, and the answer from both of them is that it is the same sentence either way and that neither of them would enjoy being the one who decided."

Adeyemi turned the sheet over and turned it back. "I used it on the thirtieth of September and I have not been able to find out since whether I have got one question or nine, and it is the only thing about this office I do not know."

"What happens when you want to ask again."

"Then I ask you, and you will have decided before I get to the end of the sentence," said Adeyemi. "That is what one question means in a room with two chairs in it. I cannot spend it in a hurry and I cannot save it up, and that is the whole of what I have got and it is yours."

"That is an arrangement and not a rule."

"It is an arrangement," said Yusuf Adeyemi, "and it costs me the only sentence in this city that cannot be walked out of, and I know what it costs you, and I am telling you the shape of it before you say your condition and not after."

"And then I made it a matter that had to be answered in a room, in front of nine hundred people, on a day a city could not get out of."

"You forced the hearing."

"I forced the hearing," said Adeyemi. "I held the chair and I drafted the wording and I put it in a room that holds nine hundred on a morning when about nine thousand people were going to walk under a wall, and I did that because an argument you can go home and write a letter about is worth the paper it is printed on and an argument that has to be answered in a room is the only kind that is."

"And the reservoir is in about a million people instead of in a tank."

"The reservoir is in about a million people instead of in a tank, and that came out of a room I called, and I am not going to argue with you about whether that was the right thing, because you have not argued with me about it once in fifteen months and I have noticed."

## What He Did Not Stop

"There is one more," said Adeyemi, "and I am going to say it because you have come down this corridor twice in three weeks and a man who keeps the other one in a drawer is not a man you can work with."

"I know about the notice."

"You know about the notice of the twenty-first," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "A notice about a matter carried under five seals, on the paper of this office, going up on a wall where about nine thousand people a day walk under a thing an office has written. It was not my instruction and I did not stop it, and I have been in this building nineteen years and I know what goes out of it on a morning, and I let it go."

"Why."

"Because it was true," he said, "and I have thought about that for three hundred and sixty-nine days and I have not got a better sentence, and you are the only man in this city who would understand that as a reason and I have never been able to work out whether that is a compliment."

The hearing had run for four days in the last week of July of the year before and this was the last week of October of the first year, and that is fifteen months. The notice had gone up on the twenty-first of October of that same year before, and a year in this city is three hundred and sixty days and the twenty-first is the twenty-first of it, so three hundred and sixty, and nine more days to this morning, and neither of them had stopped it.

## The Condition

The corridor had about a hundred people through it in an hour and neither of them lowered his voice about any of it.

"What are you going to write for me," said Lin Hao.

"I am going to tell you what I am not going to have," said Adeyemi. "I am not going to have a sentence that says this office registers the unrecorded good of the people of Ostara, or that it exists for the encouragement of merit, or anything of that shape, because that is a sentence a man writes for a plaque and it would be true of every office in this city including the bin room you clean."

"Then what."

"Then you write what the office registers." Adeyemi turned and looked at the door of the room. "A thing has to be there before it can go in. Not a sentiment and not a judgement and not a good deed somebody is remembered for. A thing."

"And whatever a thing has to have before it will go in, you will have to say it in words that about two hundred people a week will read off a public desk and understand differently, and then they will all be right, and in about four years that will be the worst sentence anybody in this city has ever been given."

## Nine

"I will write it," said Lin Hao, "on one condition and I am telling you the condition before you go back into that room and open a drawer about it."

"Go on."

"I am not writing a sentence that nine people have not tried to break," he said. "I have written sentences for four years that nobody argued with and every one of them was worth nothing by the time it was on a wall, and I am not doing that again with the only instrument I am ever going to be handed."

"Who are the nine."

"They are not a committee and they are not appointed and they are not on a list in that room," said Lin Hao. "There are nine of them, they will each say one thing, they will not answer one another,"

"and they go last in the order the table has kept since the seventeenth of October of the year the wall went up, and if you want to know who they are you will have to be in a kitchen off Ferrier Street on a working evening and you will not be asked a question while you are there."

"How long will that take."

"Longer than the eighty-two days," said Adeyemi. "I have a line to write for the thirtieth of December and I am going to put *held over, not yet* against it and send that up, and in about four years somebody will read it and find out that a small office ran for a year and a quarter on a sentence that was late, and that is the correct outcome and the alternative is worse."

He put his hand on the door of the room.

"You have given me a bad quarter," he said, "and you are going to be in that kitchen in front of nine people who will tell you what you have got wrong, and I have never in nineteen years watched a thing be made by two people in a room and argued by nine in a kitchen, and I want you to know that I am going to enjoy it and that I am not going to help."
