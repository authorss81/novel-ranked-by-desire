# A Room Takes Anything

Corvin Ashby came back on the twenty-ninth of November of the third year with the lease of number four rolled up inside his coat, and he came back because the first of January was not the end of it.

The rent board sits in a room off Cordwain with a table, three chairs, a stove and a window that has been painted shut. It has no power to make anybody do anything whatever. It cannot fine a person and it cannot put a lock on a door and it cannot send anybody anywhere.

What it does is make a finding, and what a finding is for is refusing rooms.

Otho Pearse sat at the table with a book and a pen and he wrote down what he was told, which is the whole of his office and has been since the board was started.

## Who Was In The Room

There were four people in it and one of them was not there.

Ashby was on one side of the table. The woman from number four was on the other side of it for nine years and had not been in it since the first of January, and her sister was sitting where she had been, and she came because the woman could not come.

"She cannot come."

"Give me a reason the board can write down."

"She cannot read."

Otho Pearse wrote it down, because it was what he had been told.

"She has been in number four for nine years and she cannot read the lease she signed in the eighth month of the first year, and she cannot read the demand, and she cannot read the finding when it comes, and she can count money better than anybody I have ever met and she did that sum at the end of every month for nine years on her fingers and she knew she was short before the demand was ever put under her door."

"Then she may come and say the sum is wrong."

"She cannot read what it says she is wrong about," said the sister. "That is not the same as not knowing. She knows exactly what she owes. She cannot find out what she is being told she owes."

## What Was Put In Front Of The Room

Ashby put the lease down and opened it at the ninth page and put his finger on the figure.

That is all he did. He did not argue and he did not produce anything else and he did not say that anybody had lied to him, and the room held nothing that said the figure was wrong except a second piece of paper that the sister had brought, which was the old lease, and which said that the room had taken something else entirely nine years before.

Two papers. The board cannot weigh them. That had been said out loud in her own hand, on a sheet, at a counter off Cordwain on the second of March of the first year, and it was still true on the twenty-ninth of November of the third and would be true in four years.

"This is what the room takes," said Corvin Ashby. "I did not make it up and I am not going to stand here and pretend I did. There is a figure about that street and it is in four places and mine is the one everybody uses, and the one the agency puts out is the same on about nine days in ten and when it is not the same the agency is the one that is wrong and I cannot prove that either."

## The Defence He Offered Himself

"What I want written down," he said, "is this. There is a man in a delivery coat in a lane off Cordwain who chalks those figures and who has told about nine hundred and ten people a morning for about two years and three months that he makes them up. He is right and he says it every morning and it has never once been written into a lease and it is not going to be."

"Write it down," said Otho Pearse, and wrote it down.

"And write down that it does not matter. The lease is signed. This board has never been asked whether the figure is right. This board finds what is owed under a lease and the figure is part of what is owed under a lease and that is the end of it."

He said that without any weight at all.

He is not a crook and he has never once been careful, and it has cost more than carefulness would have, because a careful man would have asked the woman whether she could read before he wrote a figure into a lease in front of her.

## What The Woman Of Number Four Is Like

Not one person in that room had met her and both of them had a picture of her, and the two pictures were not the same, and he noticed that about eleven minutes in.

"She is not a fool," said the sister.

"Not one of you has said that she is."

"I am saying it because you have both been careful with your faces and I have been sitting here for an hour watching you be careful with your faces." She put her hands on the table.

"She took that room in the eleventh month of the first year when the man before her left owing nine days and no one else in the street would take it, and she has mended for that house and the two houses either side of it since, and in nine years she has not missed a week, and when the man before her went she is the one who went and got the money owed to her out of his things with the landlord standing there."

"What is her name on the lease."

"It is her name. She can sign it. She can write the two words of it and she has never once been asked to write anything else in nine years."

"Can she read what she is being found to owe."

"No."

"Then what happens to her."

The sister took about nine seconds.

"She will be told," she said. "She will come home from my mother's in an evening and I will tell her that a room off Cordwain has said she is a woman who does not pay, and she will believe me, because I have never lied to her, and she will not be able to ask one question about it, and that is the whole of what has happened to her and it has not cost anybody anything yet."

## What The Room Did

The room took it.

That is the whole of a rent board and it has been the whole of a rent board since the board was started: a table, three chairs, a man with a pen, and whatever is put in front of it goes into a book, and nobody in the room has standing to say the book is wrong.

The finding was that the woman of number four owed the sum in the ninth page. The finding has her name on it and the street and the figure, and the board does not give copies, and if it did she could not read it.

And what a finding is for is this.

"You cannot have her out," said the sister. "The door went on the first of January and the door is not there to take her out of."

"No," said Corvin Ashby. "I have a finding and I have not got her out of anything, and I do not need to. Every landlord in this district takes a board and a book and nobody takes a board before he refuses a room. I take this board and its volume and I show them, and her name is in it, and she does not get rooms from me or from anybody who uses me."

He put the lease back inside his coat.

"That is not eviction. That is worse and it takes four years and she will not know it is happening."

## What The Register Cannot Do About Any Of It

Lin Hao was not in that room and had not asked to be, and he had gone to it at about eleven and been told by the sister on the stair that the board was sitting and would sit for about an hour and that anybody might sit in.

He did not go in.

The office could not stop a finding because it has no instrument and no standing in a room off Cordwain, and the sheet with the address at the head of every line would not have helped him if he had gone in, because the sheet has Corvin Ashby's name on it and not one figure, and because a sheet that goes out at four is a thing about the city and not a thing a landlord can be shown at a table.

He stood in the street outside for about fifty minutes and the door stayed shut.

## The Book On The Table

He asked about it afterwards, in the corridor, of the man who kept it.

"How many findings are in that book."

"I have never counted it," said Otho Pearse. "And I am not going to, for you, for a man at rank one, for an office with a room in it. A man does not count his own book. If I counted it and put a number on it in a corridor you would carry the number up the hill and there would be a figure about this city with nothing whatever behind it, which is the thing you have spent a year printing that it is not."

"I did not ask you to give it to me. I asked you what it was."

"It is a finding a day," said Otho Pearse. "Some days two. It has been a board for eleven years and a landlord who is refused a room is a landlord who takes the next one, and a landlord who takes the next one brings the book."

## What He Wrote At Four In The Morning

He wrote it up at the top of the third flight with the bin room open behind him and the bin room door not shut because the bin room is the only room in that building he is allowed to leave things in.

*Twenty-ninth of November of the third year. Rent board, off Cordwain. A lease and a figure and a sister, and the woman of number four was not in the room because she cannot read, and nobody in that building can read a finding to her.*

*Corvin Ashby wrote his own figure's origin into a board's book and then told the board it made no difference, and the board wrote it down, because a room takes anything anybody puts in front of it and this one took a man telling it that the number was invented.*

*The finding stands and there is nothing to appeal it with. What it does is not eviction. It is that her name is now in a book that every landlord in that district uses to refuse rooms, and she will not know for about four years.*

*A figure that cannot be checked, used where it does harm, which is what figures are for.*

*And the book has a finding a day in it, and some days two, and it has been a board for eleven years, and I have asked a man to count it and he has refused and he was right to.*

He read that back and it was about two minutes past four and the cough took about nine seconds to go and the line under the last paragraph was still empty, which was where a thing of his own went, and he was not going to put one there tonight.
