# The Keeper And The Quarter

The standing sheet of the quarter of the thirtieth of March went out at four in the afternoon with an address at the head of every line, and a person may go to a public desk on any working afternoon of this city and read it, and he read it on the sixteenth of April at about twenty past three, standing at a public desk off a third court with about nine people behind him who were not reading it.

Three things had happened on that page since the twenty-eighth of January and he read all three of them twice, which takes about eleven minutes, and the second reading of the middle one took him about four of them.

Reading a standing sheet is not reading a document.

It is standing at a public desk on a working afternoon with about nine people behind you who are not reading it, and turning a page of about four hundred lines in which every line begins with an address and about one line in nine is about a person, and finding the two or three that are about you, and going back to the top of your own line afterwards to see whether it says the same thing it said the last time you stood here.

## The Line That Was There Before

The line for the address carried the disposition, word for word, in the office's own hand, and it had carried it that way on the fourth of October and again on the twenty-eighth of January and it did not look like anything that had been touched in between.

*the reservoir, held by no person, claimed by the Church of the Quiet Number, answered by the subject. Disposition: given away in the open, the first of October, holder none.*

And under the disposition, in the hand of a clerk who may only enter and cannot improve what she is given and cannot be asked to hurry, the figure. The same figure. She had been given it and she had entered it and it does not move, because nothing in this city can move it and nothing is going to, and he had read it four times on the twenty-eighth of January and could have told anybody in Ostara what it was without looking.

## The Second Thing On The Page

The second thing was a street.

It came halfway down the page under the address and it said the composition of the Cordwain side between the chandler's and the yard wall, and it gave a figure and a date, and underneath the figure it said, in a small level hand that he would know on a wet step in the dark:

*carried forward. Unclosed. No holder.*

And the keeper's own name under that, in her own division, in her own hand.

There was no certificate behind it.

There has not been a certificate behind that line since before the Ferrier line was cut, and the plate at the end of the chain has the figure on it and nothing at all behind the figure, and in about four years the chain comes off that book and the plate is scraped and a new one is cut from whatever was on the old one.

And a woman in a room two floors under a sales floor will be retired or gone to the Vestry, and the eight and the Cordwain will be in metal in a hall in a case, and not one person alive will be able to stand in front of them afterward and say where any of it came from.

He had read that line in four years about nine times and he had never once been able to decide whether to be frightened of it or the other thing.

There is a thing that happens to a man who reads standing sheets and that nobody warns you about.

It is that about four years is the shortest time anybody in this city can put between the doing of a thing and the reading of it, and that after four years the page stops being an account of a thing and starts being a thing in its own right, and that there is a man somewhere who has never met you, holding a page, about to decide something about your name in a corridor with nothing in it.

He has not decided whether that is a good thing or the worst thing that has ever happened to a record in this city, and Hanneke Ruis has been on the other side of it since the fourteenth of March, and they have not discussed it, and there is nobody he could discuss it with who is not in it.

## The Landlord's Quarter Return

The other thing on that page, about a third of the way down and not in the office's hand, was a quarter return from a landlord off Lamplit Row, and it had a column for the standing quoted and the column was empty and under the column, in the landlord's own writing, four words.

*as per the Board.*

He had written that on the tenth of October of the year the wall was repainted and he had not liked the sound of it and had left it, and four months later he was still carrying the four words forward into every return he filled in, and on the thirtieth of March he had written a figure in the column instead, and the figure was a figure of merit off a slate in a district four streets away.

That is on the page too, and it is on the same page as a keeper's six words and an office's one entry, and there is not one instrument in this city that can say whether that landlord should have written it.

## The Third Thing On The Page

The third thing was at the bottom of the page and it had an address at the head of the line, and the address was off a third court, and the line was about an office that had not existed in January.

*Register of Unrecorded Merit. First quarter. Entered: one.*

He read that four times too, and then he had to put his hand flat on his knee for a second, because it is one line and it is in a clerk's hand and it is the first thing in four years that has been entered into any record in this city by an act of somebody's own and not by a reading taken off a person.

Underneath the one, in the same hand, the Registrar had put a second sentence, and it was the only sentence on the whole page that had a whole stop in the wrong place.

*Entered on the report of the only person this office may ask. The question has not been put and was not needed.*

## How One Got In

He found out on the nineteenth of April, in a corridor, from a man with a title.

"He came to me on the second of March," said Yusuf Adeyemi, "which was the day after you came out of a lane off Cordwain at about one in the morning, and he came on his own feet, and nobody sent him."

"I did not put the office's question."

"You did not," said Adeyemi.

"I have the letter of the first of February in a drawer and the sentence in it has a name in it and it is yours and I have not asked you anything, and it is going to stay that way until I decide to use it, and I am the only person in this city who can decide to use it, and that is the joke, and I am not going to tell anybody what it means for as long as it is still one."

"What did you enter."

"I entered what he brought me," said Yusuf Adeyemi.

"One act."

"A man in a delivery coat chalking a figure of merit against the address of a room over a laundry on the twenty-eighth of February, in a lane, on a slate, at about half past midnight."

"He brought the hour. He brought the address. He did not bring a name and he said he could not get one and that he had asked and been told that a name against a figure is a thing the man writes if he wants a person to be found, and he did not want anybody found."

## One In Nine Hundred

The sheet did not say what the entry was worth. It gave a figure and a date and three lines of a hand, and a thing you could not check against anything.

The corridor could.

He did it on the back of a returned sheet with a pencil that had somebody else's name on it: the standing carried on that quarter's page, and against it the office's one.

And the standing on that page is a real figure and it is checked every quarter by a division of nine that can be asked for it, and the office's one is a figure about a thing a person did in the cold in a lane, and the answer came out at about one person in nine hundred of what the disposition on the same page describes, and the sheet itself does not print the arithmetic and neither does anybody else.

One in nine hundred.

Nothing in this city could raise it or lower it, and it is the first figure to go out on a public page in four years with nothing standing behind it and nothing standing against it, and a man who reads it in about four years will have to decide what to do about that on his own.

And nothing on that page to check it against.

## Three Things On One Page

Here is what the page is.

It carries a sum of money that is a fraction of the largest thing that has ever happened in this city, and it carries the words of a keeper about a street, written out of her own head with nothing behind them, and it carries one act by a man with a piece of chalk.

Three quantities. Three hands. Two of them are about things that happened and one of them is about a thing nobody can find out happened, and not one of the three can be weighed against either of the other two, and they went out at four in the afternoon on the same sheet with the same address at the head of every line, and any person in this city may read all of it at a public desk on a working afternoon.

About nine people behind him were not reading it.

He squared the sheet into the rack the way anybody does and then stood in the corridor for about a minute and a half, and he had not one person he could ask what it added up to.

And he had said out loud in a lane in January that there were two things that could be true about the first of October and not one figure in Ostara could tell you which part of the city you were in, and there was still not one figure in Ostara that could tell you, and a sheet had gone out at four with a quarter of it on it in three hands.

It was a quarter with one entry in it. There are nine hundred of whatever the entry is, and the sheet does not know how many of those there are either, and that is what is on it, and that is the whole of what the first quarter of the Register of Unrecorded Merit is.
