# A Debt Comes Due

The number had been on a piece of paper in the inside of his coat since the seventh of September of the first year and he had never once written it out.

A thousand three hundred and twenty-nine days off the first of February of the first year. Three years is thirty-six months of thirty days, which is a thousand and eighty days, and five months of thirty days each from the first of February is a hundred and fifty, and a thousand and eighty and a hundred and fifty is a thousand two hundred and thirty, which is the first of July.

And twenty-nine days left in that July after the first, and thirty of August, and thirty of September, and ten of October is ninety-nine.

A thousand two hundred and thirty and ninety-nine is a thousand three hundred and twenty-nine.

And there is a second way to it that does not use any of those numbers. Thirty-six months is a thousand and eighty, five months is a hundred and fifty, and from the first of July to the first of October is three months, which is ninety, and a thousand two hundred and thirty and ninety is a thousand three hundred and twenty, and nine days into October is a thousand three hundred and twenty-nine.

And four years is three hundred and sixty days multiplied by four, which is a thousand four hundred and forty, and a thousand four hundred and forty days after the tenth of October in the year of the sitting is this morning.

## A Building With No Door On It

The Register of Unrecorded Merit has a room off a third court and the room has a door onto a corridor and the corridor has a door onto a court, and there is no door onto a street anywhere in it, and the only way any person in this city finds it is off the standing sheet, which carries an address at the head of every line and goes out at four on the last working day of the quarter.

A woman in a called sitting said four years ago that a debt with no creditor comes due in about four years, on a morning nobody has chosen, in a building with no door on it.

He had it by nine. Yusuf Adeyemi had it by nine and had been in the room since about a quarter to eight with his coat over the back of the chair and had not said one word about the date.

## What Came Up The Stairs

They came up the third court's stair at about nine minutes past nine, slowly, and the sister from the landing came first and stood in the doorway and did not come further in, which is what she did.

Mavis Rooke was a big woman with the hands of somebody who has not stopped, and she had stopped, and she was on her feet in a building off Cordwain for the first time in four months and she came up two flights without a hand on the rail.

She had the sheet. She had had it since the afternoon of the thirtieth of September and she had taken it off a board at the bottom of the hill and folded it in four and carried it eleven minutes.

"Sit down," said Lin Hao.

"I have not come to sit down." She put the sheet on the table and opened it at the ninth line down. "That is mine."

## The Ninth Line Down

It was nine lines long and it had been entered on the eleventh of July in a clerk's hand and it had no name against it, because the division was not permitted to ask her for one and had not asked her.

"Read it out," said Mavis Rooke. "I cannot read and I have been to somebody in this building and asked her to read it and she read it twice and both times she said the same thing."

Wenna Raut read it.

It said that a woman had told a division on the ninth of April that she had dug a street for six years in the cold, and that she had not been paid for any of it, and that nobody had ever written it down anywhere, and that she had believed for two years that a figure on a slate in a lane was about her.

"She gave you that on the ninth of April," said Adeyemi.

"She gave it to me on the ninth of April and she gave it three more times and I am not sorry about that," said Mavis Rooke. "And on the eleventh of July your clerk wrote it in a book, and there is my street on it, and there is my six years on it, and there is not one word on it about the six years being owed to me."

"Mrs Rooke—"

"Call me what you called me in June," she said. "You called me by my name and you did not ask me anything and I have not forgotten it and that is the only thing I am grateful to you for and I am not grateful."


Nobody in that room could answer her, and the reason had four parts and the room had one instrument and the instrument was a pen.

The Register had no money. It had never had money and it had never claimed any and the whole of what it did was write things down at a counter and send a page out at four.

It had no instrument. There was nothing in Ostara that could take a sentence out of a woman's mouth and set it on a page and put a figure against it, which is the sentence a man said to a woman with a folded piece of paper in his hand on the thirtieth of September of the second year and which has not been bettered since.

It could not pay, and it could not promise, and it could not be sued, and it could not be compelled, and it had never once had a creditor.

And the man at the counter was the only person the office could ask.

"So I have come to the wrong building," said Mavis Rooke.

"You have not come to the wrong building. There is no other building in this city you could have come to and been less wrong," said Lin Hao.

"There is no right building. I wrote a sentence about that in a kitchen on the twentieth of February of the second year and a woman put nine people in front of it to break it, and it took in a street and a loaf of bread, and it is the narrowest thing anybody in this city has ever written and it is still true, and it says a person at a door is a person somebody has decided was owed something."

"And finding them is not the same as owing them."

"That is the second line of the request and it went out on a public sheet at four on the thirtieth of March and about two hundred people a week in this city read it and you read it, and you have had it for seven months and you have come up two flights this morning without a hand on the rail, and I would like to know what you thought about those two sentences for seven months."

## What She Thought About Them

"I thought about them for seven months," said Mavis Rooke, "and what I thought was this. In the eleventh month of the third year a doctor read a thing out to me in a room off a ward and I heard about half of it and I got the rest of it myself, and the rest of it was that there was nothing written down about me, and so I had nothing."

"Yes."

"You have been in that room and you know what I said."

"I know what you said," said Lin Hao.

"And on the eleventh of July there is a thing about me in a book," she said, "and I have had a month with it, and what I have worked out in a month is that being found is one thing and being caught is another thing and I cannot tell which one has happened, and neither can the doctor, and she will not tell me because she has said once that she is not going to unask it."

She put her hand flat on the table, and it was the hand of somebody who had not stopped and had stopped.

"So that is where I have got to at about six this morning on a stair," said Mavis Rooke. "I have a creditor. Not you. I have got a person. For four years that thing has belonged to nobody and everybody who ever spoke to me about it has told me so, and this morning I have got one, and I have got no name to put against it, because there is no name. That is what a creditor is. That is exactly what it is."


He had three things in him and he got about four words into the first one.

"You did not do it—"

"No."

"I did not decide—"

"No." She stood up. She had been standing up for most of it. "You do not get to do that. I am the creditor and you are the person who found me and I have been up two flights and I am going to say one thing and then I am going to go down them, and I have not had it in a month because I did not know where the building was until the thirtieth of September."

She did not say it for about a minute and a half.

"You have written down that I dug a street for six years," she said. "You have not written down that I am owed for it. And there is not one instrument in this city that can make anybody write it down, and that is not a thing you have done to me. That is a thing about a city that a woman on a step found out about nine months before you did."

She picked the sheet up and folded it in four again.

"Ninety-nine days," she said. "I read that on the thirtieth of September and I counted them and I came this morning because it was the number."

## Nine In The Morning

She went down the two flights slowly with the sister in front of her and neither of them said anything on the way.

Yusuf Adeyemi had not moved and had not spoken since the sheet went on the table.

"You have got about eleven minutes," he said, "and I am going to sit here and not say one word, and you are not going to get an instrument out of me, because there is not one in this room and there has not been one since the first of February of the first year."

"I know."

"What I am going to say is a fact and not a finding," said Adeyemi. "There is a creditor in this building this morning. There has never been one in it before, not in four years, and not in the books two floors under a sales floor either. You have wanted one for four years and I have watched you want one and I have never once said whether it was the right thing to want."

He got up and put his coat on.

"I am going to go and be the Registrar of something for four hours and it will not be this."

*Tenth of October of the fourth year. Nine minutes past nine. A thousand three hundred and twenty-nine days off the first of February and a thousand four hundred and forty off the sitting.*

*A creditor came up two flights without a hand on the rail and she is one of about four hundred and nineteen and there is not one instrument in this city that can make anybody write down that she is owed six years of a street.*

*She said finding is one thing and being caught is another one and she cannot tell which and neither can the doctor.*

*And she is right that she has got a person and that there is no name against him, and I have not got one word of that to give back and I did not get four words into the first of three things I had.*

*In five days I am going to put a name on it.*
