# The Figure He Enters Himself

He wrote it on the office's foolscap at about eleven minutes past ten on the morning of the fifteenth of October, at the counter, in the room with the two chairs in it, with Yusuf Adeyemi standing at the end of the table and not sitting down.

Five days from the tenth of October to this morning.

He wrote it in about eleven seconds and he did not improve it afterwards and he did not take it out and start again, and no drawer in this city was ever made for a claim against a person, and there was a blank drawer under that book from the fourth of February of the first year until the eighth of December of the third year and nothing ever went into it.

*Entered into this Register on the first of October of the fourth year the unrecorded merit of about a million people, out of one account, and gave it back to those I found.*

*I decided who was found. I decided it in one room and there was no instrument in this city that could ask me why or stop me.*

*This office holds no money and has never held any and cannot pay this and will not be able to.*

*It is entered against me.*

And then, underneath, in the column, in his own hand, which is neither the office's hand nor the hand of a man who puts figures on a surface people walk past:

*Lin Hao. Rank one.*

## What The Clerk Was Given

Wenna Raut read it twice. Both times at the same speed, which is her one habit and the only thing she has ever had that is any use to her.

Then she squared it and put it in front of him with the second column showing.

"That is four lines and a name and a column," she said, "and I can enter four lines and a name and a column, and I am entering it exactly as given, and I have read it twice and both times the words were the words."

She picked up the pen.

"Before I do this I am going to read you the four conditions, because I have to, and because a clerk may only enter what she is given and she is given a thing and she is not given a judgment."

"Read them."

## The Four

*The first. It changed what a person held.*

"It is met," said Wenna Raut. "It changed what that Register holds and what you hold and what about four hundred and nineteen people hold after the seventh of July. I have no doubt about the first."

*The second. It happened on a day the person can name and in a place the person can name.*

"It is met. The first of October, and the Reading, and the Board."

*The third. Another person was there, or was given it, and that person can be named or described closely enough that a person reading this in four years would know which person.*

"It is met," she said, "and I want it noted that I did not expect to say that. About a million people were there and there are about nine thousand of them under that wall in a day and a person reading this in four years will know exactly which person this is about."

*And the fourth. It is not on a page anywhere in this city.*

She did not speak for a moment.

"The first of October is on a page," said Wenna Raut. "The second line at the foot of the ranks wall is on a stone and the Board carries both words and a clerk's hand put them there in the same number of minutes. The return has a return. There is a figure in a book in a building off a fourth court and it was entered twice and amended once and I have read all three entries and I am not permitted to tell you what is in them."

She put the pen down on the table.

"The fourth is not met."


"I know what I am entering," she said. "I am entering a claim against a person and not a deed, and the book on this table has had one thing in it that was not a deed before this morning and that was a street and a figure, and this office has never had a debtor in it, and after this morning it has one and he is the only man it may ask."

"That is more than the sentence allows."

"That is the sentence," said Wenna Raut. "I am permitted one sentence and I am permitted to add what I am given and I have been given that, and if you had given me a warmer version of it I would have had to enter the warm one, and you did not, and that has never once happened in eleven years at this counter."

She wrote it. It took her about eleven seconds and she did not look up while she did it.

*The Register of Unrecorded Merit has entered a claim against a person, in the column headed carried by. Of the four conditions given, the first, second and third were met. The fourth was not met, and the reason is that the thing it describes is on a page in this city.*

## Five Quarters Of Nothing

The column had been ruled on the ninth of March of the third year and had been empty on the thirtieth of March, and empty on the thirtieth of June, and empty on the thirtieth of September, and empty on the thirtieth of December, and empty on the thirtieth of March and the thirtieth of June and the thirtieth of September of this year.

Five quarters and two years of it, and about eight hundred and forty-one lines in the column beside it, and a quarter with eight hundred and forty-one doors in it that moved the figure by nothing at all.

And a woman had told a clerk in a counting room on the thirtieth of September of the third year what that column would do when it began, and she had said it would go faster than anything this city has ever watched and that not one particle of it would ever have been earned.

It has been filled once. It has one line in it and the line is his.

## What Yusuf Adeyemi Did Not Do

The Registrar of Unrecorded Merit stood at the end of the table for the whole of it and did not sit down and did not read the page before it was written, because a Registrar of that office may read a page after it is entered and not before, and he is the only man in Ostara who holds that title and nobody has ever told him the meaning of it.

He read it after.

"That is entered," he said.

"Yes."

"I have no view," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "A man at rank one has put his own name in a column of a book in a room off a third court and it is not my office to hold a view about that and I am not going to find one this morning."

"I did not allow it and I did not prevent it and there was no instrument in this room before this morning that could have done either, and that is the whole of what I have got to say about it and I have said it."

He put his hand on the back of the second chair.

"One thing and then I am going to go and be a joke for the rest of the day," he said. "You have closed something and I know what it is because I have watched you keep it open for four years, and you are not going to be able to explain it to me and I would rather you did not try."

"It is done."

"It is done," said Adeyemi, "and it cannot be un-entered by anybody including you, and the sheet of the thirtieth of December goes out at four with an address at the head of every line, and a person in this city may stand at a public desk and read it."


Fifteen days to the thirtieth of October, and thirty of November, and thirty of December is seventy-five days, and on the afternoon of the last of those the second column of this office will be printed under a heading in a clerk's hand and about two hundred people a week in Cordwain will read it.

He went up and then went back down the way he had come, and the second flight was colder going down than it had been going up.

The lamp was off and the fifth book was on its shelf with the strap across it and the shelf was chained, and it does not come off until the first of January, and it had been locked at about five in the afternoon by a keeper who has written six words under her own name four times a year for thirty years and who does not know yet that a man has put a name against them in a room two floors above her.

He stood in that room for about two minutes in the dark and did not touch anything.

## What It Is Now

A register with a debtor in it is a different object from a register with nobody in it, and every person who comes to that counter after this morning is coming to a different counter.

Before this morning a person could bring four hundred and nineteen lines in a book and find out that nothing had been paid and that nobody had been caught. From this morning there is a line in that book with a name on it, and it does not pay anybody, and it cannot, and it is the first one.

That is the whole of what he has bought and it was bought at a counter at eleven minutes past ten on the fifteenth of October by a man at rank one with no permission and no money and no instrument and no other person in the city who could have stopped him or helped him, which is the argument the whole of this office has been making for four years about itself.

And the woman it was done for is two flights up off Cordwain, and she was in this room five days ago without a hand on the rail, and she is not going to thank him, and he would not want her to, and he is never going to tell her, and the page will.

*Fifteenth of October of the fourth year, twenty past ten in the morning. Entered a claim against myself in the column headed carried by, in my own hand, four lines and a name, entered exactly as given by a clerk who may only enter and who added one sentence and it was the right one.*

*Three conditions met and the fourth not met and she told me why and it is because of the first of October.*

*I have spent everything this office had and it was not money and it was not a standing and it was not the last of a thing nobody could take. It was the option of being a man who did a thing. I have it now to be a man who owes one, and a man who owes one cannot argue about what it was for, and he cannot improve it afterwards, and he cannot take it out of a book.*

*And a keeper two floors down has written six words four times a year for thirty years with nobody's name on them but hers, and what held that account open was that there was no holder, and as of eleven minutes past ten this morning there is one, and it is me.*

*And she will find out from a page like everybody else, on the second of January, when the book comes off the shelf, and I did not go down and tell her, and I went down and stood in the dark instead.*
