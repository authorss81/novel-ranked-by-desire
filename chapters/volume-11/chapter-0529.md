# Smaller And Not Wrong

The thirtieth of June is the last working day of the quarter and the sheet goes out at four, and the Register's line on the sheet of the thirtieth of June of the third year said *Entered: thirty-one*, and the same line on the sheet of the thirtieth of March had said nineteen.

Twelve in a quarter. Nine of them in eleven days in April from about a hundred people who came down a hill, and then about three in a quarter after that, which is about one a month, and the reason for it was not a slump.

It was a correction.

## What One Person Is Worth In This Office

A person who cannot name a day does not come twice.

That is not a complaint about the four lines. It is the arithmetic of the four lines. The second condition wants a day the person can name and a place the person can name, and there are about nine in ten of the people who come to that counter who cannot name a day, and about a fifth of the ones who can are people who were the only person in the room.

A person who brings something to a counter off a third court has usually thought about it for some time, and thinking about a thing once is a strange experience and thinking about it twice is not an experience at all.

So the Register was not counting the city. It was counting the people who could describe themselves, and that number went up in April because four thousand people had read four conditions and picked the ones that pass, and it went flat afterwards because four thousand people had read four conditions and found out what it is to fail the second one.

Thirty-one in a quarter, against a counter on that corridor that turned a wheel about nine hundred and ten times in twenty-one months.

## Why That Is A Problem And Not A Complaint

A number that goes up and down is a fact about something.

He sat on the bottom step of the branch stair at about seven in the morning of the second of July with the sheet of the thirtieth of June on his knee and Adeyemi's weekly reading of the counter on the back of a piece of foolscap, and the two figures were on the same piece of paper and neither of them was wrong.

Nine hundred and ten people past a door, in twenty-one months.

Thirty-one things in the book.

Nobody in Ostara could say what the second figure was a fraction of. It was not a rate of kindness, because the thing it was counting was not kindness. It was a rate of description. It was the number of people in this city who could describe a thing they had done to somebody else, on a day, in a place, with a second person in it.

It was, in other words, a figure about people who were comfortable. And he had written it, and it was in a clerk's hand on a sheet going out at four to about two hundred people a week in Cordwain, and about two hundred people a week in Cordwain could read it perfectly well and would understand it to be a figure about how much good this city does.

It was not.

## What The Lane Had Done In The Same Quarter

The same quarter, on the same days, in a lane four streets off, a figure at the top of a column had gone from a number to a bigger number, and a man in a delivery coat had written it about forty times, and every morning for about ninety days about a hundred and sixty people had walked past it and about thirty of them had paid a grain to stand in it.

Two figures in one district for the same ninety days, made by two men, about the same people, neither of them checkable by anybody.

And the second one had gone up and the first one had gone flat, and the reason the second one had gone up was that a man in a delivery coat had made it up in a way that looked like a number and had never once pretended otherwise, and the reason the first one had gone flat was that he had written a set of four lines that could be failed.

## What He Said To The Only Person He Could Say It To

He said it on the second of July at about ten past seven in the morning, standing in the corridor of an office off a third court with the sheet rolled up under his arm, and he said it to the man who may only enter and cannot improve anything and cannot be asked a question twice.

"Thirty-one in a quarter. Nineteen in the quarter before it. Nine of them off the Church's hundred in eleven days in April and then about one a month since."

"I have got the sheet," said Adeyemi. "I read it at ten this morning with the rest and I am not going to pretend you came down here to tell me something I could not read."

"Then I am going to tell you what thirty-one is and you are going to tell me whether I am right."

"Go on."

"It is not a count of what this city did. It is a count of people who could describe it."

He unrolled it and did not put it on the table.

"About nine in ten of what anybody brings does not have all four, and most of what does not is failing for one reason, and it is always the second line. The second line wants a day."

"And a person who cannot name a day has usually been in a room by themselves. There are more of those in this city than anybody has ever written down, and they are not going to come here, and they cannot."

Adeyemi took about four seconds.

"That is the correct reading of your own instrument and I have been waiting about eleven weeks for you to say it out loud to somebody, because in about four years somebody with a folder is going to say it and by then it will be a finding about you instead of a finding about a sentence."

"Yes."

"So write it on the sheet."

## The Thing He Was Asked For

"There is a line on that sheet that a clerk is permitted to add," said Adeyemi, "and it has been on every sheet since the office was made, and it has said, in the office's own hand, what the office had and what it had not done, and there has never been a reason to use it and I have thought about this for eleven weeks."

"Go on."

"Any person in this city may read that sheet at a public desk on a working afternoon. About two hundred people a week in Cordwain read it."

He put his pen down.

"Every one of them who has ever heard the Church speak about this city would take *entered: thirty-one* to mean that the city managed thirty-one unrecorded good deeds in a quarter."

"That is not what it means. And no amount of printing the other number underneath will make it the other number, because a public figure is not read as a caveat. A public figure is read as a thing."

## What He Said He Would Do About It

"I have said that to nine women in a kitchen and not one of them improved it," said Lin Hao.

"And one of them told me in a room off a ward on the twenty-first of January of last year that about a fifth of the people a division finds cannot place a day either. They will be told they have nothing. She said write something on the sheet that says they have nothing in this register, and not that they have nothing."

"I have had that in a drawer for a year and a half and I have not done it. And I have been not doing it for a very good reason, which is that I do not know yet what I am allowed to say."

"What stops you."

"Alma Quayle classified the four lines on the ninth of July of last year and gave them a class and a date and no condition and no note. The class is what a division may carry, and it is about four lines and not about a line about what the four lines mean."

He rolled the sheet up again.

"The moment this office prints a sentence that is not a classification of its own definition, this office is publishing an interpretation of itself. About nine divisions will read two documents and find two things. The one that is easier to quote will be the one that was not written by an officer who classifies words for a living."

Adeyemi turned round in his chair.

"So the office prints a bare figure," he said, "with an address at the head of it, and a heading with a hole under it, and the correct sentence about what the figure is not goes in the same drawer, and in about four years a man with a folder holds up a page and says the Register of Unrecorded Merit published a number that was not what it was, and you say that is correct and it is."

"Yes."

"And in about four years there is also a man in this city who says the Register is the one office in Ostara that will not say what its own numbers mean," said Yusuf Adeyemi, "and he will be right, and there is not one instrument in this city that can make him wrong."

He picked the pen up again.

"I am not going to tell you what to do. I have got about nine days a quarter and you have four of them and you have a doctor off Cordwain who told you a thing in January that you have not once done, and I would like it recorded that I have not told you to do it and I would like it recorded that I would have."

## What Went Out At Four And What He Wrote Down

At four on the thirtieth of June the sheet went out with an address at the head of every line, and near the bottom of it a figure of thirty-one in the office's own hand, and after it two columns, the first of them full and the second of them headed *carried by* with nothing under it, and under the whole of it the one sentence a clerk is permitted to add, which said what the office had entered and which was correct and which nobody would read.

He wrote it up at about four in the morning of the first of July.

*Thirty-one in the quarter. Nineteen in the one before. Nine off a hill in eleven days and then about one a month.*

*The number is not wrong. The number is the only number this city has ever had about itself and it went down because a sentence I wrote is a sentence that can be failed, and I knew that on the nineteenth of July of the second year in a kitchen when I said nine in ten and did not explain it.*

Then he wrote, after a gap:

*What is going to happen to it is not that it will be quoted. It is going to be used. Somebody is going to read a number against a room and a name, and the name is going to be somebody who can describe herself.*

He read that back at the top of a stair off a third court at four in the morning with a bin room open behind him and a mop standing in a bucket, and it was the first time in five years that he had written a prediction instead of a figure, and he had written it nine days before it came true, and he knew that he had.
