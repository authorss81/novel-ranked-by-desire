# A Reading Round

He wrote it at the top of the third flight at about four in the morning of the thirtieth of March, sitting on the bottom step of his own branch stair with a bin-room tally board across his knees and a pencil that had belonged to a counting house and had somebody else's name on it.

Forty days from the twentieth of February. Ten days to the thirtieth of February and thirty more to the thirtieth of March is forty, and he had counted it on the night of the nineteenth without any reason at all and had not written it down.

## What He Wrote

Four lines and a date and his own name at the bottom, in a hand that is not the office's and is not a hand that chalks figures on walls either.

*I ask for a round. One division. Nine hundred doors, once, in a district that is not mine, reading what people did that was never written down and had been read by nobody.*

*I understand that a person found at a door is a person somebody has decided was owed something, and that finding them is not the same as owing them.*

*I am asking. I am not asking anybody to agree with me and nothing in this office can agree or refuse.*

*Lin Hao. Rank one. The thirtieth of March of the fourth year.*

He read it back on the step and did not improve any of it, and the second line is the line a doctor in a room off a ward asked a clerk to say in the January of the second year, and he had had it in a drawer for a year and a half and had put it on a standing sheet on the thirtieth of September of the third year.

About four thousand people had spent eleven weeks with it and it had cost him a whole quarter of a counter.

He had been told by a woman on a roof on the twentieth of February exactly what nine hundred doors would turn into, and then been told to ask her anyway, and he had asked her anyway.

## What Adeyemi Did With It

Yusuf Adeyemi read it in the room with the two chairs in it at about nine in the morning with his coffee going cold on his hand, and he read it twice, and the second time was slower.

"You have written a request."

"I have."

"Under a sentence that came out of your own mouth in a corridor on the first of February of the first year, which says that you are the only person this office may ask."

"Yes."

"That means there is nobody in this building who can turn this down." He put the cup down. "Not the Registrar, and not the clerk, and not the officer in the room with no window, who has not opened a file in two years and will not open one for this. You are the whole instrument of this office and you have just asked the office to do a thing, and it is on no form at all, and there is no second person in the building."

"Yes."

"Do you understand what you have made of that."

"I understand that four years ago I spent everything this office had on being the thing you can ask, and I have been the thing you can ask for four years, and this morning I have used it."

He began to say something then, about the title, which is a joke he has never explained to anybody in four years, and he did not finish the sentence and the cup came up off the table about nine inches and went back down.

"Never mind," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "It would be better if it were a worse joke."

He entered it at about half past ten.

A clerk may only enter. She read it twice at the same speed both times and entered four lines and a date and a name exactly as given, and did not ask him one question about any part of it, and Adeyemi stood in the doorway and watched her do it and did not say one word about it.

## Whose District It Is

The district is not hers and he did not choose it.

She told him in the room over a chandler's on the twenty-eighth of January and again on a roof on the twentieth of February, and the reason is nine years old and is hers: a street you walk stops being a street you walk after about four years, and a division that goes to nine hundred doors in the district it knows would not be reading anything, it would be going home nine times a day.

So it is a district neither of them has ever walked. It is four streets off Cordwain on the far side of the yard end, about nine hundred doors, and about nine hundred people who have never been asked anything by anybody.

And the nine lose their own round for the duration.

Ilma Reese has been doing it nine years and has never let a street go unwalked, not once, not for rain and not for a death in her own street, and about nine streets of their own district are going to go unwalked from the first of April until this is over, and nothing puts them back either.

He wrote that down on the twenty-ninth of March at about four in the morning and it is the second line of the page and it is the line he keeps coming back to.

## The Forty Days He Did Not Spend

The number he did not put in the request is the number a doctor gave him in a room off a ward on the twenty-first of January of the second year and has given him once in every year since, in a different room off the same ward, in a week that does not have a day in it.

About a third of anything a division finds in a cold city ends up on a table in a ward.

About one in five of what any division brings back cannot put a day to it.

He had forty days. The ward is two flights up off Cordwain and it takes about eleven minutes to walk there from the counter and he has walked it four times in four years for other reasons and once for this one.

He did not walk it in those forty days, and the reason is not a reason, and he wrote it down at four in the morning on the second of April in nine words: *I had forty days and I did not spend them.*

That is the whole of what asking for it was, and he had known the whole of it on the twentieth of February, on a roof, in the wind, from a woman who works in that ward and who told him on the roof precisely what nine hundred doors would turn into and then said that it was not an objection.

He did not ask her.

## What Went Out At Five In The Morning

Nine of them went out at about five on the morning of the thirtieth of March with a sheet of district addresses, a sheet of blank return, a rule they have carried for two years and were not going to explain to a man at rank one, and about nine hours of daylight before the light went.

He stood at the foot of the car shed ladder off Ferrier Street at about ten to five and did not go up.

Ilma Reese. Coen Ardry. Tenna Vole. Sef Ossick. Marget Halle. Duri Peck. Annis Cray. Toller Vine. And Beatrix Halm, who came past him on her way to the cabin and put two fingers flat against his chest for about a second without stopping and went in.

## The Thirteenth Sheet

At four on the thirtieth of March the sheet went out with an address at the head of every line and *Entered: thirty-nine*.

Thirty-four at the thirtieth of December and five more in ninety days. One of them on the ninth of January, fourteen more spread across the two months after it, and five of them in the last eleven days of March, after nine people had begun standing on other people's steps and asking what they wanted a division to read.

Nineteen people in ninety days against a hundred and twenty-nine at the door, and he had written at four in the morning on the second of January, in a column that was not one of his three, that he wanted one person through that door before the thirtieth of March.

And after the figure, two columns. The first with thirty-nine lines against it. The second headed *carried by*, ruled, and empty, and it will be empty on the thirtieth of June, and it is empty now because a person who cannot name a day is kept out by the second condition and not by the column, and the column has not done one day's work in the four years it has been on that sheet.

And under the whole of it, in the office's own hand, in the one sentence a clerk is permitted to add, four words longer than that sentence is supposed to be:

*Where this Register has nothing, it has not been given anything, and it does not say that there was nothing.*

## What Is Coming Into This Office

A round returns about nine hundred accounts of things people did that were never written down, and not one of them is a deed, and the four lines do not apply to any of them, because a round does not ask for a day and does not ask who was in the room. It asks what somebody did and writes down what they said.

Those accounts are going to arrive at a counter off a third court, and a clerk at that counter may only enter and cannot improve what she is given and cannot be asked to hurry, and she is going to enter them.

And by the thirtieth of June the sheet of that quarter is going to carry a figure in the office's own hand, under the same heading, in the same column, with the same address at the head of every line, and it is going to be about nine times the one on this sheet, and it is going to mean the opposite thing, and about two hundred people a week in Cordwain will read both.

He wrote that down on the step, at about four in the morning, in nine words, and it was the ninth of April before he understood that the sentence he printed on the thirtieth of September says nothing at all about it, because it is about a Register that has nothing, and this Register is about to have nine hundred somethings in it.

## What He Wrote Down At Six In The Evening, And At Four In The Morning

He went down to the lane at about six and did not pay a grain and did not go into it and stood against the wall at the side for about an hour.

Three years is thirty-six months of thirty days, which is one thousand and eighty days. From the third of October to the thirtieth of March is twenty-seven days of October after the third, and then five month-ends at thirty days each, which is a hundred and fifty. A hundred and twenty-seven and a hundred and fifty is a hundred and seventy-seven days. One thousand two hundred and fifty-seven days, and counting the first morning as the first, that is one thousand two hundred and fifty-eight mornings behind that wall.

Emil Brack came to the wall end with the chalk at about a quarter to seven and looked at him for a while and went back to the stools.

And at four in the morning of the thirty-first, at the top of the third flight, he wrote this, and it is the only line in that notebook that is not about the Register:

*Nine divisions. A division that has to carry a line goes and finds out what the line is about. There are nine of them and she is one of nine people inside one of them, and I asked for one. By the thirtieth of June there will be nine of them going out, and it will be because I asked for one in my own name on the bottom of a piece of a bin-room tally board.*

He read it back.

*I have spent the thing that could not be taken from me. A man that nobody looks for is a man nobody comes for, and I have been that man for four years, and this morning nine hundred doors were going to be looked at because of a sentence I wrote on a step, and nothing in this city can stop it and nothing in this city will stop me.*
