# Four Years Is Not A Number

Eleven at night until half past five, four nights and three off, and it was in his coat, and he was through the door of the branch at about ten to eleven with a bucket under his arm and nothing in the bucket, and the heating in that building is whatever it has been all day and at eleven at night that is nothing at all.

Eleven weeks and three days of it. He had done that sum on a step four times since the first of January and it came out the same way every time and it has not improved once.

Rank one on a white plate in his coat with no figure cut into it anywhere.

## The Rota

The rota for the first of February came out on the thirtieth of January and the man who keeps it wrote it down in front of him, because Lin Hao told him in November that if a man is going to be asked about his hours then he is going to be asked about them where somebody can see it being written.

Four nights and three off on a page about the size of a folded hand.

His name, a band, and a number of nights. Nothing else on that page at all, and the man who keeps it could not tell you the band off his own page if you asked him, and has never been asked, and cannot be asked to hurry.

He read it, and put one finger on the number of nights, and said nothing at all, and went down to the second floor.

## The Second Floor

The cistern on the second had been written about in a ledger on the first of January and had stopped running by the sixth, and a woman had come and looked at it and gone away again, and it had been dry for about twenty-six days. There is a standpipe at the end of that landing and it works if you mean it.

There was a woman on that landing at about twenty past eleven with a cloth and a bucket. She has been there every night since the eleventh of October and she has not asked him one question in all that time, and he has stopped waiting for her to and started wondering what it is she is waiting for him to.

He had a theory about her and the theory was that she did not ask because there was nothing on the page in front of her to ask about, and he had been carrying it since about the middle of November and had never once tested it.

"Water's off on the landing," she said.

"It is."

At about midnight she came past him on the landing with the bucket and stopped.

"You have gone up that stair four times tonight and down it four times and you have not asked me for anything."

"No."

"Everybody who works that floor asks me for something inside a week," she said. "Not about the water. About anything at all. You have been here eleven weeks and three days."

He did not have an answer to that which was not a lie, and she went on down the stair with the bucket, and he has still not worked out what it is that she has never once asked him anything.

## The Letter

The night man at the counter had something on the counter at about twenty past one that had come in with the night's bundle and that he had not opened.

He opens the bundle. He enters the bundle. He does not open what is inside it, and he said so without being asked, in the voice of a man who has said it before to other people.

"Your name is on the outside of that one," he said.

Lin Hao put it on the sill.

The paper was the paper the Correction Office has. One paper, one way of folding it, and any person in this city can tell you what it is by the back of it, and no second paper has ever come and none is on its way.

Four lines, in a small hand that had been writing minutes for eleven years and had never once improved a sentence.

## What It Says

The office exists. It is called the Register of Unrecorded Merit. It has an address off a third court, and that address is where he will be able to find it whenever he decides to go and look at it, which is not the same as being able to do anything with it.

Yusuf Adeyemi is its Registrar.

The third line is the one that took him longest to read, because there was no verb in it that a man could put his back against. The office may put one question to one person, and the one person is you, and nothing in this city can require you to answer it, and nothing is going to be made that can.

That is all three sentences. He read them four times, which is about what the fourth one got. There is no figure of any kind on that sheet and no date on it beyond the day it was written, and the whole of what an office with a new name on the door had to say to the man it had chosen was that it was permitted to ask him something and had not asked it yet.

The night man read it standing up, the way he reads the bundle, and then he read it again.

"That is the only thing I have had in this building since the first of October that is about something instead of somebody," he said. "Everything else that comes here has got a box on it. That has got three sentences in it and not one box, and if you asked me to enter it I could not, and I have been on this counter nine years and there has not been one thing in this building I could not enter."

"You cannot enter it."

"I can enter the name at the top of it," said the night man. "That is the whole of what is enterable. And I am not going to enter it, because you are on my page as four nights and a band and I have a rule about not improving what I am given, and a name at the top of a letter about an office would be an improvement."

He put it in the inside of his coat, with the plate, and with a torn page in Beatrix Halm's hand that had the same two words on it in a different order.

## What Is In The Office

He went up the stair at about half past one with the bucket, thinking about it the way he thought about washrooms, which is slowly and in the wrong order.

An office with a name on a door and nothing else in it. No instrument — not one machine in this city can read a thing a person did and print a number off it, and the machine that prints numbers off things prints them off readings, and a reading is a thing that happened to a person in a room with a clock on it.

No money — the Exchange deals in permissions, and a permission is stamped, and this office has nothing to sell and nothing to stamp with. And every column on every sheet in it empty, because a column is somewhere for a figure to sit down, and there is not one figure yet that anybody in this city is able to say out loud about a thing a stranger did on a stair in a February.

In its first quarter it would produce a sentence. It might produce fewer than that.

## Half Past Four, Two Streets East

The two words and the dash and the two words were there in the office's own hand, at the height of a name on a plate, under the last tile at the foot of the ranks wall, and the ordinary repainting came and went in the middle of January and took nothing with it.

Beatrix Halm was under it, where she is at half past four.

"A hundred and nineteen mornings," she said, before he had got as far as the rail. "Twenty-eight days left in October including the third, and thirty each of November, December and January, and the first of February. I did it on a card in my coat because I do not trust my own head at half past four, and because I wanted it on paper once in nine years."

He stood on the other side of the column from her with the bucket at his feet.

"That is a lane off Cordwain and not a wall."

"It is a lane off Cordwain and not a wall," said Beatrix Halm, "and there is a figure of merit on a slate at a quarter past nine every morning and it has been there a hundred and nineteen mornings, and I have watched both of those numbers go up, and I would like it on record that I have never once been able to turn either of them into a figure about anything."

## Four Years Is Not A Number

"You are going to ask me what four years came to," she said. "Everybody asks me. There have been eleven people this month and nine of them were after the figure and two of them were after the wall."

"What did you tell them."

"The same thing I am telling you." She turned round and looked at the stone.

"Two words and a dash and two more words that a boy painted at six in the morning off a sheet somebody gave him, and a wall that about nine thousand people go under in a day and not one of them is reading, and a man in a delivery coat telling everybody the line is about him, and a slate."

"That is the four years. That is all of it. It has been four years and every one of them has ended in the same place and I have never once been able to make it come out as one number, and neither have you, and you have had four years to try and you are the one man in this city who would have been able to."

He thought about that on the pavement for about as long as it took a tram to clear the junction.

"Four years is not a number," he said. "It is a thing a person says when they have run out of the other one. I have been at rank one for eleven weeks and three days and I have two figures in this city that are mine and neither of them is a person, and that is what four years came to, and I would rather you had the sentence than the number because the sentence is true and the number would only be about me."

## The Tile

She did not answer that and he did not expect her to, and he looked down at the tile while he said the rest of it.

"About sixty-five times since the first of January. Two minutes each, near enough. Four hundred people had gone past me doing it by the twenty-eighth of January, and I know it is four hundred and not four hundred a week, because you said so, and because I have stood here and counted nobody."

"That is correct," said Beatrix Halm.

"What is on it."

"Nothing," said Beatrix Halm.

"Who put it there."

And that was as far as it got, in either direction, on the morning of the first of February. She has not been asked and she has not offered and he has not asked, and there is a line between the three of those facts that nobody drew.

## The One Question

At about twenty to five he went back up the hill with the bucket and went into the branch and started his shift.

The standing sheet of the quarter goes out at four in the afternoon with an address at the head of every line, and the account is under it in a clerk's hand, and he has read it on a bench in a corridor and will read it again on a bench in a corridor in about two months and there will be nothing new on it.

The letter was in his coat.

One question in it, and no figure of any kind anywhere in it, and the office had put a name on itself and a name on the man who runs it and a name on the man it is allowed to ask, and it had not asked.

He had until half past five in the morning to work out what a Registrar asks a man at rank one for in a room with nothing in it, and it was not on any sheet in that building, and it was not on the wall.
