# Come Back With A Witness

The town hall counter opens at eight and shuts at four and there are about nine of them in this city that will take a claim off a person and do something about it, and this one is off Cordwain and has a clerk at it who has been at it for about two years and who wrote down, in her own hand, on an afternoon in the first year, that there was a second instrument in Ostara and it was worse than the problem.

He came in at about eleven on the thirtieth of October of the third year to find out what that sentence had done in two years.

There were eleven people in the hall and four of them at the counter and he stood against the wall by the door with the coat closed and did not go up, because a man at rank one from an office off a third court has no standing at a town hall counter and he knew that before he came.

## What Nell Ardin Asked For

The woman in front of the counter had been in the lane off Cordwain on the first of October at about ten to eight in the morning and had been put in a column for a grain that a woman with four rooms had paid for.

She wanted eleven days of mending.

That was all of it. She mended for four rooms and she had done eleven days of mending in the month that ended on the thirtieth of September and had asked for the money once, at the end of it, and had been told the rents had gone up on the first and that the householder was waiting on her own figure.

"When did you last ask her."

"At the end of the month. On the twenty-ninth or the thirtieth, I have it right whichever it was."

"And what did she say."

"She said the number had gone up on the first and that the number was what the rooms took now, and she said come back after the quarter."

There was nothing wrong with any of that and it was all of what she had.

## The Sentence The Clerk Gave Her

"Come back with a witness," said Margit Roon.

"What witness."

"Somebody who was in the room. Somebody who saw it and can say so and can be put in front of this counter and be asked about it and be wrong in front of nine other people if she is wrong."

"She was the only person in the room."

"Then bring the only person in the room."

Nell Ardin put both hands flat on the counter, which is what everybody does at that counter.

"I mend," she said. "There is no one in the room when I mend. That is the entire shape of my work. Four rooms, one woman, a hearth and a fire, and I have done it since I was about eleven and there has never been a second person in a room when I have been in a room for money."

Margit Roon wrote nothing, which is what the clerk at that counter does when she has been given nothing.

## Why The Rule Is A Good One

"I am going to tell you the reason, and then you are going to go, because people hear the word *no* and stop listening and there are only about four minutes in it."

She turned the book round on the counter so that Nell Ardin could see the page.

"If I take eleven days off a householder on your word, that is not a mistake. That is the counter working."

"Tomorrow there is a woman at this counter who wants nine days off another householder on her word, and she is right, and the day after that there is a person at this counter who wants what he is owed off a householder and he has made it up, and he is also right, and I could not tell the two apart and neither could the householder, and I would be the one taking the money."

"That is the rule."

"That is the rule and it is a good one and it is older than this building," said Margit Roon. "I would have given you the money in about four seconds if there were a way of my being wrong in public instead of in a book."

## The Third Condition Of His Own Definition

He stood by the door and read four lines off the inside of his coat where they were, because he had carried them for three years and had not once had to use them in a room.

*Third. Another person was there, or was given it, and that person can be named or described closely enough that a person reading this in four years would know which person.*

A mender works alone in a room with a fire in it. That was not an oversight in his sentence. That was the sentence, and it had been classified in the third week of the fourth year by the only officer in this city who classifies words, and she gave it a class and a date and neither a condition nor a note.

She refused four classifications in her first four days and has never thanked him for lifting a recusal on a slip the size of a quarter of a page.

So the office was not the way round, and the four lines were the same rule as the one at this counter, in four parts, written in a kitchen with nine women in it, and he had heard a woman say *come back with a witness* at this counter two years before he wrote them down and had not known that he had heard it.

## The Part That Is About The Lane

"I was in the lane," said Nell Ardin. "On the first of October, in the morning. Nine of us, and a woman paid for the nine, and a man in a delivery coat wrote us down in chalk on a wall, and the number on the wall is what she says my rooms take now."

She did not have it exactly and she was not asked to have it exactly.

"So there is one of you that will do something about eleven days of mending if I can find somebody who was in a room with me doing nothing, and one of you that already has me in a figure and cannot take me out of it."

"Yes."

"And neither of you can read the other's book."

Margit Roon put her pen down flat, which she did about twice a day.

"Listen to me," she said. "I am going to say one sentence and I am not going to improve it, and I have been waiting two years for somebody to be in front of this counter who needed it."

"There is a figure on a wall in this city that went up on the morning you were standing in it, and it cannot be checked, and this counter can check anything and cannot check that, and if either of us acts we are acting on whichever of the two we happen to prefer, and I have not got the standing to prefer and neither have you."

## What She Wrote Instead

A clerk at that counter has one line and she may add it to what goes out at four, and she cannot improve what she is given and cannot go and get it.

"I cannot do one other thing with you today," said Margit Roon. "Come back on the twenty-seventh of the month if you have anybody at all, and if you have not, then come back on the twenty-seventh anyway and I will write that you came back and I had nothing to give you, because a line with a name on it is worth more than a refusal with a face on it."

She wrote it the way she writes everything on that counter, which is fast and without lifting the pen, and she turned the page round and read it once and did not read it twice.

"That is a quarter of a page and it is three sentences and I have put a woman's name on a thing that goes up at four with the street written across the top of every line, and it is not the Register's sheet and it has never been the Register's sheet, and this counter was writing pages like this before the Register had a room."

## What He Came To Find Out

He had come in at eleven to find out what his own sentence had done to this counter, and it had done nothing to this counter, and that was the finding and he had to stand by a door with it for about forty minutes.

The refusal was not his. The refusal was two years older than the Register and it had a book under it and a reason that a person could check, and the four lines he had written in a kitchen were the same rule with the same four conditions in it, and he had spent three years and a quarter believing that his counter was a more generous place than the city.

It was not more generous. It was the same counter, with a narrower door and no money in it.

At about half past three he came up to the counter, which was the wrong thing to do and which he did anyway.

"The line on your sheet."

"It went out at four," said Margit Roon. "It goes out at four every working day that anybody has anything to put on it, and I have never once been asked by a man at rank one whether a thing I wrote was correct, and you are not going to be the first, and if you ask me again I shall write down that you asked."

## What He Wrote Down

He wrote it up at four in the morning of the thirty-first at the top of the third flight with the bin room open behind him.

*Thirtieth of October of the third year. Town hall counter off Cordwain. Eleven days of mending, asked for once, refused, and the refusal is a rule that predates this office by about two years and is a good rule.*

*Second instrument and first, in one sentence, by a woman at a counter and not by me: a figure on a wall that cannot be checked and a counter that can check anything and cannot check that, and whoever acts is acting on whichever of the two they happen to prefer.*

*Twenty-nine days from the first of October to the thirtieth, thirty days of a month and one day short of it.*

*And the thing I came to find out. That line I had put on a sheet on the thirtieth of September did not make this room strict. This room was strict in the first year before the Register had a room in it. I have three years of believing that what I built was a more generous counter than this city's and it is the same counter with a narrower door.*

*If it is the same counter, then it does the same work, and it leaves out the same people, and the only thing I have added is a book.*

He read the last line back twice at about a quarter to five with the cough sitting in him and did not take it out, because it was correct and there was nothing under it to take it out with.
