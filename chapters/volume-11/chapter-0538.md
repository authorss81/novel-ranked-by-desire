# Four Hundred And Nineteen

She had the three pages on the table in the room over a chandler's when he came in at about half past six in the evening of the twenty-fourth of April, and she did not put her hand on them, and she did not tell him to sit down.

They were three pages of foolscap and four hundred lines and every line had a time against it.

Not one of the four hundred carries a figure. There is a street and an hour and a thing that a person felt on that street at that hour, and about nine times in ten the thing is not a fact about anybody, it is what it is like to be on that street.

And not one of the four hundred is him.

## What He Did With Them

He read all four hundred straight through without stopping and without going back, and he did not turn one of them round to see what was on the other side of it, and it took him about an hour and a half and she sat at the other end of the table and let him.

"You have never read them."

"No."

"Four years."

"I asked you for the fourth line of the definition in a kitchen on the nineteenth of July of the second year and you said you had four hundred lines the correct word did not reach and that they were yours and they were staying yours."

"They were," said Beatrix Halm. "They are still. You have just read them."

"That is not the same as having them."

"No," she said. "It is not."

## Four Of Them He Asked Her About

He asked her about four of the four hundred while he was still reading, and she answered about two of them.

*The twelfth of the second month, four in the morning. On the corner by the chandler's. The lamps in this street go on at about eleven different times and the street is safe because of it, and everybody on it knows that and not one of them could put it in a book.*

*Twenty-second of the third month, half past six. Off the yard end. A man has walked the same eleven hundred paces every morning for nine years and he has started stopping nine paces short of where he used to and he could not tell you why and neither could his wife.*

*Nineteenth of the fourth month, about eight in the morning. Two streets. Two sisters who have not spoken to each other in about two years have both said the same sentence to this street inside a week, word for word, and they have each said it to me and neither of them knows the other one said it.*

"Sixth of the fourth month, about four in the afternoon. Off the yard end. A baker has not asked any of his customers for anything for four years and about nine of them have tried and he has said no to all of them and the shop has not been late once."

"Which two did you answer."

"The second and the fourth," said Beatrix Halm. "The first one is right and it is the kind of thing a person says about a street when the street has been kind to them. The third one is two people and I could have found that in about four days on foot by sitting on a wall, and neither of them has ever been asked anything by anybody and I am not going to be the first."

"You wrote it down and then decided nobody else should ever have it."

"I wrote down what I was asked to write down and I went back on my own time and I have not asked them a second question," she said. "There is a line between those two things and I know where it is to the pace and I have known it since about the fourth year."

## What He Wanted

He had come for nineteen.

"Two hundred and nineteen of your doors have been done since the thirtieth of March," he said. "Nine people, twenty-five days, about one a day each across the nine, and the reason it is a day and not an hour is that you have to stand on a step until a person has finished telling you."

"You have read the district book."

"Adeyemi has read the ratchet on the corridor wall and I have read the sheet and I have done the sum and I do not know what the sum is for."

"Ask me for it properly."

He asked her for it properly.

"I want nineteen off another district," said Lin Hao. "Not this one. Four streets off this one, and nine of them, and I want them on the same paper as these with the same kind of time against them."

## Why He Wanted Them

"I want to know whether a round says the same thing in a street it has never walked," he said. "That is the whole of it. It is an auditor's question and it is not a good question and I am asking it because I have four years of a number on a sheet that means one thing and about nine hundred doors of something that means another."

"And if the two do not agree then one of them is not a reading of this city, and I cannot tell which, and I cannot tell which without a second set."

"You are going to compare them."

"I am going to put them side by side and see whether they are the same kind of thing."

"They will not be," said Beatrix Halm. "These four hundred are nine streets that one division has walked for about nine years and knows the way people talk in. What comes back off four streets it has never walked will be in a different language entirely and it will be the correct language and you will hold two pages of different languages and call it a failure."

"Then it is a failure."

"Then it is a division going to four streets where nobody has ever walked and hearing about four streets it has never heard," she said. "You will get that by about the ninth of June."

## What It Cost To Get Nineteen

"A division does not hand its return to anybody."

"I know."

"It is not mine to hand. It is not yours to be handed. If I ask for it then the other division is told who asked, and the other division is nine people who have not been asked for anything by anybody in about four years, and one of them is going to have to explain why the return of a street is now on a table off Ferrier Street."

"You do not have to tell them who asked."

"There is not one way to ask for a return that does not contain the asking," said Beatrix Halm. "And I am going to do it, and the name that goes with it is yours, and you knew that when you asked me for it in this room and you have had about eleven seconds to change your mind and you have not moved."

She did not move either.

## The Nineteenth

He read those nineteen on the twenty-eighth of April at about eleven at night at the top of the third flight, on a separate sheet, and the first eighteen were about streets and hours and the ordinary weather of being looked at.

The nineteenth is this.

*The fourteenth of April, about six in the evening. Off the yard end. A man carries a chair up the outside stair of a building every morning and puts it on a landing outside a door and does not knock, and goes down again, and has done it every morning for about eleven weeks. Four people on that landing have asked him. He says the chair is not for them. Nobody has established what it is for. He does not know what it is for.*

It is a true line. It is in the correct language. It has a time against it and no figure on it, and nothing whatever sits behind it that anybody could check, and there is a man on a landing in this city carrying a chair up a stair every morning for eleven weeks for a reason he does not have.

And there is not one word on that sheet that anybody reading it in four years could attach to a name.

## Four Hundred And Nineteen

Three pages and one page is four hundred and nineteen, and a hundred of the lines on the third page have a time against them and a street and about nine words.

She wrote the total on the corner of the bottom page in the small level hand she has used since the second of July of the first year, and she did not put a date on it and she did not put a name on it, and she gave it to him because it was on her paper and not on his.

"That is yours," said Lin Hao.

"It has been on my paper since the second of July of the first year."

"You have added nineteen to it because I asked you to fetch them for me from four streets you do not walk."

"Yes."

"Four years ago you gave me four hundred of these for nothing on the roof of a tram and the condition was that I spend them on people and not on myself," he said. "I have spent them on people. About nine hundred doors went out on the thirtieth of March and I have not spent one of these four hundred on that and the nineteen are the nineteen."

"Then what are you telling me."

"I am telling you that I have not stopped noticing," said Beatrix Halm. "There are going to be more of them and I am not going to keep telling you about it."

## What He Wrote At Four In The Morning

He wrote it up on the twenty-fifth at the top of the third flight with the bin room open behind him.

*Twenty-fifth of April of the fourth year, eleven at night, reading nineteen that are not mine off four streets she does not walk.*

*Four hundred and nineteen on one piece of paper in one small level hand and a hundred of the lines have a street and a time and about nine words and not one figure anywhere on four hundred and nineteen lines.*

*Two hundred and nineteen doors gone into since the thirtieth of March. Nine people, twenty-five days, about one a day each across the nine, and a day is how long you stand on a step until a person has finished telling you.*

*The nineteenth is a man carrying a chair up an outside stair every morning for about eleven weeks and not knocking, and the round does not know what it is for, neither does he,, and I am the only person in Ostara who has ever been told and I am not going to be able to find out and I asked for it.*

Then, after a gap:

*I have begun, without noticing, to go and get people.*

*For as long as this office has existed I stood at a counter and waited for a person to bring me a thing they did not have to bring. That is the whole engine and it is the only thing I have ever been proud of.

*And on the twenty-eighth of April at about eleven o'clock at night I sent nine women onto four streets they do not walk to fetch me nineteen strangers out of their own evenings, because I wanted to know whether a page of mine and a page of theirs were the same kind of thing.*

*Nobody will ever know that, and the ruled heading has got nothing under it and is going to keep nothing under it, and she did not ask me for a line and I am not going to invent one.*

He read that back and it was the correct reading of what he had done, and the rule about writing a thing at the moment it is counted applies to counts and this was not a count, and he had written it at about a quarter to five in the morning, which is close to when it happened to him and about five hours after a door.
