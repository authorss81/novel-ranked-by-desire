# The Registrar's Room

The room was off a third court on the floor the Correction Office keeps its registry on, and it had been given to the Register of Unrecorded Merit in the first week of February for as long as the question was not put, and on the eighth of October Lin Hao came down that corridor at about ten in the morning to look at it.

He had a bucket in his hand. He had come off a night and he was going back on at eleven and the stair to the branch is faster with a bucket in it than without one.

There were two chairs and a table.

## What Is In The Room

A lamp on the table with a round base and a flex that had been mended with tape. A window that does not open and has not been cleaned on the outside since before the sitting. A door that shuts all the way, which is the one thing in that corridor a door does.

Under the table, a pigeonhole with two things in it. One is a sheet of foolscap with a heading and one act on it: a man in a delivery coat chalking a figure of merit against the address of a room over a laundry on the twenty-eighth of February, in a lane, on a slate, at about half past midnight.

The other is the same sheet with a second line under it, and the second line is the whole of what this office did in the first eight months of its life, and it is a refusal, and the refusal is in the office's own hand because a Registrar is a clerk who may only enter and cannot improve what he is given.

There is not one other object in the room. Yusuf Adeyemi had not put a shelf up. He had not asked for a second lamp. There is a hook on the back of the door with nothing on it and there has been nothing on it since February.

## Eight Months

He had the bucket down at his feet and he stood in there for about four minutes before he said anything.

"Eight months," he said.

"Eight months and eight days," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "Thirty days in each of eight months and eight days of the ninth. That is two hundred and forty-eight days counted from the first of February with both ends in, and I did it in my head because there is nothing in this room to do it on."

"How many people have come to this door."

"Two."

He said it the way he says everything, which is as a fact about a room.

"One of them was you, on the thirtieth of September. The other was a woman who keeps a slate on her own wall two streets off the chandler's and who wanted to know whether a slate off Cordwain could be produced in answer to a question, and I told her what the answer to that is, and she wrote it down, and that is the whole of the traffic this office has had."

## Five Days After A Stool

"There is a lane at the bottom of the hill that has been there a year and five days," said Lin Hao.

"Three hundred and sixty-six mornings," said Adeyemi, without looking up. "Twenty-eight days left in October including the third, thirty each of November, December, January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August and September, and eight days of October. A year is three hundred and sixty in this city and it is three hundred and sixty-six."

"You worked that out off a slate."

"I worked it out off a sentence in your coat," said Yusuf Adeyemi, "which I did not read, and off the fact that a man who carries for a chandler and does a stair is up at four and was not standing in a lane at four this morning, and I have never seen him in it at all and I have wanted to for about nine months."

## The Man In The Delivery Coat

"You did not tell her."

"I told her the truth, which is that we cannot reach it," said Adeyemi. "She wrote down the words *we cannot reach it* and put the date against them and put my name under it, which I did not ask her to do and could not have stopped her doing."

He put his hand flat on the table and did not move it.

"She came in on the ninth of October. A year and a day ago she would have been in a different lane, and this city is going to get four or five of those a year now, and every one of them is going to come to this door, and I am going to have to say *we cannot reach it* to somebody I have not met."

"There is one more thing in that drawer and it is not an entry."

"There is nothing else in that drawer."

"There is a sheet of foolscap with a box on it at the top and nothing in the box," said Adeyemi. "It is the form the room's line goes on, and it is blank, and it has been in that drawer since the fourth of February, and it is the only place in this city where the answer to your question would go if anybody had written one down."

"You have had a blank form in a drawer for eight months."

"I have had a blank form in a drawer for eight months and I have not once been tempted to fill it in myself," said Yusuf Adeyemi, "and I would like that to be understood as the only professional pride available to me at present."

## The Sentence Due At The End Of The Quarter

"There is a thing about the room," said Adeyemi, "and I have left it until now because I did not want to have it in the first ten minutes."

"Go on."

"The room is not mine. It is in a pool of eleven that the Correction Office lends out, and a loan goes for a quarter and is renewed by a sentence, and the sentence is one line, and the line says what the thing in the room is for."

He turned round a piece of paper on the table that had a box on it at the top with a line ruled under the box.

"I have drafted four hundred and six of those lines in nineteen years in this building," he said. "A line for a room that holds a machine that reads a seal. A line for a room that holds a person who takes objections. A line for a room that holds nothing yet and is to be used for nothing yet, which I wrote in about four seconds in a fortnight of February and which took a man in administration nine months to approve."

"And this one."

"I have not written this one," said Adeyemi. "I could write it in about eleven words and I have not written it, and I have had the paper in that drawer since the fourth of February."

"Why not."

"Because the line is what the room is for, and the room is for the one thing this office may ask one person, and if I write the line then the room is a room for asking him, and in about four years a stranger with a folder reads the line and finds a small office whose entire purpose was to put one question to one man, and there is no second question, because there cannot be a second question."

He put the pen down beside the box.

"And that would be true," said Adeyemi. "I would rather be a room with two entries in a drawer than a sentence that says so."

## What Is Under The Tile

The man at rank one stood with the bucket between his feet and looked at the two chairs for a while.

"You are going to ask me for something in the next ten minutes," he said, "and I am going to say no, and you already know that you are, because you have had the paper in a drawer since the fourth of February and you have not brought it out."

"I have not brought it out."

"I am going to tell you the thing I have not told anybody in this city, and then I am going to ask you for something, and you are going to say no to it, and we can have the rest of the morning."

Adeyemi did not move his hand off the table.

"There is a white tile at the foot of the ranks wall," said Lin Hao, "under the two words, and it has my name on it in chalk and nothing at all under it. I have stood under it about two minutes at a time since the first of January and I did it about sixty-five times by the end of January and I have not asked who put it there, and I am not going to ask you now, and you are not going to find out either."

"What is under it."

"Nothing under it. That is the whole of what I have got and I have been standing on a step over it since the twenty-eighth of January."

Yusuf Adeyemi was quiet for about four seconds, and then he said, "I can open an inquiry into that wall. I have drafted the wording of an inquiry into a wall out of four hundred precedents and I could have it in front of the Board by the thirtieth of December."

"No."

"I am telling you what I can do and not what I would enjoy doing."

"I know what you can do," said Lin Hao. "That is why I told you. If you open it, then in about four years there is a page in a building with a finding on it about a tile, and a finding says somebody put it there, and the only two people in this city who could put it there are me and a boy with a paste brush, and neither of them would have done it, and the page would be about two people who did not do it."

## What He Says He Will Do

He picked the bucket up off the floor and put it on the table, which is not a thing anybody does in a room off a third court, and Adeyemi moved it four inches so it would not be in the way of the paper.

"You are not going to write the line," said Lin Hao. "You have had it in a drawer since the fourth of February and you have written four hundred of them and this one is the first one you have not written, and it is the first one where the answer is about a person and not about a room."

"No."

"So I will write it," said Lin Hao, "and it will be a sentence about what this office registers and not a sentence about what it is for, because a sentence about what it is for is a sentence you can write and a sentence about what it registers is a sentence you cannot write without saying what a thing has to be before it goes in."

He put his thumb on the box at the top of the paper.

"That will have a floor in it."

"It will have a threshold in it," said Lin Hao, "and a threshold under a wall is the oldest instrument in this city and I know that about as well as anybody alive, and I am telling you now, in this room, so that there is a witness to the moment I knew it."

## Eighty-Two Days

"It goes to the Board on the thirtieth of December," said Adeyemi.

"Twenty-two days left in October after the eighth, and thirty of November, and thirty of December. That is eighty-two, and it will be in that drawer for eighty-two days, and on the last day of it you will put it in front of a room and I will read out what this office registers and what it does not, and it will go out at four on the standing sheet with an address at the head of every line."

He got the bucket back off the table.

"And about two hundred people a week in Cordwain read that sheet," he said, "and not one of them will be able to check a word of it, and that is the first instrument of its kind in this city and it is going to be the wrong shape, and I am telling you that on the eighth of October so that you cannot say in about four years that nobody warned you."
