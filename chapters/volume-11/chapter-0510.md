# The First Figure Off A Chalk

The thirtieth of September is the last working day of the quarter and the standing sheet goes out at four in the afternoon, and on that afternoon a man with a title he did not ask for asked him a question in a room off a third court with two chairs in it, and it was the only question the office was allowed to ask, and it had been sitting in a drawer in a letter of the first of February since the beginning of February.

"There is no instrument," said Yusuf Adeyemi, before he asked it, and he wanted that on the front of the conversation and not at the end of it.

"Nothing in that office can be used on a person or on a piece of slate. I cannot compel anybody and I cannot fine a slate, and about four clerks in this city have been asked whether they could put a hand on that lane and three of them have said yes and one of them has not come back with an answer. I had it checked twice."

"I know."

"Then here is what I can do and here is why it is worth something," said Adeyemi.

"I can write a sentence. It goes out at four this afternoon on the sheet, with an address at the head of every line, and any person in this city may read it at a public desk on a working afternoon."

"About two hundred people a week read that sheet in Cordwain. If the Register of Unrecorded Merit states that a figure set up by a private person on a piece of slate is not entered and is not to be relied upon by anybody, then in about a fortnight four of those nine lanes will empty, and not one person will be touched, and nobody will be fined, and it will be the cheapest thing this city has ever done to somebody."

## Why Him

"You have not asked me about the cistern," said Lin Hao.

"No."

"You have not asked me about the four hundred turned lines."

"No."

"You have not asked me about the twenty-eighth of February."

"No," said Adeyemi, "and I have had that one in a drawer on my own for about six weeks and I am not going to ask it, because I have read the sheet of that quarter and it has one entry in it and I know where the entry came from."

He put the letter of the first of February on the table between the two chairs.

"You know what it says."

"It says the office exists and it says my name and it says yours," said Adeyemi.

"You are the only person this office may ask."

"I have no other."

"That has been on paper since the first of February and in eight months I have not used it once, and I have been saving it for something I could not name until this afternoon."

"And now I can name it. It is this: I want to strike nine slates out of this city, and the only man in Ostara who has ever said out loud that anybody is allowed to ask this city a question and get an answer it cannot argue with is sitting in front of me."

## What He Has Done About It Since June

"There is a thing you should have before you answer," said Adeyemi, "and I am going to put it in about four sentences and then I am going to stop talking."

"In the last week of June a woman in a district four days from here lost two months of something on a figure off a slate that she cannot argue with, and the landlord who took it off her has never lied to her, and the only reason anybody knows about it at all is that you went and stood in that district on the evening of the twenty-sixth."

Lin Hao did not say anything.

"There have been at least four of those since the beginning of March and I have found every one of them and I have had them in a drawer since about the seventh of July, and neither of them is a matter this office can reach, and that is not the point." Adeyemi put his finger on the table once.

"The point is that in about four years there will not be two."

"There will be a district for every slate. About a hundred and sixty people will be paying a grain a morning to stand in a cold lane."

"And a landlord in Lamplit Row with a column on a return that is about two hundred grains a quarter out of line with the wall, and a great many people in this city who have been moved by a number that no office in this city can check and has no power over and does not exist on any page."

"That is not a reason to take the slates down. That is a reason to want them."

"It is both," said Adeyemi, "and I have not got the months to be only one of them."

## Why Him, Again

"You do not need me for that," said Lin Hao. "You can write the sentence. It is your sheet."

"It is my sheet and it will not be read," said Yusuf Adeyemi.

"On the tenth of October, in a room that could carry nothing at all, in front of about ninety people, a man at rank one stood at a bench and told a city that the whole job of the Bureau was to be the thing you can ask."

"Not to be right. To be there on an ordinary morning with a counter open and a clerk who may only enter. That went out at four the same afternoon with an address at the head of every line, and a man in a delivery coat has had that sentence on a wall in a lane for four months, and he has not once said the Board took his words off him, and he is right not to."

Adeyemi put both hands flat on the table.

"So if a sentence goes out on that sheet this afternoon saying that a private figure is nothing, then it is not an office that has said it."

"It is a man at rank one who said the thing you can ask and then watched a piece of chalk measure this city for a year. That is what I am asking you to prevent, and I am asking you instead of asking nobody, and I have known since about the tenth of June that I was going to have to."

## What He Said

"Two words went onto that wall off a sheet I answered, and I am not going to take another man's two words off a piece of slate for standing on the wrong surface," said Lin Hao.

He did not say anything else. That was the whole of it. He had said out loud in a room of ninety people once that a room which cannot carry an answer should not be given one, and this room could not carry this one either, and there was not going to be a second sentence or a third one.

## What Adeyemi Did With It

Adeyemi wrote it down.

He wrote it in about nine seconds in a hand that has been setting down minutes since before this office had a name, and then he stopped.

Then he took the pen and put a line under it, and then he wrote a second line, which is the clerk's line and which he wrote because a Registrar is a clerk who may only enter and cannot improve what he is given, and what he had been given in this room was one sentence and he was not permitted to add anything to it.

*The Register of Unrecorded Merit has put to the only person it may ask, whether private figures set up on a slate are to be struck out of this city. The sentence is as follows and is entered whole and is not improved: two words went onto that wall off a sheet I answered, and I am not going to take another man's two words off a piece of slate for standing on the wrong surface. The office has no instrument and no disposition. The answer was no.*

He put the pen down and turned the sheet round so that Lin Hao could read it and not the paper, which is a thing a man does at a counter and not a thing a Registrar does.

"That goes out at four," said Adeyemi.

"Read it in about four years by a stranger with a folder," said Lin Hao, "and he will see a man who could not stop nine slates and would not, and he will have no way at all of knowing that the man could have stopped them by writing one sentence, and that is the correct arrangement and I would not change it."

"You are going to say that you knew," said Lin Hao.

"I have known since about the tenth of June," said Adeyemi, "and I have had a hundred and ten days to find a second man to put the same question to, and there is not one in this city."

"Twenty days left in June after the tenth, and thirty of July, and thirty of August, and thirty of September. And that is not a thing about you. It is a thing about the letter of the first of February, which names one man, and I wrote the letter."

## What It Closed

There is a crease in the shoulder of his coat that was not there on the first of February, and he has not noticed it and will not.

Two things went out of the room with him and he had not asked for either.

The first is that he can no longer be the man who, on the tenth of October, stood at a bench in a room that could carry nothing and told about ninety people that the whole job of the Bureau was to be the thing you can ask.

That was true in October and it is true now and it stops being any use at all to him the moment he stands in a lane off Cordwain on an ordinary evening and watches a private chalk do in nine districts what his city does on one wall.

The second is that he has nothing left. Not nothing of merit — a rank and a plate and a wage, and he chose the wage and he would choose it again. Nothing left of standing.

He is not going to be, in the next four years, a man who was outside this, and he walked down a hill on the first of October and gave the last of it away at a counter with a pen in ninety seconds, and a man with a title has just used the only thing this office was ever able to ask him for, and he has used it saying no.

## Three Days Short Of A Year

At about ten past five he went down the hill, which takes about twenty minutes, and there was a lane at the bottom of it with five stools in it and a slate against the wall and a figure on the slate in four-inch chalk.

The first morning in that lane was the third of October and it is three hundred and fifty-eight mornings now: twenty-eight days left in October including the third, thirty each of November, December, January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August and September, and not one of them missed. Three days short of a year.

The five stools were all taken at ten past five. There was a woman with a pram.

And about two-thirds of the way down the column, in chalk about two inches high with a name beside it in lower letters, there was a nine, and it was the twenty-eighth of February, and it was the first figure of merit he had ever had off anything that was not a reading taken off a person with a clock on it, and it was worth nothing, and he had paid a grain for it, and the grain had cost him nothing.

There were six words in half-size chalk under the whole column that were not there in January, and a room in this city two floors under a sales floor that had a lamp on a stand in it, and neither of the two women who will see those words there is going to be told where they came from.

There were nine slates in nine districts in Ostara on the thirtieth of September of the first year and there were going to be more, and the Bureau of Merit knows about none of them, and the Register of Unrecorded Merit had two entries, and the second one said that the only person it was allowed to ask had been asked and had said no.
