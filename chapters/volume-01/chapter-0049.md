# Four Thousand One Hundred and Twelve

Rank six held through the room, and the corridor repeated it all afternoon the way corridors repeat what they watched happen. That was the sentence the corridor used about it afterwards, and it was accurate in the way corridors are accurate about things they watched happen.

Lin Hao came out of the Review room with a black six on a white tile and a finding slip stamped and signed by nine chairs, and he walked down the stair past the permissions board wall where the queue sheet still had him twenty-second of twenty-two with the word provisional on it, and by the time he reached the pavement a clerk was already up a ladder with a paste pot taking the sheet down.

He went to the Reading Office window the next morning with the finding in his hand and the deposit figure in his pocket, because rank six petitioned sealed files, and he had wanted this since the first week of June.

Behind the window sat the clerk who had put his reading into the machine on the 3rd. She looked at the finding for a long time, front and back, and then at him.

“The query is closed,” she said. “A witnessed answer in the room is an instrument. You hold one now.” She put the finding down. “What file.”

“Lin Wei,” he said. “Reading Office transfers, class. Four thousand one hundred and twelve folios.”

She wrote the number down without looking up, and went out through the back past the guard’s desk and did not hurry, and came back with a docket book and a form with the folio count printed on it.

“Deposit,” she said. “A held rank petitions on the band and not on the figure. Your band held yesterday. The deposit is the form.”

He filled the form in with his name and the file name and the folio count, in his own hand, standing up at the window the way he had stood in the box, and she checked it and stamped it and gave him the counterfoil.

“Come back tomorrow,” she said. “A file of that size is brought up in parts. The index will have it by ten. A trolley with a guard. The seals are checked at the door and again at the table, and the guard signs for each part. You will see the docket first and then the open folio, if there is one, and you will not touch the seals. Those are the rules for a file of this size and they are not mine to bend.”

He asked what the deposit bought besides the form, and she said it bought the room and the trolley and the guard, and the right to sit at the table while the docket was opened. She said a held rank petitioned on the band and the band had held in a room with nine chairs, so the petition stood.

He went back to the Fourth Branch and cleaned the second-floor washrooms properly for the first time in a week, basins and floor and doors, because his hands needed the work and the work did not need his head. The corridor board had been wiped again and the five names were gone from it, struck off by a finding instead of by a declaration this time, with the date of the Review at the top in the clerk’s hand.

The branch was different round him from that morning, and the washrooms smelled of soap instead of damp for the first time in weeks. He felt it in every doorway. Clerks nodded who had never nodded. A porter held a door for him at the plate office, deliberate, meeting his eye. Runa’s cupboard stood open with the table still in it and three chairs, empty, because observation had closed with the finding.

Tobias Renn came down with a box and stopped when he saw the board.

“They are off,” Tobias said.

“They are off by instrument this time. Not by me standing in a corridor.”

Tobias put the box down. “Then I am not third one down on a board any more, and the statement the declarant filed himself is still in the front of a Field file, and there is nothing I can do about that either.” He picked the box up again. “You are six. Held, not provisional. The whole branch knows.”

Tobias shifted the box on his shoulder. The annexe inventory ran to forty boxes and he had carried thirty of them alone, because the deputy would not spare a porter. His hands were raw from the lids and his coat was dusty from the annexe stairs. He asked whether the finding named the declaration, and Lin Hao said it stood at the front of the file with the finding number beside it.

Lin Hao nodded. There was nothing to say that would survive being said, so he did not say it.

He spent the afternoon at the Vestry, because the cold store rack had gone to seventeen and Tessa wanted the number said aloud by the person who ought to hold the count. He stood in the doorway of the cold store with the lamp on its cord and said seventeen, the number and not the names, and she nodded and squared the boards.

Beatrix stopped him on the arcade stairs on the way out. Her three ticks stood, she said. The ninth was her, in September. The promise he broke in the square was hers to name, and she named it without heat. A use and not a man, agreed out loud before anything started, and he had spent on himself. She said the price of that was his to carry and not hers to forgive in a speech, and went back up to the yards.

Nadia caught him at the terminus with her car held at the platform, though she was mid-shift. She had felt the square from the arcade, she said. Four seconds of it through the brake. She was angry and glad at once, the way she always was, and she told him not to spend her, because she was the one thing in the quarter that went up, and then she rang the bell and took the car up one floor, as far as she went.

That night Ines came to his room above the chop-house and sat on the chair while he sat on the bed, and they looked at each other for a while without speaking.

“You spent it on yourself,” she said at last. “In front of nine chairs and sixty witnesses. I was in the back row.”

“Yes.”

“Three turned at once. Nell Ardery. Mr Neve. The checker at the gate.” She said the names levelly. “And a line that says yourself. I have read eleven years of unsettled readings and I have never once seen that line in anybody’s ledger.”

“What happens to it.”

“I do not know.” She looked down at her hands. “No auditor knows, because no man has ever done it in a room with witnesses. The Bureau’s threshold is three in a season and you turned three in four seconds. An analyst could put a dot on a wall map and the dot would be that room.”

He said nothing.

“I am not filing it,” she said. “I cannot decide whether to, which is not the same as deciding not to. That is where I am and I am telling you plainly because you spent yourself in a room to close my query clerk’s query, and the query was mine as much as hers.”

She got up and went to the door, and stopped with her hand on it. The month on the rota name was discharged and the word ungiven, she said. That decision stood outside this room and outside the Review, and tonight was not the night to revisit it. Whether he gave it was not a fact for tonight.

“The file tomorrow,” she said. “Ten o’clock. I will come with you as far as the corridor, because an auditor may stand in a corridor, and no further, because what is in it is yours.”

She left.

He lay on the bed with his finding slip on the table beside the soap he had brought up from the washroom, and he felt the elation and the dread move through him in turns, the way fever moves. Elation, because six was held and not provisional, and the band was on the plate and not on the wall, and no machine could strike it off him now without a room. Dread, because the line that said himself was in the ledger permanently, and three people he knew by name had felt something cold in a room and would carry it.

At midnight he got up and went down to the washroom to check the petition copy drying beside the soap, though it was dry already. The building was quiet. The second floor smelled of soap and damp lino, and his footsteps went down the corridor without an echo. He stood in front of the wiped board a while, empty now except for the date, and then he went back up and slept four hours, the first unbroken sleep since the 17th.

Marguerite sent a note by house runner that night. Three lines. The house had been in the room and had heard the answer. The terms stayed down, signed or unsigned. He folded the note into the envelope with the three sheets and put it in his coat pocket beside the finding.

In the morning he walked to the Exchange by the long road, forty-one minutes, past every door he had counted, and the doors were only doors. Strangers held them for each other and the figure moved wherever he stood, and he let it move, because spending on no one was the only instrument he had left until the file came up.


Ines Duarte had carried a name up a corridor because a drawer is not a place to keep a person, and the finding had closed the query she had watched open with a deed she had no standing to file.

Runa found him at the yard gate at dusk with her hands behind her back. The Field file had closed with the finding, she said. His declaration stood at the front of it, above the observation of the 6th, with the finding number beside it. The five names were off the board by instrument. That part was done and it would hold. He thanked her and she nodded once, professional to the end. He wanted, concretely, to see one folio out of four thousand one hundred and twelve before the week was out — and the index had promised it by ten.
