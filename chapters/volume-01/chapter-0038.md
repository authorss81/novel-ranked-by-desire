# Five Cold Minutes

There are five people in Ostara who have felt what he does and kept track of it, and on the evening of the 20th, with a band on a wall that had not been there at lunchtime, he went and found all five of them.

He did it in one evening because he did not have an evening before the 29th and because a man who is put on a tile under a requisition has stopped being a man you have to look for.

Halvard Rees was the first, because Halvard Rees was at 11 Cutler's Row and 11 Cutler's Row had been emptied at ten past six that morning.

The row was worse than empty. That was the thing he had not been ready for. The warders had taken the doors off four of them and the furniture out of all of them and put it in a yard two streets away under a tarpaulin, and a man at the tarpaulin was writing numbers on boards, and Halvard's roll of wire was on top of a pile with a board against it reading 11B, and Halvard himself was sitting on the wall at the end of the passage with a blanket round his shoulders in twenty degrees.

"You have got a white tile," he said.

"Yes."

"Then you will not be able to come and see the wire."

"Haldar—"

"You do not have to say anything." Halvard Rees had a mug of something out of the works canteen and he was holding it with both hands. "I told you in a cab that week not to do it twice. That is all I said. I did not tell you not to do it at all, and I have had four years to work out that the difference between those two things is the difference between a man and a coward, and I have decided which one I would rather have been."

"It has not been done twice."

"I know it has not." He turned the mug round in his hands. "It has been done forty times. Not to me. I want to say that and I want you to hear it. Not one of them came off me. I have been sitting here since ten past six thinking about that and it is the only thing I have got."

Lin Hao stood in a street with a requisition under his own name and a band on a wall and forty-one people in a ledger and could not think of a single thing to say to a man on a wall.

"Eleven B," Halvard Rees said. "They have written eleven B on my wire. I have lived in that room nine years and I am a letter and a half." He put his head down. "Go on. I know what you have come to say and I have said it first so that you do not have to."

---

Nadia Ferrous was at the Vestry at nine and came up out of the arcade in her uniform and put her hands on the brass of her own car and would not let him in.

"You are six."

"I am six."

"On the wall. In the rain. With the word PROVISIONAL on it, which is worse, because it means the machine is not sure and has decided to be brave about it." She had a headache and it was in her voice and she had it on a Wednesday. "Right. And what I have felt since Thursday morning is a man being extremely careful. Have you worked out what that is? I can feel you being careful. It is the most frightening thing that has ever happened in this lift and I have had eight years of the whole quarter going past it."

"Nadia, I came to—"

"You came to warn me." She hit the brass. "Do not. If you warn me I will have to think about it at six in the morning on an empty car, and I have a good route and a bad route and I do not want a new thing in the middle of it. I have had two of yours since June and a third one is the number that gets somebody a file, and I have worked that out from Ola's diary like everybody else in this quarter, and I am telling you that I know, and I am telling you that I am not going to be careful."

"You cannot afford that. You are the only person who—"

"I am a lift attendant with a plate and a route and a flat," Nadia said, "and if they send me for a reading I will come back, and I have come back from worse, and you have never once asked me whether I can afford anything." The doors went. "Do not spend me. Whatever the band says. Do not spend me, because I am the one thing in this quarter that goes up and I am not going to be turned into a room."

The car went up to the arcade with a sound in the machinery, and he stood at the bottom of the stair.

---

Ola Bright had left her notebook on the landing rail under a saucer, and the door of the flat above the chandler's was open, and the flat was empty.

There were twenty-two new entries in it and every one of them was in her hand and every one of them said the same thing in a different way, and the last page had a line under it that she had written at some point in the afternoon and underlined twice, which is what she did.

The Vestry, week beginning the 18th. Nobody has asked me to do this.

He stood in an empty flat holding a woman's exercise book and understood that he had made a clerk.

---

Teodor Mace would not come out of the counting house office. A clerk said he was at his desk and had been at his desk since the count and would not be disturbed, and Lin Hao stood in a corridor at the Exchange and said his name twice and was refused twice by a man who had a perfectly legitimate reason and had not used any of them.

---

Emrys Vaz was at the yard gate at six the next morning with the stock docket under his arm, and there was no ledger line for him on any page Lin Hao had, because Emrys had never felt one; he had been in a corridor and worked it out on his own.

But Emrys Vaz had a plate. That was what had happened on the 3rd and it had not stopped happening.

"Four hundred and forty-one," Emrys Vaz said. "I have had it eleven days and it has not moved and the man at the gate says that is not unusual for a new plate." He put the docket down on the wall. "And I can only go down. Ask anybody who was in the arcade car in June. So it is four hundred and forty-one going one way and I do not know how far down goes."

"You should have told somebody."

"Tell them what," Emrys Vaz said. "That a man carrying stock up at six went past me in a corridor and could feel me from the Vestry? They would take me and not you."

---

Lantern Row was the last one and it was not on his list, which was the entire point of going there.

Ines had her coat on and a case half packed and the second of May clipboard on the table with a date at the top of it, and she had not asked him, and she was still not asking him, and he stood in the middle of her front room with a band on a wall and a floor under it three grains below where he was and tried to work out how much of a thing he was allowed to say.

He said nothing at all, because he had not slept since Saturday and his hands were not steady and the grey at his left temple had been there since the middle of the afternoon.

And a cold minute went out of him across a room with the door open.

Ines Duarte put her hand flat on the table and held it there, and looked at the door, and then at him, and then at the table.

"That is the third one," she said.

Lin Hao did not say anything.

"The third one since June is the line at which the Bureau stops believing it is a coincidence and starts wanting an address." She took her hand off the table and looked at the palm of it as though the cold had left a mark on it. "And it was not even from you, was it. You did not do anything. You were standing there."

"I know."

"Then where did it come from."
