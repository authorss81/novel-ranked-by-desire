# A Clean Report

The quarterly re-read has a form, and the form is small, and at ten past nine on the 18th a woman with a Wing badge put it on the counter of the Fourth Branch plate office and asked for him by name in a voice that four hundred people on the floor heard.

"Provisional band," she said. "Struck on the 11th. Re-read due at the quarter. You are early."

"How early."

"Eleven days." She wrote a date in the box at the top of the form, and the date was the 29th, which was the date the board in his own corridor had said, and the clerk at the plate office counter looked at it and then looked at the woman. "You have come eleven days early. Is there a reason I should put on the form."

"No," Lin Hao said. "Write eleven days early."

She wrote it, because there was a box for it, and then she asked him to press his plate into the socket beside her, and he did, because a man who refuses to be read in front of four hundred people has made a different kind of document.

The figure came back and he did not look at it before she read it out loud, because she was required to read it out loud and the corridor was required to hear it, and both of those things were in his favour.

"Eight thousand one hundred and forty-one," she said. And then she said, "and the reading is deferred to the 29th by the provisional band, and this form is the receipt, and you are not required to answer anything until the 29th."

He signed it with a Wing clerk's hand watching and a branch floor listening, and the whole of what had happened was that a woman had come early to do a small thing and he had braced for a large one for eleven days, and the four hundred people had watched him stand at a counter and say thank you to an instrument.

That was the whole of the 18th until four in the afternoon.

Runa Sato's report on Cordwain went in at two and came back out of the district office at half past three with a stamp on it, and Lin Hao had a copy by six because she left one on the rail of the second-floor corridor without a word, face down, under a rail clip, the way you leave something for somebody who is not supposed to have it.

He read it standing up in the stairwell.

It was four pages and it was entirely accurate and his name was on it twice and neither of them was about him.

Subject: premises, 31 dwellings, Cutler's Row, Cordwain. Unexplained district adjustment, eleven consecutive weekly returns, initials against the line marked ADJ-DT. Total removed, resident level, 2,140 grains over eleven weeks, load-bearing on one address. Finding: an adjustment applied to residential plates to reconcile a district shortfall of approximately 300 a week against a population of approximately 9,000. Finding: the same line has appeared against four other addresses in the district in nine weeks, none of them his. Recommendation: district office to produce the working for ADJ-DT, or to explain its absence.

And in the margin of page three, in Runa's own hand, the only sentence that was in any way about him:

The account under observation is not the subject of this report and is not relied on in it. The premises were executed against because an address on the observed arc required a subject. That is a defect in the method and I record it as one.

Lin Hao read that four times.

A Field report had gone into an office in his name, on the 6th, and had come back with four words in it, and it was the only document in his life that had ever been about him and been kind to him, and it had got there because the woman who wrote it did not want to be proved right by him. It was not a kindness. She had put the sentence in because it was true and because it was the sentence that would get the working produced, and the effect of the two was that Lin Hao did not appear in it.

That was the first time anything had ever done that for him and he felt sick about it, and he could not have said whether he was sick because he was not in it or because he had spent eight days building a plan around the assumption that he would be.

She found him on the stair at seven.

"The margin."

"You said you were not going to tell me what you are not writing."

"I am not." She came up two steps and stopped below him, which put her at his eye level for once. "I am going to tell you what I am. It took nine weeks and two pages and it is the only report I have filed this year that came back with a stamp on it and no question attached, and I want you to understand what that is worth, because I do not think you can see it from where you are standing."

"It got the adjustment stopped."

"It got the working produced. That is a different thing from stopped, and in nine weeks it will be stopped, and in nine weeks somebody will produce a document explaining ADJ-DT in language that a person can read and a machine cannot." Runa Sato put her hands in her coat.

"And it will have your plate number on the requisition, and it will have the row out of its flats at six tomorrow morning, and those two facts are going to be in the same file for the rest of my working life, and if you stand next to me in that file I am finished. So the margin is not a favour to you. It is me, buying room, at a price you did not have to be told about."

He believed her and it did not help at all.

"Six o'clock," he said.

"Six o'clock. Eleven warders and me and a clerk from the district office and a man from the works who has a key to the yard." She went down three steps and stopped again. "Your name is on the order. Not as a witness. As the subject, present by attestation on the 3rd. The district office requires an employer to attest that a subject is present and the Fourth Branch is the employer, so it is coming here this evening to be signed and it will be served at the Fourth Branch and not at the district office, and I have not had a chance to tell you that before now."

"Then where does that leave me."

"It leaves you in a building at six o'clock tomorrow morning with a paper about you that is going out to thirty-one households." Runa Sato went the rest of the way down without turning round.

"One more thing and then it is your evening. I did not write the sentence about the defect in my own method. It is in there because a supervisor countersigned my observation and read it, and he wrote *noted* against it, and the word noted on a Field report is the strongest thing in Ostara. He has admitted on paper that the method is defective, he cannot now unsee it, and he will do it again next quarter with somebody else."

The order was served at the Fourth Branch at twenty past six, on the second-floor corridor, by a district-office courier who had walked it round on foot because it was one page and there was no reason to post it.

The first signature was the executing officer's. The second was in the attestation box, and it was in the courier's hand and the ink was fresh and the name was Tobias Renn.

The warders' clerk had required an employer to attest that the subject was present, and the subject had been attested present on the 3rd in the front of a witness book on a lectern by the plate office door, and the witness book had been taken down and the plates struck and the ledger new, and there was one man left in the filing annexe on the fourth floor of the Fourth Branch who could find a document from the 3rd, and he had found it, and he had put his name against it without being asked to by anybody, and he had done it in courier ink in eight seconds.

Lin Hao read the order twice.

He went up to the fourth floor and Tobias Renn was in the annexe with a stack of boxes and his coat on, and the corridor outside was dark except for the light over the stair, and he did not turn round when the door went.

"You are off the register," Lin Hao said.

"I am."

"There is no entitlement on that form. A requisition served on an employer asks a person in the filing office to attest a presence, and the person in the filing office is the clerk on the register, and you are not on the register, and there is nobody above you in that room who has been here long enough to know where the 3rd is."

"I know where the 3rd is," Tobias Renn said.

He put a lid on a box and pushed it along the floor and put another lid on and pushed that one too, and did not look up, and his voice was the voice of a man reading out a filing and not a speech.

"I have been filing here since I came in and I have been off the register for eight days and I have not been replaced and there has been a requisition order in that building for six hours and nobody came to find me, so either they cannot see me or they can." He straightened up. "That is not a favour. I want it written that it is not a favour and I want it written before anybody in that building decides that it is. I did it because I know where the 3rd is."

"You did it because I asked you for nothing."

"You did ask me for nothing," Tobias Renn said. "In a corridor on the 3rd you asked me nothing. I had a witness line and I took it off you and I filed myself into the boxes for it, and then you made a speech in front of ninety-one people that was worth four thousand grains to the floor and one ruined clerk, and this week a woman has come to the Fourth Branch with a paper about you and the only man who can find out where you were standing on the 3rd is me."

He put his hand flat on the top box.

"You have got nothing to say to me and I have got nothing to say to you," he said, "and I would like that to be the arrangement, and I would like you to be aware that there is a courier's receipt downstairs with my name in the employer box of a requisition that empties thirty-one flats at six o'clock in the morning, and I did not think about that for more than eight seconds, and that is exactly as long as it took."
