# Signal

The week after the yards he laid Beatrix's four years on his table next to his own count and found they did not agree.

She counted effects. Cold minutes, cars, hours, clusters, the word headache in the margin where the night had been too loud. He held addresses. Faces, streets, hours, sixteen lines and three turned, people he had stood near on trams and in queues. When the two were laid on top of each other, the map made a shape neither of them could say out loud at first.

The unrecorded good in the Slough and the Vestry was not spread across the city. It was in one arc between the yards and the free clinics, and it moved along it four times a day, morning and evening, with the shifts.

He understood it sitting on his floor with pages all round him, with the long accumulating history of four years of pencil going under his hands. There was a geography to the account. It was not a private accident. It fed where people were kind in doorways, and doorways on that arc were the busiest in Ostara.

The geography meant the only man in Ostara fed by the whole of the Slough was a man who commuted the Ferrier line, which he did because his rent was eleven minutes' walk from the stop. His poverty was a power stat. His invisibility had an address.

He felt elation and then shame, together, because the hoard read as the correct choice for the first time and it was still wrong. He was deliberately, openly increasing what he took, and he could not defend it, and he could not stop, because the alternative was that the Slough stopped being kind in doorways, which was worse.

So he started taking the tram the long way on purpose, every day, in both directions, riding end to end to feed the lines, and Ines found out within four days because an auditor notices a pattern in a man's commuting and cannot help herself.

"You've inverted it," she said, standing in his rooms with her coat on. "You're farming it."

"I'm keeping it moving."

"You're hoarding it bigger." She put her hand flat on the map. "Lin. Nineteen lines now. Three turned. All located. You have a map nobody can intercept and you're using it to get hungrier."

He knew she was right. He wanted the audit off ninety-one people, concretely, with a date, and every long ride bought distance from that and cost cold. His want was plain and felt and it did not excuse him.

Beatrix brought him the week's plate figures herself, coming off shift with grease on her cuffs, and showed him two hundred grains on the slip where four hundred should have been.

"That's the week," she said. "Two hundred and not enough. There's an audit in five days and you're short and I'm short and the whole arc is short. You need another interchange and you hate needing it. I can see it on your face."

He walked her to the depot gate and watched her go in, with her book under her arm and her deaf side to the traffic, leaning in to hear the shunter, joking about it even then, and he stood in the rain and felt the map in his coat like a weight.

The account had a shape. The shape had an address. The address was him.
