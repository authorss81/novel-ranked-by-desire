# The Winter Queue

The board on the second floor of the Fourth Branch had a list on it again on the morning of the 26th, and the list was five names long, and every one of them had been struck off that board in the same pen on the same afternoon in March.

It had not come down. That was the part Lin Hao stood and looked at for a while. The line in the clerk's hand that had said no further action this quarter had been taken off the board and its place had not been filled, so the top of the list was not a decision at all; it was a gap with five names underneath it and a space where the reason should have been.

Ottoline Barret. Emrys Vaz. Tobias Renn, and two he had never asked the names of.

Tobias Renn was the third one down and he had been at the Fourth Branch since half past seven in the morning putting lids on boxes in the annexe on the fourth floor, and he came out for his lunch at one, read the board from about twelve feet away with his plate in his hand, and went back up without eating anything.

The ninety-one are back on it by the fourteenth week of the quarter. That is the timetable and it is printed, and a man who cleans the second-floor washrooms can tell you what his own standing costs a colleague on the fourth floor who has put four true lines in the front of his own file.

The Exchange had a sheet up on the permissions board wall that had not been there on Thursday, and by twenty past nine on the 26th there were about sixty people standing in front of it and Runa Sato was at the back of them with her hands behind her back.

WINTER REVIEW — CANDIDATES. RANK SIX.
Seats: as many as are seated. Ordered by published standing.

Twenty-two names.

Lin Hao read it twice before he read the names. The first time he read the shape of it and the second time he read his own, which was the twenty-second and the last, and which had three things on it that none of the twenty-one above it had.

LIN HAO. PROVISIONAL SIX. FLOOR 8,141. TOLERANCE 3 GRAINS. LAST WITNESSED DEED, 19TH, CORDWAIN.

The provisional was why he was on the sheet at all. He understood that within about four seconds of standing there, and it was the whole of the thing in one word.

[Winter Review queue published. Provisional band is not carried into review. Band six opens at 20,000. Published standing 8,141.]

He did the arithmetic on the pavement with his left forearm still in a shirt sling and about four hundred people going past him into the Exchange, and it took him longer than it should have because he kept having to do it again.

Twenty thousand. The band six figure had been on the table in the correction office and in his own head since the first week of June. He had 8,141 published on a wall in the largest type in the city, which was nineteen people and four years of doors, and the distance between those two numbers was eleven thousand eight hundred and fifty-nine grains, and at the interchange on the 23rd of last month a deed in front of two hundred witnesses had scored four hundred.

Eleven eight hundred and fifty-nine, divided by four hundred, is twenty-nine and a bit.

Thirty witnessed deeds in six weeks. He had nineteen people in the world and no route to a twentieth, and a deed had to be public, and a public deed had to be something he chose rather than something that happened to a stranger on a tram, and there is no life in which a man who cleans washrooms finds thirty of them.

So he stood on a pavement and understood the whole shape of the next six weeks at once, which is that six was a thing that had been done to him by a machine on the 19th in the rain and it was going to be undone by the same machine on a Thursday, and that the number of grains between where he was and where a Review would put him was eleven thousand eight hundred and fifty-nine.

"And three," he said.

"That is the other way round," said Runa Sato. She had been behind him for about two minutes. "Three grains is how close you are to losing it. Eleven thousand eight hundred and fifty-nine is how far you are from having it. You have been carrying those two numbers around since the nineteenth and they do not mean the same thing, and every man I have watched get this wrong has made the mistake in the same direction."

"What does the Review actually do."

"It strikes the band off the figure on the day, in a room, from the plate, with a clerk reading it out." She nodded at the sheet. "There is no provisional anything in that room. It takes the number you have got and it puts it against the table, and 8,141 is a five, and you go back to being a man who cleans washrooms with a week of notice and a hundred and twenty people in the queue."

She reached past him and put one finger on the last line of the sheet, which was printed small and at the bottom, the way the Bureau prints the things it wants read by the people who already know.

A PROVISIONAL BAND IS NOT CARRIED INTO REVIEW. A CANDIDATE IS SEATED ON PUBLISHED STANDING AT THE DATE OF THE REVIEW.

"That is new," Lin Hao said. "Is that new?"

"That is from the 24th." Runa Sato took her hand off the sheet. "It was not on there on Thursday. It went up two days ago and it is the only reason I am standing in a queue at nine in the morning on a pavement instead of sleeping, because somebody in an office has read a document I filed and has decided that a band struck on a requisition of a building is not a band."

"Your document."

"My file, your hand." She put her hands back behind her back. "Mr Hartle pulled the register entry on the 24th at eleven, as I said he would, and a clerk in the Wing read the thing you wrote at half past ten at night on the 23rd, and by the afternoon of the 24th the Reading Office had a query on your standing that is still open, and a query is a machine asking itself how a provisional figure got into the queue, and the answer has to be an instrument, and there is no instrument in this city for a thing that happened because a machine needed a rank-six act to hang a declaration on."

"So the queue is how they do it quietly."

"The queue is how they do it publicly in eleven days' time when the Review convenes and you are not in it." She said it without heat. "And I want you to hear the part that is not about me, which is that the five names went back on that board on the same afternoon, and the Reading Office would not touch your entry if it were not for Emrys Vaz, and Emrys Vaz is a boy at a yard gate who was handed a plate on the morning of the 3rd and has never once asked anybody for anything."

"I know."

"You filed it yourself."

"I filed it myself," Lin Hao said. "You gave me the form. That is not the same as being able to defend it to a clerk in the Reading Office, and I have been to this counter twice and read documents since June and I am going to get it wrong."

"You are going to get it wrong in a room," Runa Sato said, "in the sixth week, in front of a clerk and a panel and every person on this list, and there is no version of getting it right that does not cost somebody, and I have watched you work that out four times in three weeks, so I am not going to insult you by pretending I do not know how it ends."

She went back to the back of the queue.

There were four other people in the crowd he knew by face. A woman from the supply floor who had been a five for nine years. Two men from the Reading Office who did not look at anybody. And a clerk from the plate office on the first floor, who had been the clerk behind the half-height screen on the 3rd, and who was standing at the end of the queue with her arms folded and reading the last name on the sheet.

She caught him looking at her and did not pretend she had not.

"Provisional," she said. "I have entered readings for nineteen years and I have never once seen that word go up on a board that was going into a Review, and I put your reading into the machine on the third and I have had two days to think about it." She unfolded her arms. "A figure that cannot settle cannot be banded, so the Office fell back on the raw number, and the raw number is the exact figure of an account that nineteen separate people are inside, and it is now on a tile in the largest type in Ostara."

"Yes," Lin Hao said.

"I have never once seen that machine ask anybody for anything," she said, and did not unfold her arms. "It takes what is on the desk and it goes and finds a rank for it, and on your desk there was a figure with no rank behind it, so it made one. And there is a query on it now, which is mine, because I entered it, and I am going to have to answer it, and the only answer anybody in the Reading Office will accept is a figure that came from an instrument, and you have not got one, and I cannot invent you one."

"What happens if you cannot."

"Then your entry comes off the sheet on the morning of the Review and you are not in the room." She tipped her head at the wall. "And you will already know that, because you have been standing here for ten minutes."

He went and looked at the sheet again.

Eleven thousand eight hundred and fifty-nine grains. Six weeks. Twenty-nine deeds at four hundred each and a fraction, and he had nobody to make them on and could not buy one, and the only person who had ever offered to buy one for him had written REFUSED across the top of a page in Bellhouse on the 13th and initialled it, and had said out loud in a room that she would rather have the date than the deal.

And on the twenty-ninth, three days from then, the declared unsettled reading he had put on a public board in the second week of June would be read again by somebody, and the three-grain tolerance that was the only thing between him and a rank he had bought by accident would be measured against whatever figure was on the wall by then.

At the plate office counter in the fourth week of last month he had offered the Correction Office four thousand one hundred and twelve folios and been told to come back when he had the deposit.

He had three grains.

He stood in front of a frame on the Exchange wall for a while longer and let people go past him into the building, and then he walked back across the Reading Square in the rain to the Fourth Branch, which took him forty-one minutes, and went up to the second floor, and stood in front of a board in his own corridor with five names on it and a gap at the top where the reason should have been.

EMRYS VAZ was the second one down.

A clerk's board carries a figure against every unsettled name, because the figure is the only part of a reading that can be written down, and the one against his said four hundred and forty-one, and at the top of the list, in the same upright print, was the date the next reading fell on.

The twenty-ninth.

He stood there with his left forearm in a shirt and three days and two hours until it, and worked out that the only number in this city he could still move was one he was not permitted to spend, and that it belonged to a boy who carried branch stock up from a yard at six in the morning and had asked him for nothing at all.
