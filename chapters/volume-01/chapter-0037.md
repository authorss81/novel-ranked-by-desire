# The Provisional Band

He wrote the statement at half past two in the morning at the kitchen table in Ferrier Street with his left forearm in a sling made out of a shirt, and it took four pages and he did not soften one of them.

It said that on the 15th he had walked the arc from the yards to the free clinics on purpose and had known what the account did when he stood in it. It said that on the 16th he had been told about an address in Cordwain and had said nothing about it to the Field, the district office, or the Fourth Branch. It said that on the 17th he had gone into Cutler's Row without order and without authority and had gone down the back passage with a man who lives at number 11.

It said that the stair had been reported twice and forced twice and that he had known both of those by half past four on the 17th and had not put either of them in writing anywhere.

It said that the account standing against Cutler's Row came from him.

It said that every dwelling that came off that row after the 19th was his to answer for, and that he would rather be the answer than be an accident in a street in the rain.

Then he signed it, and dated it the 19th, and walked it to the district office at twenty to six because the district office opens at six and he wanted it in the box before the warders came back down the row.

---

Garrick Neve's counter had a queue in it again on the morning of the 20th, and it was a longer queue than the first morning, and Lin Hao understood in about four seconds that a standing reading that has been on a Board for a week attracts a queue, and that the queue would be larger every week, and that there was no version of the next year where it went back to being nine people and a clerk.

He read the plate out. Eight thousand one hundred and forty-one grains, interval unsettled. The clerk read it back.

Then Garrick Neve put the index down on the rail and turned it round, which was not a thing he had done before, and said:

"Where does the movement come from."

That was the question. He had said on the 11th that the index would ask about the figure and where the movement came from, or about the figure not moving and what held it still, and that there was a third family he would not read to him.

It was the first of the two.

There were about forty people at the rail and the rail is a brass rail in a marble hall and sound in it goes everywhere.

Lin Hao stood there for about four seconds and understood that he had three options and that two of them were sentences other people had already said about him in public.

"A man who cleans washrooms is standing in a place where strangers hold doors for each other four times a day," he said. "I did not do any of it. I stood in it."

Garrick Neve wrote it down. He wrote all of it, in the index, in the public column, against Lin Hao, with a date, and then he read it back the way he read everything back, and the hall heard it.

"That is the answer to the question," he said. "It is not a good answer and it is a true one and it is now in here, and it will be here next week, and the next question is not mine."

---

The Board was repainted by ten and he went to look at it in the rain the way a man goes to look at a thing he has done.

The tile had not moved. LIN HAO, thirty-eighth of ninety-one, white, black five, four figures under the name and the fifth of them the number, all of it exactly where it had been since the 10th.

Under all of it there was a sixth line, and it was in the Board's own upright print and it was the same size as the rank.

PROVISIONAL SIX. STRUCK ON A REQUISITION OF PROPERTY. CORDWAIN. 19TH.

He stood and read it four times and then read the two small lines under it, which is where the whole thing was, and which is the part the Board always prints in a smaller type because the Board does not want a crowd to get there in one reading.

FLOOR 8,141, BEING THE FIGURE PUBLISHED AT THE LAST PUBLICATION.

TOLERANCE THREE GRAINS. BELOW THE FLOOR LESS TOLERANCE THE BAND LAPSES AT THE NEXT PRESS.

He worked it out standing in the rain with about three hundred other people inside the rail.

He was not six because he had earned six. He was six because the machine had found a rank-six act — requisition of property, which is the verb that rank six carries and no rank below it has — and had attached it to the only account in Ostara that had a name on it and a declared reading and a statement on a desk in a district office at twenty to six that morning. Four thousand five hundred words of his own handwriting, and the band sat on top of it like a lid.

[Provisional band struck. Six, pending quarterly re-read. Floor 8,141, tolerance three grains.]

Rank six petitions sealed files. Rank six carries warrants, requisition of property, and compelled testimony. He had wanted that number since he was fifteen years old and had spent ten years not being it, and it had arrived at ten past ten in the morning on a wall in the rain because a woman had signed a piece of paper in a stairwell about a door that had been forced twice.

Three grains.

He had an account that had been standing at eight thousand one hundred and forty-one since the 3rd and had been running all summer. He had been away from it for a fortnight — a fortnight walking forty-one minutes instead of riding, and two weeks of not being in the places where the mass comes from, and an arm, and a flat in Cordwain, and the account had still moved, because it moves wherever he stands.

It was going to be a great deal higher than the floor by now, and that was worth nothing at all. The floor was a number, struck once, on the 19th, off the figure the Board published on the 10th. It was not going to move when he earned. It was going to sit at 8,141 for a quarter like a fixed stone in a river.

Below the floor less tolerance the band lapsed at the next press.

8,138.

He could lose four grains and lose a rank. He could not spend four hundred. He could not spend nineteen. He could not put his hand on one single person in this city for the rest of the quarter, and the quarter ended on the 29th, and on the 29th it would be struck again off whatever figure the Board had published, and then it would be the same three grains again.

---

He read the same three lines on the tile for a third time and then read the Board for what a man gets, which is the part nobody stands in the rain for.

Rank six carried three verbs. Requisition of property he had already watched happen. Warrants, which meant a door and a piece of paper and an officer to stand in it. And compelled testimony, which meant that if a person had a thing that belonged to another person, he could require them to say so in a room, under a seal, and they could not decline, and a refusal was itself an offence against the office that took the statement.

He stood there and thought about Halvard Rees at eleven Cutler's Row and about a padlock hanging open in a staple and about a woman on the second floor who had chained that door twice and had not said a word to anybody.

He could make her testify. He could put a form in front of her this afternoon and she would have to put what she saw on it, and the warders would be found, and the report Runa Sato had signed with her own hand twice would be a false document in a file with his name on it, and that would be the price of it.

It was the first time in his life that there had been a legal thing he could do to another person and it had arrived by accident on a Thursday in the rain and the correct use of it would have been against him.

He read the tile once more and went to find out what six cost.

He went down to the Correction Office at four in the afternoon with a slip of paper and stood at the petition rail for about twenty minutes behind eight other people until it came round to him, and the man behind the rail was not unkind and had been doing it for a long time.

"Rank," the man said.

"Six."

The man's hand went out to the left, towards the rack, and then stopped, and came back.

"Provisional?"

"Provisional," Lin Hao said. "Struck on the 19th. Cordwain. It is on the tile."

"That is the first one of those I have had up in nine years." The man wrote a six on the slip and then stopped writing and read the slip again. "Sealed file. Which one."

"Lin Wei. Reading Office transfers, classified. It is in my own file as a cross-reference and I have never seen the file itself."

"That is not a description, that is a rumour," the man said cheerfully, and stamped the slip, and then said, "Deposit for the search. Held by this office and returned in full if the petition fails."

"How much."

"Depends entirely on the file."

"I know how much it is," Lin Hao said. "It is four thousand one hundred and twelve folios. I do not know that, but I know that a file that size costs more than three grains to look at, and I know exactly what I have, and I have three."

The man at the rail looked at him over the slip for a second and then put it in the tray.

"Well," he said. "Come back when you have it."

---

Lin Hao walked up the long step out of the Correction Office into the wet with his left arm in a shirt and his figure on a wall four hundred yards behind him, and it was the first thing he had wanted since he was fifteen, and he had it, and he could not pay for it.

Nell Ardery had lost two thousand one hundred and forty grains in eleven weeks to a district return with a set of initials against a line that said ADJ-DT, and it had taken everything she had and left her band where it was.

He had three grains of room above nothing at all.

He was six, and the reason he was six was a statement he had written at half past two in the morning in which he had put his own name under a stair, and if he had not put his name under it he would still be five, and at five he would have been able to help anybody he liked.
