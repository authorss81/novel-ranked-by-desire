# A Woman With a File

The deputy's office had a door that closed. That was the first thing Lin Hao noticed, standing in it at nineteen with his new band still strange in his pocket, because no room he cleaned had one.

Cassius Rhee was forty-six and rank five and losing ground to men half his age with better files, and his district was nine hundred and eighty grains short per head per week and he had been losing that ground for two years. He was not a monster and did not behave like one. He offered Lin Hao a chair, and Lin Hao sat down because he had never been asked to sit in that building and did not know what else to do with the offer.

"I've known for eleven days," Rhee said. "I've read every plate you've submitted. I'm not going to file anything, because a filed supply is a supply somebody else owns."

He laid the arithmetic out, plainly, correctly. A portable merit source inside a branch was a stabilisation. A stabilised district was nine thousand people who did not get moved. Lin Hao, ranked and held inside the Fourth Branch, could carry the shortfall without a form, without a requisition, without the Classification Wing ever opening a room.

He wanted him employed. Not punished. Employed, with a door that closed and a roster and a quiet arrangement, and nine thousand people upright because of it.

Lin Hao felt the vertigo of being recruited. He had spent four years being looked through and the worst thing that had ever happened to him was a man in a good coat being kind to him, and he sat in the chair and understood that kindness with a seal behind it was still a hand on his shoulder.

He did not take the job and did not refuse it outright, because a man who says no to the only person in the building offering him anything gets nothing at all. He asked for one thing. The audit. Nine days for ninety-one people, deferred, re-typed, moved off the list.

Rhee granted it in about eleven seconds. It cost Lin Hao everything he was going to use the audit for, because the audit was the only clock he had, and buying the branch a fortnight bought him nothing but a longer wait with a band he had paid cold for.

He wanted the audit off ninety-one people. That was the true want, concrete and felt, more than rank six, more than the count of turned lines under a number he had never told anyone. He wanted Ditta Halse off a bench and Emrys Vaz off a deleted rota and Bernt Halse out of a room with no window, and he had just traded his only urgency for it.

He went out into the corridor with his hands shaking and the door closing behind him, and there at the end of the corridor, under the lamp, stood a woman he had never seen before.

She was twenty-nine, broad-shouldered, with her hair cut short and a file under her arm and a coat that hung like a uniform, and she looked at him before she looked at Rhee's door. The file had a wax seal on it that Lin Hao had only ever seen on one other thing in his life, on his brother's cross-reference card.

"Lin Hao," Runa Sato said. "Field. I have an order and I have a file, and both of them have your name on them."

Somebody with a seal had chosen him.
