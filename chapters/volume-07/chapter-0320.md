# The List Of Persons At An Address

The ordinary stream left the Classification Wing at four o'clock on the afternoon of the thirtieth of October in a leather tube with a number on the outside of it, and nobody in Ferrier Street was told, and a runner came up the sixty-one steps at ten past five with a slip of buff paper and a receipt and nothing else.

The receipt said that an instrument had gone out on the ordinary stream at four o'clock on the thirtieth, gave a number, gave a time, and had a signature at the foot of it that was not Alma Quayle's.

"That is the whole of what you get," said the runner. "You get that it went. You do not get what it says."

"And until when."

"The whole of this city, at nine tomorrow morning." He went down four steps, which is all of them.

Nadia Ferrous read the receipt over his shoulder and gave it back.

"Tomorrow," she said. "There is no thirty-first of October and there has never been one, and tomorrow is the next working morning and the counter opens at nine, and I am not going to say the day out loud either, because I have been handed forms in this city since I was a child and I know what a thing like that does to a floor of a building."

Sun Meiling stood in front of the shelf over the sink for a while.

"That is the first time," she said, "that anything has ever gone out of this room with my address on it and not come back."

It did not come back. It was not going to come back. Nobody on the sixty-one steps ever heard of it again.

## Nine O'Clock

The Field counter at Cordwain opens at nine and there was a queue at half past eight, and a man at the back of it who had come down from the Fourth Branch with a clean collar on and a fortnight's sleep in him and nothing on him at all except an address written on a card in his own handwriting.

He got to the grille at two minutes past nine.

Runa Sato was on the desk.

The instrument was lying on it, squared, under a weight, and it had been lying there since twenty to eight, and she had read it once at twenty to eight and then done four other things with her hands to stop herself reading it again.

"What can I do for you."

"I would like a copy of the list of persons at premises thirty-four six one one eighteen, Ferrier Street," said Lin Hao.

She looked at him for the better part of four seconds and did not put a hand flat on anything.

"Reason."

"There is no reason required."

"Then I will not enter one." She opened a book and wrote the time in it and did not write a reason under it. "Is it for you."

"No."

"Then I cannot tell you that, and I am not going to ask, because the form does not ask." She took the sheet off the weight, squared it again, and pushed it under the grille, and her voice stayed exactly where it was. "That is the first one out of the tube. The rest are in the divisions by this afternoon and a copy goes on the street every morning for a week."

He took it and stood there at the counter with a piece of paper in his hand in a queue of nine people, and he read it.

It was an ordinary morning at the Field counter. Two women before him wanted a return on a garage in the yards and one man wanted to know whether a person of reading age who had moved in the spring had to be put back on a list. The man had a piece of paper folded into four in his fist and he did not open it.

"You have to tell me which division he works for."

"He does not work for anybody."

"Then he is not on any list a division keeps, and the answer is no, and there is a form for that and it is in the book on your left."

"Right. Thanks."

## The Instrument

*Composition of premises 34-6-118, Ferrier Street. Persons of reading age at the above premises on the morning of the thirtieth of October. In the order of the stair.*

*Figure: eight.*

1. **MEILING, Sun.** Top floor, kitchen.
2. **SATO, Runa.** Top floor, rear.
3. **ILYIN, Petra.** First floor, front.
4. **KALLO, Wren.** Second floor, fourth door. Counter-forensics, Bureau of Merit.
5. **HALM, Beatrix.** Second floor, second door.
6. **FERROUS, Nadia.** Third floor, first door.
7. **MARCHETTI, Isolde.** Third floor, last door.
8. **LINDQVIST, Agnete.** Ground floor, by the street door.

*There is a date against every line. The dates are the days the eight of them took that address, entered in eight hands in a kitchen on the twenty-fourth of this month and checked by counter-forensics on the twenty-ninth.*

*This instrument is a public record. Any division may read it. No reason is required.*

He read it twice at the counter and then put it flat on the ledge with his palm on it, which is a thing he has done to about nine pieces of paper in his life.

"Marchetti," said Runa Sato.

"I am not going to say anything about her and you are not going to make me and there is a queue behind you." She turned the book toward herself and shut it on her thumb. "Go and have your tea. You have been standing in a queue for about four minutes and that was correct and I would have put you behind two other men."

He stayed where he was.

"Lin Hao."

"You have your copy. Nine of them want a queue ticket and two of them are looking at you."

"I am asking one thing and then I am going," he said. "Is it going to stop."

"No."

"That is not a thing you can know."

"I know exactly that. I was answering orders on that wall before the ones on it were this length, and I have watched four hundred instruments work," said Runa Sato.

"That thing is a list. It is not a judgement and it is not a charge, and it does not say a word about any of them except where they sleep."

"It is going to be matched against every book in this city that keeps a public list. Every match is an ordinary clerk doing ordinary work at an ordinary desk, and every one of them will be correct, and not one of them will be thinking about anybody at all."

"Eleven divisions."

"Eleven this morning. Fourteen by December, if the stream runs the way it runs." She did not soften a word of it, and she kept her eyes on him while she said it.

"And here is the part you are going to hate, so I am going to say it once and quickly."

"It cannot be stopped, and it cannot be unpicked, and it cannot be argued with, and that is not because it is a weapon. It is a piece of paper that says where eight people sleep."

"The only way the world has ever dealt with that is to stop writing it down."

## The Man With No Reason

At eleven minutes past nine the queue had gone down to two and a man came up to the counter with a broker's card on a chain and no coat worth mentioning.

"Copy of a composition list," he said. "Ferrier Street, thirty-four six one one eighteen. House matter."

"Any list out today is out to any division," said Runa Sato. "What house."

"Vale and Son, brokers. Merchant house, Cordwain." He gave her the card and she wrote the house on it and pushed the card back. "It will be in the file by noon."

She reached under the weight without any particular speed and gave him the fair copy, and he folded it once and put it inside his coat, and that was the entire transaction, and neither of them said one word about the man who was still standing at the counter.

Runa Sato opened the book and wrote it in.

*9.11. Copy issued, list of persons at premises 34-6-118. Division: Vale and Son, brokers. No reason given.*

She read the line back and it was correct and there was nothing on it to be sorry about and she shut the book.

At twenty past nine a second man came and it was a guild house off Cordwain and he gave no reason either.

At half past nine a third, and that one was a clerk from a district office on the far side of the river who had walked twenty minutes to ask for it and had been told to ask for it and did not know why.

Runa Sato wrote three lines in the book in three different hands of the day and none of the three had a reason against it, and between the line she had written at two minutes past and the one at half past nine there was nothing on that page at all except twenty-eight minutes.

"That is the whole thing," she said, to nobody in particular, with the book shut and her palm flat on the cover of it.

"That is what a list does. It does not have to be cruel. It does not even have to be used. It only has to be there, and then a division matches it against a book in the ordinary way, and every match is somebody doing their job properly, and not one of them is going to think about the seventh name on that page."

"What is the seventh name."

"Isolde Marchetti, and you knew that." She got up and opened the shutter the Field keeps half down over a counter. "And there will be fifteen copies of it in four months and none of them with a reason against any of them, and about half of them will be in offices where nobody has ever heard of any of you, and that is not a threat and it is not a mistake. It is what happens when an instrument is well made and does what it says."

At about four minutes to ten she pushed the book across the counter to him, flat, with one finger on it, the way somebody pushes a thing at another person in a queue.

"Nadia Ferrous's name is the sixth one down," she said. "You have got it and you did not need a reason and you did not have to explain yourself to a soul, and that is the gift and it is the whole of the gift." She took her finger off the page. "And by the end of the day there will be four more copies of that name in four more buildings, and Nadia Ferrous has never in her life been on more than one."
