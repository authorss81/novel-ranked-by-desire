# The Price Of A Term

He told them at about half past eight on the evening of the twenty-ninth, with the table not cleared, and with the certificate still on the shelf over the sink where it had been since the first of September.

He told it in the order he had made it, because there was a version of this where he made it better and he had promised himself in a corridor on the twenty-third that he would not.

"The description is made. It is dated the twenty-seventh and it issues at four o'clock tomorrow afternoon and it has my name at the head of it, because I asked for that in this room on the twenty-seventh with a clerk in it to write it down, and the reason I gave was four words long and a clerk wrote them down, and the words were *because it is cheaper*."

Nadia Ferrous laid a hand flat on the wood.

"Read that back."

"You heard it."

"I heard it and I would like somebody else to have heard it in a room with me in it." She was on her feet before the sentence was finished. "Seven years. Seven years you have not put a line of yourself anywhere that anybody could carry, and on the twenty-seventh a woman from a wing asked you a question and you said *it is cheaper*."

"Yes."

"That is the first thing you have ever said to this table that was not dressed," said Nadia Ferrous. "And I want it noticed that you said it out loud yourself, on purpose, in advance, before anybody could find it, because that is what you do, and it does not make it smaller."

"Noticed," said Sun Meiling.

## The Reason He Gave

He put his hands down flat on the cloth.

"A description of a man can be examined," he said. "That is the whole of the reason."

"A man can be summoned and asked things, and he can be contradicted, and he can be argued with, and he can appeal. A file about a man is a thing that a man is allowed to be inside of and come out of the wrong side of."

"A description of a house is a different act entirely. A house sits at an address, and what is written about it is simply right or simply wrong, and not one person in the world can put a hand flat on a table and say otherwise."

"In about four years an office will read it, administer it, and go home."

"That is the reason," said Runa Sato. "Say the other one."

"That is the whole of it."

"It is one of two."

"It is the reason I have been giving since the spring," he said, "and in about four seconds somebody at this table is going to work out that it is not a new one, so I would rather say it than have it said."

Agnete Lindqvist had her sleeve pushed back over her wrist and she looked at him for about two seconds.

"It is the reason you gave in July about the street," she said.

"So it is."

"You told me in July that a person is safe in a document only if the document cannot find her, and that is why I have been inside a building for eleven years and have never once been in a book." She did not raise her voice. "And on the twenty-seventh you went into a room and arranged to be found, on the grounds that being found is better than being described, and I want to know whether you think about me when you do that."

"I think about you every day of the week."

"That is not the answer to the question I asked and you know it is not." She put her hands in her lap.

"I have been indoors eleven years. In eleven years I have not been a name on anything, not once, and the reason is not virtue and it is not courage. It is that no instrument in this city can reach a door it cannot find."

"And on the twenty-fourth I put a line in a form with a year in it against me and no date at all, and I did not do it because I am brave."

"I did it because you asked me to fill in a piece of paper in your kitchen and I am not good at saying no to a table."

"Agnete—"

"And I am not sorry," she said, "and that is the part I find frightening, and I have been sitting with it since the twenty-fourth, and I would like the rest of you to know that the most frightened person in this room tonight is not sorry."

## The Offer Refused Twice

"Can it be taken off us," said Petra Ilyin. "Any of us. Walk to a counter and give the address up."

"The composition would fall," said Runa Sato. "The figure would go from eight to seven for as long as one of us is missing, and the list that issues tomorrow afternoon still says eight, in the order of the stair, with a date under it."

"So somebody gives up the address and pays for the gap," said Petra Ilyin, "and the list goes out anyway. That is the shape of it. I have carried figures for eleven years and I know the shape of it."

"Give me an hour," said Runa Sato, "and I will tell you what the Field calls that shape, and it is not a discrepancy, it is an evasion, and the person who evades is not the person who gave the address up."

"So nobody does that," said Petra Ilyin.

"Nobody does that," said Runa Sato.

Nadia Ferrous had been standing up for the whole of it.

"I am going to say the thing I offered in October and then I am going to stop offering it," she said.

"On the eighth I said I would walk to a counter and swear that I never kept that address, and that I had been put into somebody's book to make trouble."

"I have thought about it every day since and I am not going to do it. I want everybody at this table to hear me say that I have thought about it every day since, and that the reason is not the reason you think."

"That is what it is."

"Because you would be at the counter within a week." She sat down.

"That is all. You would be in the room, and you would say the whole thing was a mistake made by a clerk, and you would be right, and I would have spent eleven years of my life on a fraud to save one month of yours."

"And then in about four years there would be a description of you with eight names on it and one of them would be wrong. It would be mine, and it would be the only wrong thing in the file."

Nobody said anything about that.

## The Question Sun Meiling Asked

"Who examines you."

"You have been in this room for four years," he said.

"You have been in this room for four years and you have put me under a bar with a certificate you signed in your own hand in September. You did it because you thought you were keeping me out of something. Here it is." Sun Meiling did not move.

"That is the whole question and it has one word in it. Who examines you."

"Not Alma Quayle. Not a keeper in a cold room. Not a broker who priced a house because you asked her to. Some man in a division you have never been inside, with a stamp, four years from now."

"He will have the file. He will read your name at the top of it and your reason at the bottom of it, and he will want to know one thing, and the thing he will want to know is where it comes from."

"That is the examination."

"And do you get to answer it."

"Yes."

"You said that too quickly," said Sun Meiling.

"You get to answer it if you ask to. The description says a person may be heard before the finding is entered. The hearing is at a counter, in a room, with a clerk, and you would go to it, because that is what you have always done, and you would answer the whole thing in nine seconds and it would be true." She put one finger on the table.

"And I asked you who examines you, and you told me it was not a question about time. It was not. I heard you."

"Here is what it is. In about four years the thing that will stand in front of that counter and be examined is not you. It is the eight of us, because you have written yourself at the head of a page and we are underneath it in the order of a stair."

## The Price Of A Term

"A term is a thing you agree to be bound by now in exchange for not being described later," said Agnete Lindqvist. "It is a price paid in advance for something that has not arrived. I have known the word since I was a rank-four clerk and I have not used it out loud in a kitchen in eleven years."

She looked at the shelf over the sink.

"The price has been named. It was named on the twenty-seventh at about seven in the evening and it was eight lines long and I wrote one of them. You have bought yourself a term, and the term is that from the thirtieth of this month you are a thing that can be argued with, and what you have paid for it is that eight women are a thing that can be looked up."

She got herself out of the chair.

"I am going to bed, and I am going to sleep, and I would like it noticed that I slept eleven years in a building where not one instrument could find me and that as of the twenty-seventh eight of us can be looked up."

She went out and the door did not make a sound.

## The Order

Runa Sato did not go up with her.

"The Field has given me an order," she said, when the stair had gone quiet. "It came down the wire on the twenty-seventh, in the afternoon, and it is dated that day and it is signed, and it asks this division to produce a return on the composition of premises 34-6-118 on the morning of the thirtieth, and to produce it by four o'clock tomorrow, which is the same four o'clock as your description, and that is not a coincidence, and I have been given two days to notice it."

"What is in an order like that."

"It is in the standing orders on the wall at the head of the muster room at Cordwain and I could recite it," said Runa Sato. "It means this division has been asked a question by somebody who is not my division, and my division cannot refuse it, and I cannot refuse it either, and the answer I give has to be a figure and a date and a return in my own hand."

"And the figure is eight."

"The figure is eight and I am going to put eight, and I am going to write the whole of it out in the ordinary way, and I am going to send it up at four o'clock tomorrow, because that is what an order is and there has not been one down that wire in nine years that did not go back the same day." Her palm went down on the table the way it had gone down on a form in a wing on the nineteenth.

"And I wanted to tell you tonight, in this kitchen, with you in it, and not at a counter at about half past three in the afternoon."

"You could have said no."

"I could have refused it and I would have been right to refuse it," said Runa Sato, "and if I refuse it the answer goes up from a desk with somebody else's name on it, and I will not have this address read by an officer who has not been in the room with the eight of you and been told what eight of them are." She straightened up. "Now go to bed. It is late and you have an afternoon in it that nobody else in this building has."
