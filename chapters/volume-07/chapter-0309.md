# Nine Hundred Mouths And A Door Number

It was in Ferrier Street before nine in the morning, and it was not in the building, and by ten it was in the Square.

Lode came up the sixty-one at about half past seven with a crate lid under his arm and did not come further than the fourth step.

"The card's down," he said.

"You took it down."

"She took it down." Lode put the crate lid against the wall. "At about two this morning, after the last house, and she did it herself and did not tell the box office, and they found out at eleven when the lamp was cold. And there is a piece of paper on the card frame where the verse was, and it says *withdrawn at the singer's word*, and no name on it anywhere, because she has not got one to put there."

"Can it be recalled."

"It cannot be recalled, it cannot be sung again for money in that house, and not one person in this city can say she did it wrong." Lode shifted the crate lid. "And here is the part I came up sixty-one steps to say. It went out of that door last night with about two hundred people in the room, and it went out of the bar this morning with four hundred and eighty."

"How is that possible."

"It is not possible and it happened, and she says she does not know how and does not care, and she wants you to know she has put a thing into a city that she does not own and cannot get back, and she did it knowing exactly what that was." Lode went down two steps and stopped, which he does. "That is the whole of what she said. Nine words on a stage door, and then she went out to the bar."

## What Was In The Lane

He went down at nine with a bowl and a spoon and got about forty yards.

The first one was a boy of nine or ten on the tram steps with a satchel, doing it on the way to school, not quite the words, only the shape of the first four bars.

The second was two women at the milk cart, and the younger one said it, and the older one said *hush* and then said the next four bars anyway.

The third was a man washing down the steps of the wash-house at the corner, who had never been inside the Bellhouse in his life and had never bought a seat at the back of anything, humming it with no idea where it came from, the way a man hums a thing he heard two streets off.

Nineteen years of his life had gone into building things that could not be found.

All of them were instruments. A certificate is an instrument. A reconciliation is an instrument. A production sworn in a chained book is an instrument. A district premises return is an instrument, and this one has six words in a margin beside a blank line with a clerk's number under them. A priced line initialled and dated in a real book is an instrument, and it is the best one he has ever made, and it will be in four divisions this week and a fifth inside a month.

A tune is not an instrument. It cannot be recalled, withdrawn, entered on a page, or stopped by anybody who did not write it.

## The Wall

Beatrix Halm was under the north end of that wall at about ten with her notebook already out and her good ear turned up the square, where she stands every morning whether there is anything on it or not.

"Eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight," she said. "Six under. The two words are the two words it has said since the end of July, and nobody has scraped them off, and that is the fourth month I have stood under this stone being six under."

"And the card does not come down until the last working day of the quarter."

"Which is not this month."

"Six under for sixty-three days."

"Seventy-nine days on one count and sixty-three on the other, and you will give me the worse one on purpose because it sounds worse." She turned a page. "Say the other thing. You have been standing at this wall for four minutes not looking at it."

"The tune is in the square."

"And it is not coming out of the Bellhouse." Beatrix Halm did not look up. "Nine hundred people cross this stone every morning and I have been listening to them since seven, and the ones who have it are not the ones who were in that room last night."

She turned a corner of a page over with her thumb.

"I have been trying since a quarter to nine to work out how a thing travels that far in one night without a room. I have not managed it and I am going to stop, because I have been counting for nine years and this is the first fact that has ever arrived in my square that I cannot trace."

"How many of them have it."

"Three in the first four minutes. I will give you a better figure at eleven. I am not going to guess in front of a wall."

She shut the notebook.

"There is a thing you are not saying."

"There is a thing I am not saying," he said, "and you have known it since the eighth of August."

"Then say it."

"They have got the street."

Beatrix Halm looked up.

"How many streets fit it."

"Six streets have fitted it since July and she has never named one of them, and all six have decided it is about them, and that has been true for four months and has done none of them any harm, because none of the six has anything to be had."

He put his hands in his pockets.

"On the fifteenth she sang *a quarter of his own street*. That is new. It says a street and does not say which one, and it went into nine hundred mouths."

"And by this morning somebody has worked out which one."

"By this morning somebody has worked out which one and has been telling people about it at a milk cart, and not one instrument sits anywhere in that transaction, no division keeps a copy, no clerk has a number for it, and I could not stop it if I had a writ."

The square did what a square does.

The wall carried eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight under a floor of eight thousand one hundred and forty-four, in two words that had not changed since the thirtieth of July.

## The Third Leg

A classification needs three things, and he had spent five days trying to build the third one.

He stood on the north side of the square with the traffic going round him and worked out what a man can do when every instrument he owns has failed him.

He cannot get the eighth name off the leaf. He cannot make two figures into one. He cannot stop the book closing on the thirtieth. He cannot write a description and he cannot stop one being written.

He cannot. And that was the discovery, and it arrived in the middle of a square at about a quarter to eleven on the sixteenth of October with nothing happening at all.

He does not need to build the third leg.

It is already assembled.

It is in nine hundred mouths on a tune. It needs no office and it needs no him, and it cannot be taken apart by anybody, including the woman who put it together.

There is a phrase for a house at the top of a stair, a phrase for a man who made himself into a figure, and a phrase for a keeper whose book does not agree with her own column. Those three phrases, in a singer's mouth, in a room that agrees with her, are the reason clause of a document that has not been written yet.

They were made on the fifteenth by a woman who was not asked, has not been paid, and is not a party to any of it.

He had spent seven years making himself the only kind of object in this city that could not be found.

On the sixteenth of October he stood on a wall and got found by a tune.

"That is the whole of what has happened to you since the spring," said Beatrix Halm, without looking at him. "And you are going to want to break it, and you are not going to be able to, and I would like to watch you try. I would like it to be today, because I have a good ear and I have never in my life had anything to listen to except a city going past."

## The Note

The runner from the Exchange came up the sixty-one at about twenty past four with one envelope and did not come to the door.

The seal was the counting room's and the paper was the counting room's and the hand on the outside of it was not a clerk's.

*You asked me a question in writing on the fourteenth and I am answering in writing, because you are not to come down here again while there are two figures for one door. That is a rule of the floor and not a decision.*

*A head of premises may correct a composition. That is the one instrument in this city that can move a figure on a district leaf, and there is no other.*

*The correction has to be in his own hand.*

*The corrected figure will be eight, because eight is what that door can give.*

*The eighth name will then be on a chained page in this room and in my day book, and on the ordinary stream on the next working day, and in four divisions by the end of the week, and it will be there for as long as the account is open, which is longer than you or I.*

*You have asked me whether a correction can be un-sworn. It cannot. There will be two sworn figures out of one door in one chained book, and the second will not cancel the first, and the woman whose name is on it cannot take it back off, because she cannot see the page and cannot get to it and is not, on anything I hold, anybody's business.*

*Do not send me a third question in writing this week.*

*H.R.*

Runa Sato read it over his shoulder at the table and put her hand flat on the paper.

"Then there is nothing left."

"There is one thing left," he said, "and it has been sitting on the shelf over the sink since the first of September, and it is the reason I have spent five days and would not go to it."

"The kitchen table."

"The kitchen table," he said, "and I am going to it on the nineteenth, and once I have been, there is not one instrument left in this city that I am willing to pick up."
