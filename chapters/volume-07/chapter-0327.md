# The Seal That Matches

On the fourteenth of November at about seven in the morning he went to the wash-house yard again and did not touch the grille, the bucket, or the brush.

Seventeen days since the twenty-seventh of October. Twenty-five days since the correction left half filled at a counter on the nineteenth. Twenty days since the list had been fixed in eight hands on the twenty-fourth of October, if the month is counted at thirty days with no thirty-first.

He stood beside the driver whose cart had been there on the fifth and helped him load by holding the tailboard steady, which is not lifting and is not a deed anybody files.

The driver finished sooner because the board did not shift. He said so, briefly, to the day clerk when she came out. She wrote it down with the time against it, because clerks write down what moves.

His name was not on that slip. The driver's was.

He walked to the Fourth Branch gate and held the door for a clerk with a bad back who carried a bundle of returns up three flights every morning at about eight. She got to the top sooner because the door did not swing shut on her. She said so at the desk. The desk clerk wrote it down.

His name was not on that line either.

At about nine he put his hand on the hand of a woman from the laundry who had been coughing since October and who had fed him once, two years ago, with a cup of tea in a cold yard when he had forgotten his own. Her cough eased for the morning. She finished her round by ten and told the basket clerk she had finished early for the first time in a month. The clerk wrote early against her name with the time.

His hand had been on hers for about four seconds. Cold ran through her fingers and was gone. She shrugged it off and thought she was less cold.

He walked away before anybody wrote his name down.

That was the posture. Stand beside people finishing things. Let them lift. Walk away.

## The Shape Of An Answer

At about noon Sun Meiling found him at the tram stop with a folded slip of paper in her hand. Not a form. A note in her own hand, three words and a date.

He read it standing up.

Three words. A date three years past.

He stared at it for a long time.

"Say what this is."

"It is not an answer," she said. "You do not get an answer. The file does not open here. What opens here is the shape of an answer and a date. He carried it. You received it. The seal that matches is his because the account has his shape. That is the whole mechanism and it is now canon between us and may be referred to and never explained again."

"Why write it."

"Because you need it on paper once, in my hand and not yours, so that in four years when somebody asks where it came from you can say a woman who knew him told you on the fourteenth of November." She took the slip back and put it in her pocket. "I am not giving it to you. I am showing it to you."

The loss of it cut immediately, sharp, and then relief that she had not given it, because a paper with that on it in his hand would be a document and documents go into divisions.

"And the date."

"The date is when he gave everything he had in one movement," she said. "Three years ago. Before you ever kept a ledger. Before there were a hundred and eighteen lines. Before nine turned. You have been filling since what he filled. That is why the account has an address and the address is you."

He nodded. He could not speak for a moment.

He saw it plainly.

The account was not his by earning. It was his by carrying. Somebody had paid to make him a recipient. He had spent four years earning into a thing he had been handed.

He also saw, with a clarity that hurt, what it meant for one woman who had held her question three years until somebody asked it rightly.

## What It Costs To Know

They rode three stops without speaking. Then he spoke.

"If I use his seal."

"You cannot use it," she said. "A man with no standing cannot obtain one. You cannot apply. You cannot be given one. You can only be carried, if the holder chooses, and the holder is gone out of public record."

"Then what."

"Then you are scored round it," she said. "You become a person a movement can be heard against. You reach seven by deeds other people attest. Three attestations move the plate. That is still true. The mechanism does not replace the band. The band is the only instrument in this city that hears you."

He got off at Ferrier Street and she rode on one stop more, because she did not live behind the list and would not be on it.

He wanted, concretely, to be worthy of the carrying, and he wanted it the way a man wants to stand straight under a weight, with his knees locked and his eyes open.

At the bottom of the sixty-one he stopped and looked back at the wall far down the hill, where the painters' card still said eight thousand one hundred and forty-four and his plate still said eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight, six under, in words that had not changed since the thirtieth of July.

Two days since the twelfth, when she had told him on a walk. Three days since the eleventh, when Mr Sowerby had told him a seal matches the account.

He counted both, subtracted out loud against dates printed in other rooms, and went up to tell the kitchen that the mechanism was known and the file was still shut.

Beatrix Halm wrote the date under his telling without being asked.

"The seal that matches is his," he said. "I do not have it. I will never hold it. The account was handed to me."

Nadia Ferrous put her hand flat on the table.

"Then we put our names to what you do," she said. "That is what is left. That is what we decided on the third."

"Yes."

He sat down at the cleared table with eight women round it who could each be looked up on a public list, and the weight of being carried settled over him like a coat somebody else had paid for.

The question that ended the evening was Petra Ilyin's, asked quietly, after the lamp was lit.

"Who attests first."

## What The Yard Writes

The day clerk's slips went into the branch day book at noon in the ordinary way, three days running, each with the time against it. None carried his name. All three carried the names of women who had finished early, got to the top sooner, run clear for ten days.

On the evening of the fourteenth Beatrix Halm laid the three copies she had begged off the clerks side by side on the kitchen table, with dates under each in her small level hand.

"One is an entry," she said. "Three is a pattern. A pattern is what a wall reads. The weekly plates go up tomorrow at half past four. The quarterly card holds at 8,144 till six on the thirtieth of December. The weekly moves on attested deeds. That is the board beside the railings and not the card. Remember the difference."

"How do you know the weekly moves tomorrow."

"Because I have watched it for nine years," she said. "The gang arrives at nine and the numbers rise at ten with a wall read before paint, except when dusk falls early. Tomorrow the light fails early. Be at the north end at a quarter to six in the dark. I will read it twice to be sure."

He nodded. Sixteen days since the twenty-eighth of October, when the fair copy had come back to Wren Kallo's tray at a quarter to eight with her posting the only one on the sheet. Fifty-seven days since the seventeenth of September, when the keys had changed to a woman he had never met.

He subtracted both against dates printed in rooms he was not in, out loud, so that the table heard the anchoring and could hold him to it.

Petra Ilyin poured tea round without being asked.

"Who attests first was my question last night," she said. "It is answered. Three women you never arranged. Laundry. Gate. Yard. That is the posture working. Do not touch it now. Do not thank them where a clerk can hear it. Walk away before the clerk comes."

"I will."

"And one thing more," she said. "Marguerite Vale was at the wall at five today, before Beatrix. She read whatever went up before Beatrix did. Whatever she does tonight, let her. She has held four lines in a drawer two feet from where you stand for two months. She is about to move them."

He looked up sharply.

"How do you know."

"Because I deliver on that street once a month for eleven years and I know what a bag looks like when it is packed for a table," she said. "Go to her if she sends for you. Do not go if she does not."

The lamp burned low. The list lay on the shelf with its eight names in the order of the stair. The receipt lay beside it with its number and its time.

He went to bed with the pattern still in front of his eyes, three slips side by side, three hands that were not his, three names underneath his work.

He dreamed that night of a chained book at the Exchange with the same number twice from the same man, seven both times, in nine seconds each time, and a keeper saying a book with the same number twice is a habit.

He woke at five with the habit still in his mouth like a taste, washed in cold water, and went down to hold the tailboard steady for the driver who had no name in any book he keeps.

The driver finished sooner because the board did not shift. The clerk wrote it down. His name was not on that slip. That was the whole posture and it was working, and he did not touch it, praise it, or explain it, because explanation is the fastest way to turn a pattern back into entries.
The lamp burned low and the wicks needed trimming, and nobody trimmed them until the telling was done.
