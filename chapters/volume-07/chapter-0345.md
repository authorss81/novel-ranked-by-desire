# The Offer She Did Not Make

The chair's rooms were on the second floor above an office that shut at four on the west side, and there was a runner at Ferrier Street at half past nine in the morning of the seventeenth of December with a slip in a Bureau hand that said the room, the floor, and the hour.

She had a table with nothing on it but a slate, a blotter, and a cup with a saucer, and a window with waxed paper in it that let light in and did not let anything out.

"Sit where the other one sat," she said. "I have had two men in this room in twenty years and both of them sat there, and I am not going to have a third man stand up because I find him interesting."

## The Small Offer

She did not make him wait and she did not make him comfortable.

"Bring the account in," said Anouk Ferreira. "Let it be classified once, properly, by the office that does it, with a finding entered and a date on the finding and a subject on the finding. Then it is administered. A classified account is administered by the Board and administered means it stays where it is and it is dealt with in the ordinary way by people who are countable and whose names are on a list anybody can be handed at a counter."

"And the room."

"The room does not take it and the room does not vote on it."

She turned the slate round so that it faced him and there was nothing on it.

"There is a reason I want that and it is not mercy. A thing the room holds is held by nine people for ever. A thing the Board administers is administered this quarter, and next quarter it is administered by somebody else, and in about four years by a clerk who has never heard of any of us. I have been the chair of this room for eleven years and I have come to believe the second thing is safer than the first."

"That is not an argument for handing it to them."

"It is not an argument for anything. It is an offer, it is the only one I am going to make, and I would like it entered in your mind as an offer and not as a favour, because a favour can be returned and an offer cannot."

She picked her cup up and did not drink from it.

"It has been made once in twenty years and it was taken once. The man who took it is alive and lives on the west side of this city, it was the correct decision, and it cost him everything that is left to him. I did that to a man. I would do it again. I am telling you what it costs so that you know I know."

## What He Refused It With

He did not say no first. He reached inside his coat and took out a folded sheet, and he put it on her table, and he did not give it to her and he did not explain it.

She looked at it and then at him.

"That is the copy of the list of persons at your own address," she said. "Issued at a Field counter on the first of November to the man named at the top of it, in a queue, without a reason, because none is required."

"Yes."

She did not touch it.

"There is a description of those premises made on the twenty-seventh of October," he said, "against the head of them and not against the address, on my own asking and in my own request, and my name is at the head of the first sheet in my own hand. Fifty days ago. So the thing you have offered to have classified is not a house."

"It is a house with a head."

"It is a man." He said it flatly, once. "A description of premises made against the head of premises is a description of a person. If a finding is entered on that account then the finding is entered on a man, and a man can be examined, and an address cannot be argued with."

"That is the instrument and it is not an argument. It has been in this city since the twenty-seventh of October. It is the reason I have not said yes to you in the first nine seconds."

She sat with it for a while.

"You are refusing my offer on the grounds that being classified means being examined," she said at last, "and I did not offer you a way out of being examined. I offered you the choice of who examines you. Those are not the same thing and you have just spent your best argument on the wrong one."

"Yes."

"So say the rest of it."

"I said the rest of it on the fourteenth of December at two in the afternoon in front of nine people," he said. "I said I want it to be a page with a name at the top of it, and I said I did not know what it costs, and I have not found out in three days and I have had nothing to find out with."

## The Thing She Considered

"You have eight names on the table in front of me," said Anouk Ferreira. "In the order of a staircase. And you have thought about what happens to eight women when a man is classified with eight women attached."

"Yes."

"I have thought about it. That is the difference between this offer and the requisition, and it is the only difference there is."

She put the cup down.

"A classification follows the subject. It goes where the subject goes. And if a man at the head of eight premises is classified, then those eight premises are part of what is being classified. In about four years a division somewhere will want to know what this account is made of, and the honest answer will be: of those people."

"You have thought about that and you have made the offer anyway."

"I have thought about it and I have made the offer anyway," she said, "and I am not going to pretend to you that this makes me a good woman. It makes me a woman with four days before the close of a quarter and no better idea, and the man who tabled the requisition has thought about exactly the same thing and reached a different conclusion, and I have a chair and a seal and he has a worse argument and more patience than I do."

"Who tabled it."

"Nikolai Vester, in the third seat, on the seventeenth of November, the morning after my books went to a table of nine people." She said it without any weight. "He has been in that room eleven years and he is not a cruel man and he is right about the ground, and he wants the account bought before an office with nobody in it can buy it for a different figure."

"And you think he is wrong."

"I think he is right about the ground and wrong about the instrument, and I have said so to him in that room and he has not taken it well. He called a sitting for the twenty-third on the fourth of December, and four people put a seal on it in the nineteen days between that and the twenty-third."

She put the slate down flat on the table in front of her.

"He is not the villain. There is no villain. There are nine people who can be counted and eight of them have thought about it longer than you have."

## The Offer She Did Not Make

He looked at the door, and then at her, and made himself say it out loud, which was the last thing he wanted to do in that room.

"Is that the whole of it."

"No," said Anouk Ferreira. "It is the whole of it that I am entitled to make to you."

The room was very quiet and the waxed paper did what waxed paper does with a lamp behind it.

"That is a sentence I have not heard in seven years and I am not going to ask you the rest of it."

"Not today, and not in a hearing, and not on any day you have not offered it in the first eleven seconds of the conversation. I have spent seven years taking sentences from people who wanted something. I am not going to take one in the only room in this city where it might be worth having."

She looked at him for a moment, and something crossed her face that was not amusement and was not gratitude.

"You are asking me for a thing I have not decided to give you," she said. "That is the first time in twenty years anybody has asked me for that. And I am not going to tell you why I have not decided, and I am not going to soften it by telling you that it is because you are not ready, because it is not."

"Understood."

"It is not that you are not ready. It is that I do not know the answer yet, and I have never in twenty years put anything in front of a man whose answer I had not worked out first, and that is not a virtue. It is what has kept this chair safe."

## The Thing That Closed

She stood up, which ended it, and put the slate under her arm.

"Two things and then go, and I want them both minuted in your own head, because nobody is going to write them down and both of them are final."

"Go on."

"One. You will not ask to be classified." She said it as a sentence somebody types. "Not today, not in a hearing, not to me, not to any office in this city, not in four years when the description is four years old and somebody with a folder comes to find the subject of it. You will not ask, and I have offered you the only version of that there is, and the offer is spent."

"And two."

"And two is that I do not make it again." She said it looking straight at him. "Not to you, not in a hearing, not in writing, not through anybody. You are the third man I have said it to and the first one I have said it to and been told no by, and I have never repeated it once, and I am not going to start with the one I would most like to repeat."

He stood up. He had come into that room with an instrument in his coat and he left with the only thing he had come for, which was the knowledge that in six days five people would sit down and vote on a document with an empty box on it, and that the one door out of it had been shut from both sides in a room with waxed paper in the window.

He wanted to ask her the rest of the sentence and he did not ask it, and she did not offer it, and neither of them said one word about it for the rest of that year.

The runner was on the step at half past eleven and the light on the west side was the colour it gets at half past eleven, and he went up the hill with the copy of the list still folded in his coat because he had not taken it off her table and she had not asked him to take it off her table.
