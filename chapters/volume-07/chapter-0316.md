# The One Who Is Not Counted

The Bureau's counter-forensics room is on the second floor and it has four desks and a rack of finding-slips and about eleven officers in it at eight in the morning, and Wren Kallo had asked him to come to it at seven forty, and she had told him three things about coming.

"You do not speak. You sit where I put you and you do not speak unless I put the question to you myself, because this is my room and these are the only people in this city who read a book for the shape of an edit in it, and the moment a person speaks in here the room starts looking at him instead of at what I am holding."

"And the second condition."

"And the second is that you do not ask me anything about that address again, from today, in this building or out of it, for as long as you are alive."

She had said it on the stairs and not in the kitchen, and she had said it at about half past six in the morning with her coat still on.

"Ask me about a man in a division. Ask me about a filing convention. Ask me the thing you asked me in June and I will answer it."

"Do not ask me anything about the composition at those premises, or the address, or the leaf, or the person on the leaf."

"That is new."

"That has been true since the second of October and I have been waiting eleven days for you to be in a position where I could say it and you could not answer it in front of the other seven."

## The Fair Copy

She had it face down on the corner of her desk when he came in, and she kept it face down for about four minutes while she did a stack of finding-slips, and then she turned it over and did not push it towards him.

"It went for check on the twenty-eighth at about six in the morning and it came back to this division on the tray at a quarter to eight, and a check is why it goes early, and that is the whole of the reason it is in this building two days before it is issued." Her finger went to the top of the sheet. "Eight lines, top of the stair down, being the people at premises 34-6-118 on the morning of the thirtieth."

She let him read it.

It was better than the hand. Typed, with the rooms in a column and the dates in a column and the whole thing squared off under a rule with the figure of eight against the head of it, and at the foot of it four printed lines that any division may act on and nobody in this city has ever argued about.

Wren Kallo's line was fourth from the top.

*KALLO, Wren. Counter-forensics, Bureau of Merit. From the second of October. Second floor, fourth door.*

He read it twice and then he read the other seven lines to make sure the posting was only on hers, and it was.

## The One Who Is Not Counted

"That is the only posting on it."

"That is the only posting on it, and I signed the rule that put it there in the ninth year of doing this, and I was right, and I have thought about it every day since the twenty-fourth." Wren Kallo had not looked at him once.

"A description of premises lists the persons who keep an address at it. And an officer of the Bureau cannot be entered at an address under description without the division being named."

"An officer is a person a division can be asked about. If the division is not on the line then somebody has to go and find out which division, and the only way to find that out is to ask a person in a street."

"You asked me on the twenty-fourth whether the office went on it."

"And she said no. And she said it kindly, at four o'clock in the afternoon, and she had decided it for me. I wrote it down and I believed her, and I was wrong."

"She was not lying to me. A list is not a description, and she was talking about a list." She squared the sheet about.

"A description of premises carries the post of anybody at that address who holds one. That is a different instrument and a different rule. I have read both. I knew which one she was talking about and I asked the wrong one."

"That is not your mistake."

"It is exactly my mistake and it took nine years to make it." She put both hands flat on the desk.

"Here is the one true thing and then you can stop looking at it. Nobody counted me."

"Not the district, because I walked up to a counter on the second of this month and asked a man to write a line, and he wrote it because I was standing in front of him. Not you, because you have never once had me in a book you read until the twenty-fourth."

"Not that kitchen. I walked in and made myself the eighth name of a house I had never lived in, in daylight, at a counter, at about eleven in the morning. I told six people in a fortnight, made it ordinary, and filed it."

"You said at the kitchen table that you would like it noted that you did it yourself."

"I said it in front of them, and in a records room with the door open, and to a woman from my own division, and I will say it at the counter of the Classification Wing when the description is issued, if anybody lets me stand there." She turned her head.

"And I would do it again at about eleven in the morning at a counter, and the price of doing it again is that division."

"That division has been my life for nine years and it is the only thing I have ever been good at, and I have just put it on a list with my name on it."

## The Rack

A finding-slip came out of the rack behind her while she was talking and she dealt with it without looking at it, which is a thing she has been able to do for nine years and which is why she is good.

"That rack has about four hundred slips in it," she said. "Every one of them is an edit somebody made to a record and the whole of my division's job is to know whether the edit was honest. That is the work. Nobody asks me to feel about it and I have never felt about it."

"And a list of persons at an address is not an edit. It is not a hand that changed a line. It is a hand that wrote a line for the first time, in the ordinary way, on a form, in a room." She put the slip down on top of a tray.

"So there is nothing on that rack that can check it and nothing in this division that can hold it."

"I decided what that meant on the twenty-fourth, at about eleven o'clock at night, at a kitchen table. And I am the woman who will have to live in it in about four years."

## The Cost In One Sentence

She turned the sheet back over and put her hand on it.

"There are three women on that floor who have worked beside me for nine years," she said, "and there are nine on the other side of the city who have worked beside me for six, and as of this morning twelve people who do not have to be told anything know where I sleep, and not one of them has asked me and not one of them is going to."

He kept his mouth shut, because he had been told not to speak.

"That is the sentence. You may write it down. It is the only sentence about this business I have said out loud this month and I have said it in a room with four desks and a rack in it and not one person here heard it, because you are standing by the wall and I asked you not to speak and you have not, and I would have told you to leave if you had."

## What She Asked

At about nine o'clock she put the fair copy in a drawer and locked it, and stood with her key in her hand for a second longer than a woman usually stands with a key.

"There is a check on a composition," she said. "Every figure that goes into a real book gets certified by counter-forensics before it is carried, and the certificate says the figure was the figure on the morning named and it is signed by an officer of this division. There will be one for the composition at those premises on the thirtieth of this month. It is not a big thing. It is a sheet the size of a palm and it goes in a file in four divisions."

"And you are going to certify it."

"I am going to certify it," said Wren Kallo, "because I am the officer at that desk and it comes to me by the tray, and if it does not come to me by the tray I am going to ask for it by name, in writing, at the top of the column, where it stays."

"You have just made yourself the evidence."

"I have made myself the evidence and I have done it in the one office in this city that is paid to check figures, and that is the only advantage I have ever had and I have just spent it on being visible." She put the key in her pocket. "I want to say the other half of it out loud before you go, because you are not going to enjoy this and I would rather hand it to you than have you work it out on a tram."

"Go on."

"Somebody will open that file in about four years. There is a description with your name at the top of it and eight names under it, and a certificate of a composition signed by me."

"The stranger's first question is why a counter-forensics officer at the address in question certified the count of her own address." She did not raise her voice.

"And the answer is in a line in a kitchen in eight hands, and I would like it to stay in a line in a kitchen in eight hands. In about four years I am not going to want to be a woman who stood in a queue and made a mess."

At the door he stopped, because she had not said the thing she had led up to.

"You said you would not answer me about that address again."

"Not one question," said Wren Kallo. "Not in this room and not out of it and not in a kitchen."

"If you ask me one I will tell you I do not know what you are talking about, in front of whoever is in the room, and I will do it well, because I have been doing it since the sixth of August." She picked up the next tray of finding-slips.

"Go and find out what it costs the other seven. It is not going to be uniform and it is not going to be fair, and you are the only person in that kitchen who has not asked."
