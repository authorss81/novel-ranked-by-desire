# Two True Things About One Door

Four thousand six hundred and eighty-three was the fourth of this one and the eleventh of this one is day four thousand six hundred and ninety, and four thousand six hundred and ninety less four thousand six hundred and eighty-three, the fourth of this one, is seven, and eleven less four is seven by the month count.

## Three Flights And A Landing

He went at about eleven, because a woman at a returns desk had told him on the fourth that the woman he wanted does her own accounts and is never once angry about the stair, and those two facts together were more than he had, and he had to go and be wrong in person.

The three flights are off a court with an arch at the bottom of it and a bit of wall about the height of a man, and the building is about four hundred yards from his own and is not his and has nothing to do with him.

He went up them. The second landing has two doors on it and the light comes off the court about eleven at that time of year and goes down the wall in a long narrow piece.

He knocked on the one on the left because that is the one the man in a bundle knocked on.

## What He Came To Say And What He Did Not Say

He had it ready on the second landing. He had had it ready since the twenty-seventh.

He had come up three flights of somebody else's house to apologise for a sentence he gave away in a court on the twenty-eighth of October, in front of people, on purpose, and said in public he was finished with.

If he said it now he would be a man standing in a doorway telling a woman he was sorry about an address he had told a court he no longer had, and both of those things would be true, and the woman in the doorway would have to decide which of them he was.

He knocked.

## The Woman On The Second Floor

She had a book open on the table behind her and a cup and about four sheets of paper with figures on them, and she had a pencil in her hand and she did not put it down.

"You are the one off the stair," she said.

"I am."

"Come in or do not. I have got about nine minutes before the next one comes up the stairs and it is not the butcher."

He came in and did not sit down.

## What She Actually Wanted

"You have come to tell me it was four flights," she said.

"I was going to."

"Do not. I have worked that out." She turned a page over and did not look at it. "I have known it was four flights since the middle of last month."

"I went up three and there was a woman at a table and she read me a thing out of a book in the voice they all use, and she was very good about it, and I stayed on that chair for about half an hour being helped by somebody who had no idea I was in the wrong building."

"That is not a mistake you made."

"No," said the woman. "And here is the thing you have come here to tell me, and you may as well hear it before you say it. The words were right. All three of them were exactly right, and it was the same man who said them to me at a counter and who could not have been more kind, and the stair was wrong. Both halves came out of the same mouth on the same morning."

## Two True Things About One Door

"So you have a door in your head with three lines on it that are correct and a stair in front of it that is not," said Lin Hao, "and there is not one person in this city who can take one of those out of the other without making a copy."

"That is the whole of it and you can ask anybody who does this for a living, which I have."

He had not expected that and it took about nine seconds out of him while she went back to her figures.

"You have come here to fix it."

"I have come here to find out what it cost you."

## What It Cost Her

She put the pencil down for the first time.

"It cost me two mornings and the soles off a pair of shoes, because I went up those stairs twice before I worked out what was wrong with them," said the woman. "And then it cost me something I did not expect and I have been carrying it since the middle of last month, and it is that a man I have never seen was kind to me for about four seconds before he got it wrong."

She looked up then.

"He did not know he was sending me up the wrong stair. He thought he was helping and he thought he had helped. I have been at this table for nine years and I know exactly what that feels like from the other side, and I am not going to be angry with a man for being kind about a thing that was true of a light."

## Why He Cannot Apologise

"You are not going to apologise," she said. "You have been standing in that doorway not saying it for about nine seconds and I want to say why you are right not to."

"You know why I am right not to."

"I know you gave that address away in a room full of people and said so out loud, because the woman at the top of that building told me about it in November when she came for a thing about her sister." She put her cup down. "If you apologise to me for it, you have taken it back, and if you have taken it back then that woman told me a lie in November, and she did not."

Lin Hao did not say anything for about nine seconds.

"So do not apologise," said the woman. "Tell me the thing I actually came to ask you for, and I will tell you that you cannot give me that either, and we will both be right."

## What She Actually Asked

She asked him whether the office would be there next time.

Not whether it would be there in three flights. Not whether a woman at a table would read it to her. Whether the thing would be there at all, on the day, when a person needed it, and whether anybody would be standing in the court.

He could not answer that, and the reason he could not answer it is the reason he had come up the stairs, and he told her so.

## What He Gives Her And What He Does Not

He said the three lines out loud in her front room at the length and in the ordinary voice, the street, and the door at the head of four flights off a third court, and the hours between a quarter to ten and half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour.

She got all of them.

He did not correct the version she had, and he did not tell her which part of it was the part that had gone three flights, and he gave her nothing to put in a pocket, because a thing she can show is a copy, and the thing that walked three flights in the first place was somebody else's copy.

"I am not going to be able to tell you," he said, "whether the version you have is the version you will hear next time."

"Why not."

"Because in this city the sentence goes out of a mouth and there is no hand on it after that, and I cannot get in front of it, and neither can you, and every instrument I own was used up long before somebody said a sentence in a street."

He said it in her front room with her figures on the table and the light off the wall going the other way.

## What She Thought About For Nine Seconds

She thought about it for about nine seconds.

"I had not thought of that," she said. "I have been thinking about the stair."

"The stair is three flights. The stair is fixed by tomorrow."

"The stair is not the bad part," said the woman. "The bad part is that I can be told something true this morning and be told something different in a month, and I cannot ring a person about it, and I cannot ring a hall about it, because the man that told me is a stranger and he does not know he has done anything."

She picked her pencil up again.

"That is worse than the stairs. I want you to know that I have thought about it for nine seconds and I am telling you it is worse than the stairs, and then you can go and be the man you are going to be about it."

## What He Says In The Doorway

"The only thing this office owns is a piece of card and a hammer," said Lin Hao.

"That is not what I asked."

"It is what I have got. There is a piece of stiff card on three nails at the top of that stair and there is a hammer in a room behind the woman who types, and if a day comes when that card cannot be read then one man in the morning takes it off those nails and writes the same three lines on it and puts it back, and not one person in that building is told, and nothing is written down anywhere that it happened."

"And is that going to happen?"

"I do not know," he said. "It has not happened yet this year and it is the only thing I have got and it is the only instrument in this city that this office owns, and I have not been able to decide whether it is a repair or a way of saying a thing twice, and I have been standing in your doorway for about twenty minutes."

She looked at him for a while.

"Then come and read them again some time," she said. "Not for me. For whoever comes after me who cannot read that card."

Four thousand six hundred and ninety days. Less four thousand six hundred and eighty-three, the fourth of this one, is seven, and eleven less four is seven.

A man went up three flights of somebody else's house to repair an address he had disowned in a court in October, and a woman with four sheets of figures in front of her told him that the words were right and the stair was wrong and that both of them had come out of the same mouth on the same morning, and that she had not once asked for the address to be corrected.

*Less four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is forty-one, and that rail has carried the same sheet through every one of the last forty-one of them, and it does not move again until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand two hundred and seventy days, which are seventy-five months and twenty days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
