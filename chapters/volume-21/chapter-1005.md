# The Only Repair There Is

Four thousand six hundred and ninety was the eleventh of this one and the eighteenth of this one is day four thousand six hundred and ninety-seven, and four thousand six hundred and ninety-seven less four thousand six hundred and ninety, the eleventh of this one, is seven, and eighteen less eleven is seven by the month count.

## Half Past Ten With The Door Open

He went up at about half past ten with the door open behind him and his coat still on, and he had got as far as the table before he understood that this was going to be the easiest thing he had tried to do in a year and it was going to cost him everything, and that he had known that on the eleventh and had come anyway.

The book was on the table. The room had the cold in it that four flights of stair always have in that month and the window was shut.

Wenna Raut had her hands in front of her and no pen near them, which in nine years he had learned to read as a woman waiting to be asked something she was going to enjoy.

There was no pen near them because he had come up four flights to announce a decision, and a decision is not a thing that gets entered, and they had both known that before either of them had said anything.

## What He Had Come Up To Do

He came up to say that he was not going to renew the card.

He had said it to himself on the eleventh and on the second landing on the fourteenth and in the bath on the sixteenth, and each time he had found a fresh reason, and on the evening of the sixteenth he ran out of reasons and the thing that was left was that he did not want to.

He got as far as putting his hand on the back of the chair at the end of that table.

"I am not renewing it," he said.

## She Was Going To Ask Him Something Better

"Yes," said Wenna Raut. "You have been not renewing it since the eleventh and you have been coming up here to say so as if saying it were the hard part."

"What is the hard part."

"The hard part is what you think a renewal is." She put her hands flat on the table, which is not a thing she does. "Say what a renewal is and I will tell you whether you have said it in the right order."

## A Renewal Is A Sentence Said Again In A Different Month

"I do not know what you mean."

"You have been carrying it round the city for nine days as if it were the same as saying them out loud, and it is not, and you know it is not, and you have said in that landing two weeks running that a renewal is the only instrument this office has." She turned her chair about a quarter. "Say the two things next to each other and see which way round they sit."

He stood in the middle of that room with his coat on.

"A renewal is three lines in my own hand on three nails and nothing else."

"Now say the other one."

"A renewal is the same sentence said by the same man in a different month with a pen in his hand."

"Yes," said the clerk. "That is what a renewal is, and you have had that argument backwards for nine days, and you have never once said it to me in that order before and I am not going to let you take it back afterwards."

## He Could Not Get It Back

He tried for about nine seconds.

He said it the other way round twice and she took it apart both times, in the ordinary voice, and it did not come apart, and the whole of what she had done was to put a sentence in the order it actually belongs in and leave it there.

"You win it," he said.

"I have won it. That is different and you will find it out about four months from now, because winning an argument in this room does not do one thing to a card."

He stood there and took the two things out and put them down the other way and said the pair again, and she stopped him in the middle of it the way a person stops a man who is about to walk into a doorway with a bar down.

"You have made three sentences out of one and you have put the part that matters last twice, and that is what being on the wrong side of an argument sounds like, and I have watched you do it to other people for nine years."

## The Month Is The Whole Of It

"The difference between saying them out loud and renewing it is a month and a piece of paper," said Wenna Raut. "Say that out loud in this room and then say why the month matters."

Lin Hao took the chair at the end of that table, which he had not intended to do.

"Because what goes out of a mouth is true in the mouth that says it and is not true of the person who heard it."

"And a renewal."

"A renewal is true of everybody at once."

"That is why it is better," she said, "and that is why it is the right instrument for that door, and it is the wrong instrument for the four feet of pavement at the bottom of your own stair, and you have had those two facts in your hands since the end of November and you have been carrying them about as if they were the same thing and they are not the same thing at all."

## How Many Other Ways There Are

"How many other ways are there of saying them out loud without putting them on paper."

"That is the right question and I have been waiting nine days to be asked it, and there is one answer and you are not going to like it."

"Go on."

"The way that is still a door and is not a copy is a person standing in a room or standing at the bottom of a stair. Every other way is writing." She counted them off on the fingers of one hand without hurrying. "A card. A board. A slate. A sheet. A form. A chalk mark in a lane. A bell with a wire on it. A person put on a rota at a particular hour."

"That is eight."

"That is eight, and every one of them is a copy and four of them are a list, and you have been keeping five of them in your head since November without putting them on a sheet, and that is the only piece of good administration anybody in this building has done in a year."

## What He Says Out Loud With The Door Open

He said it out loud with the door open on the landing, at about one, to the three of them.

"You have just won an argument with me about the renewal and I am going to lose it, and I am going to say what losing it costs and then I am going to go back to not having decided, and the two of those things are the same thing."

None of the three of them answered him.

"I have told the only other person in this building what the repair is, and I told her first, and she told me what it is inside out, and now two of the three of us know it and the third has not been told and will not be told by me in a room, because a thing one person says in a room at this table stops being said out loud and starts being a practice, and I have not got room for another one of those."

## The Longer Item

"That is the first half of the cost," said the clerk. "What is the second half."

"The second half is that it goes nowhere."

"Say the whole sentence, because a thing that goes nowhere sounds like a refusal and I am the hand that would have to write it down if it went somewhere."

"A decision like that one has nowhere in this city to go," said Lin Hao.

"That is the first sentence you have said to me this morning that I agree with," said the clerk. "Say the rest of it and say it slower."

"Nothing in this building was ever written down about how anything is done here, and not one sheet in this city says what any of it is, and that is not an oversight, that is the arrangement."

"A decision like this one is not a practice. It is neither a question nor an answer, and not one line in that book is ruled for it. I have made it, I have told you about it, and it will be gone by the time I am down that stair."

## The Carrier Who Says The Practical Thing

The other carrier came up about two with the strap over his shoulder and put it down by the wall and said the only sentence in that room that could not be argued with.

"A card that cannot be read is a door that cannot be used," he said.

"You can decide in February whether you are going to renew it in the autumn. What you cannot do is stand on the top of that stair every morning reading three lines to a landing because the end of one of them has gone, and go on doing that after the middle of February."

"Why February."

"Because there is a point at which a man saying three lines from the top of a stair is not standing at a door any more, and you are the only person who would know when you got to it, and you have not got anybody."

"Who decides where that point is."

"You do," said the other carrier, "and you have not got anybody to tell you when you have got to it, because you are not one of the three of us and you have been not one of the three of us since the twenty-first of October, and that is the arrangement you made and I have carried the strap up four flights past the result of it about a thousand times."

## Afterwards

She took her hands off the table and put the pen away, which was how that room said a thing was over.

"Go down and stand at the bottom of your own stair for a morning," she said. "You have not done that since November and you have been working this out four flights above the place it is about."

He gave the three lines to the top landing at about ten past three, at the length, with the door of that room open behind him, because that is what a working morning is now, and the three of them heard it and not one of them wrote a word of it.

Four thousand six hundred and ninety-seven days. Less four thousand six hundred and ninety, the eleventh of this one, is seven, and eighteen less eleven is seven.

A clerk put a sentence about a renewal into the order it actually belongs in and would not let him put it back the other way round, and a man who had come up four flights to announce that he was not doing a thing lost that argument in about nine seconds.

He said what not doing it costs out loud with the door open, and the longer item on that list is that he has now told the only other person in that building what the repair is, and a decision of that kind has nowhere in this city to go.

*Less four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is forty-eight, and that rail has carried the same sheet through every one of the last forty-eight of them, and it does not move again until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand two hundred and seventy-seven days, which are seventy-five months and twenty-seven days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
