# Said Again In Daylight

Four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five was the sixteenth of this one and the twenty-third of this one is day four thousand seven hundred and thirty-two, and four thousand seven hundred and thirty-two less four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five, the sixteenth of this one, is seven, and twenty-three less sixteen is seven by the month count.

## At Half Past Four With The Light On

There is light on the wall opposite the bottom of that stair until about six on a working afternoon at the end of the winter, and at half past four on the twenty-third of March there was light on it, and he came down that stair and stood on his own second step with his back to nothing at all.

He had come down at half past four on purpose.

The door of that room was open four flights above him, because it is open from a quarter to ten, and the three of them were up there at the end of an ordinary day with a book on a table and a cage that had not been emptied.

There was a woman with a shawl over her head at the side of that court about nine feet off him with her back to the wall, and she works about nine feet from that stair and had been in that court every working afternoon since the autumn. She was not there for him and had not come up that arch because of anything he had done.

## What He Said On The Thirtieth Of December

At about nine minutes to five on the thirtieth of December, in the dark, at this step, with a court standing round him and the same door open four flights above, he said the three lines out loud one last time, and then he said what it costs, and then he said he was not going to say them again.

He had meant it.

That was not the mistake. He had meant every word of it and he had said the cost out loud before he said the thing, which is the order he has done everything in for a year, and a court of people had heard him say he was finished and had gone away knowing it.

## What A Court Does With A Thing Said Once

He had worked that out on the thirtieth of December itself and it was the reason he had stopped.

A thing said once in a court belongs to the court. The court heard it, the court took one of the sentences and gave it to somebody else, and the sentence stopped being his the moment it was out of his mouth in the place where he said it, and there was not one instrument in this city that could take it back.

He had been right about that.

He had also been right, standing on that step, about the light. It leaves that brick for the season at ten past four, and everything after that hour is a door in the dark with a sentence in a mouth, and he proved to himself on the twenty-sixth of November that a mouth cannot be read in the dark by somebody who has come up that arch.

That was all true at nine minutes to five in the dark on the thirtieth of December.

## Why Not At Nine Minutes To Five Again

None of it is true at half past four on a working afternoon in March, and he had worked out that too, and it had taken him about four months, and the reason it had taken him four months is that he had been treating the two as the same thing because they happened in the same courtyard.

They are not the same thing.

A sentence said in a court in the dark at the end of a day belongs to the last few people standing in it. Those people go home. They have it, they carry it, they say it to somebody on a stair, and in about a month it is a thing that was said in a court, out of somebody's mouth, once.

The same sentence said in the same court in the afternoon belongs to everybody standing in it.

That is the whole of it. That is the only thing a mouth can do that a piece of paper cannot, and he had been sitting on it for eleven weeks without knowing that was what he was sitting on.

## The Half Hour Before He Said Anything

He had been standing on that step since half past four, which is about two hours earlier than he stands in that court on any other working morning since the twenty-fourth of March, and in that half an hour not one person came up that arch and asked him for anything.

That is the arrangement. He made it in that room in October with the door open, and it has held every morning since, and he has not been the person anybody climbs that arch for once in eleven weeks.

What was in the court at half past four was light on the wall opposite and people at the far end of it going about their own business, and a woman with a shawl over her head at the side with her back to the stone.

The difference between that and the thirtieth of December was the entire reason he was standing there.

## The Reason Out Loud First

He said the reason first, out loud, with the court there and the door open above him.

"Eleven weeks ago I said in this place that I was not going to say these three lines again. I said it in the dark and I meant it, and I have not changed my mind about a single word of that."

"I am saying them again because a woman came up those stairs on the sixteenth to say that she had got two words wrong helping a stranger who was in a hurry."

"And because I have gone through the whole of what this office has ever owned this week, and there is nothing left in my hands but this, and I will not stand in a courtyard and pretend to a person in it that I still have something else."

## The Three Lines

He said them at the length and in the ordinary voice.

The street.

The door at the head of four flights off a third court.

The hours, which run from a quarter to ten to half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour.

He said the whole of the second line, including the four words that are not on the card any more, because he has been saying it for a year and he does not read it while he says it.

He said it once and he stopped, and he did not say it again to be sure, and it took about four minutes, and in the four minutes the light came off the wall opposite that step about a foot.

## What The Court Did With The Sound

A courtyard is a hard place to say a sentence in and that wall opposite gave the whole of it back to him with about half a second of the yard in the middle of it, and that was the first time in a year he had heard the end of the third line come off anything at all.

For a year he had been saying those three lines into a stairwell on the top landing and into the dark at the bottom of the same stair, and in both places the last four words went into a room that gave nothing back.

They came back off a wall that afternoon. It sounded the way it has always sounded to a person standing under that arch, which is like a man reading something he wrote a long time ago and knows by heart.

## What It Costs, Said Out Loud

"That is the second time in nine years that those three lines have been said twice in a court, and the first time is the thirtieth of December, and both of them are true and I have said both of them."

He looked at the arch and then at the wall opposite and not at anybody.

"So here is what it costs and I am saying it before I stop."

"A court in this city is holding a man who said one thing and then said the other. Nothing anywhere can check which of the two was true, and I have spent nine years refusing to make anything that could check anything, and that is why it is not checkable and it is not a different reason."

"Everybody in this city who learned those three lines from me between November and February has two true versions of them now. Two true versions, taken off one man, in one courtyard, in one winter. And not one person in this city can say which of them is older, and there never will be one, and I am not going to be able to hear it."

"And I am not going to be able to take any of it back. A card can be taken off three nails. A thing said out loud in a court stays where it was said, and that is the whole of the reason I am doing it."

## Then He Stopped

He gave no order to anybody.

He had no standing to give one and had not had any since the twenty-fourth of March of last year, and about four flights above him the three of them were standing in a room with the door open and had heard the whole of that, and not one of them came down.

He put nothing in the book, because three lines said out loud at the foot of a stair are neither of those, and that book has no ruled line for one.

Then he stopped.

## What The Court Did With It

Not one person asked him anything.

Then the woman with the shawl over her head looked at him for about nine seconds from about nine feet off, and turned her head to the man beside her, and said in the third person, at the length, in the ordinary voice:

"He has said them again."

That is what that court did with it, on a working afternoon in daylight at about five, with people standing in it who were not all of them there on the thirtieth of December and not one of them there for him.

Four thousand seven hundred and thirty-two days. Less four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five, the sixteenth of this one, is seven, and twenty-three less sixteen is seven.

A man who told a court in the dark on the thirtieth of December that he was finished said the same three lines again at half past four in the afternoon on the twenty-third of March, in the same courtyard, with the door of that room open four flights above him, and then said what it costs out loud, and stopped.

The court has him now, and not one person standing in it can say which of the two sentences is the older one.

*Less four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is eighty-three, and that rail has carried the same sheet through every one of the last eighty-three of them, and it does not move again until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand three hundred and twelve days, which are seventy-seven months and two days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
