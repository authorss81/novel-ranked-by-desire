# Whoever Said It First

Four thousand seven hundred and eleven was the second of this one and the ninth of this one is day four thousand seven hundred and eighteen, and four thousand seven hundred and eighteen less four thousand seven hundred and eleven, the second of this one, is seven, and nine less two is seven by the month count.

## Nine Stools And A Slate

He went into the lane off Cordwain at about eleven with one question, and he had carried it down four flights and about four hundred yards and he had been carrying it since the second of this one.

Emil Brack put his chalk down when he came in, which he does for him and not for anybody else.

"You have come about who said them first."

"Yes."

"Sit down on the end stool," said Brack. "That is a stool for waiting on and I am going to make you wait about nine minutes, because I want to do this properly and there is one thing about it that you are going to like and one thing you are not."

## The Lane Has One Column

"What is the thing I am not going to like."

"That a lane has one column." Brack turned the slate about on the wall so that both of them could read it. "A lane can tell you what a thing costs, and that is the whole trade, and I have been in it nine years and I have never once been wrong about a price in this city."

"The other half of that is that a lane cannot tell you where a thing started, and that is not a fault of the lane. It is the shape of the thing."

"What is the shape."

"You want two columns," said Brack. "You want the street and the corner it was said on, and a lane is one column, and I have got a street and I have got about four hundred corners, and I am not going to write down which corner for about a hundred different sentences, and if I did it would be a list of corners and not a list of sentences, and about four years from now a corner in this city would be worth something."

## The Thing He Is Going To Like

"I am going to give you the answer anyway," said Brack, "because I have been right about every fact anybody has put to me for nine years and this one is the same as the rest of them."

"Who."

"The man behind the counter under the window in that passage off Cordwain."

Lin Hao did not say anything.

"He is paid nothing," said Brack. "He has been at that counter for nine years and he tells strangers where that door is before lunch, every morning that anybody asks him, and he has never once put anything wrong into it, and there is not a book in this lane with his name at the top of a line."

"He read it off a card."

"He read it off a card to a stranger in a hurry in the last week of January of last year. That was the first time in this city that those three lines were ever said out loud to another person."

"The stranger went up four flights and got there, and he and that counter have not spoken since and neither of them ever will. The stranger did not know he was the first person alive to hear it, and that counter has never known it either."

## The Man Was Not In The Room

"You said he was not in the room."

"I did not say that."

"You said he was not in the room and he said it about himself this morning." Brack put the chalk down and did not pick it up again. "I have been standing in this lane thinking about that since the second of this one."

"He was not in the room because the room is a third court and four flights up and he has never been in it. He does not know what is written on the card, or who wrote it, or when."

"What he has is three lines he learned off a piece of paper and gave away for nothing before lunch every morning since before that card came down."

"You have watched him do it."

"I have watched him do it from across a passage for nine years," said Brack, "and he would go on doing it if you asked him to stop, and he has not been asked, and he will not be."

## What That Does To A Line

"He was there before the card," said Lin Hao.

"He was there before the card and he is there now and he is not going to stop, and the authority you have been carrying round this city for a year is not at the top of four flights," said Emil Brack.

He put his hand flat on the edge of the counter, which he does about four times in a year, and took it off again.

"It is behind a counter in a passage off Cordwain, and it belongs to a man who does not know he has it, and nothing at all in this city can fetch him into a room, because he has never owed a living soul anything and never will."

## The Woman Who Came Up And Went Down Again

"There is a woman who came up those four flights in the summer asking whether it was free," said Brack, "and went back down again, and there is a sister of hers that has never been traced. Neither of them is anything to do with a lane off Cordwain and neither of them has ever been in this lane, and I am telling you they exist because you will hear about them and I would rather you heard it from me."

## The Figure He Refuses

"Give me a number of them."

"No."

"Emil."

"No, and I will tell you the whole of why in my own words, because you will otherwise go away and think I was being careful."

Brack turned the slate about back the way it faced the lane. "A figure in a lane is a rate in about a fortnight. A rate is a thing a person can be put on."

"There is not a rate on that board for anything I have ever sold and there is not going to be one. If I chalk a figure today for how many of those three lines are going about this city, then in about a month every man in this lane will know the rate, and in about four years a woman will stand in a hall and say that office costs what it costs, and she will be quoting a lane."

"That is better than what I would have done with it."

"That is worse than what you would have done with it. You would have asked me for it and I would have said no and you would have gone away and got it somewhere worse."

## The Two Figures On The Slate

He looked at the wall.

Two figures are on that slate in small chalk and they have been on it side by side since the autumn before last. One of them is the figure that stands at the head of that office's standing sheet, put there by a different man in a different hand.

The other one is a sooner figure. It is the right one and the office has never argued with it and it has never been rubbed out.

"I am not asking you to move either of them."

"You were going to ask me to separate them."

"I was going to ask you to separate them and you were going to refuse, and it took me the whole walk over here to work out that you had refused once already and that a second one would be the same answer more politely."

## What Is Still Owed

"There is a third figure in your head."

"It is still owed," said Brack. "It will not be on this slate this year and you know it and I know it, and it is out, and you are not going to ask me what it counts and if I told you it would go straight into a street."

"The four years is still four years and it has not been argued about and it is not being argued about in a lane off Cordwain this morning."

"It has not been argued about anywhere," said Emil Brack. "Put it in a book and it is a deadline. Leave it in a man's head and it is a thing he is thinking about."

## The Thing On The Wall In A Hand That Is Not His

"There is still a line in one of these books with four in the afternoon in it."

"There is."

"Rub it out."

"No." Brack did not look at the book. "I said the whole of that in November and I have not got a better version of it now, and you are asking me the same question in March and I would be a different man to give you a different answer."

"That line is in a hand that is not mine and I did not put it there, and that is the whole of why it stays."

He shut the book and did not offer to show Lin Hao the page again.

"A correction is a copy, and a copy of an address is precisely what walked three flights off somebody else's landing in January, and you told me that at the end of January in a different building and you were right then."

## Who Is Saying Them Now

"There is a woman on a floor at the far end of a market who says them to people who cannot tell a clock from a shadow, and she has been at it since before you were at it yourself, and she has never heard of you."

Brack shut the second book. "About a month ago a man came in here with a bundle and told me she had said them to him fast and wrong, because he was about to go somewhere and she was in a hurry."

"That is the only name I am giving you today, and I am giving it to you because you have come into this lane twice in a year and both times you have gone away and done something."

"She is wrong."

"She is wrong about two words and she does not know it and she is not going to find out from anybody in this lane," said Brack, "and you are going to go and find that out, and I am telling you now that you will not be able to check her and you are going to find that out on the way."

Four thousand seven hundred and eighteen days. Less four thousand seven hundred and eleven, the second of this one, is seven, and nine less two is seven.

A man behind a counter under a window, who is paid nothing, said those three lines out loud in this city before that card came down, and he said them to a stranger he has never spoken to since, and he does not know he was the first, and he would go on saying them if he were asked to stop, and he has not been asked.

*Less four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is sixty-nine, and that rail has carried the same sheet through every one of the last sixty-nine of them, and it does not move again until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand two hundred and ninety-eight days, which are seventy-six months and eighteen days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
