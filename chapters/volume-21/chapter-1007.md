# Where The Pavement Corrects Itself

Four thousand seven hundred and four was the twenty-fifth of last month and the second of this one is day four thousand seven hundred and eleven, and four thousand seven hundred and eleven less four thousand seven hundred and four, the twenty-fifth of last month, is seven by the month count, with thirty less twenty-five taken out of the month that ended in between being five, and five with two taken out of the next month is seven, with the crossing named as itself.

## From A Quarter To Ten To About Eleven

He stood at the bottom of his own stair from a quarter to ten until about eleven on the morning of the second of this one, in the place at the foot of those four flights where the pavement is about nine feet deep between the arch and a wall about the height of a man, and he did not lean on the wall and he did not stand on his own second step.

He had come down early on purpose. He had been on that step every working morning since the twenty-fourth of March and on most of those mornings he had been on it in the dark at twenty to five, when the court had four people in it and all he could do was hand the sentence to whoever asked.

At a quarter to ten in the morning the court has daylight in it and the light comes off the wall opposite at about the height of a man's chest.

## What Four Feet Of Pavement Is For

At about ten past ten a woman came up that arch, stopped on it, and gave the three lines at the length in her ordinary voice to the arch and to whoever came up after her.

She had the second court in it.

She said a third court and said a second one.

## A Correction, Made In The Ordinary Voice

Another woman was standing at the other end of those nine feet with a bundle, and she said, without raising her voice and without any particular weight:

"Third court."

The first woman said: "Third court."

"That is right," said the woman with the bundle, and neither of them looked at the other, and the first woman said the three lines again from the beginning and got the court right and everything else the way she had it.

Lin Hao did not move.

He had heard that correction made in about four seconds and he knew that the woman who made it was right, because he had put that office on a third court himself and nothing said in that court will ever make it a second one.

## The Other Thing That Stands There

At about twenty past ten a woman came up that arch and said the three lines to a man who had a basket, and she gave the hours as four in the afternoon.

The man took them. He did not ask her where that was and he did not ask her to say them again.

Not one person in that court said a word about it. Lin Hao stood nine feet off with his hands behind his back and listened for about nine seconds for a correction, and none came.

He could hear a voice perfectly well at nine feet in daylight. He could not hear a face, and that turned out to be the whole of what a morning in that court has to give you.

## What He Knows And What He Cannot Get

He knows the hours. He said them out loud at twenty to five on the thirtieth of December in that court and he said them on the top landing every working morning since the fourth of December, and they run from a quarter to ten to half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour, and a woman giving them as four in the afternoon is wrong.

He knows she is wrong. He does not know whether one person in that court knows she is wrong, and he cannot find out, and he is not going to ask, and the reason he is not going to ask is that asking is a question about a person and there is a practice in that building about questions about persons and he is not one of the three people it belongs to.

## The Finding

He worked it out on that pavement at about half past ten, standing still, and it took him the better part of half an hour and it is the first thing he has found out about that court that he could not have found out about nine years ago.

A place where strangers correct each other is the first thing in this city in nine years that is better than a card.

And it is the first thing in this city in nine years that cannot be found out about afterwards.

He had been relieved in November. He had stood in this court at half past five in the dark and found out that the free place worked and that four feet of pavement will say three lines faster than four flights will carry one person up them, and he had gone up those stairs glad, and the gladness had lasted about four months.

## Why None Of It Comes Back

Not one of it came up four flights that morning or the next morning or the morning after that.

None of it went down and along that road to the counter beneath that window off Cordwain, where a man is paid nothing to tell strangers where that door is and has never once got it wrong.

None of it went into that book, and it did not go in because it was kept out, and it was not kept out by anybody. There was nothing there to keep it out of.

That is the shape of the whole of this city in the winter of year fourteen.

There is a book on a table four flights up with a row in it for every question that office has ever been asked. And there is nine feet of pavement at the bottom of that stair where a correction gets made in the ordinary voice, in daylight, for nothing. The second of those is better than the first of those, and it cannot be written down, and it never will be, because it is made by people who have never met each other.

## What He Does About It

He said nothing at all, he stopped nobody, and he did not go up to look.

He had been going to go up and look for about two weeks. He had worked out on the fourth that the only way to find out what happens at the foot of that stair is to stand at the top of it, and that is what he has done every morning since the third of December, and it does not work, and on the twentieth of January he found out why.

He stayed where he was.

He gave himself the reason in about nine words on that pavement at about eleven, and the reason was that he has not been the answer to that court since the twenty-eighth of October and never was the answer to it at all.

The second reason is the one he will stand behind, and it is that a man who cannot stop a thing and will not find out about it is not a man who is running it.

## The Woman With The Bundle

At about eleven he came off the wall and thought about crossing the nine feet and putting the hours right for the woman who had given them as four in the afternoon, and he worked out in about four seconds, standing still, exactly what would happen if he did it.

She would thank him. She would thank him in the ordinary voice, the way people thank a man who has been helpful, and she would go away with the right hours, and the people in this city who cannot read that card would keep getting them wrong, and not one of them would ever know that the man at the bottom of that stair had noticed.

And he would have made a correction, out loud, in daylight, in a court. A correction out loud in a court is a thing people repeat, and in about a month this city would have a version of those three lines in them in which a man in a white plate comes down and puts people right, and that version would be false, and he would have made it.

So he did not cross the nine feet.

That was not stopping anybody, because he has said out loud twice in that room that he will not stop anybody at the bottom of that stair. This was the other thing, and it was harder, and he stood still and did it.

## What He Goes And Asks Next

At about eleven he went down the arch and along that road, because there is one question about those three lines that he has never once asked anybody and that he worked out this morning is the only question in this city that can be answered out loud without an instrument.

He has been the first one. He has been the second one, and the fourth, and the ninth. He has never asked who said it first, or where they were standing when they said it, and it has never once occurred to him that a person who has spent nine years being the only true copy has never asked where the copy came from.

There is a lane off Cordwain with nine stools and a slate on the wall and people going past the end of it all day, and the man who keeps that lane has not put one thing past nine years that was not true.

He was going to ask him where it started, and he had got as far as the end of the arch before he worked out that he had not the least idea what he would do with the answer.

Four thousand seven hundred and eleven days. Less four thousand seven hundred and four, the twenty-fifth of last month, is seven, with thirty less twenty-five taken out of the month that ended in between being five, and five with two taken out of the next month is seven.

Four feet of pavement at the bottom of a third court made a correction in the ordinary voice in daylight and got the court right, and about nine feet off a man who had believed since the twenty-eighth of October that he was the answer to that court stood still and found out that the best thing about it is the one thing he can never have.

*Less four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is sixty-two, and that rail has carried the same sheet through every one of the last sixty-two of them, and it does not move again until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand two hundred and ninety-one days, which are seventy-six months and eleven days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
