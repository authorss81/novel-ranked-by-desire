# A Lighter Book On A Strap

Four thousand six hundred and ninety-seven was the eighteenth of this one and the twenty-fifth of this one is day four thousand seven hundred and four, and four thousand seven hundred and four less four thousand six hundred and ninety-seven, the eighteenth of this one, is seven, and twenty-five less eighteen is seven by the month count.

## At A Quarter To Ten On The Second Landing

They came up at a quarter to ten, the two of them together, the way they do, and she stopped on the second landing with her hand in the small of her back and the coil of the strap on the step, and he was on that landing with the door open above him and came down about nine steps to her because it saves her the corner.

That was the whole of the exchange and it happened about nine mornings in ten and neither of them had ever said good morning on it.

He had come up on the eleventh with one question and by the eighteenth he had three, and the one he had come up with was this: whether anything about that office has changed in a year.

## What She Can Tell By Feel

"You have been counting that stair in your head since you started on it," he said. "You have never written it down."

"No."

"And you have not been asked."

"Not once."

"Then tell me what has changed and I will not ask you anything else about it."

She shifted the coil at her feet with her heel and thought about it properly, which took her longer than he expected.

"Nothing has changed," she said. "That table is where it is. That room is cold in that month and it was cold in the month before. The book goes down at half past five and it went down at half past five. She types in the mornings and she is quicker in the mornings. And you are on that step, morning after morning, from a quarter to ten to half past five, and you have been since the twenty-fourth of March."

"That is nothing."

"That is everything except one thing and I have been saving it because it is not a thing anybody can do anything with."

## The Weight Of It

She put the strap over her shoulder and took it off again in one movement, the way a person tests a thing they have been carrying since a quarter to ten that morning.

"May I ask what it is for."

"You have carried it down those four flights and about four hundred yards every working evening for nine years, so you know what it is for," she said. "It is not for keeping a record. It is for keeping a thing moving between two people who are not in the same room, and a thing that is carried is a thing somebody is answerable for, and a thing that is not carried is a thing on a table."

"That is not in anybody's practice."

"Nothing in this building is in anybody's practice, and do not start it this morning."

"It is lighter," said the carrier with the bad back. "It has been lighter since it came back up that stair in the autumn and I did not say anything about it because there is nothing to say about a book being lighter, and because I said something about it to myself about a week later and did not say it out loud."

"How much lighter."

"I am not going to tell you how much lighter in numbers, because you would put the number somewhere and a number about a book is a number about that office." She put the strap down again. "I know it by the shoulder. Nine years of the same strap on the same shoulder at a quarter to ten every morning is the only instrument in this city that has been measuring that book for longer than you have been standing at the bottom of it."

"And what else."

"And that is the whole of what has changed in a year that a body can detect."

## The Change He Engineered

He had spent five months of last year getting that sentence, and he had got it, and it had arrived as the finding of a morning on a second landing from a woman who was paid nothing and had never been asked and had said no twice before she said the thing.

"It is the back board," he said.

"It is the back board and whoever let it in."

"And the tick."

She looked up at him.

"What tick."

"There was a tick," said Lin Hao. "About every four seconds, off the bottom edge of the back board of that book, and it went out through the floor and down that stair and into the bottom of that court, and anybody who stood at the foot of that stair on an evening could hear where it came from. I used to stand at the foot of that stair and tell which flight the book was on by the sound of it."

The carrier with the bad back did not answer him for about nine seconds.

"I heard it," she said. "I heard it for two years and I could tell which floor it was on, and I have not heard it since the end of September, and I have not told one person."

"Who would you have told."

"That is the half of it I have not got an answer to," she said. "I have been going up that stair for two years with the only sound in that building in my shoulder and there is not one person in it I could have said it to, and I have not spent a moment working out why, because it would have taken me about a year and I had a bad back."

## What It Took To Lose It

The only person in this city who could hear that book was standing on a second landing with her hand in the small of her back, and he had done that on purpose, in the dark, in a room with four doors open, and not one word of what it was costing had ever been written down anywhere.

He had not known it was costing that. He had known the money and the nine days and the four doors and the strap, and he had priced all of it on a bench in a room in a yard, and the sound had not been on the bench.

"You cannot get it back," she said, and she said it kindly, and it was the first time in about nine years she had said anything to him about the sound of his own office. "A new board does not tick. That is the trade and you asked for it and you paid for it and you got it, and the thing you lost is not a thing you can put back with money."

"I know what I lost."

"Do you," said the carrier with the bad back.

"Because you have spent five months telling that office that not one person can find out anything about it without a copy, and you have been right about that every single time. And the only person who could hear whether that book was on the second flight or the fourth was standing on a stair with a bad back."

"She has just told me and she is not going to be thanked, and if you thank me I will not be able to carry that strap past you another winter."

## The One Sentence About Three Voices

The other carrier came up about two steps and put the strap over his head and said nine words, and he had been getting to nine words on this landing since October.

"Three voices on that stair where there was one," he said. "And not one of the three of us knows which one a stranger is going to get."

"That was the tenth of December."

"That was the tenth of December and it has not stopped being true and you have not done one thing about it, because there is nothing to be done, and I have carried a strap up four flights about a thousand times since then and every one of those mornings I have been one of three people who sound like a man who stands at the bottom of his own stair, and I have never once said that sentence in front of him at the top of that stair."

"You have just said it."

"I have just said it and I would like it noted that I said it before somebody with a book came."

## What He Says About His Own Standing

He said it out loud at about half past two with the door open on the landing, to all three of them, and he defended none of it, because there was nothing there to defend.

"You have all three been carrying this office since the twenty-first of October on a sentence I said out loud to you in that room, and that sentence was standing at the bottom of that stair from the twenty-fourth of March."

"The cost of it is that a man in this city is now standing in a court seven hours a day telling strangers where a door is, and he cannot be produced by anybody who does not already trust him, and the people in this city who cannot read that card have not got another road up those four flights and this office is not going to build them one."

He had said all of that in October in a court. He said it again in a room, in daylight, with the door open, in February.

"That is the second time," said the clerk. "It does not get better the third time."

"I know that. I said it because a man who has heard me say it twice in two places knows it is not a mood."

## Why He Is Not Going To Thank Her

They went down at half past five with the book between them and the strap over her shoulder, and at the bottom of that stair she stopped and turned round and looked up four flights.

"Do not thank me."

"I was not going to."

"You were, about two hours ago, and you stopped, and I watched you stop." She put the strap up on her shoulder again. "I said the same thing to a man in a lane off Cordwain about six months ago and he is not the only one who has said it since, and I am not going to be thanked for telling you about a book you had a hand in."

He did not thank her.

Then he took the book and carried it down about four hundred yards and put it where it goes, and on the way he worked out, about half way, that the only sound in the whole of that building at half past five was the sound of two people going down.

Four thousand seven hundred and four days. Less four thousand six hundred and ninety-seven, the eighteenth of this one, is seven, and twenty-five less eighteen is seven, and a woman with a bad back who has never been asked anything told a man on a second landing that the only thing about that office which had changed in a year was that it had got lighter and had stopped ticking, and the tick was the only instrument in this city that could tell anybody where that book was.

*Less four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is fifty-five, and that rail has carried the same sheet through every one of the last fifty-five of them, and it does not move again until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand two hundred and eighty-four days, which are seventy-six months and four days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
