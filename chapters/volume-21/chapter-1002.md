# Three Flights And A Wrong Door

Four thousand six hundred and sixty-nine was the twentieth of this one and the twenty-seventh of this one is day four thousand six hundred and seventy-six, and four thousand six hundred and seventy-six less four thousand six hundred and sixty-nine, the twentieth of this one, is seven, and twenty-seven less twenty is seven by the month count.

## The Counter Under The Window

He walked over to the passage off Cordwain that morning, because a sentence that has left a room cannot be examined in the room, and the only instrument this city has ever had for hearing a sentence in a mouth that is not his own is a man who will say it out loud at his own counter for nothing.

The counterman was where he is always at, under the window, with his back to the wall so that anybody standing at that counter has his back to whatever is behind him.

He is paid nothing. He has told strangers where that door is every day for nine years and has never once put anything wrong into it, and Lin Hao had known him for a year and a winter and had never once seen him in a hurry.

## What He Wanted To Hear

He stood at that counter and asked a plain question, in the ordinary voice, which was what those three lines sounded like now.

The counterman told him.

## The Kindness That Started It

"It started with the light," said the counterman.

"I know where it started."

"You know where it started and you have not asked me where it went." He put a sheet down on the counter and took it up again. "Last January, the last week of it, there was a man in here in a hurry who could not make out the end of the second line, and I read it to him at the counter, and he said a thing about the light and I said it after him because he was right."

"The light goes off that street at about ten past four and you cannot read a card after it. That is all he said. That is the whole of what any of us did."

"And now."

"Now the light has gone out of it altogether." He turned round a cup that did not need turning. "It is a door at the top of three flights now. It has been a door at the top of three flights since about the turn of the year and I have had people come in here out of the half of this city that reads, saying they were told three flights, and I say four, and they go off up three."

## A Hand On It That Is Not There

"Somebody has invented that."

"No." He said it as flatly as a man reads a figure off a slate, which is what a thing sounds like when it has been true long enough to stop being interesting. "There is no hand on it. I have been sitting at this counter for nine years and I have watched the only thing that anybody has ever been able to do to three lines, and what they can do is add a true thing to them and walk away."

"That is the third time a stranger has told me that."

"Then it is right three times." He looked out of the window at the passage. "You cannot make a mouth take a thing back and you cannot find the hand that added it, because there is no hand. That man in January was being kind to a stranger in a hurry and he has probably never thought about it again, and what he said is still going out of that street nine times a day with his name not on it."

## Three Flights

Lin Hao asked him where a person would have heard three flights.

The counterman thought about it properly, which took him longer than Lin Hao had expected.

"I do not know," he said. "I have had it at this counter twice since the month turned and I did not take either of them for a thing, because a man in a hurry gets three flights into his head the way he gets his own street number into his head."

He put his thumb on the counter and thought about it for about nine seconds, which was longer than Lin Hao had expected from a man who has been answering strangers before lunch every day since before Lin Hao came to this city.

"I cannot put a finger on where either of them got it and I have been trying at it for a fortnight. I am also the wrong man to ask, because I am the last place they would have heard it before they heard it anywhere."

"You are the first place they would have heard it."

"That is what I said, and you have heard it."

## The Man Who Came After

There was a man at the counter after that, and he had a bundle under one arm that was not tied, and he was not in a hurry, which put him out of step with everybody else in that passage.

He said he had gone up three flights off a third court on the twenty-fourth and had knocked on a door and had been let in by a woman, and had been at a counter for about half an hour before he worked out that it was not that office.

He said it without any embarrassment at all and without any bitterness either, and Lin Hao had been the wrong man for him and knew it.

"You went up three flights."

"Three flights. It is three flights, and there is an arch at the bottom of it with one side walled to about the height of a man." He looked at the counterman. "Is that not it?"

"It is four."

## What The Woman Said When He Knocked

"The woman who opened the door," said the man, "spent the whole of that morning being told about a Bureau office by me."

He said it as though that were the part that had put him off, and it plainly was.

"I told her where to go and she thanked me and she has been kind about it since. Then about half an hour later I was in a room four flights up and a woman at a table read something out of a book to me in the voice that office keeps for it."

"And you thought, well, that is where I am."

"And I was not, and I have come down here because I did not want to be the sort of man who sends people up the wrong stair."

That was the whole of him and he took his bundle and went out into the passage.

## Three Flights Off Somebody Else's Court

He went and looked at it, because it had taken him a year and twenty days to learn that the only way to know what a sentence has become is to stand where the sentence goes.

There is a third court half a street off that passage with an arch at the bottom of it, three flights, and one side of it walled to about the height of a man, and Lin Hao went up those three flights and stood at the top on the wrong side of a door that is not that office.

Nobody came and nothing was said.

He put his hand on the rail and looked down, and from three flights he could see the whole of the bottom of that court, and the light on it, and two women standing at it with their backs to a wall.

That is the thing he has been unable to do since the fourth of December from the top of his own stair, where he has stood every working morning saying three lines to a landing because the end of his own card has gone soft.

Four flights up he cannot see the bottom of his own court. Three flights down somebody else's, he can see all of it, and there was not one thing on that pavement that had any of his three lines on it, and he had walked half a street to look at that.

He came down the three flights and he did not say the three lines in that court, and the reason, which he gave to himself on the second step and knew was the reason, is that there was nothing there to say them to.

## The Third Time

So a door at the top of three flights had been in the air of this city for a month, and it had come out of a man being kind in January, and it had come up to him on a stair about four hundred yards from his own, and the man who had carried it up that other stair was standing at a counter saying he did not want to be that sort of man.

The best of it, and he turned it over on the way back: for nine years he had been afraid of one thing, which was that somebody would sell those three lines or steal them or take them somewhere they were not wanted.

They had not been sold and they had not been stolen and they had not been put anywhere. A true thing about a light had got welded onto them by a man who was being kind to a stranger in a hurry, and it had walked about the city for a year changing its shape at every counter it came to, and it had arrived, without a hand on it, at the one thing in this world that cannot be taken back or put down.

## He Does Not Correct It

He had it in his mouth. It was four words long and it was true, and saying it to that counter would have taken about nine seconds.

He did not say it.

A correction is a copy. That office has refused a copy in every form since the twenty-first of October of the seventh year and the reason has never once been sentiment, which is that a copy of an address is the thing that goes three flights, and a second true version on a second piece of paper is not a repair, it is another mouth, and this morning he had found out that a mouth is enough.

He said instead, out loud, in that passage, where anybody going past could hear it: "The only thing anybody has ever been able to do to three lines is add a true thing to them and walk away."

The counterman nodded at the window. "I have been saying that for nine years."

## What Was On The Tray

Then a woman came through the passage with a tray of returns under her arm and asked the counterman whether the office that kept no figure of any person was still on the top of that stair, and he pointed, and she thanked him and went out.

Lin Hao watched her go down the passage and noticed, after she had gone, that she had asked the question in the voice of somebody who has a counter of her own and a queue at it, and that she was not a person who had read anything on a door.

He has not sent a person anywhere in nine years and he did not know that she existed until she went out of that passage.

What he had found out that morning was not a number and not a name. It was that the sentence had walked out of Cordwain and into a building where people carry trays, and that the place it had got to was a counter where a statement's weight is decided by how many hands have touched it.

Four thousand six hundred and seventy-six days. Less four thousand six hundred and sixty-nine, the twentieth of this one, is seven, and twenty-seven less twenty is seven, and a true thing about a light that a man said in a hurry in the last week of January had stopped being about the light and arrived at a door at the top of three flights without a hand on it anywhere in this city.

*Less four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is twenty-seven, and that rail has carried the same sheet through every one of the last twenty-seven of them, and it does not move again until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand two hundred and fifty-six days, which are seventy-five months and six days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
