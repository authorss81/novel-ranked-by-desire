# The End Of The Second Line

Four thousand six hundred and forty-nine was the thirtieth of December and the twentieth of January is day four thousand six hundred and sixty-nine, and four thousand six hundred and sixty-nine less four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is twenty.

## The Card Is A Year And Twenty Days Old

It went up on the thirtieth of December of the twelfth year at about a quarter past eleven in the morning, on three nails, in his own hand, with a street on the first line, a door on the second and the hours on the third, and not one name on it anywhere.

It is a year and twenty days old. Strangers have read it every day since the afternoon it went up and he has read it almost never, because a man who wrote a thing does not go on reading it, and because he can say what is on it without looking at any part of it.

The book came up at a quarter to ten on the strap between the two of them and was set down square on a square. There was a woman at that table with a question about a floor of a house, and a man after her with a piece of paper he could not read, and neither of them asked for him.

## He Says Them From The Top Of It

He gave them to the top landing at a quarter past ten, at the length, in his ordinary voice, with the door of that room open behind him, and he has done that every working morning since the third of December.

From up there he cannot see the bottom of his own court. He worked that out on the fourth morning and has gone up those four flights every morning since for a reason that has nothing to do with the card, and the reason is that a man who says a thing where he cannot see who is listening is a man performing it.

## A Foot Off It

Then he stood on that landing with his face about a foot from that card and looked at the end of its second line.

A foot is where a person stands when they are reading a door. He has used that distance every working morning since the third of December and had come up this morning braced for more of the same.

The first line was there in full.

The second line went about two thirds of the way and then stopped being writing.

## The Last Four Words

On the third of December those last four words were a grey thumbprint and the paper under them was still paper.

On the twentieth of January the paper under them is not paper any more. The corner above them has gone so far past soft that a thumb would print on it without ink, and the shine that came into it about a week ago has gone from grey to nothing at all.

He stood where he was and did not move for about nine seconds, and what came up in him was not a fact about paper.

It was that the copy of those three lines anybody in this city could check a stranger against is a man, and that the man has been unable to read the end of his own sentence off his own door since about the third of December, and has said so to one person.

## What The Card Says And What He Says

He said the first line out loud because it was there and he wanted to hear whether the card and his mouth still agreed.

Then he said the second line as far as the card goes, and stopped, and stood there on the landing with his arm still up and his mouth still shaped for a word that was not on the wall.

Then he said the whole of it out of his own memory, at the length and in the voice, and got every word of it, including the four the card had lost.

The arrangement he set up in the winter of year twelve was the door says what the door can still say and the man says what the man can still say. He built it deliberately and it has held for a year.

On the twentieth of January the man had more of it than the door did, and that is not a repair. It is the whole difference between a page and a person, and it has a date on it now, and today is the date.

## Soft Is What Paper Does When You Handle It

He had spent six weeks and five days looking at that line from a foot away, watching it soften, telling himself every one of those mornings that soft is what paper does.

Soft is what paper does when you handle it. That card is fixed at three places and loose at every other one, and the draught out of that room comes round the corner of the building whenever that door is on its hinge, and the worst of the movement is at the top corners of the second line.

He has known all of that since December and has never once stood in front of that card and watched a thing get worse.

## The Morning Goes On Under Him

From that landing he can hear that room and cannot see into it. People came up those four flights all morning and from the sound of it most of them were answered once and one of them was answered twice.

The clerk gave a woman a sentence in the voice that office reads everything in, and it took that woman a while to go down. The man who carries the strap answered a man with a piece of paper in about nine seconds and the man went down again looking at it.

None of them asked for him. He said out loud in that room with the door open in October that he would come up if anybody wanted him, and he has been at the foot of that stair every working morning since the twenty-fourth of March.

He had not intended to add them up and he found that he had stopped himself from doing it twice in an hour, and the reason is that a figure of that office is a figure of the people who come to it, and he has said out loud twice in that room that he is not going to have one.

## She Reads It Back To Him

He went in and put his coat over the chair at the end of that table and asked Wenna Raut to come out to the landing and give him the second line.

She came out. She stood where he had been standing and gave it him, the whole of it, the four words at the end included, without moving her head or putting her hand anywhere near it.

"It has not gone."

"It has gone further than it had gone on the third of December," he said. "From where I was standing this morning I could not have told you the last four words of it, and you stood beside it and gave them to me."

"Nine years," said the clerk. "And in nine years you have not asked a living person to read that back to you once, and you have asked me now."

"I have asked you now."

"Then stop trying to get a better answer out of the card and look at what you are actually reading." She went in and took her place at the end of the table. "You have stood a foot off that thing every morning since the third of December hoping it would say something different, and it has not said anything different, and you have known that for about four days."

## The Only Repair There Is

"That card is going," he said, "and there is one thing in this world that puts it right, and it is a hammer and four minutes and the same three nails, and I am not doing it."

"Say why, and then say what it costs."

"A renewal is the moment somebody decides what that door is. I have spent five months handing that decision to other people one at a time in their own words, and if I take a hammer to three nails on an ordinary working morning then it goes back into my hands and it stays there, and nothing in this city gets it out of their hands again."

She said nothing at all.

He had expected something and did not get it. He stood on that landing with the door open behind him and the cold coming off four flights of stair, and not one word of any of it went into that room, because three lines at the top of a door are not a question and are not an answer, and that book has never had a ruled line for one.

## What He Found Out Instead

He went down the four flights at about four with nothing in his hands, because that is not the hour the book comes down, and he stopped on his own second step and looked at the bottom of his own court, which he had not done since November.

That is the whole of what the morning gave him, and he had spent six weeks and five days not giving it to him.

He had been watching a card going and calling it paper.

And the other half of it, which he turned over on the second step with his hands in his pockets: in December he stood at the bottom of that stair in the dark with a whole court standing round him and said he was not going to say those three lines again, and this morning he found out that the sentence was finishing itself without him and without a hammer and without anybody deciding anything at all.

He had been trying to find out what a card does for six weeks and five days by looking at a card, and four flights of stair was not going to tell him anything else about it.

What he wanted to know was what those three lines sounded like in a mouth that was not his own, and how far they had gone by now, and he had one place in this city where that could be heard before the middle of the day, every day that anybody wanted to hear it, and it was not in that building.

Four thousand six hundred and sixty-nine days. Less four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is twenty, and on the first morning of the second year of that card the last four words of its second line were a grey thumbprint with his own thumb in it, and a man who had told a court he was finished with the sentence stood a foot from his own door and read as far as he could.

*Less four thousand six hundred and forty-nine, the thirtieth of December, is twenty, and that rail has carried the same sheet through every one of the last twenty of them, and it does not move again until the thirtieth of March.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is two thousand two hundred and forty-nine days, which are seventy-four months and twenty-nine days, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man's name under it.*
