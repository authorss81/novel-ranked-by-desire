# The Umbrella Woman Counts Copies

Three thousand four hundred and twenty-one was the second of this month and the ninth is day three thousand four hundred and twenty-eight, and three thousand four hundred and twenty-eight less three thousand four hundred and twenty-one is seven, and nine less two is seven by the month count.

## He Did Not Ask Her Anything

He came down the street at about half past three on the afternoon of the ninth of this month and he had nothing in his hands and he had a question ready, which is the shape of about nine visits in four years.

He did not ask it.

Nothing in this city lets him ask a woman who keeps an index what people say to her on a step, and the reason he said that out loud to a stairwell in June was not a rule, it was arithmetic: if he asks her once, then in about four years there is a question in that lane about a woman who keeps an index, and the woman who will ask it will be somebody who is right to.

She was under a frame with an umbrella on it and she did not look up and she said the first part of it before he had got the chair out.

“You are not going to ask me what people say to me.”

“No.”

“You came down here with a question and it is in your mouth and you have had it since the corner, and you are going to sit down and not ask it, and I am going to let you, because you have been doing that for about four years and you have never once been wrong about it.”

## The Number She Would Stand Behind

“There are three copies between that rail and a kitchen,” she said.

She put the shaft down in the vice and took her hands away from it.

“There have been three for about a year. One came in on a piece of its own paper with the canopy off the man who made it, and one came in folded, and one came in on the back of a thing that was not about this at all.”

“And I have never seen the room any of the three came out of, and none of the three has ever seen the room, and that is the whole of why they are copies and not the page.”

“And now there is a fourth.”

“Now there is a fourth,” she said, “and the fourth was made on the thirteenth of July on a sill in a corridor about four feet wide, in about four seconds, by the woman it is about, out loud in her own words after she had heard them once, and about four people were standing in that corridor when she did it.”

## What She Would Not Do With It

“You are not going to give me the other one,” he said. “You have heard me not ask twice.”

“You have heard me not ask twice,” she agreed.

“If you gave me the other one I would have five, and five is a number, and a number you gave me is a thing I would carry down about four hundred yards to a room off a third court, and I have said out loud on a landing in the last fortnight that I am not making anything that anybody could be asked to stop.”

“Then do not give it to me.”

“I am not giving it to you. I am telling you that I know about it and that it is not the same kind of thing as the other three, and I am not counting it with them.”

“That is a decision you have just made about your own room.”

“It is the first decision I have made about anything in this room in nineteen years,” said the woman who mends umbrellas, “and I have made it because you took the chair without asking, and I would have made the opposite one if you had asked.”

## About Four People A Year And No Money In The Room

There have been about four of them in about a year. The first could not read the sheet her husband brought her and brought it to a front room off this street, and the second was told about the first by the first.

“And you have not charged one of them a thing.”

“I have not charged one of them a thing and I am not going to, and I am going to tell you what would happen if I did, because you have been thinking about it for a year and a half and you have not worked it out.”

She put the frame down on the bench.

“If a thing costs money then it is a trade, and a trade can be asked to account for itself, and a trade that can be asked to account for itself is a trade somebody can arrive at and ask to be shown the books of. There are no books. There are four ribs on that wall and a bundle of canes in the corner that has not moved since the year you were born.”

“So the reason you charge nothing is not generosity.”

“The reason I charge nothing is that a woman who is paid is a woman who has been asked, and a woman who has been asked has been given something. If there is anything in this room at all then it is that about four people a year can put a thing down in it and be told that the thing they wrote down is what they said they would write down.”

“And they are never told whether it is right.”

“No. And that is the last sentence in this room and I have been saying it for about nineteen years. You cannot have an answer to it, and if you come down here in four years with one I am going to tell you to put it in your own book and not in mine.”
## A Copy Of A Line Is Not The Line

She turned the lamp shade a quarter, which she does at about three o'clock because the light comes round that room and there is a board across the middle of it.

“I am going to say the thing I have said before and then I am not going to say it again for a year.”

“Say it.”

“A copy of a line is not the line,” she said. “It is what somebody did with their own hand about nine minutes after a man who is paid nothing read a page to a rail. It is a record that they heard it.”

“It is not a record of what was said, and I have never once found out whether I was right, and I have never once been asked. If I had asked, they would not have come again, because a woman who corrects you is a woman you can be corrected by.”

He put his hands in his coat pockets.

“You are still right about all of that.”

“I have been right about all of that for about a year and a half and it has not got me anything, and you are a man who has come down this street four times in four years and been right every time, and neither of us has ever once been thanked.”

## The Thing She Asked For

She squared a rib against the frame with her thumbnail and then stopped with her hands off it, which he had not seen her do in four years.

“There is one thing,” she said. “In nineteen years I have not asked anybody for anything in this room. Not a man with a slate, not a clerk, not a woman from a church, and not you, and I have had four people write that foot of a page out for me and I have charged none of them anything and asked none of them for anything at all.”

“All right.”

“Read it out,” said the woman who mends umbrellas. “The bottom of it. Once. Here. With the door shut. I have written it out about four times for four people who cannot read it and not one of us has ever heard it from anybody who wrote it, and I want to know what it sounds like out of a mouth, and I am not going to die without knowing.”

“No.”

“You have not said why.”

“Because a sentence said once in a room by a man in a white plate to a woman who mends umbrellas is a copy in a room,” he said, “and the entire value of this room is that nothing said in it has ever been a record of anything.”

“And if I say it here then in about four years there is a woman in this city who has heard it and did not hear it at a rail, and nothing in this city can tell the two apart.”

She looked at him for about four seconds.

“That is right,” she said. “That is exactly right and I had worked it out about two thirds of the way down the street and I wanted you to be the one to say it.”

## And Then She Asked Him For Something

“You can go.”

“I will go.”

“You have been in this room about nine minutes and you have not asked me one thing, and I have thought about that for a year and a half and I have decided it is not kindness.”

“So I am going to ask you for the one thing, and you will not be able to do it, and I am asking anyway. It is nineteen years since I asked anybody for anything in this room and I would rather be refused than be polite.”

He waited.

“In four years,” she said, “when the bottom two inches under that striking have gone quiet in about four hundred kitchens, come down this street and tell me whether they came back. Not what it means. Not who said it. Whether they came back.”

“I cannot promise you a day in four years.”

“I know you cannot,” said the woman who mends umbrellas. “That is the only reason I am asking you and there is not one other person in this city I could ask.”

*Three thousand four hundred and twenty-eight days. Less three thousand four hundred and twenty-one, the second of this month, is seven, and a woman who charges nothing and keeps no list said that three copies exist between a rail and a kitchen, and that there is now a fourth made on a sill in four seconds by the woman it is about, and that she has made one decision in nineteen years in that room.*

*And she asked a man in a white plate for one thing, and it is a day in four years, and every other person in this city was somebody else.*

*Less three thousand three hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is thirty-nine, and the thirty-eighth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirty-nine of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand and eight days, which is thirty-three months and eighteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
