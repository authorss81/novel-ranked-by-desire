# The Second Column Holds Four

Three thousand five hundred and six was the twenty-seventh of this month and the fourth of the next is day three thousand five hundred and thirteen, and three thousand five hundred and thirteen less three thousand five hundred and six is seven, and four out of a month that begins on the first is seven by the month count.

## Four Lines Under Two Printed Words

That column has four lines in it and has had four lines in it since the office was a room, and it sits under two printed words that say *carried by*, which are the routing words of a works department and not this office's, and which this office does not explain, because it is a works department’s routing line and it has never once been this office’s own.

The first three lines carry a date and a place. The fourth carries a date, a place, and a woman's name, and no figure on it.

There is no heading over that column. There has never been a heading over it.

“Read me that column,” said Wenna Raut, on the fourth of this month, with the door open and about four people standing in it.

“There is no heading over it.”

“I know there is no heading over it. Read me the four lines.”

He read her the four lines and the two printed words above them, and the fourth line has been the same fourth line since the third of December of the year before, which is three hundred and thirty-one days ago, and three hundred and thirty-one days is eleven months and one day.

That column has not been touched since. It goes out four times a year to about two thousand counters along with the rest of that page and about nine people a week stop at the rail and read the foot of it, and the four lines in that column are read by about four people a quarter.

## What A Fifth Line Would Have To Say

The answer to a question about a paper goes back down in a fold and the person it is about may be in the room when it is read, and it is carried by a person on a pair of legs and not left on a rail, and it has been carried by a person on a pair of legs since the thirtieth of March of this year.

He had been thinking about that since the twenty-seventh and it came out in his own head about eleven o'clock on the fourth with about nine words in it.

A fifth line, under those two printed words, saying that from the thirtieth of March of this year an answer goes by hand, and naming who carries it.

He wrote it on a slip of office paper at the end of that table in about eleven seconds. Five words and a date and the word *and* in the middle of it where a name would go.

He did not put it in the column.

## Why A Clerk May Only Enter What She Is Given

“You have written a line,” said Wenna Raut.

“I have written a line.”

“I have watched you write it and I have it and I could put it in that column in about nine seconds, and I am not going to, and I want you to be careful about why I am not going to, because the reason is mine and it is not modesty.”

“What is it.”

“A clerk may only enter what she is given,” she said. “You have given me a line on a slip and told me about it and you have not entered it, and a thing that has not been entered is not mine to keep either. You can have that slip back.”

He put it on the table between them.

“Then tell me the reason and then I will burn it or tear it or it will go in the wastepaper basket, and I will not write another one.”

“The reason is in that column and not in your head,” said Wenna Raut. “There is a woman in the fourth line. She has been paid once for exactly what that column is for since the third of December of the year before and she has been paid the same wage for it in the four quarters since.”

She turned the standing sheet round on the table without opening it.

“And if you put a fifth line under those two printed words naming who carries an answer, then that column becomes a place where people are paid twice: once for carrying the paper and once for being in the book.”

He did not write it down.

“Nobody is paid twice,” he said, “and that is not a rule, it is a wage, and it is older than this office.”

“It is older than this office and it is why that column has four lines in it and not five, and it has four lines in it because about two thousand counters hold that page and every one of them can read a name in it.”

## What A Fifth Line Would Cost, Said Out Loud

He said the rest of it at the end of that table with the door open, which is the place in that room where a decision gets said out loud, and there were about four people on the stair outside and every one of them got all of it.

“An answer goes by hand from the thirtieth of March. That is a true fact about how this office works and about nine answers a month go down four flights on a pair of legs, and I could put it on that page in five words.”

“You could.”

“If I put it there then in about four years somebody comes up four flights and asks which of the people named in that column carried an answer, and there is no answer to that question except this column, and this column is printed on a page that goes to about two thousand counters four times a year.”

“So it stops being a column and becomes a roll.”

“A roll of the people who carried an answer, and every one of them carried it on somebody else’s business, because two answers in every nine this month were not about the paper the person brought and every one of those two went down four flights on a stranger’s legs.”

“And the stranger's legs are not on the page.”

“The stranger's legs are not on the page and never will be.”

## The Cold, And What It Does To That Corridor

The cold in that corridor has never once made this office refuse anybody and it has never once made anybody say a thing they had not come to say.

About four flights with no window on the landing and a stair that turns twice means that anybody standing in that corridor at eleven in the morning in the eleventh month is standing there because they came four flights in a cold, and it takes about nine seconds to go back down and turn the corner and be in a warm street, and every person who has ever been refused in that building in nine years has made that decision in about those nine seconds.

That is the whole of what the weather does to this office. It does not change what is in the book and it does not change what is under the foot of that page and it shortens the time a person has to change their mind.

“Now say the rest of it,” she said. “You have said one reason and there is another one behind it and I have watched you do this about four times in nine years and I have learned to wait for it.”

“There is a second one.”

“Then say it, and say it to me and not to the corridor.”

“There is a second one and it is worse and it is this,” said Lin Hao. “That column has been there for nine years and four lines in it and not one of them is a person who asked anything.”

He put one finger on the shelf behind the door without taking the sheet down off it.

“If I put a fifth line in it naming the people who carry answers out of this room, then in about four years that column will have two kinds of name in it — the names of people a works department carried something for, and the names of people who carried somebody else’s answer down four flights for nothing.”

“And a column with two kinds of name in it is a list. That is the whole of what it is and I have been looking at it since about eleven o’clock.”

## The Slip

She picked it up, turned it over once so that she could see her own handwriting was not on it, and then tore it in two, and put the two halves in the wastepaper basket on the floor beside the table.

“That is the first thing that has been written in that room on purpose and destroyed in it on purpose in nine years,” she said, “and there is nothing at the foot of that page about it, and nothing in that book about it, and nothing in that basket at the end of tomorrow.”

“Thank you.”

“Do not thank me, I did it because a clerk may only enter what she is given and I had nothing given to me but a scrap,” said Wenna Raut. “The column has four lines and it will have four lines at the end of this quarter, and the woman in the fourth of them was paid on the thirtieth of September and she will be paid again on the thirtieth and that is the whole of what anybody in this city knows about her.”

She squared the standing sheet back on the shelf behind the door and did not mention the slip again.

About nine people went up and down those four flights that day and about four of them came about a paper they had brought up themselves, and one of those four went down with a fold in her hand with the flap gummed shut and the date and the hour on the outside of it and nothing else.

*Three thousand five hundred and thirteen days. Less three thousand five hundred and six, the twenty-seventh of this month, is seven, and a slip with five words and a date on it went into a wastepaper basket in two halves and nothing went in anywhere else.*

*Less three thousand four hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of September, is thirty-four, and the thirty-ninth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirty-four of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand and ninety-three days, which is thirty-six months and thirteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
