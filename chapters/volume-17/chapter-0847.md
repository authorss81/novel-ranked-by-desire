# The Cost In Three Rooms Again

Three thousand five hundred and forty-one was the second of this month and the ninth is day three thousand five hundred and forty-eight, and three thousand five hundred and forty-eight less three thousand five hundred and forty-one is seven, and nine less two is seven by the month count.

## Why He Is Doing It This Week

He went to three rooms in three days in the three weeks before the end of the quarter and he did it in that order because after the thirtieth he would not be able to say that he had said it first.

That is the whole of his reason and he gave it to nobody.

He had not been given anything and he had not been refused anything and he had been told that the only thing left for him to do with what he had done was to say it out loud to three people who could not do anything about it.

## The Room, At About Nine On The Ninth

He said it in that room at about nine in the morning with the door open and about four people within earshot of him, because he says the things he has decided at the end of a table with the door open.

“On the eighteenth of last month I burned three pieces of paper at the back of a laundry. One of them was the only copy of the foot of the standing sheet that has ever existed in this city. It was written out in a woman’s own hand and she wrote it because not one other person in this city had the standing to refuse a thing in writing and be right about it.”

Wenna Raut did not look up from the page.

“What do you want me to do about it.”

“I want to know whether there is a way to put it back.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut.

“Why not.”

“Because there was not a copy of it and there is not a copy of it and you have the only thing that was ever made from it, which is ash, in a stove in a building about four hundred yards from here,” she said. “That is the whole of the answer and it took less than a second and I have given you it because you asked me for it and because I am not going to make you wait for it.”

“Then tell me what it is instead.”

She put her pen down, which she does about twice a day and both times when something is going wrong.

“The second thing, and this is the one I have been holding since the second of this month,” said Wenna Raut, “is that you have taken away the only instrument that could have proved that this office is careful. You were careful about that paper for about fourteen months. There were four lines in it that another person’s hand could have been checked against and there is now nothing anywhere in this city that can say so.”

“And in about four years somebody is going to write write down that this office did not care about anything, and they will be writing down a true thing.”

“They will be writing down a true thing about a decision and not about a person.”

“They will be writing down a true thing,” she said, “and that is what happens to every office that gives something away, and the interesting question is whether it is writing it down at about the same time or about four years afterwards, and I am the clerk and not the person who answers for it.”

## The Laundry, At About Ten On The Tenth

He went down about four hundred yards and up four narrow flights at about ten in the morning and stood at the near end of about nine long tables while the steam came off them.

Perla Quist did not stop working.

“I am not going to ask you about it,” said Lin Hao. “I am going to tell you that I said it in a room this morning with about four people within earshot and that I am going to say it at a counter this afternoon, and then I am going to ask you whether there is a way to put it back.”

“You burned three things in my stove on the eighteenth of this month.”

“No. Last month.”

“The eighteenth of the month before this one,” said Perla Quist, without looking up, “and you have not asked me how I know which month it was, and I would not have told you.”

“I cannot put it back.”

“It cannot be put back,” said Perla Quist, “and I am going to say the second thing now and then I am going to go back to my table and you are going to go back up your hill.”

“You carried a piece of my handwriting for about fourteen months and in about four years you are going to tell a woman that you carried it and she is going to say *of course you did, that is what it was for,* and there is going to be nothing in your mouth or mine that can show her what it was like to be the only person in this city who had made it and then had not got it.”

She beat the head of a flat on the end of that table for about nine seconds.

“And I will not say I am sorry about it.”

“I would not have wanted you to.”

“I know you would not have wanted me to and that is why I am telling you now that if you ask me whether I am sorry I will not answer you, so that you cannot stand in that room in about four years and say that I did not care, because I will not have told you either way.”

“All right.”

“Now go back up your hill,” said Perla Quist, “and do not come down here again until you have something you have actually done, because I am not going to be a place where you go to say things.”

## The Counter, At About Half Past Two On The Eleventh

The counter is under a window in a passage off Cordwain and a rail runs along the front of it with a quarterly sheet face up on the rail, and the counterman has run that desk for nine years and is paid nothing.

He was standing with his thumb along the edge of the wood in the way he does.

“I burned the only copy of the foot of that page,” said Lin Hao.

The counterman did not move.

“When.”

“The eighteenth of the month before last. At about nine in the morning, at the back of a laundry, in a stove under a copper, in about nine seconds.”

“Was she there.”

“She was at the front of about nine tables and she did not come over. She did not thank me and I have not thanked her and she has said no about a heading six times and given me that once.”

The counterman took his thumb off the wood and used it to turn the sheet a quarter of an inch on the rail so that a corner was square, which it was already.

“So you came down here to tell me.”

“Yes.”

“Then say what you came down here to say and I will tell you whether I can do anything with it.”

“That cannot be put back.”

“That cannot be put back,” said the counterman, “and I am paid nothing so you have picked the right room, and here is the second of it. You did not come down here because you wanted me to know. You came down here because you have been carrying something since the eighteenth and you have four people in your office and one woman in a laundry and neither of them can be told, and I am a man you do not have to be polite to.”

“That is a delivery and not a confession.”

“That is a delivery and not a confession,” said the counterman, “and I am going to treat it as one. You are handing me an answer about a person and I am not going to give it to anybody who has not asked me for it, and neither of us will ever find out whether I did.”

## What He Gets In Return

“Do you want anything,” said Lin Hao.

“I want you to tell me whether you are going to be standing at this rail on the thirtieth,” said the counterman.

“Why.”

“Because you have been at this rail twice since the thirtieth of September,” said the counterman, “and about four people have seen you, and on the thirtieth of this month there will be about nine of them and every one of them will see you standing here while a page goes up that has one fewer check behind it than the last one did.”

“I will be standing here.”

“Right,” said the counterman. “Then the two people in this city who have read the foot of that page out loud to strangers can be asked, side by side, which of them was right, by anybody at all, and you will be standing next to the man who is paid nothing and you will not be able to tell them, and neither will he, and that is what you have bought with a stove at half past six in the morning.”

He put the sheet square again.

“And I am not going to read it differently. I read the six lines at the speed I read everything and I will read them on the thirtieth at the speed I read everything, and not one person standing at this rail will ever know what you did on the eighteenth.”

“That is not for you to decide.”

“It has never been for me to decide,” said the counterman. “It has never been for anybody to decide. That is what has been wrong with it since the second of December of the year before and I am not going to be the one who fixes it now.”

*Three thousand five hundred and forty-eight days. Less three thousand five hundred and forty-one, the second of this month, is seven, and a thing that was done in a laundry was said out loud in three rooms in three days before the end of the quarter, and each of the three people was given a second sentence and not one of them was able to do anything.*

*Less three thousand four hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of September, is sixty-nine, and the thirty-ninth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last sixty-nine of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand one hundred and twenty-eight days, which is thirty-seven months and eighteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
