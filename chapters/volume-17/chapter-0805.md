# Four Inches Under Rain

Three thousand two hundred and sixty was the twenty-first of this month and the twenty-eighth is day three thousand two hundred and sixty-seven, and three thousand two hundred and sixty-seven less three thousand two hundred and sixty is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven by the month count.

## The Form Up Since The Seventh

Rain coming off the hill in a wind that got under the batten and made the paper talk against the wood.

He stood about eleven feet back at about half past five with his collar up, because that is where he goes, and the month's form had been on that board since the seventh and would come down on the thirtieth, and the paste along the top edge had gone to a grey nothing.

The two boxes printed at the foot of it were blank, and have stood empty since a morning in the first year when a clerk was handed a sheet and a form and nobody told her what either of them was for, and she put two words into one of them because the box was there and the words were there.

One is for the figure of the thing at the address. One is for the standing of it.

Under the lower batten, held against the board where the paste had gone thin, was a piece of office paper with a date at the top in a clerk's hand and a pencil line drawn across the bottom of it in a hand of her own, face down against the wood, where you would have to kneel on wet stone to guess that it was there at all.

It was where she had left it. Nobody had taken it, folded it, written on it, or turned it over, and the lower batten was holding it in exactly the way it had been holding it on the sixteenth of December.

## The Boy And The Step Two Doors Up

A boy came up the slope carrying the board on one arm and a paste brush in the other hand, with a corner of fresh paste on his cuff, and he set the thing down against the kerb, adjusted it with his boot without looking at it, and went back up the hill without looking at either of the two printed boxes or at the paper under the batten.

Lin Hao did not speak to him. He wanted nothing from him, and there is nobody in this city who has the business to put that question.

Two doors up, a woman with a short-handled broom was working at a step in the rain and went on working at it while he stood there, and did not look at him, and he did not look at her, and neither of them ever would.

## What Was New In The Four Inches

About forty words in about four hands had stood under the striking on that dressed stone since early October. He had been down to read them in October and in November and in December and in January, and he had counted the same four hands every time, and he had known every one of the two words in them because he had been there when they were scratched.

It was more than about forty now.

He got down into the rain on his knees to see it properly, and the light was going and the water was running down the face of the stone in four places at once, and he read what he could and stood back and read what he could not.

There were five hands in it. There might be a sixth in the wet at the left-hand edge where the water was standing.

Every word in the four inches was about a building off a third court. That had been true in October and it was still true, and about nine people in a week get down low enough to read them, and the office knows nothing whatever about any of it and has no way to be told and is not asked.

The new ones were not about a building.

The new ones were about answers.

That is the change, and it is the whole of what he had come down the hill for, and it took him four attempts to see it.

He got his eye close enough to the stone to make out four of them and had to stand up in the rain then and let the water come into his collar, because what he had read was a woman saying out loud, in a hand with a nail in it, that she had been told what the office holds about her and that it was about her neighbour, and that she wanted to know how anybody could sleep after that.

That sentence is about a person and it is not about a paper.

## The Arithmetic He Could Not Say Out Loud

So he counted, because it was raining and he had an hour and there was nothing else to do with it.

He counted hands and not words, because hands were the only thing on that stone he could be sure of in the light he had.

Five hands. Two of them were old and three of them were not. The three new ones ran about nine days apart by the weather on them, which is worse than a date, because weather is not a thing a clerk can enter.

Thirty-one days this month. Sixteen days since the office was told that any person might ask.

He put those two numbers beside each other in his own head about four times and both times they would not sit down next to one another. The third time he gave up. The fourth time he understood that there was nothing in the city that could make them sit down next to one another, and that if he tried to make them he would be building a list of about four hundred people who had gone low to that stone, and he does not build lists.

That was the whole of what he wanted to do with the hour.

Two inches is a different thing and he was careful with himself about the difference. About four hundred households in this city have a place under a mantel where something can be put in a hand, and that is where about four hundred people wrote down what was done to them, and he does not know a single one of them by name and is not going to be told one.

Four inches is one stone at the bottom of a slope, and no instrument in this city can receive a mark on it, and it is not asked to.

The two words that have never been written in any book in this city were still not among any of the five hands. There is one rubbing of them and it is in the inside of a boy's coat and it stays there, and he had not seen it in about four years and was not going to see it in this rain.

## The Branch Office At Eleven

He did the third floor that night from eleven until half past five with a bucket in each hand and about four washrooms to do between them, and it was the fourth of his four and the third of the three off after it.

There is a thing rank one gets, and it is not a privilege and it is not a punishment, and it is that his hours are not his own and not one person in that building may ask him for anything at any hour of them.

He asked for that rota in the eighth month of the sixth year and has never once been sorry for it. On a night like this one it was the only arrangement in the city in which he was going to be left alone with his own head for five hours, and he did not want it, and he used it.

He thought about the five hands for about two of the five hours and then he stopped, because there is a point past which a thing you cannot act on is only a thing you are carrying for somebody who does not exist.

At about half past one he stopped for four minutes at the top of the stairs the way he does, and on the way down he washed his face at the sink on the half landing with cold water and his hands, which is a routine he has had since the fourth year and has never once been able to shorten.

## The Last Of It Is About Him

He went back down the slope the next morning at about a quarter past six, before the light was any use, because the night had left him certain of one thing, which was that there was a newest thing on that stone and he wanted to see it in daylight and not guess at it.

There was one.

It was scratched very low, almost at the bottom of the four inches, in a hand he did not know, and it was the only sentence in the whole of that stone that was not about a building.

It was about a man who is paid nothing and stands under a window and reads the bottom of a page out loud to whoever is standing at the rail, and will not be told what any of it is for.

It was weeks old. It had been there before the office was told that any person might ask, and it had been under the rain all that time and it had not washed off, because a nail in dressed stone does not wash off.

He stood there with the water coming off the batten onto his shoulder and understood the whole of it in about nine seconds and did not like any part of it.

That dressed stone is the only place in this city where about four hundred people wrote down what was done to them. It has been empty for seven years and then it has not been empty, and in about four months, if the thing Brack has been saying since June is right, it is going to go quiet a second time and never come back.

And the three newest hands on it are not writing down what was done to them.

They are writing down what was said about them.

And the newest line of all is about him, and he did not put it there, and there is nothing in this city that can ask a person about a nail in a stone, and he stood in the rain and let it be there.

*Three thousand two hundred and sixty-seven days. Less three thousand two hundred and sixty, the twenty-first of this month, is seven, and the four inches under the striking carried five hands and not four, and the three newest of them were about answers and not about a building, and the newest line of all was about a man who is paid nothing.*

*Less three thousand two hundred and nine, the thirtieth of December of this year, is fifty-eight, and the thirty-sixth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-eight of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is eight hundred and forty-seven days, which is twenty-eight months and seven days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
