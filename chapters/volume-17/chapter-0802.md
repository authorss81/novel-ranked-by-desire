# Nine Questions In Eight Days

Three thousand two hundred and forty was the first of this month and the eighth is day three thousand two hundred and forty-seven, and three thousand two hundred and forty-seven less three thousand two hundred and forty is seven, and eight less one is seven by the month count.

## Eleven Pieces Of Paper Again

Eight working days. Eleven pieces of paper through a front door that has never had a door onto a street at it.

It had been eleven in eight days in the spring, and Wenna Raut had said so at the time, and they had both understood that the number was about the weather and the queue and the fact that a building with no door onto a street had nowhere for anybody to put a coat.

It was eleven again.

She kept the count on the corner of the page in pencil, the way she had kept it since the second of December, and she made him read it out because a clerk who keeps a number in her own hand has to say it to somebody.

“Eleven pieces of paper,” she said. “Nine questions. About nine people, which is not the same as nine and is not the same as eleven, and I have written all three of those figures down separately and I am going to keep writing them separately.”

“Why separately.”

“Because in about four months somebody is going to stand at a rail and say nine questions came through that door in eight days,” said Wenna Raut, “and either it is true or it is not, and it will be true about the questions and false about the people, and the only thing in Ostara that can hold the two of them apart after that is the fact that I wrote them down in three places.”

## Nine Of Them Want An Answer In Writing

Nine of the eleven were questions.

That was the change, and it was a change in what the counter was for, and it had been standing in the room since the second of December without anybody putting it in a sentence.

The other two were papers received, entered as received, with a date, which is what the front door has been for nine years.

The nine were questions, and the instruction of the second of December says the office shall answer each of them in writing, and the question and the answer go in the bound book with a date and an hour against each, in the words the person used, and a row may be added to and not taken from.

“Which of the nine can be answered today,” said Lin Hao.

“Six,” said Wenna Raut. “Four of them are about a paper the asker brought, and I can tell a person in about nine seconds what this office holds about them and it is one row with a date on it. One of them asks whether a thing they were told in the autumn was in this office, and the answer is no, and the answer goes under what they were told.”

“And the other two.”

“The other two are not about the asker.” She put the pen down. “One is about a paper a neighbour of his brought. And one of the two arrived on the second of this month and is the same question the man in the cap has been down here three times about, and he will not say which paper and will not say whose, and the line of the second of December does not require him to.”

## The First Answer Goes Out

She wrote the first one at about eleven in the morning on the fourth working day.

It was four lines. It said what the office held about a woman who had brought a paper in the autumn, which was one row with a date against it and an hour against the date and no figure and no name, and it said the answer in the words she was given and added nothing to them.

She dated it at the head and put the hour against the date, and read it back to herself once in the voice she reads everything in, and folded it.

Then she did not read it out.

He stood at the other end of the table.

“You have read back everything you have written since the tenth of June.”

“I have.”

“You read back nine words a man said at this counter in the spring to about nine people in that corridor, and you read a denial back to about four of them who had carried a sentence up a lane.”

Wenna Raut kept both hands on the folded sheet.

“That was a line,” she said. “That was something that office had put on a page and needed a person to hear it. This is an answer, and an answer has one person in it, and if I read it out in a corridor about four feet wide then about nine people have heard an answer that was not theirs, and one of them is standing in that corridor at this moment waiting to be told whether his neighbour brought a paper here.”

She put the sheet on the wood and squared it against the edge of the table.

“Carry it down four flights,” she said. “And do not open it, and do not stand about in the passage while you have it.”

## What It Costs, Said By Her

He came back up the second flight with the cold in his sleeves and stopped where it had always stopped him and waited until the stair was empty behind him.

“The price,” he said. “Say it out loud. You have been made the mouth of this office four times since the spring and you said the price every single time before you did it.”

“It is in that room and not out here.”

“It is in that room and I am standing in it.”

Wenna Raut had not moved from the table.

“From this month I write things I cannot check,” she said.

“Every one of the nine answers goes down in words I did not choose, about a person I have never seen before and may never see again.”

“And when I have written it, the office has said it out loud, and it cannot be called back. About nine times a month I shall be wrong in public. About nine times a month the person it is about will be told it in that room with me behind the table, and will go down four flights believing it.”

“That is the part I cannot improve and cannot stop. There is not one thing in this city that lets a person check a sentence against the thing it is about, and I am going to be the mouth of nine of those a month, and I have never once been the mouth of anything else.”

“And you cannot improve it.”

“No,” she said. “I could make it kind. That is what improvement is. If I make it kind then the book stops being what the person said and starts being what I thought they meant, and in about four years somebody is going to read nine answers in a row and work out what this office thinks of people.”

## The Neighbour Question Is Still On The Table

The man in the cap came on the sixth working day and the eighth.

On the eighth he got as far as the wood and stood there and said the same thing he had said three times, which was that he wanted to know whether a paper is here that was brought here by somebody else, and that he would not say which paper and would not say whose, and that if it was written down he would find out.

Lin Hao could have told him no in about nine seconds, and no would have been true, and he did not do it.

Because an answer here is not a fact about a paper. It is a fact about a person. The same row about the same paper is a receipt when one person asks and a whereabouts when another person asks, and the office has one kind of ink.

“You may not have that answer alone,” he said at last. “I am not refusing you. I am telling you that I do not have a way of giving it to you that is not also an answer about somebody who is not standing here.”

The man looked at him for a while, and then at the pen on the table, and then at the front door he had come in through, which is a door onto a yard and not onto a street.

“Twice in eight days,” he said. “That is what I have done. And both times I stood at that wood and said it in the one form I am able to say it in, which is a question with my own hands on the rail and my own face doing what faces do in a cold passage at the end of a day’s work.”

The clerk did not look up.

“You may not have it alone,” she said. “That is what he has just told you and he is right and I would have told you the same. There is no way of answering what you are asking that does not put a fact about another person into a room that other person is not standing in, and I am the only person in this city who would know whether that had happened before and it has.”

The man looked at him for a while.

“Then I will come back with her,” he said.

“You may bring her. You may not bring her and ask on her behalf.”

## A District Officer Says It Is Properly Put

She was in the corridor at about half past three on the eighth, in a plain coat with a book under her arm, and she had come up four flights in an office that is not hers on the last working afternoon of a week in a building with no street door, which is a thing a district officer does about nine times a year and is right about every time she does it.

Wenna Raut read her the question as it stood in the words the man used, with the date at the head of it and the hour against the date.

The district officer read it twice with her finger following the line and no expression at all.

“That is properly put,” she said. “One question. One paper, though the paper is not named. A date and an hour. The words of the person asking and not yours, which your instruction is explicit about and which almost nothing else in this city does.”

She closed the book under her arm.

“I have spent nine years at claims about paper and there is not one thing wrong with the shape of that.”

“I am telling you that as a person whose standing it is to know, and I want it understood that I have no standing whatever to tell you whether to answer it. If you want somebody in this city to tell you whether to answer it, you will have to go and find a person who has already decided. There are not many of those and none of them work here.”

“That is worth the climb,” said Lin Hao.

“It cost me one afternoon,” said the district officer, “and it is the only thing I have done this month that anybody upstairs will care about, and I would do it again. You may write down that I said it was properly put. I do not want it written down. Write down that I read it.”

Then she went down four flights with the cold coming up after her, and the question was still on the table where it had been since the second of this month, properly put, dated, hours, and in a stranger’s words, and no practice in nine years for answering one person about another.

*Three thousand two hundred and forty-seven days. Less three thousand two hundred and forty, the first of this month, is seven, and eleven pieces of paper came through that front door in eight days, nine of them questions, and about nine people behind all eleven, and the first answer of the year went down four flights in a fold and was not read out in the corridor.*

*Less three thousand two hundred and nine, the thirtieth of December of this year, is thirty-eight, and the thirty-sixth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirty-eight of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is eight hundred and twenty-seven days, which is twenty-seven months and seventeen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
