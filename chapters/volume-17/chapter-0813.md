## Nine People And A Counter That Works By The Order Of Them

## The Bundle That Came In Tied

She reached up, got a bundle out of the flap and set it on the deal shelf, and it was nine inches thick and it was tied with string in three places and there was a tag on the end of it with a day and an hour and a counter number.

“Those come up from the hall tied,” she said. “They come up in the order they were handed in, and they go down to me in that order, and I untie them in that order and I answer them in that order, and when the string comes off they go back to the hall as one lot.”

She turned the bundle over without untying it.

“Now tell me what happens when I open one in the middle.”

“You answer somebody before the person who was in front of them.”

“No,” said Wren Kallo. “I do not answer anybody out of order. Nothing in this hall makes me and nothing in this city makes me. What happens when I open one in the middle is that the string comes off a bundle that is not tied, and I put the bundle back, and it can never be tied again in the order it came in.”

“A question answered out of order is a favour,” she said. “That is the whole of what it is. ## What It Costs The Man At The Front

He put his hand on the boards to shift his weight and she looked at it and did not say anything about it.

“Tell me the cost,” he said. “Not the rule. The cost, in the room.”

“The man at the front.”

“Yes.”

“He finds out two things and he finds them out at once,” said Wren Kallo. “The first is that he is not the front. The second is that there was something behind him worth more than what he came with, and he did not know that and he is now going to spend the rest of his life being a man who came second.”

She set the bundle down.

“And the one it goes to does not find out anything except that it worked. He will walk out of this hall with his thing done and come back every time he has one, and he will come back expecting the middle of the bundle. The reason he gets it is that I have a face, and there is not one form anywhere in this building he can put in front of me about it.”

“That is the cost.”

“That is the cost to two people and the benefit to one,” she said, “and I have watched the arithmetic of it work in three other halls in this city in eleven years and it does not improve with the number of times.”

## What She Offers, And What It Is

“You may have it,” said Wren Kallo.

He looked up at her in about three and a half feet of headroom.

“I am not joking and I am not testing you,” she said. “You came down here on the fourth of a month to find out whether an office can answer one person about another. I have just told you that the second counter of a division building can do it in about four seconds.”

“I have a queue at my rail at this moment with about nine people in it, and the fourth of them is a woman who has asked me twice this morning whether this office holds anything about her husband, and I can answer her out of order by half past twelve.”

“Then why do you not.”

“Because I would be doing it once for a man I do not owe anything to, and he would go back up four flights and say that a counter in a division building answers people out of order, and by the end of the month there would be a queue at that counter with about nine people in it and no order in it whatever, and the order is the only thing I have.”

“You have spent four months deciding that this office cannot have a favour,” she said. “That is the most expensive decision anybody has ever made for nothing and I am not going to argue with it, because I have had eleven years of it and I would make it again.”

## Two Days

He got his breath on the boards for a minute with the lamp on the bracket over him and the boots going past above the grating.

“Here is what you are actually doing,” said Wren Kallo, tying her apron, “and you have not seen it yet and I am going to say it now while it is in front of me.”

She set the untied bundle on the shelf in the order it had to go back in.

“You have one book. It has two kinds of thing in it and a clerk who may only enter what she is given. A question belongs to the day it was asked. An answer belongs to the day it is given. That is not bookkeeping, that is the only reason either of them can be found again.”

“And if the two days are the same day.”

“Then the book is a book of answers with a question in it somewhere near each one,” she said, “and order stops meaning anything at all in it, and after that a clerk cannot tell anybody which of the two things she is looking at, and you will have lost the one instrument you have got.”

## About Four People At A Counter Asking About An Office That Is Not This One

“Nine at my rail this morning,” she said. “Four of the nine asked me this month whether this office holds anything about their neighbour, and not one of them would say which office they meant, and three of the four came back a second time, and I have sent four people away from a counter they had no business at.”

“Why are they at your counter.”

“Because your office is up four flights in a building with a door onto a yard, and because you have a queue in a corridor about four feet wide, and because about nine people have been standing in that corridor since the second of December,” said Wren Kallo. “This is the ground floor. You can walk in off the street.”

She came up out of the well with the untied bundle under her arm in the order it had to go back in.
