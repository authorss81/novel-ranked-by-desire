# Two Records And No List

Three thousand four hundred and thirty-five was the sixteenth of this month and the twenty-third is day three thousand four hundred and forty-two, and three thousand four hundred and forty-two less three thousand four hundred and thirty-five is seven, and twenty-three less sixteen is seven by the month count.

## Twenty-One Days After Five On A Landing

He found out at the sink on the half landing at about eleven, washing his own hands, which is a thing he has done on every night for six years and has never once watched.

The six short lines were off.

He had put them there on the morning of the second of this month, one for each of five people refused between the second and third floors, with the hour against each, in his own hand, in pencil, because it was the only piece of paper in that building that could not be produced in a room and could not be taken off without taking the man with it.

Three weeks later the pencil had gone in patches, the way it goes, and there was not one moment in any of those three weeks at which he could have said afterwards that he had watched it go.

He got four of the six back.

The first, the second, the third, the fourth, in that order, which is the part that matters. The fourth was a woman wanting a sheet of a thing she had heard read out in a corridor and he could see the hour against it as clearly as this morning.

After the fourth it was clean.

The fifth and the sixth were gone, and the fifth was the one he had wanted to say yes to, and what she had said and what he had said back were both still in his head in the order they had been said in, and where she had been standing when he sent her away, and he could get none of it back off his own skin.

“You have washed that hand twice,” said Wenna Raut from the sill with the pen laid across the book.

“I have washed it twice.”

“You are looking at the back of it.”

“I am looking at the back of it.”

## The Easy Shape, At Half Past Ten

Then the morning did what about nine mornings a month do.

A woman came up the four flights at half past ten with a paper of her own in her hand about the size of a folded sheet, and that shape makes everything simple, because an answer about a paper a person brought up themselves is about that person and goes back to that person in a fold with the flap gummed shut and the date and the hour on the outside and nothing else on it anywhere.

Four lines. She read them at the wood standing up in about nine minutes and did not ask for the room and did not go into it.

He carried it down four flights and along about four hundred yards in daylight and put it into one hand and came back up.

There are four things in the inside of that coat on the left and nothing on the right, and twenty-one days ago his right hand had been carrying six lines about five people who never knew they had been refused.

## The Scope Of A Count, Which Is New Work

She had a piece of work she had not had before, which is why he was in that corridor at eleven on a morning he was not on.

“A printed thing goes out from the branch office on the twelfth of this month and it has a figure on it,” said Wenna Raut. “The figure is a count of rows. It has never been in words before and the count has never been taken, and it cannot be taken, and I am not going to take it.”

“What does it say then.”

“It says what you are about to say. I want it in a hand before I have it in a voice.”

He said it standing at the end of the table with the door open, because there is one person in this city who can tell a record from a decision and she was on the other side of that wood.

“About four hundred and thirty rows on the first of February of the year before, with rows added since and not counted.”

“That is the whole of it?”

“That is the whole of it, and it carries its scope on it, and a reader will know exactly how much of it is a count and how much of it is a date.”

## Three Things That Would Have To Be True

“Then tell me the three things that would have to be true,” she said, “before those two books could be made into one instrument.”

“There are two books about this city four hundred yards apart and four flights apart, and I have read both of them, and they are not the same kind of thing, and they have to stay that way.”

“Yours and hers.”

“Mine and hers. This one holds questions and answers put to a door. Hers holds days a person wrote down with no door in front of them at all. If I set a day from one beside a day from the other and the two are the same day, then I have matched a day about a paper against a day about a kitchen, and in about four years I will not be able to say which of the two it was that moved.”

“That is the first thing. What is the second.”

“The second is that a person can stand at both ends. About four rows in every nine that this office adds are a day somebody asked about another person, so a day in your book stopped being a neutral thing some time ago and there is no small setting on a comparison like this one.”

“And the third.”

“The third is that the result can be put down. It cannot. Every way there is of setting two records beside each other puts them in one line, and one line is a list, and a list is the thing this office was built not to make. The day somebody does it with a pencil instead of a pen it will still be one.”

## What The Cost Is

“The cost is that the only person in this city who could put those two books side by side is me,” said Lin Hao, “and I am a man with no standing, and there is nothing I am allowed to ask anybody to help me do. So the reason I cannot do it and the reason no other person can do it are the same reason, and the reason is me.”

“You have said that four times in four years and the reason has not moved.”

“It has not moved and I have now used it three times: once to stay out of a doorway, once to go into one, and once to decide I would not go into one again. And I am not going to improve it, and I would like it written down that I would like it written down.”

## A Year To The Day, And A Space In It

“Read me my own row,” he said. “The twenty-third of August of the ninth year.”

She read it without the book, which she did not say and he noticed.

A date at the head of it and an hour against the date and then nothing whatever after the hour, and a space in the middle of it.

“That was a year ago this morning. Three thousand four hundred and forty-two days less three thousand and eighty-two is three hundred and sixty, and a year in this city is three hundred and sixty days, and that space has been in it since the morning a clerk put the date at the head of it and went on to something else.”

“Do you want it filled.”

“No.”

“I am going to ask why, because in about four years somebody else is going to ask.”

“Because a row with two mouths in it is the whole of what that book is for,” said Lin Hao. “That space is the most honest thing in the book and it is honest because it is empty, and if I fill it then it is a question a man asked about himself and an answer a man gave himself, and there would not be one other person anywhere in the sentence.”

She wrote nothing down.

## What She Would Not Enter, Said Out Loud Once

He put his right hand up on the table with the back of it showing, which he had not decided to do.

“What am I to do with that, now that you have shown it to me,” said Wenna Raut.

“Enter that on the twenty-third of August a man found the six lines had come off his own hand.”

“No.”

“Then enter that you did not.”

“No,” she said. “And here is the reason and I am giving it to you once, because in about four years I am going to be asked this and I will not remember having answered it.”

She put the pen down across the book and looked at the open edge of his coat pocket, where a white plate showed with a name on it and nothing cut into it anywhere.

“The first row in that book about a person inside this building tells every stranger who comes up these stairs that there is a man in this room worth a row. You have no standing, nothing on any plate that anybody reads, and no permission of anything. That is the whole of the reason about nine people a month come up four flights and not nine a week, and I am not going to be the one who puts a row about you under it.”

## The Price, Paid About Four Seconds After It Was Chosen

Then he did the thing he had already decided he would do, about four seconds before he did it, which is the only order this office has ever kept anything in.

He said the four he could remember out loud in that room with the door open, at the end of the table, in the order he had written them, with the hours, and he did not write one of them down and she did not write one of them down.

About four people in a corridor about four feet wide heard four sentences that do not belong to anything, and two of them will remember the second of them for about a week and neither of them will ever know it was a refusal.

*Three thousand four hundred and forty-two days. Less three thousand four hundred and thirty-five, the sixteenth of this month, is seven, and six short lines a man put on the back of his own right hand on the morning of the second of this month came off that skin inside twenty-one days, and four of them could be said out loud and two of them could not.*

*Less three thousand three hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is fifty-three, and the thirty-eighth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-three of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand and twenty-two days, which is thirty-three months and thirty-two days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*

*Less three thousand and eighty-two, the twenty-third of August of the ninth year, is three hundred and sixty, which is a whole year and not one day more or less, and a row with a space in the middle of it has stood in a bound Register about four hundred yards away for every one of them with no hand near it.*
