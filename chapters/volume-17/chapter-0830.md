# The Reading Back Is Refused

Three thousand four hundred and twenty-eight was the ninth of this month and the sixteenth is day three thousand four hundred and thirty-five, and three thousand four hundred and thirty-five less three thousand four hundred and twenty-eight is seven, and sixteen less nine is seven by the month count.

## Nine Minutes After Eleven

There were four people in that room on the morning of the sixteenth of this month at about eleven o'clock, and about four other people in the corridor about four feet wide outside the open door.

The man who had asked. The man it was about. The clerk, who read in the voice she reads everything in. And a man in a white plate with a name on it and no figure cut into it anywhere, standing at the end of the table because there are four chairs and there have been four chairs since the first year.

It was the second time since the rule was spoken on the tenth of June that an answer about a person had been read out in that room with both of them in it. The rule was spoken once, in that room, by a man, and was not written at the foot of anything, and there are about nine questions a month through that door and this was the second answer about a person out of them.

## The Line That Is Wrong

The clerk read the date at the head of the row first and then four lines, and the door was open because the door is open.

Three of the four lines were right. She read them at the same speed as she reads everything and they said that this office holds what it has been given and that it holds nothing further about either man and that the answer to what was asked is that the office has no other record of the man who brought the paper.

The fourth line said that the paper came through the front door of that building on the day of the question.

The man it was about put his hand up.

## What The Man In The Room Said

“That is not the day it came.”

The corridor did not go quiet. About four feet of air does not go quiet.

“It came on the eighteenth of the last month of the ninth year,” he said, “because I have it on the docket, because I am the man who brought it, and because I was standing on the step when the man behind the counter gave it back to me and it is stamped for the eighteenth.”

The clerk looked at the row and not at either of them.

“The row says the day of the question because that is the day that is in that book,” she said. “The day the paper came is a different day and it is not in that book.”

“That day is not in that book either, and nothing in this city lets me enter it, because the eighteenth of the last month of the ninth year has not been asked about by anybody, ever.”

“That one I can put in,” she said. “It came out of a man in a room with four people in it and the hour against it, and that is the same kind of thing as a question asked out loud.”

She entered it, under the four lines, with the date of the morning at the head of it and the hour against the date, in a clerk's hand, in his own words as she always is.

A correction goes under and never instead. That has been the arrangement since the first year and it is the reason that book can be read at all.

## The Asker's Request

The man who had asked stood up.

“Read it again,” he said. “Both of you are still in the room. It is nine minutes since you read it. Read the four lines and then read the other one and then not one person in this room ever has to wonder which one the office means.”

“No,” said Lin Hao.

## Why He Refused, And Said So Out Loud

He said it standing up, with the door open, in the room, because he makes his own decisions out loud to one person and there is a corridor full of them and that is the arrangement now and it is going to stay the arrangement.

“An answer that has been read once in this room may not be read again in this room with different people in it. From this morning, once.”

“You have just said why once is enough four times in nine months and you have never once said what comes after once.”

“What comes after once is a draft,” he said. “A row that can be improved while it is warm is a row that is a draft, and a book of drafts is a book of about nine things a month that anybody may come up four flights and ask to have done again, and the first person who asks will be the person who did not like the first one.”

He did not improve it.

“And I am not going to be asked to say it again, because from the thirtieth of March this office has had one practice that cannot be stopped and one practice that anybody can ask about, and this is the second one, and I am not having two.”

The clerk said it back once, standing up, in the voice she reads everything in, and entered it as a practice with a date on it and an hour against the date, in her own hand and not in the office's, and nothing went under the foot of anything.

## What He Was Not Going To Be Given

The man it was about had been standing by the window for about a minute.

“You can put it right,” he said. “Say the four lines again and then say the other one, and I will tell you both times that I have been told twice and that neither of you can tell me which of the two is the one the office means, and that will be true and it will be all right.”

“No.”

“It is a kindness you are throwing away.”

“It is not a kindness,” said Lin Hao. “It is a procedure. The moment a man in this building can be forgiven by a stranger for reading a thing wrong about him, then this is an office that will read things wrong about people at about nine a month and be forgiven about nine a month, and the rate at which it happens will be the rate at which it is forgiven, and not one person will ever look at either number.”

He stood at the end of the table with his hands at his sides.

“You have come up four flights to be told something about yourself and you have been told it wrong and then told it again right. That is a better morning than this office has given anybody in nine years, and it is better because you were in the room.”

“And if I let it be read a second time then next month somebody will be read a thing wrong in a room and told it again in a room, and not one person will be in the room for either of them.”

## What Went Under That Row, In Order

There are four things under it now and all four are in a clerk’s hand with the date of this morning and the hour against the date.

The wrong four lines. The day the paper came, in the words of the man it was about. The fact that the answer was read out once in that room with both of them in it. And the practice, spoken in that room by a man and not written at the foot of anything.

“I have not entered that the first four lines were wrong,” said Wenna Raut.

“No. And that is the part I want to say before I go down those stairs with the book. If I enter that the office got it wrong then this room holds a sentence in its own voice saying it is fallible, and in about four years somebody will be able to point at it and say the office knows it gets things wrong. Nothing goes under a row about what we are. Only what happened, and who was in the room, and at what hour.”

About four people in that corridor heard a man in a white plate say the word once, four times, in the voice he uses for things he has decided, and not one of them knows what it is about.

“You have just cost this office the only thing it had left,” said the man who had asked, at the door, with his hat on.

“It had a correct row with a wrong line in it and a right one underneath, nine minutes apart, for ever. That is what it had left. I have taken the other thing away and I would like somebody to write it down who is not me.”

## The Cost, Said Plainly To Four People

“So the wrong one stays on top,” said Wenna Raut.

“The wrong one stays on top,” he said. “And the right one stays under it, nine minutes apart, in your hand, dated this morning. And in about four years there will be a row in that book with a wrong sentence above a right one and no instrument anywhere in this city that will let anybody take the top one out.”

He turned round to the man who had asked.

“You will get the wrong one if you ever ask again. Not deliberately. It is simply above.”

“That is not fair.”

“It is not fair, and it is not a rule I invented this morning, and I have said out loud that I decided it, because in about four years somebody is going to want it undone.”

He stopped, and then said the rest of it to the room.

“About nine months ago a woman two floors down refused to move a day in her own floor for me. She was right. I was the man who would have asked her. This morning the day was wrong and I could not move it either, and it is not a rule any more. It is a thing I am standing in the middle of.”

That room said nothing.

The man it was about picked up his hat, and put his docket back inside his coat where it had been for nine years, and said, “Then I have two days and one of them is mine,” and went down the four flights.

*Three thousand four hundred and thirty-five days. Less three thousand four hundred and twenty-eight, the ninth of this month, is seven, and an answer was read once in a room with four people in it and was wrong in one line, and the right day was given under it nine minutes later by the man it was about.*

*And the reading back was refused, and the wrong line is on top of the right one in a bound Register about four hundred yards from where he was standing, and there is nothing in this city that will let anybody take the top one out.*

*Less three thousand three hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is forty-six, and the thirty-eighth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last forty-six of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand and fifteen days, which is thirty-three months and twenty-five days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
