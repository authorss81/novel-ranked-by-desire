# He Carries Nothing Up

Three thousand four hundred and fifty-four was the fifth of this month and the twelfth is day three thousand four hundred and sixty-one, and three thousand four hundred and sixty-one less three thousand four hundred and fifty-four is seven, and twelve less five is seven by the month count.

## Seven Days Of Carrying It And Not Carrying It

Seven days.

The twenty-sixth thing on that table is at the near end, on the drawer side, and it is a sheet of office paper about the size of any of the other twenty-five and it has nothing on it at all, and it has been at the near end for about three years, and about four people a week look at that table.

On the first of them he was on the night shift and got up at ten and went down the four flights to that table with nothing on it but about three years of nothing, and he stood in that room alone and thought about the twenty-sixth thing on it for about nine minutes.

On the fourth of them he got out a sheet and wrote four lines on it at the table in the branch office, and then read them back, and they were wrong, and he knew they were wrong before he had finished the second one, because four lines on a dated sheet is a row, and a row says what this office holds, and what this office holds about that twenty-sixth thing is nothing at all.

On the sixth of them he wrote them out again on the back of his own right hand, which is a thing he did once in August and has not done since, and he got the whole of it back off his own skin in under nine seconds and then washed it off at the sink on the half landing because the water is hot on that landing and the paper dries too slowly to fold.

## The Fifth Of The Seven, And A Door About Four Hundred Yards Off

The fifth of them was an ordinary morning and an ordinary delivery.

An answer about a paper a man brought up himself in the spring went down four flights inside his coat on the left with the date and the hour on the outside and the flap gummed shut, and it came back at eleven with nothing in that pocket at all.

What was in his head went to the landing on the second, because you cannot stand at a door about four hundred yards off with two things in you and not put one of them somewhere, and he stood on that landing for about nine minutes with his hands in his pockets while a woman read four lines at a wood in front of her own mother.

The thing he put down the landing was that a blank sheet of office paper is what you put in front of a person who cannot write down the question they came up four flights with.

He said it out loud on a landing between the second and third floors, in about four seconds, to an empty corridor, and then went back up and carried on.

That was the moment he understood that the reason he could not carry it up was not the reason she had given him. Hers was about the book.

His was that he had already lost it twice.

Then he came up the four flights on the seventh day with nothing in either hand.

## What A Clerk Does At Eleven On A Morning

Wenna Raut had the book on the sill and there were about four people in that corridor and the door was open because the door is open.

“You have come up at eleven for seven mornings and you have not had anything in your hand since the twenty-third of August,” she said, “and that is either a very good habit or a thing you have come to tell me not to ask about.”

“It is the second one.”

“Right.” She put the pen down across the book. “Then I am not going to ask, and I am going to say that not asking is the whole of my job this morning, and I am going to go on being right about it for about nine years.”

“There is a reason and it is not mine.”

“Then it is not yours and I would rather have it than have you be careful.”

## He Gives Her The Reason And Does Not Improve It

He said it standing at the end of the table, in the room, with the door open, which is where he says the things he has decided.

“A woman who works a lift at a market end told me on the fifth of this month that a thing said on a platform with no lamp on the run and no clock in the building and the day chalked on a slate sixty feet away has not got a day on it and has not got an hour on it, and that there is not one line in that book which will take a thing without both of them.”

“You are repeating her.”

“I am repeating her.”

“You are repeating her exactly.”

“I am not improving it because I have tried for seven days and every version I have made of it is worse than hers, and because the day I improve it is the day it is a rule, and a rule is a thing that goes in a book and this one may not go in a book or it is about a person.”

## What She Says About Practices, And Why She Is Right

“Then it is a practice,” said Wenna Raut, “and you have had three of them since the thirtieth of March and every one of them is a practice and not one line of ink, and I have been standing on the other side of this wood for nine years.”

“Yes.”

“I am not going to say they are a habit with a good name. I said that once and you had the answer ready and I have not used it since.”

“What is the answer now.”

“There is no answer now, and that is worse.” She squared the book to the edge of the sill and took her hand off it again. “A habit with a good name stops on the morning the first person who has to keep it is busy. A practice stops on the morning the first person who has to keep it does not feel like it, and those are the same morning, and I have watched which one of the two turns up first.”

“Which one.”

“The second,” said Wenna Raut. “In nine years I have never once seen a thing I did not feel like doing fail to get done. I have seen it twice and both times it was a person who was tired, and both times it was me.”

## And Then She Asks Him For The Only Thing She Wants

“So say it in the room.”

He looked at her.

“You have just handed me a reason that has no date on it and no hour on it, and that belongs to a woman who is not in this building and works at night for a wage,” said Wenna Raut. “And it is now in my head, and in about four years one of the two of us will not be here, and then the reason goes with her and you will have lost it.”

“That is a practice. That is the whole of what a practice is.”

“Yes, and this morning it is held by two people, and one of them is not paid to hold it, and she works nights, and she does not live with you, and she has not asked you for one thing in nine years.” She put two fingers on the cover of the book. “Say it out loud in this room with the door open. Not to me. To the corridor.”

## About Four People In Four Feet Of Air

He said it.

He said all of it, in her words, in the order she had said it in, standing up at the end of the table with the door open, and he did not improve one word of it and he did not say what it was about.

About four people in a corridor about four feet wide heard a man in a white plate explain, at eleven in the morning, that a sentence with no day on it and no hour on it cannot go into a bound book, and not one of them knows what it is about.

Two of them will remember the middle of it for about a week. One of them has a child at that school and will say it at a table in about nine days because it sounds like something worth saying, and it will arrive in about four years in a kitchen in this city with the whole of what it is about missing out of the middle of it.

Two people in that corridor had understood it on the seventh of September, in a passage about four feet wide off Cordwain, standing at a rail, and neither of them had said a word.

“That is what you wanted,” he said.

“That is what I wanted and I would like somebody to write down that I asked for it and did not get it, and not one person in this building is going to do that.”

## One Plain Sentence About A Person

The woman who works that lift has told not one other person in the city and has been asked nothing by anybody and has not asked him for a single thing in nine years, and she is paid a wage for moving crates and for nothing else, and as of the twelfth of this month she is the only person alive who knows what the twenty-sixth thing on that table is for.

He understood the reason perfectly.

He also understood that he had a week, and a corridor, and about four ears, and that the whole of what he had bought for her on an iron plate was now a sentence in other people's heads in his own voice.

*Three thousand four hundred and sixty-one days. Less three thousand four hundred and fifty-four, the fifth of this month, is seven, and a man went up four flights on the seventh of those days with nothing in either hand, and said out loud in a room with the door open a reason that was not his, and did not improve one word of it.*

*Less three thousand three hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is seventy-two, and the thirty-eighth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-two of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand and forty-one days, which is thirty-four months and twenty-one days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*

*Less three thousand and eighty-two, the twenty-third of August of the ninth year, is three hundred and seventy-nine, and the row with the space in it stood in that book for every one of those days, and it was still standing when he went up the four flights on the twelfth of this month with nothing in his hands.*
