# The Hundredth Form

Three thousand four hundred and seventy-nine was the thirtieth of this month and the seventh of the next is day three thousand four hundred and eighty-six, and three thousand four hundred and eighty-six less three thousand four hundred and seventy-nine is seven, and seven out of a month that begins on the first is seven by the month count.

## A Board Against A Kerb And A Boy Going Back Up The Slope

One of those forms goes on that board every month and has done since the first year, up on the seventh and down on the thirtieth, and no office in this city owns it and no pay goes with it.

The hundredth of them went up on that board on the morning of the seventh of this month.

A boy carried it up on one arm with a paste brush in his other fist and a corner of fresh paste on his cuff, set it down against the kerb, brought it square with the toe of his shoe without looking at it, and went back up the hill with the brush still in his hand.

Lin Hao did not speak to him. He wanted nothing from him, and not one person in this city has the business to put that question, and it has been the arrangement for nine years.

It is not an event, and nothing happened on that slope this morning except that a piece of paper is where it is every month because a boy brings it, and not one person is praised for it.

## What This Office Is Not Allowed To Do About That Board

There is an arrangement about the four inches of dressed stone at the foot of that wall, and it is not an instruction, because there is nowhere an instruction could be entered.

This office does not go down there and does not send anybody, and does not buy the paste, and does not ask the boy what is on the board when he is carrying it up, and does not keep the day it went up.

It came about in the first year in about nine minutes. Somebody worked out that if this office watched that board then the four inches of stone below it would stop being a place a person could put a nail into, because a watched place is a place where everything written on it is a statement to somebody.

About nine people a week get down low enough. Every one of them is doing it because nothing is keeping an eye on it.

Not one person has ever sent a man in a white plate down that slope on purpose in nine years, and if anybody had done it on the seventh of this month the office would have been different by the following morning, and there would have been a question in this city about who was watching the wall.

## Two Printed Boxes

Two ruled squares are printed at the foot of that form and they are for two things about an address, a figure and a standing.

Both are empty and they have been empty since the eighth of October of the first year, when a clerk was handed a sheet and a form and no instruction whatever, and wrote two words into one square because the square was there and the words were there.

Nothing has been written in either of them since and nothing is going to be, and the reason is not that anybody has forbidden it.

Under the lower batten, in the place where the paste has gone thin, a piece of office paper is held flat against the wood with a date at the top of it and a pencil line ruled across the bottom of it in a hand of her own.

It has not been folded or turned over or taken and the batten is holding it the way the batten was holding it when she put it there.

## The Half Of The Form He Had Never Read

There is a printed heading at the top of that form, about eleven lines of it, and it has been at the top of the same board on the seventh of every month for nine years.

He had never read it.

Not once, in nine years, at any of about nine hundred mornings, and not once on any of the four occasions he has been down that slope and looked at that board properly.

He read it on the seventh of this month at about half past five in the wet.

It is the ordinary printed heading that goes on a printed form. It says what district the form belongs to, when it is to be put up, that it comes down on the thirtieth, and that any person may make use of the two squares at the foot of it.

*Any person.*

He stood on a slope with a printed sentence in front of him about four feet high and understood that this office had spent nine years leaning on a permission that had been printed on a board and had never once been read by anybody who came up that hill.

## Four Inches, Nine Feet Back

Four inches of dressed stone are set into the ground at the foot of that wall and have not shifted since the first year.

He stood nine feet off them and did not come nearer, because a man standing on a stone gets nothing out of it.

About forty words in about five hands are in there, and the two words scratched into that stone are not among them and never will be.

About nine people a week get down low enough to read what is there. This office knows nothing whatever about any of it, is never told it, and does not ask.

Two inches of that subject are a different thing and he took care over it. About four hundred households in this city keep a place under a mantel where a thing can be put down in a hand, and about four hundred people have used one, and not one of them is known to him by name and none is going to be.

Four inches is one stone at the foot of a slope with five hands in it, and four inches does not go into a book.

## Four Sentences, Said Out Loud, By A Man Who Is Not A Stonemason

He said them out loud on the wet slope at about half past five, to an empty hill, in the ordinary voice, which is what he does with things he has decided and it is the only reason they get said at all.

“Those two boxes are for the figure of the thing at an address and for the standing of the thing at an address.”

Not one person answered him, because not one person was there, and he went on.

“A person fills one of them in after they have been told that an address is worth something, and not one person at that kerb has ever been told that in nine years.”

“An address with a stone cut into it is a place in forty years and an address without one is a thing two people remember differently, and the whole of a list is a figure and a standing and a place, in that order.”

The water came off the batten onto the shoulder of his coat.

“This office was built not to make one, and it has not made one in nine years, and it is going to stand at the foot of that hill with two empty boxes on it for as long as I am able to stand here.”

He is not a stonemason and he is not the boy and he has never cut a stone for money in his life, and there was not one person within a hundred yards of that kerb who could have told you where any of the four sentences had come from except the man who said them.

He said them because there was not one person there, and because that is the only condition under which he can find out what he actually holds. He has made his own decisions out loud to one person for nine years, and the one person is in a room four floors up with a pen, and the slope is not that person and the slope is the reason he can think.

## What He Had Been Leaning On

And then he stayed on that slope for about four minutes and worked out something he has been standing next to for nine years without once looking at it.

The two boxes have never been filled because not one person has come to that kerb and wanted to fill them. Not because the office forbade it, and not because there is a rule against it, and not because one person stopped another.

Because about four hundred people a week walk past that board and about nine of them a week get down low enough to read what is under it, and every one of those nine has looked at two empty boxes and gone away.

That is the whole of the protection. It is not a decision this office took. It is nine years of a city that has not been curious, and he had been standing on it, in the rain, at half past five, as though it were something somebody had decided on his behalf.

“Nine years,” he said, out loud, to the slope, “and the reason is that not one person has ever wanted to know.”

And then, standing there, he worked out the other half of it, which is worse and which he had not worked out before.

About nine people a week get down low enough to read what is under that board, and about nine people a month come up four flights, and nothing in this city has ever put one of the first lot next to one of the second lot.

The two places are about four hundred yards apart and the wall and the stair are in the same district, and this office has now gone down to the foot of a hill on a working afternoon and worked out a rule about a board.

He did not go down again that month.

And in about four years somebody is going to notice that a man in a white plate has been down that slope on the seventh of a month, and nothing in this city would let anybody ask him what for, because asking him what for is asking a question about a question.

And then he went back up the hill in the wet, past about nine people coming down it who did not look at him, and did not go down there again that month.

*Three thousand four hundred and eighty-six days. Less three thousand four hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of this month, is seven, and a form went up on a board against a kerb at the foot of a hill with a boy carrying it and a paste brush in his fist, and two printed boxes at the foot of it were empty, and a man said four sentences out loud on the slope and went back up.*

*Less three thousand four hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of September, is seven, and the thirty-ninth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seven of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand and sixty-six days, which is thirty-five months and sixteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
