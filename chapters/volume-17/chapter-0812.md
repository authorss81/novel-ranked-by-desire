# A Question About A Neighbour

Three thousand three hundred and six was the seventh of this month and the fourteenth is day three thousand three hundred and thirteen, and three thousand three hundred and thirteen less three thousand three hundred and six is seven, and fourteen less seven is seven by the month count.

## A Sheet In His Own Hand

The man in the cap came up those four flights at about eleven in the morning and he had a piece of pencil paper in his hand, and he held it the whole way to the wood and did not put it down.

In December, in March and in the first week of this month he had stood at that table three times and asked the same question, and he had said, on the first of them, that if it was written down he would find out.

He had written it down himself.

It was four lines in a working man's hand, and the date was at the top in the same hand and the hour was against the date, and the hour was the hour he had stood at the wood the second time and said it out loud.

“You wanted it dated,” said Wenna Raut.

“I wanted it dated and I wanted the hour and I have put both on it,” said the man, “and I have not written a name on it and I am not going to, and if a person who can read comes up those stairs and looks at that page they will find out what I want and the hour I want it and nothing else whatever.”

He said it standing up and he did not take his coat off.

## It Goes In In His Words

She read it twice before she entered any of it, standing at the wood with the page under both her hands so that he could see her reading it and not see the pen moving.

Then she took the book round and put the date at the head of a new row and the hour against the date, and she wrote down in the words he had used that he asks whether a paper is held at this office that was brought to this office by some other person, and that he does not name the person and will not name the paper, and that he asks it a fourth time.

She read it back to him standing up in the voice she reads everything in, and he nodded at the words and not at the reading, the way a man does when he is listening for his own sentence and not for the sound.

Then she closed the book, and that was the whole of the entry, and there was not one word of it anywhere else in the city.

“A row may be added to and may not be taken from,” she said. “That is the oldest rule in that building and it is the rule of the book on this table. If you come back in a year with a better wording it goes in underneath you and yours stays.”

“I understand that,” said the man. “I do not want a better wording. I want an answer.”

## Four People In A Corridor Heard It Entered

That part took nine seconds and it is the part that matters.

Outside that room is a passage about four feet across and nine paces long, and on the fourteenth of this month there were four people standing in it, and every one of them heard a clerk read a stranger's question about another stranger word for word, twice, at the only volume a stair carries.

They heard it because she read it back to the man. She reads everything back. It is the only thing about her that has not changed in nine years and it is in the book as an instruction from the tenth of June, and she has never once been able to do it quietly.

What they heard was a man asking whether a piece of paper is in a room off a third court that some other person put there.

They did not know who either of them was. One of the four was a woman who had asked about her own paper in March and had been told what this office holds about her in one row with a date on it.

She stood in that corridor with her own answer under her arm and heard another person's question about somebody else read out loud at four feet, and she looked at the floor of it and then went in and asked her question again as though it had not been asked, which gave her the same answer in the same words for the second time.

That is what the door opened in December is for, and there is no line at the foot of any page that stops it.

## He Walks It Down To A District Office

He carried the page down four flights and along about four hundred yards at about half past eleven in the morning, in daylight, and it was ten minutes instead of nine.

The district office in that part of the city is on the ground floor of a building with a brass plate and a bench outside it, and the woman whose office it is was in, and he had not arranged it and she had not asked him to.

He put the page on her desk with the four lines facing her.

She read it once through and then again with two fingers following the line, and she did not hurry the second one, and there was nothing at all in her face while she did it.

## She Is Right About It

“That is properly put,” said the district officer.

“I know. You said so in the corridor in February.”

“I said the shape of it was right and I want to be exact, because you have now got four lines on a piece of pencil paper in a working man’s hand with a date and an hour on it, and that is not the shape of it. That is the whole of it.”

She set her fingertips on the paper either side of the page, which is a thing her hands do at a desk and is not a thing she did with it.

“One question. One paper, though the paper is not named. A date at the head and an hour against it. The words of the person asking and not yours, and I will say again that almost nothing else in this city does that and this office has been doing it since the second of December.”

“And the answer.”

“There is no answer.”

“That is not an answer either.”

“No,” she said. “And that is what I have come down here to say to you and I am going to say it once and then I am going to make you put it in your own book, because I am a district officer and I am not permitted to be the reason a thing is unanswerable.”

## What She Actually Went Down The Stair For

“This month I have been asked the same question twice by other offices,” she said.

Lin Hao did not sit down.

“One from a works department and one from a house of correction, and in both cases it was a properly put question of exactly your shape, and both of them asked me whether it could be answered and what would be done if it could not.”

“What did you tell them.”

“I told them the same thing I am telling you, and I told each of them that I could not be cited on it and that they ought to write down that I had said so and that if they did write down that I had said so then the thing would be in three places instead of two and they would be worse off.”

She took her hands off the desk.

“Neither of them wrote it down. A man from a works department has a place to put that in and did not use it, because he is answerable for what is in it, and a man from a house of correction has no place to put it in at all.”

“So the question has been put in four offices this month and it has been refused in three and written down in none.”

“It has been properly put in four offices and properly refused in three and properly entered in one,” said the district officer, “and your office is the one that put it in a book, and that is not a fault and it is not what you are afraid of. What you are afraid of is that you are the only one doing it, and you are not. You are the only one doing it in writing.”

## What She Would Not Do

“Can you be cited on this.”

“No.”

“Can I say that you said it was properly put.”

“You may say that I read it,” she said, which is what she said in the corridor in February and which is the only thing she has ever permitted anybody to record, “and you may put the word *refused* in your book against it, because a refusal is not a statement about a person and there is nothing in it for a person to be hurt by.”

Lin Hao stood in that office for about a minute and understood that she had just given him the honest refusal and taken the dishonest one away, and that the honest one costs him a row in a bound book that says in his own office’s hand that a man came up four flights and was refused.

“A row saying that I refused.”

“A row saying that you were refused,” she said. “There is a difference and you will want to be careful about it and so will I.”

She stood up and put the pencil paper back into his hand and squared it against the edge of her desk before she let go of it, which is a thing counters do and which she did without appearing to notice she had done.

## What He Cannot Put Down

He went back up four flights at about twenty past twelve and put the page on the table under the window, and it lay there all afternoon beside a bound Register with four lines of a working man’s hand in it and an answer owed against them.

The office shall answer each question in writing.

There are two things it can do with that and he had both of them in his hands.

It can write an answer, and any sentence about whether a paper is held is a sentence about a person who brought it, and the person it is about is not in the room.

Or it can write that it will not answer, and then the book says that this office was asked about a person on the fourteenth of this month and declined, and that row goes to no counter and is seen by about four people a day at the most and one clerk and one man who may only read what she is given.

*A row may be added to and not taken from, and both of those sentences are for ever.*

He stood there at about five o’clock with his hands in his pockets and understood that he had been wrong about the shape of the difficulty for four months, because he had been thinking about what the answer was, and the answer was the easy part.

The fourteen of this month was the first properly put question about another person that this office has ever had, and he could not answer it, and he was not permitted to make the book look as though it had never been asked.

*Three thousand three hundred and thirteen days. Less three thousand three hundred and six, the seventh of this month, is seven, and a man wrote his own question down in his own hand on pencil paper with a date at the top of it and an hour against the date, and the office entered it in the words he used, and a row may be added to and not taken from.*

*Less three thousand two hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of March, is fourteen, and the thirty-seventh sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fourteen of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is eight hundred and ninety-three days, which is twenty-nine months and twenty-three days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
