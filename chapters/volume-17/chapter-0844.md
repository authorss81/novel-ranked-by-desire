# He Burns The Copy

Three thousand five hundred and twenty was the eleventh of this month and the eighteenth is day three thousand five hundred and twenty-seven, and three thousand five hundred and twenty-seven less three thousand five hundred and twenty is seven, and eighteen less eleven is seven by the month count.

## The Seventeenth, In That Room, With The Door Open

On the seventeenth of this month he put his right hand into the inside of his coat on the left and counted them, and there were four, and it had been four for about a year, and he had been keeping that number in his head the way a clerk keeps a count that is done with the eyes.

The first is four lines written in that room on the fourteenth of April and carried out of it on the third of May and never delivered, because what they said was a day on which a paper came through that door, and a day like that is a fact about whoever carried it.

The second is Perla Quist's copy: one piece of paper in her own hand, on her own paper, dated, four lines on it with no figure on any of them, and the only copy in this city of the foot of the standing sheet.

The third is a blank sheet that came out of his own drawer.

The fourth is four more lines that went back out of a doorway in daylight on the third of May after a woman who came out of her own house to ask for them read all four of them and handed them back and said nothing whatever about them being wrong.

He took his hand out with all four of them where they were and said out loud, standing at the end of the table with the door open, that on the eighteenth of this month he was going to burn three of them in a building about four hundred yards and four narrow flights away.

“Why three,” said Wenna Raut.

“Because the third one has nothing on it.”

“Write that down.”

“No,” he said. “You cannot enter it and you know why you cannot enter it. A clerk may only enter what she is given, and I have given you a thing I intend to do, and a thing a person intends to do has not happened yet, and you are not going to have it on a page at nine o'clock tomorrow to find out whether I did it.”

“That is the correct reason,” said Wenna Raut, “and I would have given you the same one and I am not going to thank you for making me say it.”

## Why The Eighteenth And Not The Twentieth

On the sixteenth of this month a man at that counter, who is paid nothing and has read the foot of that page to strangers for nine years without knowing what it was for, read the six lines of the foot of it out at about half past ten in the morning to a woman who had asked what a word in the fourth of them meant.

Then on the thirtieth of September, in the same passage, a man at the front of that rail who is not the counterman read the same six lines out at about half past four in the afternoon from a piece of pencil paper in his own coat, holding it up beside the wood.

Not one person in that passage put the two against each other. Two people said afterwards that the first man was right and two said afterwards that the second man was right, and the two sets of two are the same four people and not one of the four has changed his mind.

“You were not in that passage,” said Wenna Raut.

“No. And you were not either.”

“I was told about it on the nineteenth,” she said. “By a man who is paid nothing, who has come up a yard and four flights to tell a clerk that there are now two versions of a page in this city and that neither man who is reading them has ever had the page.”

## Down Four Flights And About Four Hundred Yards And Four Narrow Flights

He went down at about ten in the morning on the eighteenth and along about four hundred yards and up four narrow flights, and he was breathing wrong by the second landing and had to stand on a half landing for about a minute.

The laundry was hot and wet and full of white steam and about nine women were working the ends of about nine long tables, and Perla Quist was at the near end with a hand-truck and she had been at that end since before he came into the building.

He did not go past the front of the tables.

The nine sheets of her own paper are under a third table at the back and they have been under it for six years and he was not going to look at them and he did not.

## What He Came To Say

She put the hand-truck down when she saw him and wiped her hands on her apron and came about four feet round the end of the table.

She said nothing at all about the fact that he had come down a hill in the cold to a laundry and had not brought anything with him.

“I am going to burn it,” said Lin Hao.

“You are going to burn what.”

“The copy. The one you made of the foot of that page in your own hand on your own paper in the spring of the year before. It has been in the inside of my coat on the left for about fourteen months and it is the only copy of that foot in this city and it is in my pocket at this moment and I am going to put it in the stove at the back of this floor and light it.”

She looked at him for about four seconds.

“You have not asked me.”

“No.”

“I would have said yes,” said Perla Quist. “I have been waiting about fourteen months for somebody to ask me and I would have said yes in about four seconds and I want you to understand that I am not saying yes now, and that the difference is not in the answer.”

“What is it in.”

“It is in that you have come down here to tell a woman what you are going to do to a thing that was hers to make and was hers to keep and was not yours to keep and not hers to take back, and you did not once say may I,” said Perla Quist.

“And you were right, and I am not going to help you have been right, and you can put that in whatever it is you are keeping it in.”

He put his hand in and took it out.

## Nine Seconds At The Back Of That Floor

The stove is at the back under the copper and it is lit at about six in the morning and banked, and there is a shovel by it and a kindling box under the bench, and Perla Quist did not come behind the tables and did not look.

He put three pieces of paper into it in about nine seconds.

The copy went in first. It is one piece of paper in her own hand with four lines on it and no figure on any of them, and it took about four seconds to catch and about nine more to be anything else.

Then two more, which are four lines written in that room on the fourteenth of April and carried out of it on the third of May and never given to anybody, and four lines that went back out of a doorway in daylight and came back into that coat, and not one person in this city could ever be sent either of them, and there never was going to be one.

“Why those two as well,” said Perla Quist, from the front of the tables.

“Because a man who has just closed the only instrument this office had for finding out who is asking about whom does not get to keep two more pieces of paper in a pocket as an instrument for finding out what he said to two people in April and May.”

“That is a good reason.”

“It is mine and it is about half the reason.”

“And the other half.”

“The other half is that I have been a piece of furniture for about fourteen months,” said Lin Hao, “and a piece of furniture is a place where things are put when the people who own them cannot hold them, and the twenty-six things on that table four flights up are the other half of that and I have been the other half since about the third year.”

## The One That Stayed

He took his hand back into the inside of the coat on the left and counted what was left in it, and there was one.

The blank sheet out of his own drawer, unfolded, with nothing written on it at all, and it has been in that coat about three years, and not one thing on it can be checked against anything.

Perla Quist came round the end of the table for the first time in about nine minutes and stood about four feet off him and did not come nearer.

“You burned three and you have got one,” she said, “and you did not burn the one that has been in there longest.”

“No.”

“Why not.”

“Because there is nothing on it,” he said, “and a thing with nothing on it cannot be a copy of anything, and cannot be wrong about anything, and cannot prove anything, and not one clerk or woman or counterman in this city would be able to tell me what it is for if I asked them.”

“There are three people,” said Perla Quist.

“There are three people and you are not one of them and I have not told either of the other two and I am not going to.”

“You have just told me you have not told me.”

“I have just told you the shape of it and nothing else and you are a woman who keeps an index and I am not going to hand anybody your index.”

She nodded at that.

## What She Did Not Say

She did not say one word about it for about nine minutes.

He washed his hands in the trough at the end of that floor and the ink did not all come out of the lines of his own hands, and there was about nine seconds of it where neither of them said anything.

Then she said: “Do not thank me either, because you have not thanked me and I have not expected it and I am not going to notice.”

“I have not thanked you.”

“You have not thanked me in about fourteen months,” said Perla Quist, “and I gave you the only copy of that foot once and I have said no about a heading six times since the spring of the year before, and I have never once been thanked and I am not going to start.”

## And Then She Asked Him Something Else

“And the flues in this building have not been right since about the fourth month,” she said. “There is a woman up two floors whose mother cannot get warm and it is not her mother’s chest, it is the smoke going nowhere in this building. Do you want to come up and look at them with me, or would you rather go back up your hill.”

“I would rather go back up my hill.”

“Right,” said Perla Quist, and turned round and got hold of her hand-truck, and that was the whole of what happened between them on the eighteenth of this month, and about four people on that floor at the far end saw a man in a white plate go out of the door with both hands empty.

*Three thousand five hundred and twenty-seven days. Less three thousand five hundred and twenty, the eleventh of this month, is seven, and the only copy of the foot of a standing sheet that existed in this city went into a stove at the back of a laundry in about nine seconds, and one piece of paper is still in the inside of a coat on the left.*

*Less three thousand four hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of September, is forty-eight, and the thirty-ninth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last forty-eight of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand one hundred and seven days, which is thirty-six months and twenty-seven days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
