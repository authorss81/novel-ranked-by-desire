# Five Mornings Of Paper

Three thousand three hundred and twenty-seven was the twenty-eighth of April and the fifth of this month is day three thousand three hundred and thirty-four, and three thousand three hundred and thirty-four less three thousand three hundred and twenty-seven is seven, and five out of a month that begins on the thirtieth of the one before is seven by the month count.

## Five Mornings, Five Folds, One Pair Of Hands

There were five mornings of it in five days and he carried all five himself.

The twenty-ninth of April, and the thirtieth, and the first of this month, and the second, and the third. Five answers in five folds, out of that room and down four flights and along about four hundred yards to five doors, and five hands took them and four of the five people shut the door on him and one of them asked him to wait and went and put it somewhere and came back and thanked him.

Nine minutes each way at a walk, and ten on the third of this month, and he is rank one with a chest that has been spent since the fourth year and he made every one of them.

Four of the five were about a paper the person asking had brought up themselves. That is the easy shape. You can carry that down a street and give it to one hand and go back up and not think about it again for a fortnight.

The fifth was not that shape.

## What Four Mornings Of It Are

The fourth morning was a man who has rooms on the second floor and asked, twice, whether there was anything under his own name at this address, and who wanted it said in a room.

It was four lines and it was the fourth of the five that went down four flights and came back nothing. He handed it over at the top of the stairs on the second floor with the door open behind him and said, before the man took it, that it was about a form and a date and not one grain of a figure.

That is the sentence a clerk puts at the top of about nine answers a month. This man wanted to hear it in his own hallway and he got it.

A delivery is a very small thing and it is the whole of what this office has agreed to do.

Four of them in four mornings was a good week. He came back up four flights each time at about eleven and at about half past two and he had nothing in his coat that anybody could use and he could answer the question anybody in the corridor wanted to ask him about him.

## The Fifth Morning, And The Mistake He Made At A Door

The third of this month was a woman who had asked in April about a paper her husband brought in the autumn, and she had asked it in the plain way, and the clerk had entered it in her words and read it back at her.

The answer was four lines. It said that this office holds one row against that paper, with a date on it and an hour against the date, and no figure and no name, and it said what that is, and it stopped there.

He had it in a fold inside his coat on the left with the other two things and he went down four flights at about half past ten and along about four hundred yards and the street was about nine feet wide.

He had done this four mornings running and he was tired in a way that had nothing to do with the stairs, and when she came to the door he was so tired of carrying a thing he could not say that he said the first line of it out loud, in the doorway, in the ordinary voice.

“There is one row against that paper.”

He heard himself do it about a second and a half after it had happened.

“Stop,” said the woman.

## What It Cost Him, And What It Cost Her

He stopped.

That was the first cost. The second was four seconds later, when she put her hand out for the fold and then did not take it.

“Give it to me,” she said, “and do not say anything else.”

He gave her the fold. She opened it in the doorway with her back to the street and read the whole of it, four lines, standing up, in about eleven seconds, and then she folded it again and held it out to him.

“You have just told me something,” she said. “Standing in a street. In daylight, where it is.”

“Yes.”

“So I cannot unhear it, and I cannot unhear it from you, and you are the first person who has ever given me a piece of paper about my husband and you did it on a doorstep with the whole street in it.”

She did not raise her voice. That was worse.

“Take it back.”

He stood in that doorway for about four seconds with it coming back toward him and did not put his hand out, and the four seconds were the whole of the rest of his life in that street.

“Ma’am —”

“Take it back,” said the woman, “and do not apologise, because an apology is another thing you say to me standing here.”

He took it back.

## The Fourth Flight, Twice That Day

He carried it back up four flights.

It took him about fourteen minutes and he had to stop on the second landing with his back against the wall for a minute and a half, and the answer was in his hand and not in his coat, and a person coming up behind him had to stop in the stair and wait and did not say anything about it, and he knew the sound of that person turning round and going back down.

At about a quarter to twelve he put it on the table under the window, and Wenna Raut did not touch it.

“The row stays,” she said.

“The row stays.”

“You want me to enter that it was refused.”

“I did not say anything about that.”

“You were standing at that table for about four seconds with your mouth shut,” said Wenna Raut, “and you have been thinking about a line at the foot of that page since the third of this month, and I have watched you think about it four times this week, and if you put one line down about a delivery refused at a door then in about nine years there is a page that says it, and that page goes out four times a year to about two thousand counters.”

He did not answer that.

“So I am not going to ask you for it and you are not going to give it to me,” she said, “and there is going to be no line, and this is the fifth time since the thirtieth of March that the answer to something has been that the answer is nowhere.”

## The Two He Is Carrying

It stayed in his coat.

He put it in on the left with the man in the cap's four lines and beside them the copy Perla Quist wrote out in her own hand on her own paper, and he shut the coat and stood at the end of the table and did the only arithmetic that was available to him.

Two answers in writing that this office has written and not given. Two rows in a bound Register with a date and an hour against each and nothing under either of them. One of the two rows is a fortnight and two days old at about noon on the fifth of this month and the other is two days old, and the gap between them is four months and one week, and both gaps are in a book that about two people in this city can open.

“There is one more thing you should have on the table,” he said, “and I am going to say it because you are the only person who will write down the true version of it and I am not going to.”

“Say it.”

“I gave a woman four lines of an answer about her husband in a street doorway in daylight before she had asked me for it, because I was tired.”

Wenna Raut looked at him for about four seconds.

“That is not in the book,” she said, “because you have not asked me to enter it and a clerk may only enter what she is given.”

“I know.”

“You told me anyway.”

“I would rather it came out of my mouth in a week than out of her mouth in four years,” he said, “and you are the only person in this city I can say a true thing to and get nothing done about.”

She put the pen down across the book.

“That is the first true thing anybody has said at that table about a delivery in nine years,” said Wenna Raut, “and it is not going in, and I would not have wanted it to.”

## The Second Of The Four, And Three Floors

He did the third floor that night from eleven until half past five. It was the second of his four and about four washrooms between the hours, and on the way down he stood at the basin on the half landing and put cold water on his face and his hands, which has been the habit since the fourth year and has never once got shorter.

At about half past one he stopped for four minutes at the top of the stairs, and the reason he stopped was not the cold and was not his chest.

It was that he had spent nine seconds of the morning of the third of this month saying out loud, in a street, in daylight, the first line of a thing about a man he has never met, to a woman who had come out of her own house to ask about it.

Nine seconds.

And nothing in this city will ever be able to tell her or him or anybody else that it happened, because the only record of it is in a room four flights up that about two people can open, and neither of them was asked for anything.

He had spent four months building a rule about hands, and he had broken it in a doorway without meaning to, and the rule had held in four mornings out of five.

And the fifth of the five was not the worst thing in his coat. The worst thing in his coat was the four lines of the fourteenth, and next to that was a piece of pencil paper in a woman's hand from the fifth of September of the ninth year, and all three of them were about somebody else and not one of them could be produced.

*Three thousand three hundred and thirty-four days. Less three thousand three hundred and twenty-seven, the twenty-eighth of April, is seven, and five answers went out of that room in five mornings on somebody’s legs and four of the five were about a paper the person asking had brought, and the fifth was refused in a doorway in daylight after he had said the first line of it out loud standing in a street.*

*Less three thousand two hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of March, is thirty-five, and the thirty-seventh sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirty-five of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is nine hundred and fourteen days, which is thirty months and fourteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
