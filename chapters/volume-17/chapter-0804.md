# How The Register Keeps It

Three thousand two hundred and fifty-four was the fifteenth of this month and the twenty-first is day three thousand two hundred and sixty, and three thousand two hundred and sixty less three thousand two hundred and fifty-four is six, and twenty-one less fifteen is six by the month count.

## A Paper And A Person

The Register had taken nineteen rows since the second of December, and Lin Hao read all nineteen in the morning with the door open and the cold coming under it every time somebody used the stair.

Seventeen of them were about a paper the person asking had brought up themselves, and two of them were about a person.

That was the count and it was a count of rows. It was not a count of questions, because one of those rows held two questions, and it was not a count of answers, because one answer in there had been given twice to two people on two afternoons by a man who had not entered either time, which was a thing he had to look at for about four minutes before he understood he was glad of it.

He wanted to know whether the two kinds were the same thing.

He knew the answer in about nine seconds and he did not like it, and he spent the rest of the morning trying to get round it.

## What The Book Will Not Let Him Do

The instruction of the second of December says the question and the answer are entered in the Register with a date and an hour against each, in the words the person used, and may not be taken from.

Every word of that is load-bearing and one word of it is the problem.

*In the words the person used.*

You may not rule a line down the middle of a book that has stood open on nine hundred and something pages of dated lines since the first year of this office. You may not rule one down this month either, not for any reason, because the book belongs to the office and the office has never once in nine years had a column in it that was not a column somebody else had already ruled on.

You may not enter a heading. A heading is a thing a person can be asked to stop, and in about four months somebody is going to ask this office to stop using it.

So the two kinds of row stand in the same run with the same date and the same hour against it, and nothing on the page anywhere says which is which.

## Both Halves Said Out Loud

“Read me the first of the two,” he said. “The one about a person. Read it exactly as it stands and then say out loud what kind of row it is, so that nobody can ever say afterwards that a clerk put a question in the wrong place.”

Wenna Raut read it. She read everything in the voice she reads everything in, and the voice was the only thing about her that had not changed in nine years.

Then she said, “That is a row about a person. It is not a row about a paper. The paper in it came through this door on a day in the autumn with a different person standing behind it, and the person in this row is a neighbour of the person who asked, and there is a third person in it who is not in the room and has not been asked anything.”

“And the other seventeen.”

“Seventeen rows about papers the asker brought up themselves. One of them is two questions in one row, which happened because the person asked two things before she got to the door and a clerk may only enter what she is given.”

“You want a heading,” he said.

“I want to be able to tell somebody in about four months why there are nineteen rows and not eleven,” said Wenna Raut. “That is all. I have wanted to be able to say that since the second of December and I have not been able to say it once, and I have entered every one of those nineteen without changing a word of any of them, which means I entered seventeen correctly and two incorrectly if you have two kinds.”

“I have not put a heading in and I am not going to.”

“I know you have not,” she said. “I am telling you that not having one is the thing that is wrong and I would rather hear it from this table than from a rail, while there is still somebody in this room who can be asked what to do about it.”

## Two Months Of A Line Unread

Here is what had actually happened, and it took him until about eleven o'clock to see it standing there.

The instruction of the second of December had stood in that book for two months and had been obeyed every single day of those two months, and in that time it had never once been read by anybody, including him, including the man who wrote it.

It had been producing answers to askers. It had also been producing questions about neighbours.

Those are not the same work.

One of them is a person asking about a thing they brought, and being told in a room, in one voice, what this office holds, and going down a stair with it and being the only person in the city who knows it.

The other is one person asking whether another person has been here.

That cannot be given in a room with the asker in it. It cannot be given at a rail with about nine people at the rail. It cannot be written on a piece of paper and put in a coat and taken down a street. And it certainly cannot be entered in the same run of rows as the first one, and it is, twice a month.

And nothing in nine years had said any of that, and nothing in that instruction said any of it, and the instruction had been working perfectly for two months.

“We have been running two offices out of one book,” he said. “And we have known which was which every single time, and we have written it down both times, and we have not been able to write down that we have known.”

## The Shape Of The Hope

What he had, then, by about eleven in the morning, on the last working afternoon of a week, in a room with the door open and nineteen rows in it, was this.

He had a practice forming for answers. Six answers had gone down four flights in a fold in the first eight days of the month, and not one of them had been read out in a corridor, and the reason was not in any book and could not be put into any book.

And he had the beginning of a practice for the other kind, and the beginning of it was a woman at a door saying to a carrier that she had already been asked about it and had not answered either, and a man in a cap who had said he would come back with her and had been told he might bring her and might not ask on her behalf.

Two practices. Neither of them written at the foot of any page.

He made himself say the second one out loud in his own head, which is a thing he started doing in December about numbers and does not enjoy.

The first one is a delivery. It belongs to the room it is done in, it is done by two people, and one of the two can be checked, because there is a clerk at a table and a date and an hour against what she wrote.

The second one is a joint reading, and at this moment it has no room, no clerk, no second person and no practice, and the only evidence that anybody in this city wants it is a row written on the ninth of a month by a man standing at that wood with his sister-in-law behind him.

One of them is held. One of them is a sentence in a book. Both of them held by the people who were doing them and by those four people only, and if the clerk went home sick then the first practice would not happen that morning, and nothing in this city could make it happen anyway.

He had gone nine years without a list, and he was about to be asked to hold two of them in the shape of two things four people could read.

## She Spends Her One Question Of The Morning

She spent it at about ten past eleven, standing up, with the pen across the book, which is what she does when she has a question and an hour and is going to give both away together.

“May this office keep two kinds of answer,” said Wenna Raut. “That is my question of this morning and I am asking it out loud so that you cannot answer it privately.”

He had been holding the answer since about half past nine and had not wanted to give it away before the end of the morning.

“Not yet,” he said.

She waited about four seconds.

“That is not an answer.”

“No,” he said. “It is where I have got to and it is not an answer and I know the difference, and I have known it since eleven o’clock this morning and I have been holding on to it for an hour because I am rank one and I clean washrooms four nights and three off and there are nineteen rows in that book with my handwriting on the arrangement of them and I am not going to be careless about it in front of you.”

Wenna Raut picked the pen up and squared the book to the edge of the table.

“Then I shall write that down as no answer given,” she said, “which is a thing that bound book has never had in it in nine years, and which is also the first true entry anybody has made about this office since the second of December.”

In the middle of the run of nineteen, his own row from the twenty-third of August stood with a date and an hour and nothing whatever after the hour, and a space in the middle of it, and no hand had ever been near it and none was going to be.

*Three thousand two hundred and sixty days. Less three thousand two hundred and fifty-four, the fifteenth of this month, is six, and nineteen rows stand in the bound book that have been entered since the second of December, seventeen about papers the asker brought and two about a person, and the two kinds are kept in the same words and the same way and nothing on any page says which is which.*

*Less three thousand two hundred and nine, the thirtieth of December of this year, is fifty-one, and the thirty-sixth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last fifty-one of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is eight hundred and forty days, which is twenty-eight months and no days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
