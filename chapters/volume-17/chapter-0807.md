# The Second Week Answers

Three thousand two hundred and seventy-three was the fourth of this month and the eleventh is day three thousand two hundred and eighty, and three thousand two hundred and eighty less three thousand two hundred and seventy-three is seven, and eleven less four is seven by the month count.

## Four Folds In Seven Days

Four answers went out of that room in seven days and every one of them went in a fold and every one of them went to the one person who had asked.

He carried two himself, down four flights and along about four hundred yards, and it is nine minutes each way at a walk and he has a chest that has been spent since the fourth year and he made it on both mornings without stopping.

Runa Sato carried the other two and wrote whose hand took them and in what order, and neither of the two had come back.

Three of the four stayed out.

The fourth came back on the ninth, in the same fold, with the crease pressed flat again and a thumbprint in it on the outside about halfway down, pressed hard enough to leave the paper shining.

She had sent that one out herself, on the fourth, and her own two lines in his book said the hand and the order, and they were right, and she had not been wrong about anything, and the fold had still been opened.

That was the part he kept coming back to and could not get round. There was no error anywhere in the chain. The clerk wrote it. He carried it, because he would not have let a woman in a lane carry the fourth one, and he had not been asked to explain that and never would be. The carrier wrote the hand and the order in her own book in her own hand, and it was true.

And between that door and that hand there had been four minutes and a room and a person in it, and not one of those three things is an error.

## The One That Came Back

He had asked his own question of it at about nine that morning, which is what he does now, and there is one question of his own a day and it is not in the book.

“Who carried this one.”

“I did,” said the man. “It was the fourth of the month. She wrote my name against the hand and I have the day book at home with my name on it and the hour on it and I have not torn the page out.”

“Then the answer went to your hand.”

“It did.”

“Did you open it.”

“Not in front of anybody,” said the man. “I have rooms on the second floor and I have had them nine years and my sister-in-law was in them on the evening of the fifth and has been in them about nine times since the spring, and I do not say she read it and neither will you, and I have not come up here to have it said.”

Wenna Raut turned the fold over on the wood and looked at it for about four seconds without touching it.

“It has been opened,” she said. “A crease does not press flat again by itself and it does not take a thumb in the middle of a panel from inside a coat. Somebody opened it, held it open long enough to read what is written on both sides, and shut it again, and they did it with a hand that has been near a press.”

“Why a press.”

“Because that is what is in the grease,” said Wenna Raut. “I have had about four hundred papers through my hands in nine years and that mark is the one mark I have never seen on anything that came through a door in this city.”

## The Practice Holds And The Hand Does Not

He stood at the other end of that table and did not say anything for long enough that the cold came under the door twice.

Here is what the seven days had established, and he had it in front of him in the shape of nineteen rows and four folds.

The practice held. An answer in writing, put in a fold, carried down four flights and along about four hundred yards by one pair of hands, arrived at the hand it was for and at no other hand that anybody could put a name to.

He had designed that on the thirtieth of December in about eleven minutes and had said out loud in a room that every hand it passed was a place an answer could be read, and had then gone home and slept.

The hand did not hold. The fold had been carried correctly and had still been read by somebody, and the reading had happened in a room with two people in it about nine feet by eleven, and the instrument that had recorded whose hand took it had recorded the truth and had not prevented anything at all.

A carry is not a delivery. Brack had said it in a lane in the rain on the fourth of this month and Lin Hao had argued with him about the word and had been wrong about the word.

Every hand an answer passes is a place an answer can be read. He had known that when he said it out loud in that room on the thirtieth of December. He had believed, for eleven minutes after saying it, that knowing it was the same as providing against it.

## The Sentence He Would Not Say

He wanted to say something about the man who had opened it.

He had it ready and it was four sentences long and it went: somebody in your rooms read a thing that was put in a fold for you, and that is a thing that happened to you because this office put it there, and the office does not know who and will not be told and cannot prevent it, and there is an honest apology in that and about four hundred of them will be owed by the thirtieth of June.

He did not say any of it.

He had worked out in about four seconds, with his hand on the back of the second chair, that he was about to apologise to a man for the working of an office he had opened on purpose nine weeks earlier, with everybody's name on the instruction except the man's, and that every word of the four sentences would be true and every one of them would be an improvement.

## The Sentence He Said Instead

“What I can do is narrower than that and I would rather tell you the narrow thing exactly than the wide thing nearly,” he said. “You may ask for it again. Not the same sheet, because a row may be added to and not taken from and that answer is in the book and it stays where it is. A second answer, put under it, in writing, dated, with the hour against the date, in the words this office is given.”

“I have that answer already.”

“You have it on a piece of paper that a person can open,” said Lin Hao. “What I can offer is that it is said out loud in a room, to you, once, with a clerk behind the table, and that the reading is put in the book as a row with its own date on it. And that is the whole of what I can do about a room.”

The man looked at him for a while.

“Nobody has ever offered me that anywhere,” he said.

“I am not offering it to anybody. I am telling you it is a thing this office can do, and that if anybody in this city asks to hear an answer read instead of carried, the clerk at that table will say the same thing, and she will not be saying it because of you.”

## She Writes Down A Request In The Words Used

The man did not use it that morning. He said he would think about it and he went down the stair with the fold in his coat.

At about half past two he came back up four flights, which he had not said he would do, and stood at the wood.

“I want it read out,” he said. “Both of us. My sister-in-law and me. In the same room at the same time, so that neither of us is hearing it after the other one has.”

Lin Hao felt the floor of that room go out from under him the way it had gone out from under him in the middle of February when a woman asked about a building.

There was a practice for answers by hand. There was about half of a practice for something else, and what had come up that stair had just doubled it, and it was doubled by a man who wanted to hear the thing he was owed spoken in front of the person who had opened it.

“There is not a practice for that,” he said. “There has never been one. The clerk can read an answer out in that room in the voice she reads everything in, and I have not forbidden it, and I am not able to promise it, and if she says no then it is no and you will have wasted a morning.”

“Write down what I asked for,” said the man. “In my words. I want it in the book with a date on it and an hour.”

Wenna Raut opened the Register to the run of nineteen and put the date at the head of a new line and the hour against the date and wrote down in the words he had used that the person who asked on the ninth of this month has asked to have the answer under his question read out in that room with his sister-in-law present at the same time.

She read it back once, in the voice she reads everything in, and the man nodded at the words and went down four flights.

And nothing went into the office’s hand at the foot of anything in that room. She had not asked him for a line. She had wanted one in the ordinary way and had not asked.

*Three thousand two hundred and eighty days. Less three thousand two hundred and seventy-three, the fourth of this month, is seven, and four answers went out in folds in seven days and three of them stayed out and the fourth came back with a thumb in the middle of it from a hand that was never carrying it.*

*Less three thousand two hundred and nine, the thirtieth of December of this year, is seventy-one, and the thirty-sixth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-one of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is eight hundred and sixty days, which is twenty-eight months and twenty days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
