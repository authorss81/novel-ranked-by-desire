# She Files It Under The Day

Three thousand three hundred and twenty was the twenty-first of this month and the twenty-eighth is day three thousand three hundred and twenty-seven, and three thousand three hundred and twenty-seven less three thousand three hundred and twenty is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven by the month count.

## A Row With A Fortnight In It

The question the man in the cap put in on the fourteenth of this month had been standing in the bound Register for fourteen days with nothing whatever under it.

Not a refusal. Not an answer. Nothing.

Two thousand two hundred yards of a book that has stood open on dated lines since the first year of this office, and one row in it with a date and an hour and a working man's four lines of pencil paper in it, and under those four lines there was nothing at all, and it was the first row in nine years that anybody had entered and then left alone.

Wenna Raut had the answer written. It had been finished since the twenty-fourth and she had it in a drawer of her own under the counter and it was four lines long and it was dated the twenty-fourth.

## Why It Is Dated The Twenty-Fourth And Not The Fourteenth

“A question is filed under the day it was asked,” she said. “That is not a rule anybody made. It is what a day is.”

“And an answer.”

“An answer is filed under the day it is given.” She had the drawer open and did not take it out. “Not the day it could have been given. Not the day the thing it is about was done. The day it is given, by hand, to the person it is for.”

“So the fourteenth has a question on it and the twenty-fourth has an answer on it and they are a fortnight apart.”

“They are a fortnight apart and that is correct,” said Wenna Raut, “and I want you to sit down, because you are about to say something about joining them up and I have had two weeks to get ready for it.”

He did not sit down.

## What He Tried To Do With A Word

He stood at the end of that table for about four minutes and tried to join a row of the fourteenth to a row of the twenty-fourth and could not do it in any way that was allowed to exist.

The instruction of the tenth of June says every paper is entered with a description of the person who brought it, in that person's own words. A join can be made on a thing that is in both rows.

The question names no paper. It cannot. A man who will not say whose will not say which, and the office is not permitted to guess at one for him.

So the join has to be a word.

And a word that says *the answer to the question of the fourteenth is here* is a heading. That Register has never carried one, and anything that can be named can be withdrawn, and somebody will want it withdrawn inside four months.

“We have had that argument in this room already,” he said.

“We had it in February and you won it and I entered NO ANSWER GIVEN in a clerk’s hand because you would not give me a better one,” said Wenna Raut. “It is the same argument with a different date on it.”

## The Thing She Cannot Be Given

“You want me to say the word.”

“I want you to hear what you are asking for and then decide.”

“You want me to put in that register a word of your own that is not in anybody’s mouth, linking two rows that you have put there, and you want me to do it because you have asked me to.”

“Yes.”

“I cannot do it,” said Wenna Raut, “and it is worth telling you why in the plainest words I have, because you have been treating it as a difficulty and it is not one.”

“A clerk may only enter what she is given. Everything in that book got there because a person said it at this counter or handed it over that door. Every one of nine hundred and some pages of it is somebody’s mouth with a date against it, and that is the only reason the thing can be believed at all. A clerk cannot originate. That is not modesty, it is the whole of what the trade is.”

“If I write *answer to the question of the fourteenth*, then for the first time in nine years a woman at a table has written a thing she made up and dated it, and put an hour against it, and put her own hand to it, and there is not one person alive who could stand up afterwards and say she only wrote down what somebody told her.”

“Within two years somebody would use that. I do not have to guess how. I have been a clerk for nine years and I know that a thing you may do once is a thing you may do again, and the second time is always for a better reason than the first.”

She shut the drawer.

“So you can have the word or you can have a clerk, and you are not having both, and you are standing in front of me at half past ten in the morning deciding which one it is going to be for the rest of our lives.”

## The Two Rows And The Gap

She took the answer out of the drawer and put it on the wood where they could both see it, and it was four lines and it had the date at the head of it and the hour against the date.

“The book now has two rows about one thing,” she said. “Fourteen and twenty-four. And anybody who can open that book can see there are two of them, and can see how far apart they are, and cannot tell from anything on the page what either of them is about.”

“Who can open it.”

“Two people,” said Wenna Raut. “Me, and a man who may only read what he is given, and neither of us can put a word against a heading. That gap of fourteen days is in front of about two people in this city and not nine, and you may have the whole of that comfort and it is real.”

“And it is a fortnight in which this office is advertising that it is holding an answer about somebody.”

“It is a fortnight in which this office is advertising that it is holding an answer,” she said. “You have not managed to get a person into that sentence yet and I have watched you try four times this fortnight.”

## The Part He Cannot Put Down Anywhere

He read the four lines on the wood and understood at about the ninth line that he could not carry it.

Not because of the rule. Because of the content.

It said what this office holds. And what this office holds, in the case of a paper brought by somebody else, is a day on which a paper came through that front door, and a day on which a paper came through that front door is a fact about the person who carried it, and the person who carried it is standing at a door in a cap on the fourteenth asking for this.

Every other answer this office has written since the second of December has been about the person who asked. This one could not be. It was the first one.

“You cannot send it down four flights,” he said.

“No.”

“You cannot carry it. You cannot give it to Runa Sato. You cannot put it in a fold and take it to one door, because in four flights and about four hundred yards every single person it could be overheard by is somebody he has met in a street.”

“Right,” said Wenna Raut. “That is it. You have had four months and about nine weeks of it and you have arrived at it on your own and I am not going to be the one to say it.”

## What He Did With It, And Where He Put It

He picked it up off the wood.

He did not enter it. He did not enter a word about it. He did not enter a refusal against the row of the fourteenth, and he had wanted to do that for about a week and had two nights of it in a drawer of his own where he keeps nothing at all.

He stood at the table at about eleven in the morning with a folded sheet in his hand and understood that the thing he was about to do was exactly the thing he had spent four months refusing to do, and he did it anyway, and he told the clerk he was doing it out loud.

“I am taking this out of the room.”

“That is your coat and not my book,” said Wenna Raut. “Say the rest of it.”

“It is going in the inside of my coat on the left, under the paper Perla Quist wrote out in her own hand on her own paper in the eighth month of the ninth year, which is the only copy in the world of four lines out of a column, and which I have carried since the fifth of September of the ninth year.”

“Then you will be carrying two things.”

“I am going to be carrying three, if you count the empty sheet I took out of that drawer in the seventh year, and there is no line in this office that governs any of the three and there is not going to be one.”

He went down the four flights at about half past eleven with the four lines inside his coat on the left, beside a piece of pencil paper in a woman's hand that has been in that coat since the fifth of September of the ninth year, and he was carrying three things and there was no line in the city that governed one of them.

## What That Is

He put the coat on and the four lines went in beside the copy and both of them were paper and one of them was a record and the other was an answer nobody has got, and he was the carrier of both of them and could produce neither one.

“You have just become a piece of furniture,” said Wenna Raut.

“I know what I have become.”

“No, you do not, so I am going to say it in about nine words and then you can go and do the third floor tonight and think about it properly.” She squared the book. “A man who carries answers in a coat is a man who cannot be asked where an answer is. That is the whole of the comfort and it costs the whole of the office, because a clerk cannot ask you anything either.”

*Three thousand three hundred and twenty-seven days. Less three thousand three hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of this month, is seven, and a question put in on the fourteenth stood with nothing under it for fourteen days while an answer dated the twenty-fourth sat in a drawer under the counter, and a row may be added to and may not be taken from and the two of them are a fortnight apart and nothing on the page joins them.*

*Less three thousand two hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of March, is twenty-eight, and the thirty-seventh sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last twenty-eight of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is nine hundred and seven days, which is thirty months and seven days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
