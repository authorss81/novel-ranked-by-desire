# A Pencil Line Holds

Three thousand five hundred and forty-eight was the ninth of this month and the sixteenth is day three thousand five hundred and fifty-five, and three thousand five hundred and fifty-five less three thousand five hundred and forty-eight is seven, and sixteen less nine is seven by the month count.

## Ten In The Morning With No Light To Speak Of

The light in the sixteenth of this month came up over the roofs about twenty past eight and was gone off that kerb by about eleven, and he went down the slope at ten with his hands in his pockets.

He did not bring a lamp. The whole of the arrangement about that wall in the first year was that nobody keeps an eye on it, and a man in a white plate carrying a lamp down a slope in the middle of the morning is the end of it.

There were about nine people on that hill in that hour and about nine hundred mornings a year go past that board and not one of them has ever looked at the four inches under it.

## The Squares

Both printed boxes at the foot of that month’s form are empty.

They have been empty since the first year, and they were empty about a year before that, and the reason has never moved in nine years.

He stood about nine feet off the wall, which is where he stands, and looked at two empty squares in the last of the light on a wet board, and thought about a man named at a railway and about four pieces of pencil paper in about four hands, and thought about how little any of that takes.

Then he thought about the top half of that form, which he had read once in the wet in October, and gave the whole of that subject about nine seconds of his walk back up the hill.

## Her Sheet Under The Lower Batten

A piece of office paper is held against the wood under the lower batten of that board, in the place where the paste has worn thin.

A date stands at the top of it and there is a pencil line ruled straight across the bottom of it in a hand of her own, and it has not been folded and it has not been turned over and it has not been taken, and the batten is holding it the way the batten was holding it on the sixteenth of December of the year before.

That line is the only mark a person who cannot read has made at that corner in nine years.

She came down that hill about three years ago with a sheet and a form and no pencil, and she completed her own row in that office out of her own mouth on the twenty-third of December of the ninth year, and the answer that went under it was a permission and not a fact, and she has not been asked anything since and she is not asked anything now and not one person has been sent to find her.

He came within about two feet of that batten, which is nearer than he has come in nine years, and put two fingers against the paper through the gap without touching it, and took them away again.

## The Thing He Nearly Said In That Room

Somebody came down that hill behind him while he was still looking at it and stopped about nine feet off on the other side and looked at the four inches for about eleven seconds and went on down.

That is what about nine people a week do. They come down that hill, they get down low enough, they read what is there, and they go on. It has been going on for nine years and it is the whole of what that corner is for.

He went back up about four hundred yards and up the four flights and told Wenna Raut what he had looked at, and when he had finished she said the thing he had been carrying since the second of August.

“You have not told me about the sheet.”

“No.”

“You have looked at it twice now in about six months and you have told me about it twice, and there is a piece of paper on a table in this room that came out of a drawer and has been in your coat for about three years, and if you have started going and looking at that batten and then coming up here and telling me about it, then in about four years the two of those things are going to be the same thing in your mouth.”

“That is exactly what I was about to say,” said Lin Hao, “and you have said it in about four seconds and I would not have said it in nine.”

“It is what that sheet is for,” said Wenna Raut. “Not that. Not her sheet. Mine, and it is the one I was handed, and it has been on that table for about three years and it is not the same thing and it must never be the same thing.”

“I know.”

“Then say it once and then we will both shut up about it.”

“The twenty-sixth thing on that table is not her sheet and her sheet is not the twenty-sixth thing on that table,” said Lin Hao, “and I have not confused them and I am not going to.”

## What He Is Going To Do About The Nineteenth, And What He Is Not

He wanted to find the woman. That was the first thing he wanted at about ten past ten and he had it in his head before he had finished walking back up the slope.

There is about one way to do it and this office knows the way, because every one of the four hundred yards between that hill and that door is a street a person has to walk, and nine years of this office has produced about nine hundred mornings on which somebody saw a man in a white plate go up four flights.

He stopped thinking about it at the bottom of the hill.

“I am not going to look for her,” he said, “and I am not going to send anybody, and the sentence on that stone is the only record of that morning in the whole of this city and it is not this office’s and it is not this office’s to improve.”

“Why not,” said Wenna Raut.

“Because she did not put a question to anybody and she was not refused anything and she came up four flights on a cold morning and went down again because of a landing, and the whole of what she did was not ask.”

“If this office goes looking for her then from that morning it has been asking on her behalf and she never asked, and there is a woman at that rail in this city who told me on the eighteenth of last month that the right thing to do about a woman who was refused is to keep still and not be the man who fetches people.”

“You have worked out how to leave it alone.”

“I have worked out that I want about four things I cannot have this month and one of them is that woman,” he said, “and I am not going to have it and I am not going to be the reason there is a thing in this city about her.”

## Six Hands In Four Inches

About nine people a week get down low enough, and every one of them is doing it because nothing is keeping an eye on it.

There were about forty words in those four inches in about five hands in October and there are about fifty in about six hands now, and the six hands have not all been the same people, and the stones do not care and the office has never kept a count.

The newest of the six is not about a building. It is not about an address. It is not about a man who is paid nothing, which was the newest of the five in the second month of this year.

It is about a woman.

It says that on the nineteenth of the month before last a woman came up four flights in the cold and stood in a corridor and went back down again without saying anything to anybody, and that not one person in that building asked her anything and that it was a cold morning and the stair turns twice.

## And Then It Stops

That is the whole of the sentence.

It stops there, about four inches from the left-hand end of the stone, in the middle of a gap between two chisel marks, with the rest of the chisel still clean and waiting.

Whoever put it there was not finished. He had not come to the end of what he wanted to say about that morning, or he had come to the end and had about four more inches and not the nerve to use them, and he has not been back since, and about nine people a week go past that stone and read what is on it and not one of them in nine years has ever stood down there and finished another person’s sentence.

The two words scratched into that stone are not among the six hands and are never going to be, and no person in this city has ever been able to say whether they are the same words as anything on any page, and he has not come within nine feet of them in nine years and did not come within nine feet of them on the sixteenth of this month.

He went back up that hill at about eleven in the last of the light with about nine people coming down it and not one of them looked at him.

And the sentence will still be stopped four inches from the left-hand end of that stone in about four years, with the chisel clean behind it, and the woman it is about will have been about three years older and will not know that anybody wrote it down.

*Three thousand five hundred and fifty-five days. Less three thousand five hundred and forty-eight, the ninth of this month, is seven, and about fifty words in about six hands stand in four inches of dressed stone at the foot of a wall, and the newest of them is about a woman who came up four flights and went down again, and it stops in the middle.*

*Less three thousand four hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of September, is seventy-six, and the thirty-ninth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last seventy-six of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand one hundred and thirty-five days, which is thirty-seven months and twenty-five days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
