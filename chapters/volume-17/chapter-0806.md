# A Man With A Slate Counts Again

Three thousand two hundred and sixty-seven was the twenty-eighth of this month and the fourth is day three thousand two hundred and seventy-three, and three thousand two hundred and seventy-three less three thousand two hundred and sixty-seven is six, and four out of a month of thirty is six by the month count.

## Nine Stools And Nine Districts Behind Them

Two streets off Cordwain there is a lane nobody has a name for. It is about nine feet wide. On one side of it there are nine stools and behind the other side there are nine districts, and at the head of it a man sits with a slate under his arm, and on a shelf on the wall behind him there are twelve books that belong to no district in this city.

Emil Brack is paid a grain a head, and a grain is nothing, and he has been sitting at that head of lane for nine years.

He came down it at about ten past ten with the rain coming off the awnings sideways and stopped short of the first stool, the way he has stopped short of it since the twenty-ninth of December.

Nobody was on any of the nine. That is not unusual for that hour; the stools fill after eleven and empty before two.

What was unusual was that Brack had turned the slate face down on the shelf behind him, which he does on a morning when somebody has been down that lane inside four days, because he does not want to be seen keeping a count while he is talking to a man in a white plate.

There are twelve books on that shelf behind him and they belong to no district in this city, and he has had them nine years, and he has never once opened one of them in front of another person.

“Sit down,” he said, and then did not wait for him. “You want two numbers and I am going to give you both of them, and you are going to go away and do something with one of them, and I want you to hear me say first that I have been right every time I have said either of these out loud.”

“I have been to the corner,” said Brack.

“You have.”

“The twenty-eighth, in the rain, about ten minutes past six.” Brack put the slate down against his hip and did not open it. “Say what you saw and then I will say what I saw, and if we have seen the same thing we will both be wrong and if we have not we will both be right, and one of those is worth the morning.”

## What He Wanted To Be Told And Was Not

“There are five hands in it,” said Lin Hao. “There were four. The three newest ones are not about a building.”

“There are five hands in it,” said Brack. “The three newest ones are about answers, and one of the three is a woman saying she was told what the office holds about her and it was about her neighbour, and the bottom one is a man who is paid nothing and will not be told what any of it is for.”

They stood in the rain about nine feet apart and neither of them had said anything else for a while.

“Four years,” said Lin Hao.

“I know what four years means,” said Brack. “And I am going to tell you the thing you actually came down this lane to hear, which is that it is not the same four inches, and you have known that since the eighth of October of the first year and you keep standing on that kerb as though it were.”

He turned the slate over and did not write on it.

“Two inches is a kitchen thing and four inches is a stone at the bottom of a slope, and the two have been confused by everybody in this city, including you.”

“Two inches is about four hundred households with a hearth and a place under the mantel where something can be put in a hand. Four inches is one dressed stone that about nine people a week get down to read, and it is the only place in Ostara where about four hundred people ever wrote down what was done to them.”

“Same height. Same two inches of nothing to put a hand in. Not the same thing at all, and the only person in this city who confuses them is the man standing at this end of this lane.”

He put the slate against his ribs.

“You have been going down that hill for about five months because you want it to be something you did,” said Emil Brack. “It is not going to be. In about four years it is going to go quiet a second time and there is not one person in this city that I could send to you to tell you otherwise, and if there were I would be obliged to tell you he was wrong.”

## The Two Clocks, In His Own Mouth

“Now the two numbers, and I am going to keep them apart, because there was a year when somebody mixed them, and I heard about it a week after the fact, and it cost a man in a white plate a fortnight of standing in a queue that was not his fault.”

“All right.”

“Four kitchens by the end of this week, and about nine by the thirtieth. Two figures, and you know them both, and I have said them to you before and I am saying them again.”

He turned the slate over and wrote one figure on it and held it out where Lin Hao could see it, and then wiped it out with his sleeve.

“It starts again at nothing on the first, and I do the whole sum over, and it comes to the same two every month, because a name only ever does one afternoon of work and the kitchens holding it in March were holding it in February.”

“And when the two figures ever come out different, you will hear it from me before you hear it from anybody, and I have said that to you three times now and I have never once had to do it.”

“Your word, not mine.”

“My word, and I have not been paid for it and I am not going to be.”

## The Other One Is Four Years

“And the other one.”

“And the other one runs on four years, and it is a different kind of number and it is the only one of anything I have ever said that has ever frightened me.”

Brack turned the slate over in his own hands and did not write on it.

“In about four years the space under the mantel in about four hundred kitchens will be empty, in about four hundred houses of people who have never once been near your building and never will be.”

“About forty of them will say where it came from, out loud, in a kitchen, in the ordinary voice people use for a thing they have just worked out. And about four hundred will hear it, and they will believe it, because a person has no reason on earth to disbelieve a person who has just told them something about their own street.”

He wiped the slate down his sleeve.

“And not one of them will ever be told the rest. That is the part I have never been able to put down anywhere, and I have tried on four.”

“They will believe it because it is set in type.”

“They will believe it because it came out of a man’s mouth in a voice he had already used for other sentences, and not one of the four hundred will ever find out that he does not know what he was saying.”

He put the slate against his ribs and took it off again.

“Set in type is your office and two thousand counters and four times a year, and that is a press, and I have stood at a press and I will not pretend otherwise.”

He turned the slate over and back.

“But a man repeating a sentence he has read out nine hundred times is not a press, and he is worse than a press, and I have watched that window for nine years, and I have never once seen him come to a place on it and have to stop.”

## What Has Changed Since The Answers Started Going Down Four Flights

“Sit down.”

“I have nine figures before eleven.”

“Then stand up, because this part is about you.” Brack did not wait. “Six answers have gone down that stair in the first eight days of this month. Each one in a fold. Each one to the one person who asked.”

“I know how many went.”

“You know how many left. You do not know how many were read on the way.”

Lin Hao stood in the rain and did not answer that.

“A carry is not a delivery.”

Brack said it the way he says a figure, without any weight on it at all.

“A delivery means the hand it was written for took it. A carry means somebody else held it for a while and the hand it was written for never knew it had been held.”

He put the slate against his ribs.

“Every one of those two is a person in this city standing in a street with a sheet about somebody else in their coat for about four minutes, and none of it has ever been caught, because no instrument in Ostara can ask a carrier what was in the fold.”

“There is one on the card now,” said Lin Hao. “She writes whose hand took it and in what order.”

“Then that is the first honest thing that office has had for nine years and I have not heard about it from a clerk or a counter, I have heard it from a man standing in the rain, which tells you what I think of the arrangements.”

## About Nine Will Ask Who Else Has Asked

Brack got the slate up at last and wrote nine figures on it in a column he had already filled twice this morning, and did not count them out loud.

“About nine will come up that stair before this month is out,” he said, in exactly the voice he used for a figure.

“Yes.”

“And about four of the nine will not ask about their own paper.”

Lin Hao felt the cold go from his collar into the back of his neck.

“They will ask about a neighbour,” said Brack.

“They will ask who else has been up there, and they will ask it in the ordinary way, the way a person standing in a lane asks a person standing in a lane about somebody living four doors up. And they will not know that they have walked into the only thing they have ever been afraid of in the world.”

“And they will ask it here, of me, because I am the only person they have spoken to in nine years who has never once put anything in a book. And I will not have to lie to them. And I will not be able to answer them either.”

He squared the slate against his hip.

“I will not answer them either,” he said. “Not because I owe you anything and not because I am paid nothing. Because I have never once in nine years written a name in a column and I am not starting in March.”

*Three thousand two hundred and seventy-three days. Less three thousand two hundred and sixty-seven, the twenty-eighth of this month, is six, and a man with a slate was right in the rain about five hands in four inches and about two in four hundred kitchens, and said the month and the four years in two sentences in his own mouth, and neither of them said so out loud.*

*Less three thousand two hundred and nine, the thirtieth of December of this year, is sixty-four, and the thirty-sixth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last sixty-four of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is eight hundred and fifty-three days, which is twenty-eight months and thirteen days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
