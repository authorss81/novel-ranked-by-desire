# A Door With Two Names On It

Three thousand three hundred and forty-one was the twelfth of this month and the nineteenth is day three thousand three hundred and forty-eight, and three thousand three hundred and forty-eight less three thousand three hundred and forty-one is seven, and nineteen less twelve is seven by the month count.

## Two Of Them, One Paper, And Neither Of Them Is The Person

They came up the four flights within about nine minutes of each other on the morning of the nineteenth of this month and they did not come together and they did not know each other.

The woman had been at that counter in March and had been back twice since. The man had been at it once, in April. Each of them had asked in their own words whether this office held a paper that some other person had brought.

The paper was the same one.

It had come through that front door in the autumn with a man in front of it who is not in that building and has never been in it and does not know that either of them came up those stairs on that morning, and there is no rule in this city that obliges anybody to tell him.

Both questions were entered in the words each of them used. They were in the same run of the bound Register with the same kind of date and the same kind of hour against them, and there was no heading, and the two rows said the same shape of sentence about a man who was not in the room.

## The Obvious Thing, Which He Did Not Do

At about half past ten he stood at the end of the table and worked out in about four minutes what a clerk with two names and one paper would do, and it was the first thing anybody in this city would have done and it was wrong.

There is a second column on that quarterly page with four lines in it under two printed words that say *carried by*, and the fourth of those four was entered on the third of December of the year before and it has a woman’s name in the middle of a sentence and no figure on any of the four.

A fifth line would go in under it. It would say that two people asked about one paper on the nineteenth of this month, and it would carry no name, and it would carry no figure, and it would be true.

He said it out loud to the clerk before he did not do it.

“I am not going to put a fifth line in that column.”

“You were about to.”

“I was about to write five words and a date in it and it would be the best sentence that page has carried since the thirtieth of March.”

“Why.”

“Because the two words at the head of that column are a works department’s routing line,” said Lin Hao. “A clerk at that table showed me one of their dockets out of a drawer, the same stock and the same press, and a woman four flights up had known it months before her. The line under those two words takes the name of whoever is carrying a sheet from one counter to another.

“If I put two askers in it, then the two askers are on a page that goes out four times a year to about two thousand counters, and about two thousand counters hold a routing line for a man who can come down here with a docket and ask where somebody has been.”

## Nobody Is Paid Twice

He stood there for a minute with his hand on the back of the second chair.

“There is a woman’s name in the fourth line,” he said, “and it has been in there since the third of December of the year before, which is one hundred and sixty-six days, and it has been read out of a page by a man who is paid nothing about four times a week for all of them.”

“Yes,” said Wenna Raut.

“She is paid once for it.”

“She is paid once for it.”

“And if this office writes two names into that column, then whoever works in that department finds out about two more people in the next eleven minutes, and neither of them has been paid anything and neither of them has been asked.”

“Then write one line and do not put their names in,” said Wenna Raut.

“That is worse and you know it is worse, and I put it in front of you so that you could have said it.”

He stood there.

“No fifth line goes in,” he said, “and no name at all goes in, and the two of them are going to be in a bound Register with a date and an hour against both of them like every other row in it, which is the only place in this city where a person’s own words about themselves are kept by somebody who did not choose them.”

## The Two Printed Words At The Head Of It

There has been a form in this city for longer than the office has existed, and it is printed with two columns, and above the second of them there are two printed words that say that the line beneath them is the name of whoever carries the sheet from one counter to another.

They belong to a works department. They have belonged to a works department for the whole of the nine years of this office and for a long time before that, and about nine hundred pieces of that form go out every month from a press in another building.

What the office does with the second column is write the name of a woman who carried a sheet. It has four lines in it. It went on a quarterly page in the first year of this office and it has been printed four times a year ever since, and a man who is paid nothing reads it out to about nine people a week between four sentences and a striking, at the same speed as everything else, and he does not know what it is for.

Two of those four lines have been there since the first year and carry nothing at all.

The third was entered in the fourth month of the ninth year and carries a day.

The fourth has a woman’s name in the middle of a sentence, in the middle of a line of ordinary words, with no figure on it anywhere, and it is the only sentence in nine sheets and nine years that is a claim, and there is no second keeper for it anywhere and no person in this city who could strike it out.

## What He Said To The Woman

He did not say it in the corridor. He made her come to the wood, and he stood in front of it and said it once and slowly.

“I cannot answer you about that paper alone.”

She looked at him for about four seconds.

“Then what can you do.”

“Nothing today. That is the true answer and I am not going to put anything under your row that is not true.”

“Why not. There is a sheet about nine hundred pages long in that drawer and you write down whatever anybody says in that corridor. You wrote down what I said in March.”

“I did.”

“So write this down too.”

“No,” said Lin Hao. “I am not going to write down that a person may not have an answer, because a row saying that is a page in this city that says there is an answer about you and this office will not give it to you, and it goes nowhere, and in about four years somebody will be able to point at it.”

The woman looked at the floor for a moment and then up.

“You are telling me my question is about a person and not about a piece of paper.”

“Yes.”

“Not one person in this city will be able to help you then.”

“No,” he said, “and I am sorry about that, and I have said that to about four people this month and I have not improved it once.”

## What He Said To The Man, Four Minutes Later

It took about forty seconds and it was the same words in the same order, because there was nothing else to say, and the man took it better than the woman did and said one thing before he went.

“You have thought about this.”

“I have thought about nothing else since the second of December.”

The man went down the four flights and the woman went down about nine minutes behind him, and they were both seen standing at the bottom of the stair in the yard.

They were not speaking to each other. They were standing about four feet apart with their backs to the wall and looking at the yard gate, and about nine minutes later one of them said something short and the other one nodded, and then they went out through the gate in opposite directions.

Neither of them knew the other one had been up those stairs that morning. Lin Hao watched that from the top of the stair through the window in the door and could not tell whether that was luck.

## What Four People In A Corridor Heard

That is the part of it that has changed.

He refused both of them with the door open, which is the way that room has been run since the autumn, and the corridor outside it is about four feet wide and runs about nine paces, and there were about nine people in it at about half past eleven on the nineteenth of this month.

Nine people heard the word refused.

Not the answers. There was no answer to hear, because there was not one. What they heard was a man in a white plate say *I cannot answer you about that paper alone* twice, to two different people, in a flat voice, on the same morning, at four feet.

And one of the nine said, to the woman beside her, in the ordinary voice people use for a thing they have just worked out:

“So there is one.”

That is the whole of what he got wrong on the nineteenth of this month and it took two hours to happen and nine seconds to say.

An answer carried into a street can be opened by four feet of weather and come out the same. A refusal is not carried anywhere. A refusal is said, in a room, with the door open, to a person, at the hour it happens, in the voice of a man who has refused something before and will refuse something again.

He had spent four months building a practice about hands, and the practice had held in five mornings out of six, and on the sixth the thing that got out of the room was not an answer at all.

It was the shape of the answer. He had said no twice, in order, about the same paper, and nine people had watched a shape go past them in about forty seconds and had made of it exactly what anybody in that corridor would make of it.

He stood in that room at about half past eleven on the nineteenth of this month with the door open and understood that a rule about what an answer may be carried in does nothing whatever about what a refusal sounds like from four feet, and that he had not thought about it for one second in four months.

*Three thousand three hundred and forty-eight days. Less three thousand three hundred and forty-one, the twelfth of this month, is seven, and two people who had never met asked about one paper on one morning and the paper was about a third person who was not in the building, and the second column stands at four lines, and about nine people in a corridor about four feet wide heard a refusal given twice.*

*Less three thousand two hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of March, is forty-nine, and the thirty-seventh sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last forty-nine of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is nine hundred and twenty-eight days, which is thirty months and twenty-eight days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
