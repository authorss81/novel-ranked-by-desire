# The Door That Stays Open

Three thousand two hundred and nine was the thirtieth of December and the first of this month is day three thousand two hundred and forty, and three thousand two hundred and forty less three thousand two hundred and nine is thirty-one, and thirty-one is a whole month with nothing left over, because a month in this city is thirty days and a quarter is ninety.

## Nine Rows With A Date And An Hour

He was in that room at about ten in the morning with the door standing open, which was a thing he had begun doing in the autumn and had never once said out loud in that room.

The bound Register lay open on the table under the window where it had lain open every working morning since the second of December, and it had gained nine rows in the month behind them. That is a count of rows. It is not a count of people, it is not a count of claims, and it is not a count of kitchens, and it is the only kind of number this office has ever been able to put against anything.

Nine questions, and an answer entered under each of them, and every one of the nine with a date at the head of it and an hour against the date, and all of it in the words the person used.

He counted them twice and got nine both times, and he had wanted nothing else out of the morning.

What he had wanted, underneath the counting, was to be able to say that the door had cost something and to be able to say what.

He could not say it with the nine in front of him.

The nine were the whole of what a door that anybody may knock on looks like from the inside of it, and there was nothing in them that hurt. Nine people. About nine. Eight working days. No refusals, and one clerk who has not once put a hand on what she was given.

A queue like that is what a door is for, and he had spent nine years with a building that had no door at it, and had believed for about four of those years that this was the same thing.

He had stopped believing it on the second of December and had not known that he had stopped until the twelfth of January, in the rain, on a slope, looking at two blank boxes.

## Two Of Them About Somebody Else's Paper

Two of the nine were about papers other people had brought.

That was the part he had not been waiting for. He had gone round all nine four times in as many days looking for the thing he had asked for, and the thing he had asked for was a question about the asker's own paper, and not one of the nine was that, and two of them were a question about a paper that had come through that front door with somebody else standing behind it.

He had wanted an office people came to about themselves.

He had got an office people could point at one another through.

## The Figure He Put On The Table

Yusuf Adeyemi came at about half past ten with his coat open and stood in the doorway, because the door was open and he had noticed that it was.

“You have stopped shutting it.”

“In the autumn. I did not say so.”

“You did not have to.” He put a folder under his arm and did not take it off. “Give me a figure for a thing I have to put in a return. The age of the office, in days, with the arithmetic on it, because I am not going to write a figure in a division hand that I cannot show.”

Lin Hao had it by heart and had had it by heart for about four months, and he said it out loud.

“Three thousand two hundred and forty days. Thirty times one hundred and eight, with nothing left over.”

Adeyemi did not write it down.

“It comes out even,” he said.

“It comes out even.”

“It is the fourth time this office has printed a number that comes out even,” said Adeyemi. “Two thousand one hundred and sixty was the first and it was thirty times seventy-two. Two thousand five hundred and twenty was thirty times eighty-four. Two thousand eight hundred and eighty was thirty times ninety-six. This one is thirty times one hundred and eight. I have all four of them in a drawer in a division building and I have never once had cause to open the drawer for any of them.”

He stopped there and looked at the Register on the table.

“Nine years,” he said. “Not one person in this city has ever come up four flights and asked this office for any of the four.”

“That is what I am telling you.”

“No,” said Adeyemi. “You are telling me a number. You are telling me, afterwards, that you know what it is worth, and I have just told you the same thing in a different order and neither of us had to be told by the other one.”

## What The Door Is Now

The fear came up in him about four minutes later and it was the old one, the one that had come at the thirtieth of March and at the tenth of June and at the second of December, and it was not the fear of being wrong.

It was the fear of a man who has had a door he controlled for nine years and has just discovered that the door was the only lock in the building.

Everything else in that room was furniture against theft.

The drawer in the wall on the left of that table has been shut since the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, and the instruction that shut it carries his own name at the foot of it, and it has never been opened since and never will be by any hand including his. The bundle on the table beside it had twenty-six things on it and would have twenty-six things on it next month.

The copy in the inside of his coat, on the left, folded once along the short edge, was a woman's paper and a woman's hand and a woman's date, and it was in no book in this city and it would go out to no counter and it would be carried down about nine hundred streets a year by a man with a chest that has been spent since the fourth year.

And the door was open, and from the second of December any person in Ostara could put one question through it about any paper on the table, and there was not one figure in nine years he could enter that would let him choose which.

He wanted the office asked. It was being asked. He had never once considered that being asked and being able to answer to one particular person at a time were different things, and he had not considered it because for nine years there had been exactly one person to answer.

## Four Carried Out Into The Rain

The thirty-sixth sheet had been face up on a rail in a passage off Cordwain for thirty-one days.

On the afternoon it went up, at four o'clock, about four of the about eleven people standing there took out pencil paper and wrote the line about any person out in their own hands, and folded it, and went out into the rain with it inside a coat.

The clerk at the branch watched all of it through a window and entered nothing, because a clerk may only enter what she is given, and a copy made by ear in a passage is not a piece of paper that came through a door.

Four copies by the ear. Nothing in Ostara will let anybody set four copies made in a passage against two thousand originals of a printed form, and there never has been one, and one of the four has been back at that rail twice since the thirtieth of December and has asked the man under the window a question about what the line says.

That is what an answer does when it is put in front of nine people a week.

## The Corner At About Half Past Five

He went down to the foot of the hill because that is where he goes, and the month's form had been on the board by the kerb since the seventh, and the batten at the bottom of it was holding a piece of office paper against the wood where a woman had put it.

The two ruled boxes printed at the foot of that form were blank, and have been blank since a clerk was handed a sheet and a form and no instruction on the morning of the eighth of October of the first year. A pencil line ran across the bottom of her paper in her own hand, face down against the board, where you would have to kneel to guess at it.

He had an hour and not a week, and two people knelt in that hour and read four inches of dressed stone under the striking and went back up the slope, and neither of them looked at the two boxes, because a box on a public board belongs to whoever fills it and the stone belongs to everybody.

## The Tenth Question

He was back up four flights at about a quarter to six and standing at the end of the table counting the nine again for the third time that day, because a number you have looked at three times is still a number and a number you have looked at once is a feeling.

The front door of that building has never had a door onto a street at it, and the corridor outside it is about four feet wide, and on the thirtieth of December three men had stood in that corridor having climbed before the second had well begun.

The door opened at about a quarter to six.

The man who came in had a cap in his hands and he had not taken his coat off, and he did not sit, and he said the whole of it in one go the way people say a thing they have walked up four flights to say.

“I want to know whether a paper is here that was brought here by somebody else,” he said. “I am not going to tell you who brought it and I am not going to tell you whose it is, and if you write that down I will find out.”

Wenna Raut had the pen in her hand about four seconds later and she had not written anything, because a clerk may only enter what she is given and what she had been given was a man refusing to say which paper.

“I have to write the question in the words you used,” she said. “Stand closer to the table. I cannot hear you over the stair.”

He came and stood at the wood.

Lin Hao stood at the other end of it with his hands at his sides and understood, in about nine seconds and without being told, that the tenth row of this year would have a question in it about a person who was not in the room, and that the person who had asked it had made sure of that himself, and that the answer was going to have to be written anyway.

*Three thousand two hundred and forty days. Less three thousand two hundred and nine, the thirtieth of December of this year, is thirty-one, and the door has been open all of them and nine questions came through it in the month behind and two of them were about papers other people had brought.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is eight hundred and twenty days, which is twenty-seven months and ten days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*

*Three thousand two hundred and forty days is thirty times one hundred and eight with nothing left over, and it is the fourth figure this office has ever printed that comes out even, and not one person in this city has ever asked it for any of the four.*
