# The List He Will Not Make

Three thousand four hundred and fifteen was the twenty-sixth of this month and the second is day three thousand four hundred and twenty-one, and three thousand four hundred and twenty-one less three thousand four hundred and fifteen is six, and two out of a month that begins on the first is six by the month count.

## The Landing Between The Second And Third Floors

It is about four feet wide and it has a window on the yard side with a catch that has not worked since the eighth year, and at about ten past eight in the morning on the second of this month there is light coming across it from the other side of that court and staying in it.

He had not been in that room on the morning of the second of August. He had been on the landing, because the clerk had carried the book down four flights to the sill of the corridor window on the sixth of July and had carried it down again every morning since, and because a landing is where things go that are not going in anywhere.

Five people came up between eight and about eleven.

## The First

The woman wanted an answer carried to a house where the person who asked for it did not live.

“It is his mother,” she said.

“Then it is about her and it is read out in that room, once, with her in it and with you in it, or it is not given.”

“She is not going to come.”

“Then it is not given.”

## The Second

The man had been in that corridor on the fourth of July and had heard four words of a page being read to a stranger and wanted them read again.

“I was not deaf,” he said. “I want it read again.”

“No.”

“I was standing nine feet from you.”

“You were about four feet from a woman reading four words out loud in the ordinary way and you want them again,” said Lin Hao, “and from the sixth of this month a clerk in this office does not read anything back from memory, and that is in a row with a date on it, and it is not mine to take back for a man on a stair.”

## The Third

The woman wanted the four lines that were read out on the thirteenth of July in a sheet of her own.

“You wrote them. I heard them. I want to walk out of here with them.”

“There is nothing I can do with that and I said so to the other one in that room and I am saying it to you here.”

“I am not asking for it in a room. I am asking for it on a stair.”

“That is the same thing with a different floor under it.”

## The Fourth

The man wanted to know whether his brother-in-law had come up those stairs.

“He has not,” said Lin Hao. “And I am not going to tell you that he has not, and I am not going to tell you that he has, because you asked about a paper you brought up yourself and you are entitled to that, and what you have asked for in the second half of it is about a person, and the office may not ask a person a question they have not been asked.”

“So you do not know.”

“I know what is in the book and I know what is not in it and there is a difference between those two things and about four people in that corridor learned it in May.”

## The Fifth

The woman wanted a name put into the second column of a page that goes out four times a year to about two thousand counters.

“There is a form for it,” she said.

“Those two printed words have meant that since there was a press behind them, and your brother-in-law is not a woman who carries sheets between counters for any department in this city.”

“I would carry one.”

“You would have to be on a rota somewhere and there is no rota anywhere in this city that has that on it, and not one line is printed above those two words unless a department has shown it to this office, and it is paid once for showing.”

She went down without saying anything, which was correct of her.

## The One He Wanted To Say Yes To

He wanted to say yes to the fifth one.

That is the whole of what he wanted out of the morning of the second of August and he had not known it until about eleven o’clock and he did not say it to anybody at the sill, and it is in here because there is nowhere else in this city it is going to go.

A woman had offered, on a landing, to carry a sheet from one counter to another. Not for her brother-in-law. For the page. She had worked out in nine seconds at the foot of that stair that the second column on that quarterly page is a routing line and a routing line is a woman’s name, and she had come up four flights at about half past ten in the morning to say that she would stand at a counter and put her own name in it.

And the second of those two printed words means that, and there is no rota anywhere in this city with a counter on it, and about two thousand counters hold that page, and there is a woman in the fourth line of that column who has been paid once for exactly this since the third of December of the ninth year.

If he had said yes, there would have been a second name on that page, and it would have been the first name put there by anybody who came in off a street, and it would have been the first time in nine years that anybody had offered that office something instead of the other way about. He wanted it. He wanted it the way he wanted the thirty-eighth sheet to carry a new line at the bottom of it.

Instead he told her that nothing gets printed above those two words that the office has not been shown by somebody who has to be paid once for saying so, and she went down without arguing, and she was right to, and he sent her away from a thing she had come up four flights to give.

That is the cost of rank one with no standing. He can say no to about nine people a week and he cannot say yes to one of them, and there is no instrument anywhere in this city on which a man in a white plate with no figure cut into his plate says yes to anything.

## Five On A Landing And Not One Word Of It In Any Book

That is five people between ten past eight and about eleven on the morning of the second of August, and he has given that away four times and one refusal he had to explain twice, and about four other people were going up and down that stair behind them and heard all five.

He said it out loud to the clerk on the sill at about a quarter past eleven, because he makes his own decisions out loud to one person.

“Do not enter any of the five.”

“You have just refused five people.”

“I have just refused five people on a landing, and there is nothing in this city that says a refusal has to go in a book, and if I put five rows under those five refusals then in about four years this office holds five pages that say five people asked and were told no about other people.”

She wrote nothing on the sill and looked at him for about four seconds.

“That is either the best decision you have made since the second of December or the most expensive,” said Wenna Raut, “and I cannot tell you which and neither can you, and I am not going to write it down either, and that is the first time in nine years I have declined to write down a true thing.”

## The Sixth Line, On The Back Of His Hand

“You cannot decide any of it,” he said. “That is the thing I have understood this morning and I have understood it properly and I am not sure I understood it before.”

“You decided all five.”

“No. I was asked for five decisions and I gave five refusals, and a refusal is the only thing in this building that a man with no standing is allowed to give.”

He held his right hand up and looked at the back of it.

“There is no figure cut into the plate in my coat anywhere, nothing in this city has to do anything I say, and I cannot decide one thing anybody asks me about. About nine people a week come up those four flights and about four of them ask for something, and I am the instrument.”

“You are the instrument that says no.”

“I am the instrument that says no, out loud, on a landing, at the hour it happens, and that is the whole of what I have got, and it is worth more than a band and it costs me a chest.”

He got out a pencil, and turned his right hand over, and wrote six short lines on the back of it in his own hand, one for each refusal, with the hour against each, and put the hand down inside his coat.

“A copy on the back of your own hand,” said Wenna Raut.

“A copy on the back of my own hand. It cannot be produced in a room, it cannot be taken off without taking the man with it, it comes off in about a week, and there is no line at the foot of any of our pages for it.”

He put the hand deep into the coat pocket where the four other things were and did not look at any of them.

*Three thousand four hundred and twenty-one days. Less three thousand four hundred and fifteen, the twenty-sixth of this month, is six, and five people were refused on a landing between ten past eight and about eleven on the morning of the second of this month, and not one word of the five was entered anywhere, and a man with no standing wrote all six of them on the back of his own hand in his own hand with the hour against each.*

*Less three thousand three hundred and eighty-nine, the thirtieth of June, is thirty-two, and the thirty-eighth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last thirty-two of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand and one days, which is thirty-three months and eleven days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
