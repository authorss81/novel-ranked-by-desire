# The Copy Is Ash

Three thousand five hundred and thirty-four was the twenty-fifth of this month and the second of the next is day three thousand five hundred and forty-one, and three thousand five hundred and forty-one less three thousand five hundred and thirty-four is seven, and two out of a month that begins on the first is seven by the month count.

## The Form That Has Been On That Board Since The Seventh

The hundred-and-first of those forms went on the board against that kerb on the morning of the seventh of this month and it comes down on the thirtieth, and the two ruled squares printed at the foot of it are both empty and have been both empty since the eighth of October of the first year.

It is not an event and nothing happened on that slope on the seventh of this month except that a piece of paper is where it is every month because somebody brings it.

## What He Told Her At About Nine In The Morning

He told her at about nine in the morning on the second of this month, standing at the end of that table with the door open, because that is where he says the things he has decided.

He said that on the eighteenth of last month he had gone down about four hundred yards and up four narrow flights and put three pieces of paper into the stove at the back of a laundry, and that one of the three was the only copy of the foot of the standing sheet that has ever existed in this city, and that it had been written out in a woman's own hand on her own paper in the spring of the year before.

“That goes in,” said Wenna Raut.

“No it does not.”

“It is a fact about a paper this office has handled and a clerk may enter what she is given.”

“You may enter what I give you and I am not giving you this and I am going to tell you why, and the reason is not that it is a fact.”

He put his hands where he could see them.

“If that row goes in, then in about four years a stranger comes up those four flights and asks what this office holds about a copy of the foot of that page, and there is a row that says there was one and that it was destroyed on a day, and every question he can ask about it from that row has an answer in it.”

“Where it was kept. Who made it. That it went out of a building four hundred yards away and four narrow flights up from this one, in a room with nine sheets of paper under a third table.”

He let his hands drop.

“And there is one thing on that page that a person could not have worked out for themselves in four years, which is that it was not alone. Once a man knows that a thing was not alone then the building it was in is a building worth asking about.”

“That is four answers he could not otherwise get.”

“It is a description of this office's insides in about eleven words, and the woman who wrote it is in it, and she has said no six times in substance and been thanked for it not once, and the price of putting those eleven words on a bound page is paid by her about four years from now by a man she will never meet.”

## The Version She Gave Him That He Did Not Want

“Then do not enter it,” said Wenna Raut, “and do not tell me that you have decided not to enter it either, because there is a difference and you know it better than I do. A clerk who is told not to write something is being instructed.”

“A clerk who is told a thing and has to work out herself that it must not be written down is being trusted, and she will get it right, and she will also be the only person in this city who is able to say that she got it right on her own.”

“I want it in neither column.”

“Then say the whole of it out loud at that table with the door open and I will hold it the way we hold the reasons for not going into doorways, which is nowhere, and one of the two of us is going to be dead in about four years and I do not know which and neither do you.”

## What It Closes, Said Plainly To The Room

He said the rest of it and it took about nine seconds.

“Three things are closed from this morning and all three of them are mine and none of them is anybody else's business. I will not go up four narrow flights and ask that woman to write it out again. I will not go down to a flat file four hundred yards off and take the sheet out and read the foot of the page off it and put it back.”

“And I will not sit down in this room and write out of my own head four lines I have not looked at since the fifth of September of the year before.”

He put his hand on the table and took it off again without touching the papers on it.

“You have looked at them since.”

“Not since the autumn of the year before and not properly, and the four I could have reproduced tonight would be a man’s memory of a woman’s handwriting and that is worse than nothing, because it would look right.”

“And in about four years somebody asks what that column held.”

“In about four years somebody asks what that column held on the second of December of the year before, and there will be the page and about two thousand counters and this room, and that is the answer, and it is the only answer there is.”

## What It Costs, Said Out Loud At The End Of That Table

“And here is what it costs, because the last two weeks have been very cheap and I want the whole of that corridor to have it.”

“There are twenty-eight days between this morning and the thirtieth of this month and about nine answers a month go out of that room by hand, and about four rows in every nine of them are about a person and not about a paper the person brought up themselves.”

“You are telling me that for twenty-eight days there is no check at all.”

“I am telling you that for the rest of my life there is no check at all,” said Lin Hao. “That copy was the only thing in this city that could have proved what that column held on a day, and it was mine to burn and not one person in this city could have stopped me and a woman watched me do it and did not thank me and was right not to.”

He stopped there and did not improve it.

“Where does that leave us.”

“It leaves us with a page, a foot, a rail and a book, and with about nine strangers a month who bring us something and go away with something, and with not one thing in this city that can tell anybody what we said about a person on any day but the day we said it.”

“And that is a practice and a refusal and not ink, and nothing went under the foot of any page this morning and nothing ever will.”

“That is what it is,” he said, “and the sixth line of the second of December of the year before is still the last one this office will ever make at the foot of one of its own pages, and I said that on the seventeenth of last month and I am not saying it now.”

## The Question He Put To Her

He stopped at the end of the table and put one question to her, and he had been carrying it since about half past eight.

“Would you have entered it if I had not told you not to.”

Wenna Raut did not answer for about nine seconds.

“No,” she said.

“Then it was not the office that refused it.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut. “The office would have entered it and would have entered it correctly and would have entered it within about four minutes, and it would have been a good row, and it is not there because a man in a white plate is frightened of four answers in a bound book in about four years.”

## Half Past Six In The Morning At The Back Of A Laundry

He went down at about half past six the next morning, which is before the tables are up and while the stove under the copper is banked down to almost nothing, and there were about four women at the far end of that floor and the near end was empty and there was no hand-truck against anything.

Perla Quist was at the far end. She did not come over. She watched a man in a white plate get a shovel out from under the bench and put one piece of paper into a stove that was barely lit, and then watched him stand there, and then she went back to her end, and that was the whole of what either of them said.

It is a blank sheet. It came out of his own drawer about three years ago and it has been in the inside of that coat on the left for about three years and not one thing was ever written on it.

The thing on the table four floors up is not this and it is not going anywhere and the count at the end of this month will be twenty-five and one sheet again.

He stood at the door of that laundry about four minutes with both hands empty while about nine people walked past on the way to work, and the smoke came out of that flue straight up into the cold of the third of December and did not bend, and about four of them looked up at it.

*Three thousand five hundred and forty-one days. Less three thousand five hundred and thirty-four, the twenty-fifth of this month, is seven, and a decision was made in a room off a third court which is a refusal and not ink, and a piece of paper with nothing on it went into a stove at half past six in the morning and the inside of a coat on the left is empty.*

*Less three thousand four hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of September, is sixty-two, and the thirty-ninth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last sixty-two of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand one hundred and twenty-one days, which is thirty-seven months and eleven days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*
