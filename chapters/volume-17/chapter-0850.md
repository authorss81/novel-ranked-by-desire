# Twenty-Four Sheets And Twenty-Three Quarters

Three thousand five hundred and sixty-two was the twenty-third of this month and the thirtieth is day three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine, and three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine less three thousand five hundred and sixty-two is seven, and the thirtieth is the last working day of the month and of the quarter.

## A Working Day

It was a working day and there were about nine people up the four flights in the morning and one of them was a woman with a paper she had brought up herself, and the answer went back down in a fold, the flap gummed shut, with the date and the hour on the outside of it and nothing else anywhere on it.

The form came down off the board against that kerb on the morning of the thirtieth, as it comes down on the thirtieth of every month, and about four hundred people went past that board on their way up that hill and about nine of them stopped.

## What Is On That Page, In Order

The standing sheet is not the Register and it is not about anybody.

The head stands at the top of it at eight hundred and fifty-two.

Below that are about four hundred and nineteen lines with a date and an hour against every one of them and no figure and no name.

The second column carries four lines under two printed words that say *carried by*, and the fourth of them carries a woman's name and no figure, and there is no fifth line and there never will be.

Then four sentences at the foot of the page.

Then six lines in this office's hand. Five of them are instructions to a stranger. The sixth is a sentence a man said out loud in a room on the second of December of the year before, and nothing has ever gone under that foot of that page since and nothing is going to.

Under those, six lines more. The fourth has been struck out from its first letter to its last, a name stands under the striking, and under that there are two inches of nothing.

## The Head, Read Off The Page And Not Out Loud

He read the head off that page at about half past two on the thirtieth of this month with the door open and Wenna Raut in the room and about four people on the other side of that door.

Eight hundred and fifty-two. Unmoved since the thirtieth of March of the fifth year, which is one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine.

Unmoved across twenty-four sheets and twenty-three quarters. Both of those counts are right and they differ by one, and the reason is that the first sheet that was ever written is a sheet and has never been a quarter-end, and the difference is one piece of paper and not an error and not a rounding.

Three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine less one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine is two thousand and seventy, and two thousand and seventy is twenty-three times ninety, and the twenty-three is the number of quarters and the ninety is the number of days in a quarter and the whole of it has not moved.

“You have read that in this room once,” said Wenna Raut, “and at a counter once, and you told a clerk at a counter in September that a figure said at a rail goes home in about nine heads instead of staying in one.”

“Yes.”

“And you have just read it in this room again.”

“I have just read it off a page to one person,” said Lin Hao, “and the difference between that and a rail is that it is going into the flat file at three o'clock and it will be face up on a rail at four with about nine people standing at it, and neither of those two women at that rail this afternoon is going to hear either count from me.”

“In about three months they will have been told a number by about four people who have it wrong and there is nothing this office can do about that, and I am choosing it, because the alternative is that I say it at a rail every quarter for the rest of my life and it goes into about nine heads and it cannot be got back out.”

“It cannot be got back out now either.”

“It cannot be got back out now either,” he said, “and the difference is that this office made the choice at about half past two with the door open and about four people on the other side of that door, and did not put it on anybody.”

## The Corner, Accounted For

There are about four hundred and nineteen lines at the foot of that page, and about four hundred people in this city have never been told why any one of them is there, and the two numbers are not the same number and have never been compared with each other.

At the foot of that hill there is a board with a form on it and two ruled squares at the bottom of the form, and both squares are empty, and they have been empty for the whole of the nine years this office has been a room.

Under the lower batten of that board a piece of office paper is held flat with a date at the top and a pencil line ruled across the bottom of it in her own hand, and it has not been folded and it has not been turned over and it has not been taken.

Nine feet back from that wall there are four inches of dressed stone, unmoved since the first year, and about fifty words stand in them in about six hands, and the newest of the six is about a woman who came up four flights in the cold and went back down again, and it stops in the middle about four inches from the left-hand end with the chisel clean behind it.

The two words scratched into that stone are not among those six hands and are never going to be.

## One Wrong, And An Answer Under

One answer given out of this office in the last four months is wrong, and it is a wrong hour in the hour against a morning in October, and the right hour went in underneath it nine minutes later in a clerk's hand in the words the woman used, and the wrong hour is still above it and nothing in this city can take it out.

Eleven sentences went into the Register this year in the words of the people who used them, from twelve strangers, because one of them asked twice.

One row of those has nothing under it and is a question about a sister who cannot leave a house, and the answer under it is a permission and not a fact and it is the only thing this office has ever given anybody that tells them what this office will not do.

And one row has a space in the middle of it with the twenty-third of August of the ninth year at the head of it, and that space has been there for four hundred and eighty-seven days and nothing has gone near it.

## At Four O'Clock In The Afternoon

The thirty-ninth of those sheets had been face up on that rail for ninety days and it came off at four, and the fortieth went up in its place, and it carried no new line in this office's hand, because the sixth line of the second of December of the year before is still the last.

The counterman read all six lines of the foot of it out at the same speed he reads everything else, to about eleven people standing at that rail in the cold on the last working day of the quarter.

Not one of the eleven at that rail knows that there was a copy, and not one of them knows that there is not one now, and not one of them knows that a man who reads that page for nothing asked to be told about it on the twentieth of the month before last and was told no, and that the reason he was told no is that being told would have made him a man who had been told.

## The Plainest Sentence In It

He chose to lose the only instrument that could have found out who was asking about whom, and then he burned the only copy that could have proved what he had spent.

Neither of those was a loss of rank.

He is rank one and he is on a branch night shift four nights and three off, and it is the shift he asked for.

Neither of those was anybody else’s fault.

A woman did not thank him and she was right not to. A clerk entered no reason for any of it, ever, and she was right to. A man who is paid nothing went on reading a page at a rail without knowing what it was for and he will go on doing it.

## The Page For The Thirtieth Of This Month

At about five he came back up the four flights and Wenna Raut had the Register open on the table and turned round to the page for that morning.

The last row on that page went in at about ten past ten in the words a woman used: that on the twenty-third of this month a person asked whether this office would send somebody to her sister, and that the answer is that it will not, and that it will not send to anybody.

The date is at the head of it and the hour is against the date and the answer is under it, and there is nothing at all under the answer, because a day that has not happened is not a day and a person who has not come up four flights has not come up four flights.

She turned the page and the last thing he saw before she shut the book was a ruled line and about two inches of the next page, which is what a page of that Register looks like when nothing has been put under anything, and there were about nine of those on that table by the time he went down the stairs.

*Three thousand five hundred and sixty-nine days. Less three thousand five hundred and sixty-two, the twenty-third of this month, is seven, and the thirtieth went down off that rail at four in the afternoon after ninety days and the fortieth went up in its place carrying no new line, and a man who is paid nothing read all six lines of the foot of it out to about eleven people.*

*Less three thousand four hundred and seventy-nine, the thirtieth of September, is ninety, and the thirty-ninth sheet has been face up on that rail for every one of the last ninety of them.*

*Less two thousand four hundred and twenty, the twenty-first of October of the seventh year, is one thousand one hundred and forty-nine days, which is thirty-eight months and nine days after them, and a drawer in that room has been shut for every one of them under an instruction with a man’s name under it.*

*Less three thousand and eighty-two, the twenty-three of August of the ninth year, is four hundred and eighty-seven days, which is sixteen months and seven days, and a row with a space in the middle of it stood in that bound Register for every one of them with no hand going near it.*
