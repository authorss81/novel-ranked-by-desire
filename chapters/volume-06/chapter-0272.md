# The Hundred And Thirty-Fourth

The Exchange takes a valuation at ten to nine on the first working morning of a month, and the chained book comes up from the counting room on a brass tray with a woman walking beside it, because the tray is not the point and the wrist is.

Five minutes to nine, at the bottom of the sixty-one steps, Petra Ilyin was waiting with the bundle.

She had it under her arm in a square of oilcloth with a drawstring at the corner, and she had been carrying it, on and off, since the fourth of June.

"Fifty-seven days," she said. "Once, and then I go back to the job."

"Say it."

"Fifty-seven days on no roll of any kind, and this morning I am going to walk up sixty-one steps with a docket that does not have my name on it." She held the bundle out of the wind. "There is a form on the second sheet. It is the one you have to sign and it does not have a heading. You write the figure of the production and the date and your name, and that is the whole of it."

"How long does it take."

"About eleven seconds, and the pen is chained to her wrist and she has to unlock it and she does it every time without looking." Petra Ilyin turned to the steps. "The docket is one of a hundred and thirty-four and yours is the hundred and thirty-fourth, and I have carried a hundred and thirty-three of them since the fourth of June."

"And if this one does not go in before the docket is called."

"The run breaks, and the account goes on the dead list on the fourth, and every security standing against it comes off with it." She looked up at the sixty-one steps. "Give me the rest."

"The rest is that you are not paying it and you are not producing stock, because you have not got any and everybody in this building knows you have not got any, so there is one answer left and it is the only answer you have ever been able to give anybody."

"I know."

"Do you. Because I have listened to you say *I know* four times this month and three of those times you did not." She turned her collar up. "A production at thirty-four six one one eighteen is the household. That is all it has ever been. It is not a crate and it is not the clinic and it is not a hundred and eighteen lines in a book you keep in a drawer."

She nodded towards the Exchange steps.

"It is the people at the address, and the figure of them goes on the line, and the account takes the lower of the roll and the file, and you are going to write a number on it in your own hand, and this is the first instrument you have ever put your name to that costs you other people."

The Exchange steps are sixty-one and they are stone and they are worn in the middle, and at the top of them there is a counter with a window in it, and a woman behind the window who had a key already in her hand when they came up.

The wrist was a clerk's wrist, forty-ish, with the chain going through a loop in the frame of the window and round to it, and the key was in the other hand and she turned it twice.

"Fourteen," she said, to the window.

She wrote the hour at the top of a page in a chained book, and the book was chained to the desk as well, and then she took the pen off the chain with her other hand and turned it round and held it out across the counter with the nib toward him.

The form on the second sheet of the bundle had no heading. It was four inches wide and there were two lines on it and one of them was longer than the other.

He read the first line of the sheet.

Production against the account at a valuation. Figure of persons answerable at the premises on the day. Date. Name of the head.

Below it, in the same print, a line he had not been shown, and it said that **a production is a figure and not a list**, and that a list is certified in a room.

He had known that. He had known it since one o'clock in the morning in a cage on the other side of the city, from a woman twenty-three years old who works nights and counts things for a living, and he had said nothing to her at ten past six that morning and he was not going to say anything to anybody now.

Seven.

Six was his own hand on the fourteenth of June and it was a lie and it was the only figure in the file that was his, and he had been unable to put it in front of a chained book for seven weeks.

Seven was the index. Seven was a district officer's accident on the third of June at ten past seven in the evening. Seven was a correction filed in Cobb Street twenty-five minutes past eleven at night by a woman standing on the bottom step of these steps four hours ago, in a coat, who had put it on the record on the last day she held the office so that it could not be sent back for a fresh one.

Seven was the figure of the roll and the figure of the returned lines and the figure of every document in this city that had ever been produced about that address by anybody who was not him.

And five was what an office would find. And eight was what a building held. And five and eight were both true and both went into four divisions on Thursday in a woman's own book, and neither of them was on this form, because a valuation is not a room and a form is not a place to put a true thing that has not been agreed.

He took the pen.

It was heavier than a pen should be. It was chained to a wrist and the chain went back through the window frame and the clerk's hand was four inches from his, and the whole of the transaction was that her hand stayed where it was.

He wrote **seven**.

Then he put the date under it, which is the first of August, in figures, and then he put his name under that, in his own hand, the way he had put his name under four other things in thirteen days.

The clerk took the pen back without looking at it, chained it, turned the key twice, and read the hour she had already written and said, "Fourteen."

That was eleven seconds and a clerk's arm.

Petra Ilyin was outside on the steps with the oilcloth under her arm and the hundred and thirty-fifth docket in it, because she always went up on the fourth whether there was anything on the list or not.

"Fourteen days," said the clerk, to the window.

"Sixty days," said Petra Ilyin, on the steps, to the wall.

They were the same figure and neither of them had said so.

Below the counter there was a bench and he sat on it with the bundle in his hands because his hands had gone the way hands go after a thing, and Petra sat down at the other end of it and said nothing for about two minutes, which for Petra Ilyin was a speech.

"It is in," she said. "The run is not broken. The fourth is the day after tomorrow and there is going to be no list on it, and every security standing against that account is still standing on the sixth, including a guild's hold on a hearing that has been on a public wall in a shade this city has not had since the twenty-seventh."

"Beatrix."

"Beatrix." Petra Ilyin said it and then stopped, and then said it again differently. "That is what you bought this morning and I want somebody with no bundle in their hands to say it out loud, because I have carried one since the fourth of June and I have not once in sixty days been allowed to say that the mark on the first of August was for a woman's hearing. It was for an account."

"I want somebody else to say the other half."

"Not me." She stood up, squared the empty oilcloth and folded it under her arm. "I am going to go and carry the hundred and thirty-fifth on the fifth and I am going to do it the same way, and if you want to say something to me about that you can say it at the bottom of these steps at about the same hour tomorrow."

He was on the bench for another ten minutes.

There were three people in the city who could have told him what he had just done and two of them were on the record already and one of them was at a desk two miles away with a certificate that had a bar on it. So he did it himself, which is what he had been doing since the sixteenth of July and which is what had cost him the ability to be a man whose answers were all true.

A production is a figure and not a list.

Seven went into a chained book at the Exchange at ten past nine in the morning, under his own signature, in the same hour in which a division acknowledged a change of auditor of record. The account took the lower of the roll and the file. The roll said seven. The file said six in two places and seven in one and the correction of the thirtieth said seven.

It had taken the lower, which was six, and it had taken it from his own hand on the fourteenth of June, and the hundred and thirty-fourth mark had still gone in, because the hundred and thirty-fourth mark is about the account and not about the figure.

And the figure was going into four divisions, because a production is a figure and not a list but a figure on a chained book is a fact in a book that reconciles, and this book reconciled on the third of August, which was the day after tomorrow.

He could not take it off.

That was the part he had not been able to say to a woman on a step at ten past six in the morning. A production could not be withdrawn, because withdrawing it would be a return, and making a return means making a second answer, and a second answer is read in four divisions with the first one lying underneath it.

He had known that about the standing since the thirteenth of July, in a cold room, from a woman who keeps the movements and not the hands.

So from the first of August at ten to nine in the morning there would be two documents in this city carrying a figure of persons against premises 34-6-118 in his own handwriting, one of them in a chained book and one of them in four divisions, and neither of them could be got off.

One of the two said six. One of the two said seven. A third document said five in a different hand, and a fourth said eight in a fifth hand, and a fifth said seven in a woman's hand in a division. The only one of those five that had ever been written by anybody who had walked into that building and looked at the people in it was his own.

Seven of the eight women in that building were now answerable to a broker's book.

He had known that when he signed the change of auditor on the twenty-sixth at a kitchen table. He had known it again at one o'clock that morning in a cage. He had known it at ten to nine with a pen chained to somebody else's wrist.

He had written it anyway. The alternative was a form at the north desk with a space at the foot of it left empty for a week, and the fourth of August, and a run that broke, and a guild's hold coming off a hearing that a woman had asked him to buy.

Six of them slept there. One of the seven did not.

There was no office in this city that could serve Ines Duarte a copy of the hundred and thirty-fourth answer line where she lives, and there was a man in this city who had promised in the dark on the ninth of July that no paper would ever be read to her in those rooms, and he had kept it for twenty-two days.

Keeping it and swindling his own household out of a year of a woman's tail turned out to be one act and not two.

He sat on the bench until the Exchange opened properly and let about two hundred people go past him, and not one of them looked at him, and that was the last morning of his life on which that was true.

The lift operator came off the night side at half past six and had gone up to the roof of that building at twenty past seven to stand in the wind with the book in a cloth bag, and from the Square you could see her, and she was a small figure on a roof on the first of August, four months and nine days after a plate she had never wanted was put in her own hand on a counted return.
