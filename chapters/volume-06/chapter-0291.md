# The Figure That Cannot Move

The certificate went out of the office at a quarter past ten on the first of September in four copies, and by the time the kitchen light came on the second morning it had been read in four buildings by six clerks who had no reason to know it was about the room they were sitting in.

He came in at ten past five with the tram on his coat and the table was bare for the nineteenth day running.

Sun Meiling was already at it with her copy, because she had been up since half past three and had read it four times, and she had brought it down and squared it against the edge of the table in the way she does.

"Six lines," she said.

"Six."

"And the hour of it is not written anywhere, and I checked twice, and I checked the clerk's minute and the hour is in the minute."

"Then it is in the minute."

"It is in the minute," said Sun Meiling, "and I did not want it to be in the minute and I have not decided today whether I mind."

She put both hands flat on the sheet.

"I want one thing said in this kitchen this morning and I want it said once. Do not apologise to me about the seventh line."

"I was not going to."

"You were going to do it about four seconds after you put your coat down." She took her hands off the paper. "You have been ungrateful on my behalf since the ninth and I have watched you do it in a kitchen, and it is the only thing anybody in this house has done with me in the last month that I did not ask for."

Runa Sato came in behind her with the docket under her arm and did not take her coat off, which she does when she is going to be four minutes.

"Say the count."

"Four." She put the docket down. "It is still four. I did it again before I came down and I did it on the backs of two dockets and I did not get five."

"That is three days of counting on two pieces of paper."

"It is." She took the pencil out and put it on the table and did not write anything with it. "What do you want me to find."

"I want you to count what is not a document."

Runa Sato stopped.

"That is a strange sentence and it is the fourth one you have said to me in a month that I have had to sit down over."

"Solveig Rask said on the third of August that the books in this city come in threes and that not one of the three can see the others. You said on the first of September that the certificate is not a book and is not in any of the three."

"And there are four documents about this house in four divisions and a fifth thing somewhere that is not a document, and you want me to go and find a fifth thing so that the number can be five."

"I want to know what it is."

"You want a number to have a five under it." Runa Sato picked the pencil up and put it behind her ear. "That is not the same and you can hear it is not."

Petra Ilyin came in at about half past six with the bundle under her arm and the slip in her hand that she had not put in the bundle, which is what she has done since the seventeenth of August.

"Say the date you have to lodge it."

"The thirtieth." Petra Ilyin did not sit down. "A quarter round is lodged with the house on the last working day of the quarter and served on the first, and I have carried it for nine days and I have not been able to put it down, and I will be at this gate at eight."

"The first of October."

"The first of October, at eight, and there are twenty-one addresses and yours is the twentieth, and I have already walked the first nine."

She put the slip on the table and turned it round so the number on it faced up.

"So a docket is lodged tomorrow month and served on the first, and anything you want done to your household before the first has to be in before the thirtieth."

"What is left of September," said Runa Sato.

"What is left of September," said Petra Ilyin. "And I will tell you the part that matters and I have not said it to anybody. The quarter turns on the first. That is the one date in this city that can call a figure with nothing behind it, and it has been sitting there since the twenty-fourth of August, and the figure is eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight."

The kitchen went quiet for about four seconds.

"You told me that on the twenty-seventh of August," he said, "in your own rooms."

"I did not tell you that. A broker told you that in her own rooms and she has spent four years making sure that on the first of October there is a woman standing on your step." Petra Ilyin shifted the bundle. "I am the woman on the step. That is what I have been doing since March and I have just told you, and you may work out the rest of it on your own and you may not work it out."

He stood with his hand flat on the table.

"A called figure is answered with a production."

"Yes."

"And the answer is the number of people of reading age keeping an address at the premises on that morning."

"That is the whole of it and it has been in a file since the first of August." Petra Ilyin did not look at Runa Sato. "So on the first of October there is a figure of eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight with an unsecured account of thirty years behind it, and the only thing in the world that can answer it is a number of people, and that number has to be sworn by somebody on the morning itself."

"And I will not be in this building between seven and nine."

"You will not be on that step tomorrow, and you will not be in that yard, and you will not be behind that door from seven until nine, and you said all three of those to me on the nineteenth of August and I wrote them down and I will hold you to every one."

"So I cannot take the docket."

"You cannot take the docket." She picked the slip back up. "I will knock at eight and knock again and there will be nobody, and I will put it under your own door and write the hour on it, and I will not read it, and I have not read one in eleven years and I am not going to start at a man I live with."

"So nobody takes it."

"Somebody takes it." Runa Sato had her pencil out. "That is where you have not been for three days, and you have been standing in front of it."

He put his hand flat on the rail of the steps.

"The round is served at eight and the figure is called at the turn, and that answer is sworn by a person and not by a house, and the person has to be answerable at the premises." She wrote nothing down and looked at the pencil. "So it is not a question about the first of October. It is a question about who."

"You said, on the twenty-seventh of August, to a woman standing in her own rooms, that you had no intention of feeding merit into a figure by way of another human being."

"I heard you say it."

"That covers this." He put his hands in his pockets. "It covers it completely."

"It covers merit." Runa Sato said it quietly. "That answer is not merit. It is a number of addresses, and every one of them belongs to a woman, and not one grain of anything moves anywhere."

"I have been waiting three days for you to say that to me and you have not said it once and you know exactly what you are doing."

The room was very cold at that end of the kitchen because the window is two inches open and it has been two inches open since July.

"You are hiding behind a word," said Runa Sato.

"I am."

"Good." She put the pencil down flat. "Then say the other reason, because you have got one, and it is not the twenty-seventh of August."

He did not say it for about nine seconds.

"The last time I swore a figure of persons it took nine seconds," he said. "It was the first of August and a clerk's pen was chained to somebody else's wrist and I did not have to think and I have been sick about it every day since, and that figure has been in four divisions since the second of August."

"And on the first of October it would be the same nine seconds and it would be about the same seven people."

"It would be about seven people who sleep in this building."

Runa Sato picked the pencil up again and put it behind her ear for the second time.

"Give me the names," he said. "In order. Whoever could swear it, in order."

"There are three and I have had them for about a week and I have not said them out loud because the moment I say them out loud it is a plan and a plan is a thing I file."

"Three."

"Runa Sato, and my own name goes first because a thing sworn like that is a Bureau act, and I am the only one of us who can do one cleanly."

He kept both hands in his pockets.

"Sun Meiling, and she is on the certificate in ink under the bar, so she is on the instrument that answers it."

"And the third."

"Ines Duarte." Runa Sato shut her notebook. "And she is the only one of the three who has ever made a correction in this city on purpose and got away with it, and she is not under your roof, and her address is on a return of the twelfth of June, and if any of the three of us is asked for that figure in a room with a clerk in it, she is the one who will be believed."

Petra Ilyin said nothing at all on her way to the door, which is how she tells him she agrees.

"The round starts at eight on the first of October," said Runa Sato. "And the only thing in this city that can call that figure is the same morning, and both of them are about the same door."

She went out into the yard with her coat still open.

Twenty-eight days to the thirtieth, and twenty-nine to the morning.

He stood at the table with his hand on a bare piece of wood and worked out, standing up, the only thing a man can work out standing up: that a figure of eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight could not be moved, could not be paid, could not be earned, and could not be answered by anybody except a number of women who kept an address at his door.

And that he had promised a woman on a step not to be on the step.
