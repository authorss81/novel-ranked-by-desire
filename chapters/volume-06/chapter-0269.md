# A Cage With A Door On Both Sides

The instrument died at midnight without anybody doing anything to it, which is what a week does when the head of a household does not write the number at the foot of a form, and he found out about it at ten past four in the afternoon at the north desk, where the form was still lying where he had left it with the space at the foot still empty.

It had not been taken away. It was dead on its face and it would sit on that desk until a broker cleared it, and the broker had been in his kitchen the night before putting a bag on a bare table, and it would have taken her about eleven seconds to put a foot on it if she had seen it.

He wrote under the foot of it instead. **No figure is given. The head declines on the twenty-ninth of July and the week has closed.** His own name and the date and the hour, which is ten past four on the twenty-ninth, and the clerk at the desk read it back because she read everything back at that desk.

Four documents in his own hand in thirteen days: a return, a dispute, a change of auditor, a refusal.

All four went into a file with his name across the top of it, and he had gone five years in this city with nothing of his own writing anywhere, and he stood at that counter and worked out that he could not go back to being a man whose answers were all true and could not go forward either. That is what the middle of that is. It is not comfortable and it is not noble.

The lift in that building works from six in the morning to midnight and it belongs to the hall porter and Nadia Ferrous has run the night side of it alone for two years because the hall porter is seventy-one and comes in at nine.

He went down at eleven at night with nothing in his hands, on purpose, having been told on the sixteenth of July that anything with a number on it came to that building in a coat.

Nadia Ferrous had the book in a cloth bag across her knees.

"That was quick of you," she said. "I did not expect you tonight."

"How long have you been writing it out."

She did not pretend.

"Ten nights," she said. "Since the nineteenth. A page a night, in my own book, in my own hand, with the night on it. I do it in the back booth at half past twelve when the yard tram empties out and there is nothing on the floor and about forty people use this cage between one and three."

"And not one of them has asked."

"About eleven thousand people have been in this cage since the nineteenth and not one of them has looked at a book, because I put it in a cloth bag like a woman's knitting and because a lift is the one room in this city where a person looks at the doors and not at anybody in it with them."

The doors opened on the ninth floor and a man got in with a cat basket and went down two floors and got out. The doors closed. She looked at the numbers going down over the man's head.

"You have not come down here to ask me about the book," she said. "You have come down here to ask me where to put it."

"Yes."

"I have been working on that since the nineteenth and I have got nowhere, and you have come at eleven at night and told me you have got nowhere as well, and I would like to say something about it before you tell me."

"Go on."

"The book is mine," Nadia Ferrous said. "That is the part I have not said out loud since the fifteenth, and I have written it in my own book four times in ten nights.

"On the fifteenth Beatrix tore a page out of her own notebook, put it in this, and said that by then there would be a roomful of us who knew what this house owed and which of us was paying it. I wrote it down. My name went against the line, because that is what I do.

"And for fourteen days I have been the woman in a building who holds the only record in this city that no office can be served on. And every one of the eight of you has said to me, in eight different ways and without ever once saying it directly, that it would be better if it were somewhere else."

"I said it to a broker's bag last night," he said.

"You said it to a key. There is a difference and it is not the one you think." She turned the bag over on her knees. "If that key had stayed in this kitchen then any division in this city that wanted to know what is at thirty-four six one one eight could have come to Ferrier Street with a form and been given the one thing that opens a room where accounts are kept. And that is a thing you could not have undone in a year.

"This book has no key. This book is a woman's hands."

"That does not answer where it goes."

"No," she said. "It answers who is holding it, and the answer is that it is me, and it is going to be me on the first of August, and it is going to be me in September when my row — " She stopped and started again. "It is going to be me when the schedule says seven."

"You are on the schedule now. You have been on it since the first of July this year and before that."

"That is not what I am saying and you know it is not." Nadia Ferrous looked at him, and she was young enough that he had spent four years not seeing how angry she could get.

"Beatrix is on the roll and Beatrix keeps a notebook. Runa is on the roll and Runa has a hearing. Sun wants to be on the roll and cannot get there. Marguerite is on the roll and she has an office. Tessa is on the roll and she has a counter. Agnete is on nothing and she has a door. Petra is on nothing and she has a stair.

"Every single one of them has a thing that is theirs, and not one of the things is a book. I have got a book and no thing."

The lift went down two floors on its own and stopped between the fourth and the third and sat there, and she put her hand on the rail the way a person puts a hand on a rail in a building that stops.

"That is what I want," she said. "Not to be counted. You have all been counting at each other for a month and I have watched it and I have written it all down and I have not once been one of the people doing it.

"I want to be in it. Not on the roll. In it."

The doors opened on the third and a young man in a dispatch coat got in and stood at the back and looked at the indicator and said nothing, and the doors closed, and Nadia Ferrous waited until they had gone one floor before she said anything else.

"There is one room in this building that is not anybody's," she said, "and it is the one I am in from half past twelve to six, and it is a cage with a door at this end and a door at that end, and about forty people go through it a night, and a thing said in it is said in front of forty people a day without a single one of them choosing to hear it.

"You are asking me where a book should live that no office can be served on." She took the cloth bag off her knees and set it down on the floor of the cage between their feet. "It lives here. That is my answer and I have had it since the nineteenth and it took me nine days to be sure it was not a stupid answer."

"Nadia—"

"You will not want it there." She said it kindly and she said it as a fact.

"You have spent a month making everything in this house into something that can be produced, and then making the one thing that cannot be produced into a woman who holds it, and holding it is not a safe place for a person to be. That is the whole of what I have worked out in ten days and one night a week.

"It is a stupid answer and I am giving it to you at eleven o'clock at night because you have not asked me anything since the sixteenth."

They went down. The dispatch man got out at the ground and went out into the yard and the doors closed and the cage stood empty at the bottom with the street door four feet away and the cloth bag on the floor.

"Tell me the last page," he said.

"The last page is the twenty-eighth." She crouched and untied the bag and turned the pages back one at a time until she got to the end, and turned it round and held it out at the bottom of the lamp, and it was blank from the top of the page to the bottom with her own hand waiting to start it and nothing on it at all.

"Ten nights," she said. "Nineteenth to twenty-eighth. Ten pages with a night on each one and a thing in it, and I cannot write this one, and I am not going to leave it out and go round to the next one, because I have never skipped a day of that book in eleven years and I am not starting on the day a woman came into my kitchen with a bag.

"So it is blank. And the reconciliation is on the Thursday, and the last Thursday in this month was the twenty-seventh, and that page has to be in this book before a woman four divisions away reads it on the third."

"That is the third," he said.

"That is the third of August," Nadia Ferrous said. "And you are not to help me write it. That is the one thing I would like said out loud tonight, and I would like it said out loud by you, because I have written down twice this month what another person wrote and both of those times I was right and one of those times it cost a woman an office, and I am not doing it again for a page about a thing I did not see."

He looked at a blank page in a lift at the bottom of a night with a young woman crouched over it who had been on his roll for four years and had never once been in anything.

"I am not going to help you write it," he said.

She tied the bag and put it back across her knees and stood up and the lift doors came open on the yard where the last tram had gone forty minutes ago.

"Get out at the second," she said, "and walk up the far stair, and I will not know that you were ever in this cage tonight, and neither will anybody else, and that is the first thing this room has been able to do for anybody in four years."
