# The Seventh Returned Line

The division sat on the second floor at Cobb Street and the room had a door on it and a window that gave on the back of a warehouse, and it was ten minutes to ten on the second of August.

Nobody had sent for him.

That was the first thing he established, standing in the corridor with his hat in his hand: the notice of the thirtieth named one person and required one attendance, and the person it named was a woman who had left this building on the sixth of July, and the head of premises 34-6-118 was not on it and had not been on it.

He went in and sat down at the back anyway, and a clerk wrote his name against the hour, which was the second thing he established, and which he had known would happen before he came.

"There is no business for the head of this premises," said the woman at the left hand end of the table. "You are welcome to stay and you cannot speak unless you are asked."

"Then ask me," he said.

Nobody at that table liked it, and it went into the file anyway.

The required attendance had been Ines Duarte's because nothing about that schedule could move until the impediment on her certificate was dealt with face to face. That had been true on the thirtieth. At about eleven minutes past ten on the second it was not true, because at twenty-five minutes past eleven on the night before last she had put an answer on the record that did not need a room.

The woman at the middle of the table turned a sheet round.

"There is a correction on this file dated the thirtieth of July and lodged at twenty-five minutes past eleven at night," she said. "It comes from the woman who held the schedule of the fourth of May. It returns the figure at seven. It treats the seventh line of the district return of the third of June as a line that came back empty and was read as a person anyway."

"That is my wife's filing," said the woman at the right hand end, who was younger than the other two and had not spoken yet. "She filed it at a counter and it is on the record and it is correct and it is in her hand."

"She is not in this room."

"She is not in this room because ten o'clock came and went and she is not coming." The woman at the middle of the table said it without heat. "I am going to enter non-attendance and I am going to read the composition and those are two separate things and the order is not mine."

She entered it. He watched her write four words and put the hour against them and turn the sheet so it faced her.

Then she read the composition.

It took a quarter of an hour and it was the most careful thing he had ever sat through.

The composition of premises 34-6-118 is made out of returned lines, and returned lines are the district's own and not the head's. The district's return of the twenty-second of March carried six lines filled. The district's return of the third of June carried seven lines and an officer left one of them empty at ten past seven in the evening, and the office read the lines and counted seven, and the office was not unkind and the office was exact.

The head's sworn answer of the fourteenth of June said six, in his hand.

The certification schedule of the fourth of May carried six members on page two.

The certificate of the twenty-seventh of June carried six.

A correction of the twelfth of July, in a hand that was not his, carried seven.

A correction of the thirtieth of July, in the hand of the auditor of record of that schedule, carried seven.

And at ten past nine on the morning of the first of August, in a chained book at the Exchange, the head of these premises had entered a figure against a production at a valuation. The figure was seven. There was no name against it, because a production is a figure and not a list.

"Six figures and one mark," said the woman at the middle of the table. "Every one of them is on this file and every one of them is a true statement about a different question, and I am going to read the composition at seven."

"I want the head of these premises to understand that I am doing it because of a correction filed on the night before last and not because of anything he said at ten to nine yesterday morning."

"I understand."

"Then say it, because you are about to be asked something and I would rather you had it in front of you."

The question was this.

"Is the auditor of record of the certification schedule of the fourth of May a person of reading age keeping an address at these premises."

Every room he had been in for four months had been a room in which a figure about that address was on a table.

He had spent thirteen days putting his own handwriting on five pieces of paper about the number of people in his own house, and in four days he had sworn the same number into a chained book with a woman's figure behind it. He had done all of it to get one thing: a re-issue of that schedule on the first of September with a figure on it that a division could not deny.

And the thing they wanted to know, to read the composition at all, was whether Ines Duarte was one of the people living at his address.

He said yes.

He said it without qualifying it and he did not look at the clerk, and the clerk wrote one word.

"Which of the seven," said the woman at the middle of the table. "Take me to it."

That was the moment. He had known it was going to be the moment since half past twelve in the early hours of the first, in a cage, and he had thought about it on a roof that afternoon and on a bench outside the Exchange yesterday for about ten minutes.

If he gave the name, the name went against a returned line of the district's own return of the third of June, and a returned line belongs to the district, and a head may not put a name on another office's return. The only thing a head can do about a wrong line is to file a correction of his own answer, and that is a second answer, and a second answer is read beside the first, and the first says six.

If he did not give the name, the seventh person was counted and unnamed, and the composition stood at seven, and the division had no ground to refuse a re-issue, and on the first of September a schedule went out with a row for a person who could not be produced and could not be struck.

"I cannot give you a name against a returned line," he said. "The line is blank and it is yours. It has been blank since the third of June and the officer who left it is not me and the return is not mine, and if I put a name on it I am not correcting my own answer, I am correcting the district, and I am not the district."

"That is correct," said the woman at the middle of the table, and she wrote something, and it took longer than the rest of it. "Then the seventh person is counted and unnamed, and that is **how it goes on the record**."

"Enter it."

"It is being entered." She put the pen down. "And I am going to tell you the rest of it, because you have been decent about this and because you came to a room you were not summoned to and I would rather you heard it from me than found it in September."

The composition of premises 34-6-118 is read as seven. The seventh line is blank. A blank line on a composition does not stop a re-issue; it sits on it.

On the first of September this division re-issues the schedule of premises 34-6-118 at the figure of seven, on the head's sworn answer, dated and in his hand, with the certification of the auditor of record.

The head may fill the blank line once. His own hand, his own date, on the re-issue, before the certification. After the first of September, if the line is still blank, the certificate issues with the line blank, and a certificate with a blank line on it carries a bar, and a bar on a certificate is not a thing anybody in that room can lift except the head of the household, and the head of the household is the only hand that can fill it.

"There is no third hand," he said.

"There is no third hand," she said. "You worked that out on the twenty-sixth at a kitchen table and you worked it out again about eleven seconds ago and you have now said it in front of three officers and a clerk, and I have written it down, and it is going to be in your file in a month and it is going to be read by somebody who has never met you."

He sat with that for about four seconds.

"How long have I got."

"From this morning until the certification on the first of September." She looked at a card. "Twenty-nine days, and there is no thirty-first, and the certification is in the morning."

The composition was read at ten forty.

Seven. The address. The band, out of band since the thirtieth of July, which had been written on the wall without any authority of that division in it and which they noted and did not discuss.

Six members on page two of the certificate of the twenty-seventh of June, superseded. A seventh returned line, blank, counted, unnamed. An answer at a valuation at ten past nine on the morning of the first of August in a chained book at the Exchange, figure seven, no list. A non-attendance entered at eleven minutes past ten on the second of August by the auditor of record of the fourth of May, required, absent.

The last line had his name nowhere in it and her name nowhere in it, and it was the second line in a division's record about Ines Duarte that she had not written herself, and he had walked into this room and made it happen by asking to speak.

He got up to go and the woman at the right hand end, who had said four words all morning, said his name.

"Lin Hao." She had a file of her own and she had not opened it. "The correction of the thirtieth. Who was it filed for?"

"It was filed for me."

"It was filed by a member of this division against a record in which her own name stands as the seventh returned line." She looked at him the way a woman looks at a man across a counter. "You have not worked that out yet and I am going to allow you twenty minutes."

It took him four, on the walk.

He went down the sixty-one steps and across the yard and up the hill, and he worked it out on the hill with his hands in his pockets, and then he sat on the low wall at the north end of the Reading Square and made himself do it all the way through from the start.

She had filed a correction returning the composition as seven, and had entered the seventh returned line as blank and counted as a person. A district cannot be corrected by a head, so the seventh line stays blank and the seventh person is counted and unnamed, and the only hand that can fill it is his.

Which meant the unnamed seventh was **hers**.

She had done it on purpose. She had done it on the last day she held the office, in the last hour the counter took it, at twenty-five minutes past eleven at night, with a clerk and a gas lamp.

She had known that the only way to stop a district's empty line ever being closed by an office was to make the household's own answer agree with it. The price of agreeing with it was that she would be counted forever in a place where her name could not be written down.

A correction put in on the last day a woman holds an office cannot be recalled for a fresh answer. She had told him that at six in the morning on a step, and he had thought she meant the answer.

She had meant herself.

Marguerite Vale was in her own rooms with a glass of something she did not drink and she listened to the whole of it standing up.

"The seventh returned line," she said. "You are telling me that she has made herself the one person in that household who cannot be produced."

"Yes. And she did it in writing, on the record, with a date, in a division that will hold it for thirty years. She has no office after yesterday, and a review that has not been filed, and a fourteen-day answer from a sitting she did not attend. From this morning. The sixteenth of August."

"And she will be at a desk on the sixteenth of August being asked to read something, and the thing she will be asked to read will be her own name in the seventh line of a return she answered on purpose."

He stood in that room and let it happen to him.

"She told me at six in the morning that she was not living at my address," he said, "and she did not tell me why, and I have spent twenty-three days not asking her."

"Then ask her." Marguerite Vale put the glass down on the sill. "She has put her own name into a record in the one form in this city that is worse than a sworn page, and she did it for a man who was going to swear her figure at ten to nine the next morning."

"If you have any sense you will ask her. If you have none at all you will do exactly what you have done for four years, which is wait for her to come and tell you."

"There is a line that can be filled," she said. "Once. In your hand. Before the first of September. Twenty-nine days, and no thirty-first."

"I know how many days."

"Then you know what it is for." She did not drink it. "It is not for Sun Meiling's row, whatever the kitchen thinks. Sun's row is a pencil row on a roll in a coat pocket and it goes into ink on the first of September whether the seventh line is filled or not, because the auditor of record is a man living at those premises and the certification belongs to him."

"I priced a change on the twenty-sixth and I priced it wrong, and I have been wrong about it for six days."

The kitchen table was bare for the nineteenth day.

There were seven of them in it and the eighth was at her counter and the ninth was on a tram in another city. There was a cloth bag on that table and the youngest of them had not opened it and had put her hand flat on it twice in the hour, and Sun Meiling had been planted at the end of the table since he came in without saying anything at all.

She had asked a wall three days ago whether it was bad, and then what it did.

"Twenty-nine days," he said. "And there is no thirty-first."

He took the whole of it into that kitchen in about eleven minutes, the way he takes in a shape, and gave them what Ines Duarte had done on the thirtieth and why, and he told them what it had cost her, and he did not tell them that he had sworn her figure into a chained book at ten to nine that morning.

He had not told them that and would not. It was the one piece of it that belonged to her office and her hand and to no other person in the room.

Nadia Ferrous took her hand off the bag.

"The seventh line is unnamed," she said. "And on the third it goes into four divisions with a page of mine on it that says eight and a list of the eight, and one of the eight is Wren Kallo, and there is not one name in this room that is going into that line by anybody's hand except yours."

"Twenty-nine days," Sun Meiling said.

She had not asked a question. She had taken the number out of his mouth and put it on the table, which is a thing she does with everything, and she planted herself at the end of the table with her palms flat on the wood and looked at a clean rectangle on the wall where a roll had hung until the twenty-ninth of last month.

"Nineteen years," she said. "That is how long I have been a shape in other people's data."

She did not say the rest of it. He had been in a kitchen with six women in it for two months and he had not once learned how to be in one.

"Sit down," Sun Meiling said. "All of you. Not because there is going to be a speech, because a figure is going to be said out loud in this room before breakfast and I will not have it said standing up."
