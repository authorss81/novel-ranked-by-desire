# Both Roads Are The Same Door

The district premises counter on Cobb Street opens at nine and takes returns on the last working day of a month, and on the seventh of August it took questions, which it does for about four hours a day and which is the most dangerous half hour in the city.

He was in the queue at ten past nine with three questions written on the back of a docket, and he read them out four times to four different clerks before anybody gave him an answer that was not the same as the last one.

The fifth clerk was about sixty and had been on that counter since before he came to this city.

"Ask me the third one again," she said, "and ask it short."

"Can the head of premises fill a blank line on a composition return with a name of his own choosing."

"No."

"That is three answers."

"That is one answer, asked three times." She turned the docket round. "The line may take the name of a person of reading age holding an address at those premises. Not a name of your choosing. A name that is true of that address on that date. Put a name on it that is not true of that address and the sheet stops being a composition return and becomes a second answer, and a second answer is always read beside the first."

"The first is the sworn answer you made at ten past nine on the morning of the first, and four divisions end up holding both."

"So if I write a name on it, it has to be hers."

"It has to be the name of whoever is on that line." She looked at him over the docket. "And the line is blank, and the blankness of it is not mine and not yours and not an error. An officer of this office let the seventh line stand empty at ten past seven on the night of the third of June, and the office counted the lines and read seven off them."

"I was not here. I have looked at it twice this week because a composition return is not served and it is not a thing a private person can read, and I have looked at it because there are seven lines on it and six of them have names on them, and a return with six names and a seventh line is the shape of about nine hundred returns in this city and there is nothing wrong with it."

"There is something wrong with it."

"There is something **unusual** about it, and unusual is not a thing this counter corrects, and I am going to tell you the reason and then I am going to say good morning."

She put the docket down.

"This counter corrects a return when the office that owns the return is told by an office that has standing over it that the return is wrong. The district owns that return. The only office in this city that has standing over a district premises return is the office of the auditor of record of the certification schedule of the premises."

"I am the auditor of record."

The clerk looked at him for slightly longer than she had looked at him.

"So you are," she said. "You were the man in the book on the first of August and I read that entry in about a second and I did not put the two together until this morning, and I would have put them together in half the time if you had come to a counter in this city more than once in five years."

She squared the docket.

"Then you can go and tell the district that the seventh line of the return of the third of June is not blank. And the district will ask you how you know, because a head's sworn answer about his own premises is an assertion and an auditor's certification of a schedule is an instrument, and an instrument has to be supported by something."

"There is nothing behind the seventh line except the fact that somebody who went to that counter on the third of June was a person of reading age holding an address at those premises."

"There is you, and you are a person of reading age, and you hold an address at those premises, and there is a line on that return with your name on it already." She said it without any cruelty, which is the way of that counter. "You are the head line. The head line is not one of the seven."

He stood in a queue that was going nowhere for a while and then went out into the street and stood in it.

Two routes. He had gone in with two and they had come out as one.

The first route was his own hand on the re-issue, on the first of September, before the certification, once, with a name on it.

The second route was a correction of the district's own return of the third of June, and that needed the office with standing over the district, which was the office of the auditor of record of the certification schedule of the premises.

That office had been a woman's office on Lantern Row until the first of August. From the first it was his, at ten past nine in the morning, with a pen chained to somebody else's wrist.

Correcting the district's return required him to certify that the line was not blank. Certifying that required him to produce the person who had returned it. Producing her required him to say her name.

Both roads were the same door and both of them were her name and there was not one hour in the twenty-four days left to him in which that door opened by any other means.

He got as far as the corner of the yard and then a woman came out of the gate of the building opposite with a lever-arch file under her arm, and she came across the cobbles without being introduced, which is a thing that Wren Kallo had been doing for four months.

"You have been in a queue for an hour and ten minutes," she said. "I have been outside a counter for an hour and ten minutes waiting for you to come out of it, because you went in there at ten past nine and I could see the back of your head from this side, and I have decided that I am not going to wait about it."

He walked with her. She did not ask what the queue was about and he did not tell her.

"You have read Nadia's page," he said.

"Nadia has had it in a cloth bag in this building for thirteen days and she reads everything, and on the third there were four copies of it in four divisions, and on the fourth I had to go into a room in my own division and sit at a desk with a return on it."

She shifted the file.

"And the return is a composition return of the fourth quarter and there are nine hundred and forty addresses on it, and one of them is ours. And I read it, and it is not a return I have ever seen. It has eight names on it where a composition return has one line, and the eight names are in the handwriting of a lift operator, and under them there is arithmetic."

"That was not meant for you."

"That is not what that is," Wren Kallo said, and stopped walking. "That is what you have said every day for two months and it is the single thing you say to keep a thing where it is.

It was not meant for me. It was not meant for Hadewig Mers. It was not meant for the four people I told in April, and I told four people, and one of them is a man who does counter work in a building on the north side and he has it now. There are five of us, and I did not choose that and I cannot un-choose it, because that is what I am."

She put the file down on a low wall and squared it.

"I have been in this division nineteen years and I have never once been in a room where a return had an addition in it that a person had made by hand, and I have spent nineteen years looking at the shape of edits, and an edit is a thing with a before and an after, and if you have the before in your head you can look at the after and say what was taken."

"That is my gift. I am very good at knowing what was taken out."

"And yours is a household." She said it flatly. "On the third a piece of paper went into four divisions with my name at the bottom of a list of eight, and in about eleven years of that list there has not been one addition. There have been removals. There have been four edits in five years and every single one of them was something somebody took off a record to make a record fit a man."

She picked the file up again.

"That is what the division is for, that is what I built the tool for, and I have been the woman who finds the removals, and on the third there was an addition, and it was made at one in the morning in a lift by a twenty-three-year-old who was not asked."

"Wren—"

"Do not." She picked the file up. "I am not angry with you and I have thought about being angry with you for four days and I have decided that it would be the cheap version and I am not going to have it in a week in which I am about to be the reason a page of mine is read in four divisions."

"You cannot take it off her."

"I cannot take it off her and I cannot stop the addition and I cannot stop myself, because I told five people and the fifth one got it on the fourth and I did not tell the fifth one, I was simply in a room with him and I am not able to be in a room with a person and not tell them a thing."

She started walking again.

"So there is going to be a sixth, and it is going to be the man who files in my division, and it will not be on the third, it will be on an ordinary afternoon."

They came to the sixty-one steps.

"What do you want," he said.

Wren Kallo stopped on the bottom one, with a lever-arch file under her arm, in the middle of a morning with about two hundred people going past a wall in a new blue.

"I want the household," she said. "That is the answer and it is the whole of it and I have never said it out loud to anybody including myself, so you may have it once and you may not have it twice."

He stood in the kitchen at about eleven that night with six of them and took the whole of it into the room in the order he had worked it out in, and the first thing he gave them was that there are two roads out of the seventh line and both of them are her name.

He told them that she had said on the first of August, from a tram step, that she was not living at his address, and that a composition return does not ask about living.

He told them that the desk which outranks a district return of that kind had belonged to her until the first of August and belonged to him from it.

He did not tell them about the counter, because the counter was her gift and it was not his to describe.

Sun Meiling took the notebook, wrote a figure in it, and shut it.

"Twenty-four days," she said. "And no thirty-first, and the certification is in the morning."

"Yes."

"So on the thirtieth of this month a card comes down on the ranks wall and the floor goes to eight thousand one hundred and forty-three. And on the first of September a certificate either has a bar on it or it does not. Those are the two dates in your life."

Sun Meiling shut the notebook. "Twenty-three days and a day. I have been counting them since the second and I have not said so out loud, because I have not wanted to hand you a countdown in your own kitchen."

Wren Kallo was at the table. She had put the file on the table and had not opened it and had not taken her coat off.

"Twenty-four days," Wren Kallo said, "and I have told you what I want and you have not answered me, and you are going to answer me at some point in the next twenty-four days, and when you do I am going to say no."

"You said no at the bottom of the steps."

"I said what I want. The no is coming and it is mine and I am keeping it until I know what the room looks like."
