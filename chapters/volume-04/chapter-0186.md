# Absent With Reason

The sheet came up the sixty-one steps on the morning of the sixth in the post, and it was the shortest thing that had ever come to that door, and Sun Meiling had it on the table by eight.

"There are four lines," she said. "One of them is a question and two of them are about the question and the fourth one is a consequence. Read the consequence twice."

THE CERTIFICATION SCHEDULE OF THE FOURTH OF THIS MONTH RECORDS SIX PERSONS OF READING AGE KEEPING AN ADDRESS AT THE PREMISES AND FIVE MEMBERS. THE SIXTH IS TO BE ACCOUNTED FOR. THE SIXTH MAY BE ENTERED AS A MEMBER, OR THE SIXTH MAY BE ENTERED AS ABSENT WITH REASON, THE REASON TO BE GIVEN BY THE PERSON AND IN THAT PERSON'S OWN HAND. THE SCHEDULE CANNOT BE CERTIFIED UNTIL THE SIXTH IS ACCOUNTED FOR.

"They have given me two doors," said Sun Meiling, "and one of them is a name."

Six of them were in the kitchen by ten and two of them were arguing before anybody had finished the tea.

"Enter as a member," said Tessa Okonkwo. "You are the person at that door. Every paper since the thirteenth has come to you and answered itself in about ninety seconds in your own hand. You are the point of service of a household in the Slough and there is not a piece of paper in this city that gets further into that building than you, and if they want the sixth accounted for, they have it, and it costs one word and a signature."

"It costs one word and a signature and then I am the sixth member of a certified household for sixty days and then for ever, and it will be on a roll and on a certificate and in a service file in Cobb Street by the middle of next month." Sun Meiling did not look up from the sheet.

"I have been offered the seventh row of a board on a landing since the thirtieth of April and I have not touched it and I am not going to walk into a building and ask for it in a clerk's queue."

"Then take the second door," said Runa Sato.

"I am going to take the second door. That is what I came down for." She turned the sheet round and put it flat on the table. "And I am going to tell you what the second door costs, because all of you are about to be kind about it and I would rather have it out in the open.

"It says the reason is to be given by the person, in that person's own hand. Not by him, and not by a person at the premises, and not by whoever keeps the books. By me.

"So I have to write my name on a piece of paper, and my reason under it, and give both to a counter in the Reading, and it will go into a district file in the same week as his four pages and as Nadia's page nine and as whatever Runa puts her hand to on the seventh, and every document this city has about this address will then have my name on it.

"And that is the price. Four months of refusing to be written down, and the thing I get for it is a document that says I am not on the roll, and the only reason it is a document at all is that my name is at the top of it."

"That is the whole of the trade," said Ines Duarte. "And it is a good trade, and you should take it, and I want it recorded that I advised it and that you were going to anyway."

"You advised it because you could not see any other outcome and you are wrong about that and I will prove it at the counter in about twenty minutes."

Marguerite Vale stopped her at the bottom of the four stairs.

"I will price it," she said. "That is what I do and I have been not doing it for four months and it is costing me more than it used to. Give me the two documents and I will tell you what the second door costs against the first one in a year."

"No," said Sun Meiling.

"Then I will say the one thing I know about it and then I will stop." Marguerite Vale did not move. "A household that cannot be defended can only be lost, in instalments, by people who never have to say they took anything. I said that in the second week of April and I have been living on it since and it has been true four times over.

"And what you are writing on that sheet is the first instalment. You are the person papers come to, and you are putting your hand on the only document that says so, and from today that arrangement is on a page in a district file, and in about four years the arrangement I have spent thirty years in will know what it costs to keep a door open."

"Then let it know," said Sun Meiling, "and let it write to me, and I will be extremely difficult to take from a building I am standing in."

"You will be taken from a building you are not standing in," said Marguerite Vale. "That is the whole of what a broker knows and it is not a thing you can write on a form."

They offered her four other arguments in the next twenty minutes and she took none of them.

At half past eleven she put her coat on.

"You are not coming," she said.

"I was going to come as far as the stair."

"You are not coming as far as the stair. It says the reason is to be given by the person, and a man at my elbow in a queue is a man at my elbow, and it is the first time in four months that this house has sent me to a counter with something in my hand and I am going to do it the way I signed the notice on the doorstep, on my own, at half past ten, with the hour on it."

"Then I will wait here," he said.

"You will wait here," said Sun Meiling, "and that is the second time in a month you have been left out of a room in this building and I am not sorry about either of them and I want that said to my face."

The counter at the Reading on the second floor was the same counter and the man behind it was Doyle and he read the whole of the sheet twice before he looked at her.

"You are the sixth," he said.

"I am the sixth."

"And you want the second door."

"I want the second door and I have written it and I would like you to take it while I am standing here, because I have never once in my life handed a piece of paper to a clerk in this city and I am not going to start by leaving it on a tray."

She gave him the sheet.

ABSENT WITH REASON.

Then, under it, in her own hand, with the date and the hour:

I KEEP AN ADDRESS AT THE ABOVE PREMISES AND I AM OF READING AGE AND I AM NOT A MEMBER OF THIS HOUSEHOLD AND I AM NOT GOING TO BE ONE.

I AM THE PERSON AT THESE PREMISES AT WHOM PAPERS ARE SERVED. I SIGNED THE NOTICE OF PREMISES STANDING ON THE THIRTEENTH OF LAST MONTH AT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING ON THE STEP OF THIS HOUSE, AND THAT NOTICE SAYS IN CAPITAL LETTERS THAT FROM THE FIFTEENTH OF LAST MONTH ANY OFFICE IN THIS CITY MAY SERVE A PAPER AT THESE PREMISES WITH NO WARRANT AND NO REASON, AND THAT IT IS SERVED ON THE PREMISES BY A PERSON OF READING AGE AT THE PREMISES.

I AM THAT PERSON AND I HAVE BEEN SINCE THE THIRTEENTH. I ANSWERED FOUR PAPERS AT THAT DOOR. I ANSWERED A SCHEDULE OF PERSONS OF READING AGE ON THE TWENTY-THIRD IN MY OWN HAND AND I SIGNED IT TWICE ON THE BACK.

BEING THE PERSON AT WHOM A PAPER IS SERVED IS NOT BEING ONE OF THE PEOPLE IT IS SERVED ON. I CHOSE THE FIRST ON THE THIRTEENTH AND I AM NOT GOING TO BE MOVED OFF IT BY BEING OFFERED THE SECOND AS IF IT WERE A REWARD.

S. MEILING.

She did not use the standing at item eleven. Doyle looked at the margin of the sheet where the second issue had printed the fifth permitted answer and then at her, and she said, "No," and he did not ask again.

He read it back to her in the voice of a man reading out a form and put it into the tray with the four pages and clipped it.

"That is accounted for," he said. "It goes down with the schedule and it is in the file today, and in about nine years there will be a woman in an office who reads it and will not know whether I did it or whether it happened to me, and that is the correct outcome and I am not going to pretend to you that it is anything else."

"It is the outcome I have been designing since the fourteenth of March," said Sun Meiling. "I have been designing it out loud for four months in this room and not one of you has written it down until now and it took one page."

She went down the public stair and out and got the half-hourly at the junction and walked the last eleven minutes, and she did not run, and she arrived at Ferrier Street at about twenty past one in the middle of a working afternoon with a document in a district file and her own name at the top of it.

The room was there and had been there since half past eleven.

"You did not come as far as the stair," said Nadia Ferrous.

"No."

"You wrote it out."

"I wrote it out in about four hundred words and I did not have to soften one of them, which is the first thing that has ever happened to me in this city." She put her bag down. "There is a second thing and it is not about the form, and I am going to say it once and then I am going to stop being interesting and make something to eat.

"There is a shape. There has been a shape since the first of March in the things I read, and it comes back about once a fortnight, and it has said February eleven times, and I have been wrong about it eleven times, and I told him in March that I would not put a number to it and I have not.

"It came back this morning at about ten to twelve in a queue in this building while I was waiting to be served, and it was the shape it has always been, and for the twelfth time I do not know what it is,

"and I am telling you that it came back, and not telling you what I think it means, because I have told four people what I think it means and I was wrong four times and there is a fifth person in this room who will be told in about a month and I would like him to hear it without my opinion attached."

He got up when she was through the door and got as far as the landing.

"You left me out," he said.

"I left you out of a counter in the Reading for an hour and fifty minutes," she said, "and I have never once done anything in this house on purpose that was not for you, and you are going to have to sit with that for a day before I give you anything about it."

"Sit with it," he said.

"Good." She went up four stairs and stopped. "And I am not thanking you for waiting. Do not thank me for waiting. That is a different debt and it is one I am going to want paid and you would not know what you had bought."

He had heard that sentence in almost the same words from a woman in a nine-foot room at Cordwain a week ago, and he said, "Right."

She went to the kitchen.

He had been left out of a room for an hour and fifty minutes and he had been in a building he cleans every working morning for four years, and the fourth floor of that building had a bench with a grey cloth over an instrument on it and an empty slot in a rack of nameplates, and none of that had anything to do with anything, and he had thought about it for an hour and fifty minutes.

She had wanted for four months to be the one woman in that house who was not on a piece of paper, and she had got that, and the price of it was a page in a district file with four hundred words and a date and an hour on it,

and the seventh row of a board on a landing was still empty, and nine days of the sixty had gone that morning and fifty-one of them were left, and she had made certain of that in a clerk's queue in a building a mile and a half away, in her own hand, on purpose.
