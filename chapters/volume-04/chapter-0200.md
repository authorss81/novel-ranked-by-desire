# A Household Of Seven

The twentieth of this month was the last day of a thing, and the only person in that house who knew it was the man who did every figure out loud at the top of sixty-one steps, and there is no instrument in this city that marks the last day of anything.

He cleaned six floors at the Fourth Branch and did them well, and at about half past six in the evening he came up the sixty-one steps to Ferrier Street with his coat over his arm.

The roll was on the wall of the landing. The men had been to the square at nine on Thursday and had not been back, and the pale rectangle over there was still bare. Seven rows. Six names. One space, numbered, empty.

Above them, in a heavier line, THE HEAD OF THE HOUSEHOLD, and under that a name in his own hand, dated the twelfth of May.

Under the sixth row, in Ines Duarte's hand, a rule from the left margin to the right edge, the second of May, an initial, and four words: WITHDRAWN BY THE MEMBER.

That is the roll. That is what was on the wall. It is not the instrument and it has no force at all and a clerk from a district said so on a landing ten days ago and it has been true since the day it went up.

The instrument is four pages in a tray in the Reading with his name at the top of page one and six members on page two and, since yesterday morning, a head's assumption clipped to the front of it in cream weight paper with an indemnity printed at the foot of it.

Marguerite Vale came up the stairs at about seven and stood in front of the board for a long time.

"Six names," she said.

"Six names and a space."

"Six names and a space, and the space is the seventh row, and it has been the seventh row since the thirtieth of April, and in thirty-seven days it will be a number in a column, and there will be a man in an office who reads it and sees that there is one and that it is not filled.

"He will not ask why, because there is a box on the schedule of the sixth of this month that says ABSENT WITH REASON and four hundred words in her own hand, and that is why."

She put her hand flat on the wall beside the board, not on it.

"She wrote those four hundred words on the sixth of this month at a counter in the Reading and she sent you out of the room and she came back with it at twenty past one, and she said she was not going to soften one of them.

"And on the eighteenth she came down the stairs and said the seventh row was hers and it was empty, and wanted it noticed that she had done that herself, by her own hand, at a counter, and not by anybody else in this house."

"And it is hers," said Sun Meiling.

"It is hers," said Marguerite Vale, "and as of yesterday it is also a fact about a legal document, and those are two different things and they have been two different things since the sixth of this month, and what has changed since the sixth is that one of them has become evidence.

"She has spent four months insisting that not being written down was a choice. It is a choice. It is also, since the sixth of this month, a four-hundred-word statement in her own hand in a district file, timed and signed, saying why she is not on a roll and that she is the person at these premises at whom papers are served and not one of the people they are served on.

"A preference is not evidence. A statement at a counter is evidence. And the reason she is not on this roll is no longer a preference, it is a document, and in nine years there will be a woman in an office who will read it and will not know whether she chose it or whether it happened to her, and that is the correct outcome and I am not going to pretend it is anything else."

She took her hand off the wall.

"That is the last of it and I did not want to say it in a kitchen because it is not a kitchen thing. It is a landing thing. It belongs on a wall."

The others came up. Six of them and him. Beatrix Halm was on the stairs with her bag and Nadia Ferrous had a receipt in her hand from a counter in Cobb Street dated the fifth of this month and Runa Sato had nothing at all in her hands because she had signed the last thing she was ever going to sign on the nineteenth and it was not in her pocket, it was in a tray on the second floor of the Reading.

Ines Duarte came up last and stood at the fourth stair and looked at the board the way a woman looks at a thing she has read.

Seven rows on a board on a wall at the top of a stair. Above them, a head. Five names filled in. A sixth with a rule through it, dated and initialled, and four words at the end of it in the hand of the woman standing at the fourth stair. A seventh, numbered, empty.

"Six names and one space," she said. "That is the sentence. That is the whole of it and it has been the whole of it since the thirtieth of April and it is still the whole of it today, and I want it said by one person in this house and then I am going to bed."

"It is not," said Sun Meiling, from the fourth stair. "It is six names and one space and a head above them who signed an indemnity yesterday morning in a room where a clerk wrote down what was in front of the clerk.

"And the whole of what that is, is that the space has stopped being empty. It is the only empty space in this house that anybody in this city has written down, and it is written down, and that is mine, and I want that said as well."

The landing was quiet.

"There are seven of us," said Nadia Ferrous.

"Seven of us," said Beatrix Halm.

"Seven women at an address over a chop-house," said Ines Duarte, "and a roll on a wall with six names on it and one space, and six lines a day in a copybook under a table kept by six of the seven of us, and Sun Meiling, who keeps nothing and is not on it, and Agnete Lindqvist, who sleeps in this building.

"Agnete is on no list of any kind in this city and has not been on one since she came out of a room she had not been outside in eleven years."

"There is still no place for her," said Sun Meiling.

"There is still no place for her," Ines Duarte agreed, "and I have been looking, and I am the auditor, and I have read every instrument this household has touched since the twenty-seventh of April, and there is not one of them that will hold a woman who was a rank-four clerk eleven years ago and has not filed a return in any year since.

"I am telling you because it is the truth and because not one of you asked me and because somebody should say it on a landing once."

He stood at the eighth step, where he stood most nights, and looked at the board, and did the arithmetic out loud, because he had promised himself on the first of this month that he would do every figure out loud in that house, and he was going to do it until the day he did not.

"Twenty-three of the sixty are gone," he said. "Thirty-seven are left. Ten days to the end of this month and twenty-seven in the next one, and on the twenty-seventh of June a certificate issues on a schedule of the fourth of May, with his name at the top of page one and six members on page two.

"It is a standing household, and it is bound to the highest standing in it, and the highest standing in it is a guild broker's, and he knew that on the seventh and signed anyway on the nineteenth, and the reason is not on the certificate and never will be."

"You are still at eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight," said Runa Sato. "You have not moved a grain in five years and you are about to be the head of a household and you are about to be the easiest man in this city to find, and the plate does not know and will not know and there is a margin of three."

"There is a margin of three," he said. "And there is a warrant. And there is a name at the top of a page. And there are five names on a wall and a space that is a woman's, and a man who has no rank at all."

"Then that is the shape of it," said Nadia Ferrous.

"I did not want to be moved by it. I wrote myself into a service file in ninety seconds so that it would be my decision and not this house's, and I have been waiting since the fourteenth of March for a document with my name and my decision in the same place, and there is not one, and this is not it, and I am not going to pretend it is. Go to bed."

Sun Meiling went down first, to the ground-floor door that the rent comes to, with a roll of paper under her arm and a pencil, because a roll produced to a caller is produced, and the call is entered, and the number of names and spaces upon it goes on a district sheet by the end of the week.

She came back up about an hour later and put a card on the kitchen table face up.

It was the size of a call slip. It had a date on it, the twentieth of this month, and an hour, and a street, and the number of names and spaces upon the roll, and in the fifth column, which was the one she had ruled herself on the sixth of this month, she had written:

SIX NAMES. ONE SPACE. THE SPACE IS NUMBERED AND NOT FILLED. THE NAME ABOVE THE ROWS IS THE HEAD OF THE HOUSEHOLD AND HE SIGNED AN ASSUMPTION AT A COUNTER IN THE READING ON THE NINETEENTH OF THIS MONTH AND A CLERK WROTE DOWN WHAT WAS IN FRONT OF THE CLERK.

She had signed it at the foot, in her own hand, and dated it, and timed it, and she had not put his name in the column because the column wanted the number and not the name, and that is what she has said she was going to do since the sixth of this month.

The room read it.

"That is the seventh row in a district file," said Sun Meiling. "That is the first time it has ever been written down by anybody but me, and it went to a counter on my own, and it is the only piece of paper in this city that says both of those things at once — that there is a woman at that address who is not on the roll, and that a man is."

She put her hand flat on the card.

"I have been four months insisting that the seventh row was mine and that it was empty on purpose and that I put it there. And I have, and I did, and it is the only piece of authority I own.

"And today it is on a district sheet with a date and an hour and my hand at the foot of it, and from today the reason I am not on that roll is a fact about a legal document and not only a choice I made, and it is going to be read by a stranger in a building I have never been inside, and I have no way at all of taking it back."

She looked round at six women.

"I want every one of you to understand that it costs me something to hold that tonight and I am holding it anyway, and that I am not going to be thanked for it, because it is not a favour. It is a position.

"And on the sixth of this month I told him in this room not to thank me for waiting, and what I have never said out loud to any of you is that a thing done for a door does not get thanked, it gets logged, and there is no book left to log it in, and there is now a card, and I have written it, and it is mine."

The lamp went off. The light on the landing stopped about two inches above the seventh row, as it did every night, as it had done since the thirtieth of April, and the board stood in the dark with six names on it and a head above them and one space.

The certificate would be dated the twenty-seventh of June. It would go into a post by somebody who had never met one of them. And his name would be at the top of page one of it. And not one person in that house would have to do a single thing for that to happen.
