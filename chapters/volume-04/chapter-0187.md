# No Entitlement Established

Two things came up the sixty-one steps on the morning of the seventh and both of them were post, and one of them was a day late getting started and the other one was early.

The first was a slip of a sheet with a form number and a date on it and six lines under it, and it said that the certification schedule of the fourth of this month could not be certified, that it recorded six persons of reading age keeping an address at the premises and five members, that the sixth had been accounted for and accepted on the sixth and was in the file,

and that there remained one member whose standing was entered as at the twenty-seventh of April past against a correction due at that office on or before the seventh of this month, and that a schedule with an uncorrected standing in it is not certified.

Six days. The first of this month to the seventh of this month, and he said the subtraction out loud in the doorway before he had got his coat off, because he had promised himself on the first of this month that he would do every figure out loud in this house from now on.

Sun Meiling took it off the table and read it twice and put it down.

"That is today," she said.

"That is today."

The second thing was the rail at the junction by the half-hourly, and he had been reading the same sheet off it once a month since December without once stopping, because it is posted there and it is the Exchange's only way of reaching a person who is not already standing at a counter.

PERMISSIONS FOR THE MONTH. BAND ONE TO NINE AGAINST WHAT IT BUYS. ACCOUNT — CLASSIFICATION ENTERED, ruled down the margin, with the standard line against any account that does not carry one.

He stood in the cold and read the margin for the fourth month running and the line against him was the one it has always been, in the Exchange's own type, and it has never once been a complaint about what he has done:

NO ENTITLEMENT ESTABLISHED.

There was one line on that sheet that he had not read before, because it had not been there before, and it was printed under the heading about household standing, and it was about four feet down from the band table and it said that the entitlement of a standing household is the entitlement of its head, and that where the head carries none, the household carries none, and that no permission may be sold to a household on the account of any member of it.

He read it three times at a rail in the cold with the tram going over the bridge.

The women at that address had spent seven days behaving as though they were behind something. Nadia Ferrous had put an address and a head on page nine of a service file on the strength of it. Beatrix Halm had struck herself off a roll on the strength of it. Runa Sato had told a Field division in writing that she wanted out of its hands.

Five of them had read it on a roll on a landing wall and understood it to mean that an office had to ask him first.

And at a counter in this city, at this moment, the whole of that household was worth exactly the word on that sheet.

Runa Sato left at about half past ten with the correction in her coat and the slip from the post in her hand and got the tram.

She was back at ten past one and she had been in the Reading for two hours and forty minutes and she came up the sixty-one steps with her hands in her pockets and did not take the form out.

"Say it," said Sun Meiling.

"They took the standing." Runa Sato stood in the doorway with her coat still on. "In my own hand, at a counter, and the clerk read it back and initialled it and dated it as the seventh and it is in the file and it cannot be taken back and it is the only thing I have ever written that I would write again exactly as it is.

"Here it is, and I am going to say it in the words on the sheet because Ines will want the words.

"Field division, Cordwain. Entered as refusing a count on the twelfth of this month past at ten past two in the afternoon. Struck from the strength on the thirteenth of this month past by a duty clerk over a reconciliation the building had owed since the middle of February. On the strength of a standing household at the above premises since the twenty-seventh of this month past. Not on a roster. Available to a division under a document I have not yet signed."

"That is four sentences," said Ines Duarte, "and it is the whole of your life since the eighth of April in about eighty words, and you wrote it in a queue and you are going to have to read it in a district file in nine years."

"Yes," said Runa Sato. "And the last sentence is a document I have not yet signed, which means that in about three weeks there will be a second page with a second sentence on it, and the two of them will be read together, and anybody who reads them together will know exactly what I did on the twenty-ninth of March and will be able to prove it from a piece of paper I wrote myself."

The kitchen let that sit.

"They also told me the schedule is not certified," she said. "It is not refused and it is not returned. It is simply not certified, and the certificate does not issue while an uncorrected standing is in it, and the sixty days run from the twenty-seventh of April past whether it is certified or not, and every day it sits here is a day off the end."

"So the clock is running either way," said Marguerite Vale.

"The clock is running either way and the certificate lands at the end of it whether we are behind a thing or standing in the open." Runa Sato unbuttoned her coat. "Nine days of the sixty have gone. Fifty-one of them are left, and Doyle wrote both numbers on the front of the tray in front of me, which I did not ask him to do and which I am going to think about for a long time."

Nadia Ferrous said, "Then say the other thing, because you have been round it twice, and it is the other thing or it is not worth saying."

"The other thing," said Runa Sato, "is that I stood at that counter for two hours and forty minutes and I worked out that nothing any of us has done in five days has bought a single thing at any counter in this city, and that includes the page I wrote into on the fifth of this month and the rule you drew through a row on the second, and I want that said in this room by somebody other than me."

The room said nothing.

"So I am saying it," she said. "A band five buys one thing and it is being asked before you are touched, and a certificate does not buy the asking, it buys the place in the chain that makes the asking work, and the certificate has not issued, and the band five is three grains above a floor, and a man three grains above a floor is a man this city looks through.

"So this week the women at that address were standing behind a word on a sheet, and the word was on the sheet this morning, and it had been on the sheet every month since December."

Marguerite Vale put her cup down.

"And I am going to say the fourth thing," she said, "and then I am going to stop, and I would like the room to notice that I am pricing it and not explaining it.

"The Exchange does not care whose entitlement a household has. It cares what it is worth. A standing household is bound by the highest standing in it, and it is the same rule the law uses and it has been the same rule for about two hundred years, and it is not a rule anybody argues with because it is how a score survives a death.

"The highest standing in that household is not his. It has never been his. His is a band five with three grains of margin and a classification that is blank in a schedule."

She did not look at anybody.

"It is mine. I have held an entitlement in this city for nineteen years and I have never once used it on a house and I have thought about using it on a house more times than anybody in this room would find interesting.

"So a certificate at the end of fifty-one days binds six women to a guild broker's standing, and puts the Vant in a position it has not been in for nineteen years, and it happens whether I do anything about it or not, because the rule does not ask me."

The kitchen did not move.

"You have not asked her to do anything," he said.

"I have not asked her to do anything and I have just told her that there is something she can do that not one person here has asked her to do, and I would like it recorded that I told her at a kitchen table at ten past one in the afternoon with a certificate that does not issue for fifty-one days in front of it." Marguerite Vale picked her cup up again.

"And I am not going to be thanked for the information and I am not going to be talked out of it, and I have priced it and the price is not free and it is not me who pays it, and I have known that since I was thirty-one."

He came in at about five and did the washrooms and got the half-hourly and walked the last eleven minutes.

The card by the time clock on the ground floor of the Fourth Branch said that the men were coming on the tenth, which was three days off, and the tenth was the second Thursday they had printed on that card and he had checked it twice on the first and it had not moved.

Nine days of sixty gone. Fifty-one left. And on the rail at the junction, in the Exchange's own type, in a ruled margin with a heading over it, a sentence that had not been there on the fourth of this month and was there on the seventh, and which said that where a head carries no entitlement, a household carries none, and that no permission may be sold to a household on the account of any member of it.

Six women. One head. And the whole of what any of them had bought in the last five days was a line of type with a heading over it, printed by people who had never met one of them and were not required to.
