# The Head Of Household

She came to Ferrier Street at about ten in the morning on the twenty-fifth with a coat on, which she did twice a week, and she put her bag on the floor beside the table, and he came and stood at the back of the kitchen with her.

He did not ask her about the twenty-second. She had said two words to him in a corridor and one of them had been a request, and he was not going to be the man who asked anyway, and she saw him not asking and did not comment on it, which was the only thing about her that was different.

"There is a composition return on the third shelf of the drawing-board room," he said.

She did not move.

"Twenty-second of March. Late, period closed, one address, six names under it, and the box at the foot empty, and a clerk's line at the bottom with a name and an hour on it. About ninety seconds with the file."

"Who completed it."

He did not answer that.

"That is not the useful part and you know it is not," said Ines Duarte. "Go on."

"I read it and I put it back and I came out of the building."

"You did not come out of the building empty-handed. You came out of it with the only copy of a fact in your head, which in this city is the same as being empty-handed with a price attached."

She hung her coat over the back of a chair and put her bag on the table. "I am not going to ask you who signed it. I am going to tell you why I am not going to ask you, and then I am going to work, because I have about an hour before the table notices we have been in here for eleven minutes.

"You did the right thing by not telling them. I want that said before I start, because in about a fortnight somebody in that room is going to work out that there was a piece of paper in a building you have been in a dozen times and that you did not mention.

"It was my decision and you did not argue. That is the whole of what I am going to say about it.

"Here is the work and it is four things and I have never said them in this order to anybody.

"One. A composition return is not filed incomplete. There are two states a return can be in and one of them is a return and the other one is a set of answers to a form.

"If it comes back complete it goes to a district and somebody acts on it. If it comes back with the box empty, or with the box filled with a standing, or with two different answers to the same question, it does not go to a district, because it is not one.

"It goes on the third floor, which is where you found it, and it stays there. That is not a hiding place. It is a heap, and every period it is cleared and every card that is not complete goes back on it, and the heap is read by a woman doing a bundle.

"Two. So the Office has never held a household at that address, because there has never been one. That is what your four months have been, and it is exactly right, and I am saying it plainly because it stops being true within two days whatever anybody does.

"Three. A return is completed by the Office itself if it comes back without a head. Not by order. Because a clerk with a return in her hand and a schedule in front of her has to finish the sheet, and the five permitted answers are printed at the foot of it, and four of them are persons and one is not.

"And if that happens then those premises are held under a schedule at item eleven, and that is a description, and a description is what you get instead of a household, and every part of a description can be served on anybody and none of it can be answered."

She sat down.

"Four. A return is not completed by a member. It is completed by a head of household, and a head of household is a person of reading age who may be required to answer for these premises, and may be answered for, and this is the part you are going to want and you are not going to like: the head of a household does not have to keep an address at those premises."

He did not say anything.

"That has been true for longer than either of us has been in a building," said Ines Duarte. "I have kept two rooms on Lantern Row for eleven years and I have never once been near a return at those rooms, because being findable is the thing I have been refusing in three separate documents since the eighth of March."

She set her hands down flat, one on top of the other, on the table.

"So there is a legal way out of the whole of this in which you keep your plate and your address and your distance, and I go in that box, and six people at that table never have to think about you again.

"And I am going to tell you why I am not going to do it, and then you can decide what you have got in front of you.

"If my name is at the top of that return then six women are answerable to an auditor. Not to you. To the woman who reads records for a living, and who is the officer of record on a finding dated the twenty-ninth of March against the man the return came out of. I would be able to open any one of them in any office in this city without leaving my chair, and so would the office that made me."

"That is not fair to you," he said.

"It is completely fair to me. It is the only arrangement I could get." She turned the cup on the table a quarter turn.

"Now go and eat. At about four this afternoon you are going to tell six women that they need a head of household, that it is not going to be any of them, and you are going to do it without telling them what is on a shelf in a building. I want you to have eaten. That is hard on an empty stomach and harder having not done it."

That afternoon.

He told them at about four. He told them the rule, which Ines Duarte had let him take out of her office and put on a table in a kitchen, and he told it without the shelf, and six women listened to him not say a thing and not one of them said so.

He got as far as the box.

Sun Meiling worked it out in about nine seconds, which was faster than anybody else and which she had plainly been doing since the twenty-third.

"The fifth answer," she said.

"The fifth answer. And the four before it, which are a person, a firm, an employer, and a person, and then the fifth, which is eleven thousand people, and not one of them can be sent for anything."

"That is the whole of what is in that margin, and Agnete Lindqvist said so at this table on the twenty-first, and I have been carrying it round since."

Sun Meiling came down four stairs and stood on the fourth one. "So either there is a person in that box or the Office puts a standing in it. And if it puts a standing in it then this house is held under item eleven for as long as the schedule exists, and I signed item eleven onto a schedule at this door on the twenty-third in my own hand."

"I would like every person at this table to connect those two things. I have been not connecting them for two days out of what I am about to describe as self-preservation."

"You signed a schedule," said Ines Duarte. "On the twenty-third. A schedule of persons of reading age keeping an address at these premises."

"The answer I gave was a number, and then the standing underneath it. Six, and item eleven." Sun Meiling did not move. "I wrote it because I was not going to write six names, and there was a permitted answer that was not a person, and it was the fourteenth of March all over again in a different room and I did it in about four minutes."

"And you told us," said Beatrix Halm, from the doorway.

"I told a woman in a yard and I did not tell six people at a table. That is a different thing and you know it is, because a yard is two women and a table is a household, and a household finds out."

That sat in the room for about four seconds.

"Right," said Sun Meiling. "Then you all know what the rest of it is, because you have had it since the twenty-first and so have I, and not one of us has said it out loud.

"The box wants a head of household. I am not going into it, and you know the reason, and not one of you is going to make me say it, and I am grateful.

"So it is one of the six of you, or it is him, or it goes in with an empty line in it and the Office fills that in itself with eleven thousand people who have never heard of Ferrier Street."

Nothing came out of the room for about four seconds.

"Sun," said Tessa Okonkwo.

"I have not finished."

"I know you have not."

"You are going to want to say that it can be you, and it can, and I have thought about it and I am not going to let you. I would rather you did not offer and had to be refused than the other way round."

Sun Meiling put her hand flat on the rail. "The clinic is unfunded and the chart is in a Wing file since the seventh. If I am answerable for this house then the next thing at this door has my name on it twice, and it will not be a chart, it will be a schedule, and the schedule will be about the clinic."

"Then it is him," said Runa Sato.

That stopped the room, and not because it was a surprise.

"Yes," said Ines Duarte. "It is him, and it has been him since about ten o'clock this morning, and he has known it since the twenty-first, and I want to say the last part of it before he makes him say it, because he will make a bad job of it."

She stood up.

"He has been the least findable person in this city for four years," she said. "That is not a policy. It is a thing he built out of not being written down, and it has cost him nothing and it has cost every one of you something, and it is also, at this moment, the only reason there is a version of the next two days in which a piece of paper about six names does not go to a district office.

"And he has said in this kitchen that he will take it, because he said it in a corridor at ten this morning and I made him say it twice. I am reporting it because he will not be able to say it at that table without looking like a man who has been saving something up.

"So I am going to read out what he has accepted, and he is going to sit there while I do it, and then he is going to tell me whether he still wants it."

She did not read from anything.

"Runa Sato. A written statement filed in advance and a count declined on the twelfth of this month, a hearing that has not resolved, and a rule through a line on a strength wall, because a duty clerk owed that building a count since the middle of February. Two papers, one of them hers.

"Tessa Okonkwo. Mass, in a Wing file, in her own hand, on the seventh of this month, and a chart, and a clinic unfunded for nine years that is now inside a file.

"Nadia Ferrous. Forty-one names in a transcript for sale over a counter for a shilling, with not one line in any document in this city saying she chose it.

"Beatrix Halm. A broken price, unpaid, uncollected, unforgiven since before this month began, a ninth of September, and a year of her handwriting on a map.

"Ines Duarte. Two refusals in her own hand since the eighth of March, and a review on the morning of the twenty-second that she will not discuss and has not discussed.

"Marguerite Vale. Terms declared off on the fourteenth of March, down, unsigned, out of force, and no household can bring them back."

She stopped.

"Sun Meiling, who is not on it and will not be. And Agnete Lindqvist, who could not go into it if we had a column for her, and there is not one."

"Runa," said Tessa Okonkwo.

"I know what I am putting on him."

"Two," said Tessa Okonkwo. "Then you know there are two of us."

"There are two of us," said Runa Sato. "One has a rule through her line and one has a chart in a Wing file, and he is going to take both, by writing his name at the top of a piece of paper, and I have never in nine years seen a man put himself in front of a thing and I did not think he was the sort."

"Go on," said Ines Duarte.

"You say that because he is going to be able to stop a requisition on any one of us."

"He is going to be able to be asked before any one of you is touched. That is band five and it is a bond, and it runs one way and it runs through him, and it is worth exactly what he is worth on a plate, which is about three grains of margin." Ines Duarte sat back down. "That is the whole of what he is buying, and I want the room to have said out loud that it is that small."

He got up out of the chair.

"Six days ago it would have been zero," he said. "Put my name in it."

He said it out loud in a kitchen with six women in it, and it went across the room, and it could not be taken back, and every one of them heard it, and not one of them thanked him, which is correct, and which he had not expected, because it was not a gift.

The room said nothing at all.

"Sun," he said.

"Do not."

"You said the last thing."

"I said the last thing about the box and I have not said anything about the other thing, and you are not going to make me, because I am going to spend the rest of my life being the one woman in that house who is not on a piece of paper, and I decided it today, and I am not going to be talked out of it by somebody who has just volunteered to be on one for me."

She went up the stairs. She did not shut her door.

Ines Duarte put the cup down.

"Then the return goes in complete on the twenty-seventh," she said. "That is two days. And you will have to be entered, and being entered is a form, and a form is completed in a room in front of a clerk who writes down what is in front of the clerk, and there is no version of that which happens at a counter in five minutes. I have watched people try.

"So you are going to have to go back into that building," she said, "and this time somebody will write down that you came."

She picked up her bag.

"That is the part you cannot put back. Not the name on the return. The fact that there is going to be a piece of paper, somewhere, in somebody else's hand, saying that you came, which you did not ask for and cannot stop."
