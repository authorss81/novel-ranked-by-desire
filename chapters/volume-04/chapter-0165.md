# Ines Duarte

She was at the table at about half past seven on the fifteenth with a coat still on and her bag on the floor beside her, which is what she did twice a week, and she had a cup in front of her that had gone cold.

"Before anybody comes in," she said. "Come and stand at the back with me."

He stood at the back with her.

"I am going to tell you one thing and I am going to ask you for something in the same conversation, and I want both of those facts on the table before either of them happens.

"The thing is my review. It is on the morning of the twenty-second. That is a month of this month and it has been in a room somewhere since the winter and I have not said it out loud in any room in this city, including this one, until now."

He did not say anything.

"Do not do a face," she said. "I have seen you do a face at a doctor and I have seen you do it to six women at this table and I have watched you do it to a clerk on a stair. It is a good face and it means you have already started solving me.

"I am not going to be solved. I am going to say a rule and then I am going to stop, and the rule is the only thing I have that is worth having this month, and I have never once been able to get anybody to listen to it."

"Say the rule."

"The only thing that defends a person against an audit is being the same woman all the way back."

The kitchen made the noise it made.

"It is not a metaphor and I would like you to hear it as a piece of machinery, because I have been in a room where it was used and I watched it work on a person who had done nothing.

"An audit does not ask what you did. You know what you did and you have a version of it and there is no room in the city where anybody can make you give up the version. An audit asks three questions and it has asked them for twenty years and it will ask them in a fortnight.

"Who were you on this date. Who signed for you on this date. And is the person answering me the same person the second of those questions is about.

"That is all it is. It is a machine for finding out whether a person has been continuous, and it works, and it has never once failed, and the reason it never fails is that a person is not continuous. People change. Records get copied and copied and copied. A clerk retires and the next clerk's hand is different and the second clerk has a worse memory than the first. Four years of a department is four different women with the same stamp."

She turned the cup a quarter turn on the table.

"And the machine cannot tell the difference between a person who changed and a record that changed, so it calls all of it a change, and after about nine years of that a person has been described by eleven hands and there is not one of them who could say whether the first of them and the last of them had ever met.

"And that is not a record of a life. It is a record of a building."

She picked the bag up off the floor and put it on the table and did not open it.

"Now. What we have done since the first of December, every bit of it, is the opposite of this, and every bit of it is clever, and every bit of it would fail an audit in about nine minutes.

"Not one of our names. Not on a plate, not in a file, not in a service, not on a return, not in a box. A seventh space that has never been used. Six hands where the instrument wants one. Four hands where the form wants one. You, four months, keeping yourself so exactly legible in one direction that you are illegible in the other.

"That is a defence against an exposure. It is a magnificent defence against an exposure and it is worth more than anything else anybody in this house has done, and against an audit it is not a defence at all. It is a confession, in a machine's language, that six people have changed four hundred times in four months."

"Sun signed a stub on the thirteenth."

"Sun Meiling signed a piece of paper on the thirteenth of this month and put her name and an hour on it, and she did it because a man on a step could not make anybody else."

Ines Duarte did not raise her voice, because she never did, and this was the loudest thing she had said in four months. "That is the first document this house has produced since the first of December with a continuous person in it, and she has not read the copybook to find out what it cost her. She knows exactly what it cost her."

"Six months," he said. "She started refusing in the autumn."

"Then she has been a different woman every month for six months and she has been the same woman since the thirteenth, and that is the whole of what a document about this address can take off a person. It does not take the house. It takes continuity."

He put his hand flat on the table, which was a thing he did when he had nothing to do with his hands.

"The form that came on the seventh," he said. "You read it."

"4-C." She said it the way she said a clause number. "Composition return, fourth series, issue C. 4-A and 4-B are the same form completed in advance at the premises by the person who answers it, which is a thing four people can do and one person has to do. The Office does not issue a second issue of a composition return to a household it can complete out of what it already holds, and it does not need to, because the exhibit roll of the twenty-ninth holds it.

"A second issue goes out for one of three reasons, and only one of them is arithmetic, and the arithmetic is the one you would expect.

"A clerk cannot reconcile four counts on a paper.

"Four hands answered your form over a weekend and every one of them gave a correct and complete answer to the first question, and not one of them left the box at the foot empty in the same way twice. So there are four counts of the same premises on one address, and the difference between them is not a matter of opinion, it is arithmetic.

"A clerk who cannot reconcile an arithmetic difference has one thing left to do, and the Office has a printed instrument for it."

"What."

"A shorter clock."

She looked at the stairs.

"I am not going to tell you what is on the second issue because I have not seen the second issue and I do not know. I am telling you what kind of a thing it will be, and I am telling you that it will be shorter, and I am telling you that it will arrive at these premises and not at a counter, and that is as far as I get without guessing."

"Ines."

"I have been in an audit once." She put both hands flat on the table. "It was eleven years ago and I was on the other side of it, and I read a file for four hours and answered three questions, and every one of my answers was correct.

"And the woman opposite me had not been in that room for nine of the years her file covered. She was seventy-one and she was a tram inspector, and she had signed a return in the winter of her first year, and there was a column in it.

"The column is what took nine years off her. Every clerk who touched it had a different hand, and every one of them had improved on the one before. At the end of it there was not one person in that file who could say whether the woman who wrote the word in the first year had written the word.

"She was asked three questions and she answered two of them correctly and she could not answer the third one, and I put that in my report in the flat register that is the only register there is, and she is alive, and she lives in Kestrel Hill, and she is a different woman because of eleven minutes of my handwriting."

The lamp buzzed.

"That is the rule and that is what it does, and I have told you the whole of what I know about it.

"I am going to stop talking about it now, and I am not going to tell you what I think is going to happen to me on the twenty-second, because I do not know, and because the moment somebody in this house gets a position on it I will spend the rest of my life being the woman who had one before the room did."

"Now the something," he said.

"Yes." She took her coat off and hung it on the back of a chair. "I have told you one fact in a corridor in a kitchen in a house in the Slough, and there are seven women in this building, and six of them do not know it.

"I am not going to be told not to tell them, because I would say no and it would be the third time I have said no in this city in writing and I have never once been wrong. I am going to tell you that I have done it anyway, and that I did it to you and not to the table, and that it is not a breach of anything I have ever signed, because I have never signed anything.

"And I am going to ask you for the thing I actually want, which is that you do not tell them."

He looked at her.

"Not for a fortnight," she said. "Not for a day. Not in a corridor, not in a bed, and not in about four words on a landing.

"I have watched six women in this house decide things in four months by the time one of them had said a thing out loud. I am not one of them. The only advantage I have ever had in this city is that I know what a document is going to cost before the room does, and I cannot have that if four people in this house get to a position on my review before I get to a room with four people in it."

"That is a fortnight of your life."

"That is seven days of yours." She picked up her bag. "I am going to be perfectly honest with you about the arithmetic, because you would do it anyway and I would rather do it with you.

"I have been the same woman all the way back for eleven years. Every refusal I have filed is in my own hand, and every one of them carries the same signature and a date and a reason, and that is not modesty and it is not a principle.

"It is a habit I built in my first year in that office, because I could see what an audit does. It has cost me one career and a great deal of memory, and it is the only reason there is not a file in this city with my name at the top of it.

"And I have built that on a foundation of nothing. Eleven years of being the same woman with no document anywhere that says so. There is no record of a single day of it. I am continuous and I am invisible, and those two things have been the same thing for as long as I have been able to do this job."

She got to the door of the room at the back and stopped.

"Sun Meiling has a document. That is all she has ever wanted and she got it on the thirteenth for ninety seconds on a doorstep and it is worth four months of everything I have done.

"You are going to find that insulting and you are not to, because it is not about worth. It is about which of us is standing on paper and which of us is standing on memory.

"Memory is a thing you lose an inch of every year, and I can tell you what I have left because I hold records in my head and every one of them takes a piece of me to keep. I have been paying that bill in that office ever since I started, and I am going to be asked on the twenty-second to pay it in an afternoon."

She went out.

He stood at the back of the kitchen for a while and then went and got the visitors' book off the shelf.

A hundred and eleven entries, going back four months, and every one of them in the same hand.

It was the only document in Ostara in which six women were demonstrably the same six women all the way back, and Marguerite Vale had kept it for four years without being asked and read it out loud once a week and attributed not one line of it.

He put it back on the shelf with both hands, carefully, the way you put down something that has just become a different thing.
