# A Legal Person

The Vestry clinic does not take appointments and does not have a waiting room, because a waiting room is a thing you sit in and a free clinic in the Slough is a thing you stand in until somebody can see you.

The dispensary hatch opens at eight. It had a queue along the wall by half past, and the queue was made up of people who had got up before it was light to be at the front of it, and he had walked past that queue a hundred times in a year and a half.

Tessa Okonkwo came round the end of the counter at about ten past nine with her sleeves rolled and a pen behind her ear and said, "You have been standing there since I opened the hatch."

"Since about eight."

"Then you have been standing there for two hours to ask me something you have decided to say quickly." She put her forearms on the counter. "Say it."

He said it.

He had thought about how to say it for two days, and every version of it had a preamble in it, and Sun Meiling had come down a stair two nights before and told him not to use one, and so he stood at a hatch in a queue of people who had got up in the dark and asked the best diagnostician in the Vestry whether she would put his name on a piece of paper.

She did not answer for about four seconds, which was longer than he expected and shorter than he had been bracing for.

"No," she said.

"That is faster than I thought."

"That is the fastest it can be, because you asked it straight and you are not going to get a better one." She reached past him and moved the queue along by about a foot, which is the whole of her authority over that room. "Say the next part."

"I want to know what a description is for."

"That is a different question and you brought it in the same bag, because you have four months of not asking me anything and you have made one visit do the work of a year." She looked at him properly for the first time. "Do you know what I am, in a file?"

"You wrote a word about me in your own hand."

"Yes. Mass." She let that sit for exactly as long as it deserved. "So you know the only document in Ostara in which you are a body rather than a man is a document I wrote to refuse to describe you.

"That document is in a Wing file, and it is about nine hundred words, and every one of them is wrong on purpose. A description that is wrong is a description somebody can check. So you know what I am, in a file, and you know it is a wall."

"And a clinical fact would not be a wall."

"A clinical fact about you would be the first true document about you with your name on the top of it, and there is a difference between a true document and a document that can be used."

She straightened up. "I can hold a body past the point it wants to stop. That is what I am for, and what I am for does not include the Bureau, and it has never once included the Bureau, and the price of this clinic being open is that it is unfunded. A clinic the Bureau funds is a clinic the Bureau owns, and that is not a thing anybody in this district has ever wanted."

She said all of it in about forty seconds and then went back to the hatch and called the next person, and a man with a child on his hip came up, and she was gone from the conversation entirely.

He stayed at the counter.

"You did not answer me," he said, to the back of her head.

"I answered you and then you interrupted a man with a child on his hip, which is the correct order of priorities, and you know that it is."

She came back about four minutes later with her sleeves still rolled and put both hands flat on the counter, which he had watched her do at a table in Ferrier Street and nowhere else in two years.

"Let me tell you what it is worth being described, because you have not worked it out, and you have been treating it as a mystery for four months when it is not one.

"A name is a thing you have to be right about. I have watched men bring a name to this hatch and be told it was four streets over, and go home. A description is a thing you have to be able to fit. A man who has read a description can walk to a building and put his hand on a door and be certain of himself.

"And you know which of those two the Office has, because the Office does not issue names. The Office issues findings."

She turned her head to the queue for a second, and then back.

"A member in this city is entitled to have his plate held rather than read, and to be asked before anything is taken off him, and to have a hearing moved up a week, and to hold an account of his own without another person's name on it. Every one of those is a permission.

"Every permission is bought at a counter with merit, and merit is grains, and grains are a weekly figure off a plate, and a plate carries a band, and a band comes out of an account, and an account in this city has a classification."

She stopped there and let it stand.

"Your account does not have one. It has a description in your own hand, in four places, saying that you cannot explain it. A counter in Ostara cannot sell a permission to a man whose account has no classification, because to sell him a permission the counter would have to say what he may do, and the only true answer to that is a blank, and a counter is not permitted to print a blank in a schedule."

"So band six buys me nothing."

"Your band six is on a piece of paper, and a piece of paper cannot be presented at a counter." She said it without putting any weight on it at all, which was how she said the things that mattered most. "You have a finding saying your account is what it is. You have no band that does a single thing for you.

"If a man came in here tomorrow with a paper about you, there is no version of your position on this earth in which you are asked first. Being asked first is a permission. You cannot buy one."

The queue went on shifting behind her. A woman with a burn on the inside of her wrist was saying something to somebody about a tram.

"Now the part that is actually for you," Tessa Okonkwo said, "and I am giving it to you because you earned it by not putting a preamble on it, which is the only thing anybody has ever earned anything from me with.

"A requisition of property does not need a person. It needs a place. Rank six may requisition property, and property in this city is not a house. It is anything a person is using — a table, a chair, a book, a stove, a door a person has had put on in the last ten years.

"The Office does not have to know a single name to do it. They write that the household of the member described in the finding of the twenty-ninth is the holder of the thing, and they carry it to Ferrier Street, and it is delivered, because the woman who comes down to the step is the woman the description is about."

"And then it is taken off the step."

"And then the next one comes. And after about four of them there is nothing left in a room that anybody owns, and a person in a city where the only record is a plate cannot prove she owns a chair."

"Whose," he said.

"That is the part I have thought about for longer than you have." She looked at the burn on the woman's wrist going past. "Because I can tell you which of them will go, and it is not the ones with the most rank.

"It is the ones with nothing in their name. A woman who is in a clinic file has a file. A woman who is in a service file has a service. A woman who is on a roll has a roll, and you cannot take a chair off a household that can produce the roll."

She picked up the cloth and wiped a counter where nothing had been spilled.

"So a household of six women who have declined to be written down, in a building above a chop-house, with a book that has six lines a day and no signature on any of them, is the only structure in this city that a paper cannot get into. I have thought about that and I have got no way to stand here and tell you it is good news."

He shifted his weight. The hatch had gone quiet for a moment.

"You have something in your hand," she said.

"Two ribs. On the left."

"Which two."

"Third and fourth."

Tessa Okonkwo did not ask him to take his shirt off, which he had been braced for, and did not ask him to sit down, which he had been braced for more.

"Then somebody has read a description of you that is better than mine," she said, "because I would have written the fourth and I would have asked about the third twice, and I have not examined you and I am not going to.

"When I do examine you, it will be in a room with a door that shuts and you will have asked me for it and not me. Until then I have two facts and they are yours and you can do what you like with them."

She put the cloth down and moved the queue along again.

"Now go and stand somewhere that is not in my queue. And Lin Hao."

He stopped.

"You are going to go and do a thing that is not this and that you have not told me about. Do not come back here and ask me for a card when you have done it.

"The day a person's name goes on a chart in this building is the day this building stops being a place where people come who are not on a plate, and I have spent two years being the only doctor in the Slough that the Correction Office cannot say it holds a record of. I am not losing that because you have found a use for it."
