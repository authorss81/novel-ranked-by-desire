# Sabine Rooke

He went in his coat at ten past one on the afternoon of the twenty-fourth and he was in the third-floor corridor of the Reading at four minutes past one, and forty feet from the door he had stood in front of for about a minute two days before, having read the plate and gone down the stair.

He had thought about it for two days. On the twenty-second he had come out of that building and gone to work. On the twenty-third Sun Meiling had come down sixty-one steps and said in a doorway that somebody in that house was going to go and read what was on the shelf, and that it was not going to be her.

The door was ajar with a bar across it, and through the gap he could see about forty feet of a room with the lights on and a bench at the end of it.

There were four shelves along the left-hand wall and they went to the height of a man, and the third one had a paper label on the end of it with about four words on it which he could read from the doorway and did not need to.

Late returns and period bundles.

"That is not open," said a woman from behind the bench.

He looked at her. She had a grey armband on and a bundle open in front of her and a flat file in her hand, and she was about twenty-five and had been doing it long enough that she had stopped being polite about it.

"It is not open to the staff," she said. "Are you staff."

"No."

"Then it is not open." She put the file down on the bundle. "You can go and ask at the counter on the ground floor and they will send you to whichever floor it is not on, and it will be this one, and then you will come up here and I will say the same thing."

"That is a strange way to spend both our afternoons."

"Yes," she said. "It is a strange way to spend both our afternoons and it is not the worst thing about it. If you are here about a late return, they are on the third shelf and they are in the files by street, A to Z, and the bundles go in behind you and not in front of you, and if you pull one out you put it back in the same order."

She went back to her bundle.

He stood in the doorway for about a second and a half.

Then he went in and went along the third shelf and put his hand in on the F, eleven files along, the way you go eleven drawers along in the F in the second-floor trays at the Fourth Branch, which is the identical machine and the identical order and which he had learned on the second of this month by watching a stranger's name become a written address in eight minutes and forty seconds.

Ferrier Street. Fourteen inches of the file. He went along it.

Eighty or ninety cards. A card is a folded sheet with a stub on the front and a number on the stub, and eighty of them were there in about forty seconds because the stub is what you read.

THE TWENTY-THIRD OF THIS MONTH. PREMISES ABOVE THE CHOP-HOUSE. RETURN. NO ANSWER.

THE SIXTH. SAME PREMISES. RETURN, FIRST ISSUE.

THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF LAST MONTH. NUMBER 6. WORKSHOP ACCOMMODATION. COMPLETE.

And then, about two thirds of the way along the fourteenth inches, a card with a different stub on it, the stub saying LATE, PERIOD CLOSED, and the date on the face of the sheet.

TWENTY-SECOND OF MARCH.

Composition return. One address, and under it six lines, and against every line a standing and a band.

The six bands were not the same and the six standings were not the same, and he read the first name and knew it, and the second, and the third, and by the fourth his hand had stopped moving along the file.

At the foot of the sheet, under the last line, the box.

PREMISES HELD UNDER THE STANDING OF.

Four inches wide, ruled, and empty.

Not crossed out. Not marked. Not held open pending a completion. Empty, in the way that a thing is empty when the person who wrote the rest of the sheet got to it and had nothing to put in it and went on to the line underneath.

The line underneath was the clerk's line. Form 4-A has one: the name of the person who completed it, and the date, and the hour.

S. ROOKE.

About half past four in the afternoon on the twenty-second of March.

He looked at it for about forty seconds and then went and did the arithmetic, because that is what he does, and because the arithmetic is the only thing in this city that has ever been on his side.

The twenty-second of March was seven days before the hall. It was ten days before the first of April, which is the day this began, which is the day a nameplate came off a rack and a bench cloth went over a machine and he walked into the Reading on a day the branch was shut and read a sheet on a rail.

So the return had been in the building for the whole of the twenty-ninth and the whole of the thirtieth and the whole of the first ten days of April. It had been in the building when six women were arguing at a kitchen table about whether the blank at the foot was a wall. It had been in the building when it was not a wall.

Every protection this household had believed in for four months had been a description of a piece of paper.

Six women at an address, on a sheet, in a filing system, with their standings and their bands printed against their names, completed and signed by a woman who had held a grey armband for eleven years and had never once entered anything in any register. The man the whole of it had been about was not on it. The space where he would have gone was four inches wide and had been empty since the day it was signed.

He put it back in the same order, at the same angle, with the stub facing out, which is the thing you can do and which he had learned in April.

Then he stood in a room on the third floor of a building he worked a mile and a half from and understood, in the way you understand a step that is not there, that he had been wrong about everything.

Not nearly wrong. Not wrong in a detail.

There was a piece of paper with the names of six women on it and it had been on the third floor of this building since the afternoon before a chair.

The chair was the reason. A chair that goes into a room on the morning of a hearing goes into the room on a sheet, and the sheet has a name on the clerk's line, and on the twenty-second of March a woman named her price before she asked for it. He had agreed to it in a corridor in about eleven seconds, because he had believed he was the only thing on the sheet.

He had believed that.

He had believed it and she had not corrected him, and she had been a clerk for eleven years, and there is a thing about that which he was not going to think about for at least another day.

And then he did the other arithmetic, which was worse, and which took him about four seconds.

He could have had it in ninety seconds.

Forty feet. He had read the plate on it on the twenty-second and had stood at it for a minute, in a corridor, on his way out of a building, after four hours of the most frightening ordinary experience of his life, and he had not gone in, and there had been a woman inside doing a period bundle who would have told him which shelf.

He had cleaned six floors of the Fourth Branch every working morning for four years and he had learned the exact shape of this machine twenty-two days ago by watching a clerk put a stranger's name into a written address in eight minutes and forty seconds, and the machine was thirty-five feet long and had four shelves and one of them was labelled.

He had spent four volumes being the least findable person in Ostara and he had confused it with being safe. Those are not the same thing and the difference between them is a shelf.

Nothing had been hidden, and that was the part that would not sit still on him in the room, the way a man cannot stop checking a pocket he has already emptied. It was not on a shelf because somebody had decided it should stay there. It was on a shelf because it had missed its period, and a filing system does not conceal late returns. It is the place late returns go.

The woman at the bench had not looked up again. He turned round and she was still in the bundle and she had a pencil behind her ear that she had not had when he came in.

"Was there something on that shelf," she said, to the bundle.

"Nothing I need."

"That is what the last four of you have said." She turned a page. "You are the second person this month who has come up that stair and gone along the third shelf and come out without telling me what for."

The strip of card on the front of her bundle was dated and timed, because bundles are timed, and the time on it was about twenty past one, and there was a space on the card under the hour where the count of people who had come in off the public stair and gone to the shelves would go at the end of the day.

He had been the cheapest thing in the building for ninety seconds and it had cost him a line on a card that a woman would fill in at about five o'clock.

That was the price. He had gone four years without a single person in Ostara being able to put a name to him, and the price of ninety seconds of standing in a room was one number on a card about a bundle, and the number said that a man had come off the public stair, and not what he came for, and not who he was.

He could do it once.

He went down the outside stair and out into the square and stood under the tiles where four hundred people were walking, and there were ninety thousand people in this city who could be reached by a name and one who could not, and he had known that about himself for four years and had treated it as a position.

It is not a position. It is a shelf.

Everything that has ever been found out about a person in this city has been found out on paper, and the paper has an address on it, and there is a floor, and on the floor there is a woman with a bundle and a pencil behind her ear.

The answer to the only question he had come in with was lying on the third shelf with six names on it and his name missing from it.

He had it. That was the whole of what he had, and he said it to himself in the first person standing in the square, because he was the only person he had said it to and it was going to have to be carried by one person and it was not going to be any better in company.

He had found out that the six women he had spent four months protecting had been written down before he understood that there was anything to protect. He had found out that the room had a shelf in it and that the shelf was on the same floor as the room where he had sat for four hours on the twenty-second, forty feet away, with a plate on the door that he had read.

And he had found out that the space at the top of it had been empty since the afternoon of the twenty-second of March, and that it was empty because he had not been on the sheet, and that a return in this city does not exist as a thing that can be refused. It exists as a thing that can be *completed*.

There is no use I can put to that yet, he thought. There is going to be one, because there are six names on it and a box with a rule round it and the whole of this month is going to end on the twenty-seventh at a counter, and one of those two things is a wall and the other one is not.

He had not taken it out of the file.

That was the choice he made in the room and it was the only one available and he made it in about a second and it took him the rest of the day to work out what it had cost, which was that he now knew something that he could only ever have learned by standing in a room and being a line on a card, and there is no second time for that.

He went and did the sixth floor and the second and he got the tram.
