# The Household Roll

The men came up to the Reading wall at nine on the tenth with a ladder and a crate and four buckets, and the white-bearded man handed him a bucket without being asked, and the pale rectangle was still bare.

Fourteen days.

"Fourteen days," the man said. "It is a fortnight and that is all I have got for it, and I have stopped saying anything about it, and I would like you to stop asking me."

"There is a slot on a public wall with nothing in it for a fortnight because an office has not decided whether a man left a building," he said, "and I do not think that is in a document anywhere."

"It is ceramic," said the white-bearded man. "That is the entire answer and it has been the entire answer since the twenty-sixth of April, and you read it off the wall every Thursday and you could read it off in the dark."

The sheet came down off the rail at a quarter to eleven with the week's figures on it and went up on the wire in the usual way, and he did not read it, because he had not read one since the third of April and there was not one thing in the sheet about him.

At ten past eleven a woman came up the sixty-one steps past the chop-house and knocked on the door of the ground-floor flat, which is the door the rent comes to, and she said she was from the district and she had a card, and she asked for the roll.

Sun Meiling came down four stairs before anybody had said anything, which is where her hand goes and which is what she does.

"It is on the wall," she said. "Top of the landing. I will come up and get it."

"You do not have to come up," said the woman with the card. "I will come up. It is produced to me and it is produced where it is kept."

They went up together. Ines Duarte was at the table and got up and did not say anything, and the six of them were in the room within about a minute, because a person asking for a roll at that address is not a thing that happens in a kitchen with the door shut.

The woman was about thirty-five and her name was Nunn and she had a card with a number on it and she did not sit down.

She stood in front of the board on the landing for about four minutes and did not touch it and did not ask anybody to explain it.

Above the seven rows, in a heavier line: THE HEAD OF THE HOUSEHOLD. Under that, in a man's own hand, a name.

Five rows with a name and a standing and a band against each of them, and a sixth with the name still on it and a rule struck from the left margin to the right edge of the line, and a date, and an initial, and four words at the end of it in a hand that was not in any of the others.

And at the bottom, a seventh row with a number in the left-hand rule and nothing at all in the other three columns.

Nunn read it from the top down and then read the bottom two rows again.

"Seven rows," she said. "Five names. Two spaces."

"Five," said Sun Meiling.

"There are six members on the certification schedule of the fourth of this month." She did not say it as an accusation and she said it in exactly the tone of a woman reading out two numbers. "I have it here. Six, with a standing and a band against each. And there is a line through the sixth row on this board with the second of this month on it, in a hand that is not the hand of anybody who signed the schedule."

"That is my hand," said Ines Duarte.

"Yes." Nunn looked at the line for about two seconds. "You initialled it. WITHDRAWN BY THE MEMBER."

"That is the whole of it."

"That is the whole of it and I am obliged to enter it." The woman took a card out of a satchel and wrote on it, standing up, on the landing, in about forty seconds.  "I am entered to ask for a roll kept at premises and to enter the hour, the person I asked, the number of names upon it and the number of spaces.

It is the tenth of this month at about ten past eleven, and there are five names and two spaces, and one of the spaces has a rule through it and a date and an initial on it."

She wrote all of that down in the ordinary way and did not ask a single question about it, and Sun Meiling watched her do it from the fourth stair with her hand on the rail.

"Now I am going to tell you the part that is not mine," said Nunn, "and I am going to tell it to all of you at once because all of you are standing on this landing and it is better that it is said in a room than found out in a fortnight.

"The roll on this wall is not the instrument. I did not come for this. This is a thing that is kept at the premises and produced to a person who calls, and I have entered it and it goes into a district sheet by the end of the week, and it has no force at all.

"The instrument is the schedule of the fourth, which four pages of which are in a tray on the second floor of the Reading with your man's name at the top of page one and six members on page two, and it is the schedule the certificate issues on."

She put the card away.

"And there is no instrument in this city by which a member can come off a certified schedule between the filing of it and the issue of the certificate. I have looked for one this morning, because it is my job to look for one, and there is one way and it is not available to you."

"Name it," said Ines Duarte.

"A fresh return. A new composition return, completed, with the corrected figure in it." Nunn said it without any weight at all, because she was reading it.  "A fresh return opens a fresh sixty days from the date of its filing. It does not amend the one you have.

It stands beside it, and the certificate issues on the earliest complete one, which is the one you have already filed, and the second one is a second filing on the address, which is the thing that put the re-issue through your door on the first of this month in the first place."

The landing was very quiet.

"So she cannot come off," said Beatrix Halm, from the fourth stair.

"I did not say she cannot. I said she cannot come off the schedule, and that the schedule is what the certificate issues on, and that the certificate is the only thing in this city that makes a household a household rather than a description of one." Nunn put her satchel over her shoulder.  "The rule at the bottom of your board is correct and it is dated and it is initialled, and it is a true statement about a board on a wall.

It is not a statement about the household. Those are two different things and you have had five days of believing they were the same one."

She went down sixty-one steps.

They stood on the landing for about a minute and a half.

Then Ines Duarte said, "Well."

"Do not," said Beatrix Halm.

"I was going to say the sentence and you are not going to have it and I am going to say it anyway." Ines Duarte had her hand flat on the wall under the board. "She asked on the second of this month for the one thing this city has that would let her be unfindable, and I did not want to do it, and I did it because she asked and because I could not give her a reason.

"And it has cost her the whole of the only protection she had, and it has bought her a line in ink with a date on it, and on the morning of the tenth she is still standing on a landing in front of eight people who watched a clerk from a district read that line and write down the number of names and spaces on that board."

"Both of those are true," said Nadia Ferrous. "That is the thing I keep getting stuck on. They are both true and they are both about her and she did both of them."

"They are both true and neither of them is in the certificate," said Ines Duarte.

Sun Meiling came down four stairs and stood in front of the board.

"The seventh row," she said.

The room waited.

"It is numbered seven and it is empty and the light stops about two inches above it, and I have looked at it every evening for ten days, and it is still empty, and it is going to be on that wall for another forty-seven days,

"and I want every one of you to understand that I did that on the sixth of this month in a queue in the Reading, in my own hand, four hundred words, and the answer I gave was that I am the person at whom papers are served and not one of the people they are served on, and it was accepted, and it is in the file,

"and there is not one column in this city for me and I have finally got a piece of paper that says so in the language an office uses."

She put her hand flat on the seventh row without touching it.

"That is my row and it is empty and it is mine, and there is a second piece of paper about this address in a district file that has my name at the top of it, and both of those are true at the same time, and I have made sure of it, and I would like it noticed that it was done in a clerk's queue by a woman of my own hand and not by anybody else in this house."

He did the arithmetic out loud on the landing, because he had promised himself on the first of this month that he would do every figure out loud in this house from now on.

"Sixty days from the twenty-seventh of April. Three days to the end of April. This month has thirty days, because the card by the time clock at the ground floor of the Fourth Branch says the men are coming on the third, the tenth, the seventeenth and the twenty-fourth and then on the seventh of next month, and there is no thirty-first in it.

"Three, and thirty, and twenty-seven. The certificate is dated the twenty-seventh of June."

"That is forty-seven days," said Runa Sato. "It is forty-seven days off and I am on it until the twenty-seventh of June whatever it says and I would like it printed that the three months run from the signature and not from the certificate."

"They do," said Ines Duarte.

He stood on the landing at about eleven in the morning with the stair window light across the board and five names on it and two empty rows,

and a sentence in pencil four feet below it from the tenth of April that was going to outlast him, and a piece of paper in a district file with four pages and a head's name at the top of it and six members on the second, one of whom had ruled herself out of the only copy of the household that existed in the world.

Forty-seven days. And the certificate would be dated the twenty-seventh of June and it would go into a post by somebody who had never met one of them, and his name would be at the top of page one of it, and not one person in that house would have to do a single thing for that to happen.

And the only instrument in this city that could have changed a figure on that schedule before the twenty-seventh of June was a fresh return, which opens sixty days from the day it is filed and does not amend the one they have,

and a fresh return filed on the tenth would be dated the tenth and would run to the tenth of July, and the certificate would issue on the twenty-seventh of June anyway because that is the earliest complete one, and a second filing on that address is the thing that put a re-issue through that door on the first of this month in the first place,

and not one person in that house had asked for it, and a woman had told the rest of them at a kitchen table three days ago that there was something she could do that not one of them had asked her to do.
