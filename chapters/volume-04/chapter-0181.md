# The Re-Issued Form

The card by the time clock on the ground floor of the Fourth Branch had a different month on it.

He read it standing up with the bucket in his hand and the stair going up behind him, the way he read it every month, because the men who paint the wall have to know which days they are coming and that card is the only thing in the building which says so.

MAY. THIRD. TENTH. SEVENTEENTH. TWENTY-FOURTH. AND THE SEVENTH OF NEXT MONTH.

He read the last line twice.

The fourth Thursday on that card is the twenty-fourth, and seven days from the twenty-fourth is the thirty-first, and there is no thirty-first in this month, because the last entry is not the thirty-first and the card is written by four men who come here and paint a wall every Thursday and would not print a day that does not exist.

This month has thirty days.

He had been waiting since the twenty-seventh of April to be able to say that, and it took a card and about nine seconds, and it is the kind of thing this city runs on.

He did not have to take anybody's word for it. Last month's card ended in a day of this one — the third of next month — and the men had carried a tile down off the Reading wall in a crate on the twenty-sixth of April, and seven days from the twenty-sixth is the third.

So the third is a Thursday. The first of this month is two days before a Thursday, and from here to the tenth every date can be had by counting from the third.

He had wanted the card to say something else for about four seconds, and he was honest with himself about the four seconds. He would have liked a thirty-first. A thirty-first means a month with a spare day in it, and a spare day is a day on which nothing is required of anybody.

Then he went up at seven and did the second floor, and the third, and the fourth, and the fifth, and the sixth, and washed his hands at a quarter past eleven.

The post had come up sixty-one steps over the chop-house before he got there, and Sun Meiling had a sheet on the kitchen table with her hand flat on it.

"They have used the same paper," she said.

He put his bag down.

"They have used the same paper as the two in April," she said, "and it is not one of those two, and it is not a second issue, and it has been in this house since about ten and I have read it and I am not going to say one word about it until Ines gets here, because I did not get it from a counter and I am not the woman in this house who reads forms."

She took her hand off it.

"That is not modesty. That is the instruction on the doorframe. It says a paper comes to me, and I answer it, and I do not ask anybody else in this house what the answer is. I have answered it by keeping my hands off it, which is the most I can do, and it went to eleven and I have been standing here since."

Ines Duarte came at ten past eleven with her coat on, which she did twice a week, and she had it in her hand before she had her bag down.

"Sit down," she said. "Both of you are going to want to stand and I am going to read this out twice, and the second time is the one that matters."

She read it once.

It was Form 4-C, composition return, fourth series, issue A. The first issue, the one that went out on the sixth of April and had twenty-one days on it and was due on the twenty-seventh. The same four questions in the same order, the same box four inches wide at the foot with its heading over it, and it was not a second copy of a form that had been answered.

It was a form that had been answered, and was being asked again.

The letter said four things.

One. A return was received at that district on the twenty-seventh of this month past, complete, and was entered.

Two. A return for the same premises, earlier, marked LATE and PERIOD CLOSED, is held, and cannot be acted on, and cannot be withdrawn by the person who filed it.

Three. Where two returns stand against one address, the earlier is the return, and the district requires it in a form it can act on.

Four. The answer given at item three on the later return is not borne out by the office named in it, and the district requires a corrected answer, in one hand, with the name of the person giving it at the foot, and it requires it on or before the seventh of this month.

"Six days," said Sun Meiling.

"Six days," said Ines Duarte, "from the first, which is today, and I am not going to let anybody in this room do that subtraction twice."

She read it out a second time, and on the second reading she stopped at item three and made everybody listen to the shape of it.

"It is not a new question," she said. "It is the same question. It is a return. Six names, a standing, a band, a head. Every word of it is in the paper that went down on the twenty-seventh, and the paper that went down on the twenty-seventh went down with everything in it true except one line.

"And what the district is saying is: one of you told us something on the twenty-seventh that we have since found out to be untrue, and we do not know which one of you it was, and we are not going to tell you, because if we told you, one of six people would go into that kitchen and fix the other five, and we would have a return that is corrected and a household that has stopped telling the truth to itself."

She put the sheet down on the table.

"And in exchange for that we have printed the only form in this city that requires a household to produce one of its own members. It does not say which member. It says a corrected answer, in one hand, with a name at the foot of it. Somebody in this building has to put their hand to that, and the Office does not have to do anything at all to find out who."

The range let go of its heat and came back about four minutes later, which is a thing the kitchen does and has done since the first of December.

Beatrix Halm had come down at about half past eleven and had been standing at the back of the room since.

"Item three," she said. "Who held a standing that is not borne out."

"Read it again."

"Read it again." Beatrix came forward. "The answer given at item three is not borne out by the office named in it."

"That is what it says."

"It does not say the answer is wrong. It says the office does not bear it out." Beatrix Halm put one finger on the table about a foot from the paper and did not touch it. "And there is one name in this house whose standing on the sixteenth was an office at Cordwain, and that office has not borne it out, and everybody at this table worked that out inside four seconds, and the paper does not have to say a word more than it says."

Runa Sato came down the stairs with her kit over her shoulder and stopped at the bottom of the fourth.

"Give it here," she said.

Sun Meiling gave it to her.

Runa Sato read the whole of it standing up, once, and then she put it face down on the table with her hand on the back of it, and she did not sit down and she did not take her hand off.

"I entered premises held by the Field division at Cordwain on the eighth of this month past," she said. "It was true on the eighth. I wrote it because it was true and because a woman in this house told me to write what was true and not to make it tidy, and I would write it again tomorrow."

She said the rest of it with her hand still on the paper.

"And I was entered as refusing at ten past two in the afternoon on the twelfth, and a duty clerk I have never spoken to ruled my line off the strength wall at about four the afternoon before that, over a count that office had owed since the middle of February. Two pieces of paper on me and one of them was mine.

"So there is a corrected answer and there is a signature at the foot of it, and there is exactly one person in this house who can put her name there truthfully, and it is me, and the Office has worked that out from a form and a wall without sending anybody to this street."

She lifted her hand off the paper.

"And I am going to write it, and I am going to write it in my own hand at a counter in my own clothes, and I am not going to tell any of you what it says before I do, and that is not defiance. That is the standing instruction on the doorframe, which is my sentence and which I wrote, and it says that a paper comes to me and I answer it and I do not ask anybody else in this house what the answer is."

Ines Duarte said, "It also says, in the version a man asked a woman to copy six times in six hands on the tenth of April, that if any one of you cannot answer a question on it you write the word no and your name and post that as well."

"That sentence was written about him," said Sun Meiling, "and it was written on the tenth of April, and it has been on that wall for three weeks and it is the only sentence on it, and you are both of you going to leave it where it is."

She said it from the fourth stair and she did not come down.

Runa Sato picked the form up.

"If I write that standing correctly," she said, "then the Office has the answer and it has my name under it, and my name goes into a district file in the same week as everything else about this address.

In about six days' time there will be a line in a district file that says a person at these premises gave an answer that was true on the eighth of this month past and is not true now, and it will be in my hand, and it will have a time on it."

"Yes," said Ines Duarte.

"And there is already a line in a district file about this address that says a member of this household gave the Office an answer that was not borne out, and it does not have my name on it, and the only reason it does not have my name on it is that the Office cannot make me admit to it in a room without a form in front of me.

"So when I sign that thing it does not create the line. It signs it. And the line has been in that file since the fourth of this month and I have known about it since the twenty-seventh, and I would like it noted that I have been waiting for it for four days and that I am not going to be thanked for waiting."

She put the form inside her tunic and went up.

He went down at about twelve and did the washrooms and came back up at about five.

Two days to the third, which was the first Thursday on the card by the time clock and the day the Board came down, and it had a bare rectangle on it four inches by two that had been bare for five days, and the men who had carried Ivo Sarn's tile off in a crate had left it bare on purpose, and there was nothing on this week's list for that slot either.
