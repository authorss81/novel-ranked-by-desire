# On His Own Account

The second sheet came up the sixty-one steps in the post on the twelfth and it was not addressed to a member and it was not served on a member, and it was the first paper that had come to that address since the fourth of this month with his name at the top of it and no woman's underneath.

It wanted a table and two chairs at the premises.

That is all it wanted, and it wanted them on his own account, and it was two lines long with a form number and a date on it and a box in the corner for the hour a person might be asked before they were touched.

He read it at the table in the kitchen with the other four around it and then he read out the only sentence in it that was not ordinary.

"In the case of a standing household the requisition is served on the head of the household first, and not on any member, and the head may be required to produce the property within nine days or the property may be taken from the premises without further notice."

"Nine days," said Runa Sato.

"Nine days from the twelfth," said Ines Duarte, "which is the twenty-first of this month, and I will tell you now that the twenty-first is not a day we are going to survive, because you have not signed anything and until you do, there is no standing household, and there is no head for it to be served on, and so it falls by its own words straight back onto six women."

They looked at the box in the corner.

"He is the head," said Nadia Ferrous. "On the schedule of the fourth. Six members and his name at the top of page one. The district read the board and the instrument and they told us the roll was not the instrument, and the instrument has his name on it, and this sheet has been served on him and not on us, and that is the whole of what nine seconds at a counter on the second floor was for."

The kitchen did not answer that.

"It is also the whole of what we have been arguing about for ten days," said Ines Duarte. "Because a standing household is not standing yet. It is a schedule and a certificate dated the twenty-seventh of June and an instrument neither of us has seen. This sheet has come to the wrong man, or the right man, and I want to know which before anybody agrees to anything."

"They are the same man," said Sun Meiling, from the fourth stair.

"I want to say it once. He has got one number and it is on two pieces of paper and neither of them is a certificate, and until one of them is a certificate he is a man who cleans washrooms, and this sheet has been served on six women through his name because his name is the only one it could find. It was never served on him. It was served on us and it went through him to reach us."

Marguerite Vale put her cup down.

"Then let me price the two possibilities," she said, "because you are all going to do it tonight and it will be done badly.

"If he signs the assumption this month, that sheet is served on a head, and a head may be required to produce, and that is a door between six women and a cart. If he does not sign it, the sheet falls onto the members, and there are five of them on the roll with a name and a band against each, and the office takes a table and two chairs off them and, if it wants to go further, off the woman who cannot prove she owns them.

"So the nine days are not really nine days. They are a count of how long it takes him to sit at a counter and sign one side of a sheet, and every day he does not do it is a day nearer a cart at that door."

The room sat with it.

"I will get it done," he said.

"You will not get it done this morning," said Ines Duarte. "Because there is a thing at the bottom of it and you have not read the bottom of it yet, and neither have they."

She turned the sheet round and put her finger on the second line.

Read on his own account. Not on the account of the household. Not on the account of any member.

"That is not a formality," she said. "That is the whole of the trap and it has been sitting on that sentence since the day it was printed.

"If the paper is served on him on his own account, then it is not served on the household at all. It is served on him, personally, as a man with a plate, and it asks a man with a plate to produce a table and two chairs at an address he does not live at.

"And a man with a plate cannot be served at premises, because a man with a plate is found wherever he is. It will follow him to the Fourth Branch. It will be on a counter in a building he works in every day, and it will stand there until somebody reads it, and he will be the man who reads it, because that is his whole job on the ground floor of that building."

Beatrix Halm said, from the stairs, "And when it follows him to the Fourth Branch, the woman at that counter looks at a name and does not ask a man who cleans the washrooms why a requisition has his name on it. She asks the plate."

The kitchen was very quiet.

"That is what on his own account means," said Beatrix Halm.

"It means the paper is not about the house at all. It is about him. And you can serve a house at premises in nine days and take a table. You cannot take a plate off a band five in nine days, so they will not try to take the plate.

"They will serve it on the man, at his work, in front of the other cleaners, and watch what he does in front of them, because a man who folds a paper small and puts it in his coat before eight in the morning is a man with something on a paper."

"He will not hide it," said Runa Sato.

"He will stand in the entrance hall of the Bureau of Merit and read it out loud to a clerk and four cleaners, and it will be in a minute, and it will be correct, and the address will be read out in a building where forty people hear it every day."

"He is not going to do that," said Tessa Okonkwo.

They all looked at her.

"I did not say it was wrong," she said. "I said he is not going to do it, and I am going to say why, and then I am going to go and open a hatch at eight tomorrow because I have said my piece.

"The man who wrote that sentence did not write it to trap him. He wrote it because it is the form the Office uses when it wants to serve a head personally, and he has never once had a head who cleaned washrooms and he does not know what he is doing to a man who has to stand at a counter in his own coat in front of the people he works beside.

"And there is not one instrument in this city that will take that word on his own account off the sheet and leave the rest of it standing. So the choice is his, and it is not the choice we thought it was an hour ago."

The room sat with that for about half a minute, and the range let go of its heat, which it does twice a day whether or not anybody is in it.

"Then we will leave it," said Ines Duarte.

"Leave it where."

"On the table, with the box, until tomorrow, and in the morning he goes in at seven and does his floors and reads it at the counter in the entrance hall in front of whoever is standing there, and he says the address out loud, because a man who says it out loud is a man who has nothing to hide, and that is not nothing. It is not protection. It is the only shape the truth has in a building where forty people work."

"And that makes him findable."

"That made him findable on the fourth of May," said Ines Duarte, "when he wrote eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight into a document in a room with Doyle and signed a page that carries a standing he knows to be false. He has been the most findable man in this city since the fourth of May and he has been it for a week, and none of this morning is new."

She stood up.

"I am going to say the last of it and I want it said before I go and read my own post, because it is the only thing in this room I am certain of.

"The household learns what it has signed on the nineteenth of this month, or it does not learn it at all. That is eight days from the twentieth and it is the twentieth because the Office burns an unused pad at the close of a month, and Doyle wrote that on the wall of a room yesterday and it is the truest thing anyone has said to us in a month.

"Nine days is this sheet's own figure and it is not Doyle's, and both of them are running. And on this sheet there is a box for the hour a person might be asked before they are touched, and that box is the same width as the box in the reading, and the man who printed both of them does not know that they are the same size, and I do."

She went out, and they heard the stairs, and then the landing door, and then nothing.

At about twenty past seven that evening he was in the washroom on the fourth floor of the Fourth Branch, which he had done, and in the entrance hall of the ground floor, which is a room forty feet long with a counter at one end and a mail rack behind it and a floor that gets wet every morning from about forty sets of feet.

There were four cleaners in it and a clerk and the clerk was reading the day's sheet.

He read the requisition out loud.

He said his name, and he said the form number, and he said the date, and he said the nine days and the twenty-first, and he said the premises in Ferrier Street above a chop-house with the words standing household and the head's name at the top of page one, and he said the words on his own account twice.

He had practised them at a sink on the fourth floor that afternoon and he was not going to get them wrong in front of forty people who had heard him say them a hundred times about a wet floor.

He did not say his address and he did not have to, because the paper had it, and a man reading a document in a hall is a man reciting it.

The clerk looked up. One of the cleaners said, "That's Ferrier," and another said, "That's the Slough."

"That is the Slough," he said. "And it has my name on it and not theirs, and I am going to do my floors now, and if anybody wants to ask me about it they can ask me at the counter at the end of the day and I will answer anything they put to me."

He took his bucket and went up.

At the end of the day he answered four questions at the counter and two of them were from the cleaners and two of them were from the clerk, and the clerk's second one was the good one.

"You are going to sign it," the clerk said. "The assumption. Doyle has the pad."

"How do you know about Doyle."

"Because Doyle's pad is the only pad in this building that has had a name on the stub and not been entered," the clerk said, "and he tells me, because he is a man who has worked behind that counter for thirty years and there is not one week in that time that Doyle has not told the mail clerk something that had no business being said out loud in this hall.

"So you are going to sign it, and I want to say to you, as the clerk who reads this rack, that in four years I have not seen a requisition with a head of household served personally on a band five, and I have seen about four hundred with a member served on nothing, and I know what happens to those and I know what happens to these, and the difference is you.

"The ones with a member on them get taken quietly, and there is no minute of it. The ones with a head on them get a room and a question and a piece of paper with a name at the top of it, and everybody in that room has to write down what they think, and the cleaners hear about it in the hall for nine days."

"Then it is the better of the two," he said.

"It is the more expensive of the two," said the clerk. "I did not say it was the better. Go home."

He walked the last eleven minutes past the rail at the junction, and the bench sheet was still on it from the third, and he did not stop.

At the top of the sixty-one steps, at about ten, Sun Meiling had the lamp off on the landing and the board was bare and the seventh row was numbered and empty and the light stopped about two inches above it, and Ines Duarte's pencil sentence was four feet below it from the tenth of April.

He stood at the eighth step and did the arithmetic out loud, because he had promised himself on the first of this month that he would do every figure out loud in that house.

"Fifteen of the sixty are gone," he said. "Forty-five are left. Eight days to the twentieth, and nine days from the twelfth to the twenty-first, and the twenty-first is a cart at that door, and the twentieth is a sheet in Doyle's pad, and they are not the same date and the two women in that room this morning are the only people who know they are different."

The landing did not say anything.

He went down and did his floors and got the half-hourly and slept about four hours.

Nine days.
