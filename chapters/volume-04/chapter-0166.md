# Form 4-C Returned

It came in the post at about twenty past nine in the morning and Ola Bright brought it up without touching the outside of it, which she had learned on the seventh of this month, and stood on the third step and said so before she said anything else.

"It is the second one. It is the same window and the same window has got a crease in it down the left where the first one was folded, so it is the same form and it has been in the same hand twice and I have not opened either of them."

Ines Duarte took it off her at the door and read it standing in the kitchen with her coat still on.

"4-C," she said. "Second issue. Eleven days." She turned it round and put it on the table. "The sixteenth plus eleven is the twenty-seventh. It is due on the same day the first one is due."

"That is not an accident," said Marguerite Vale.

"That is not an accident, and I want everybody at this table to understand that it is not an accident, because the office that posts it has put a shorter clock on it and left the date alone, which means it is not thinking about the date. It is thinking about the time it has to spend on us."

Sun Meiling had come down four steps. She stood at the table and looked at the sheet for about a minute without touching it.

"It has a fifth question," she said.

That stopped the room, because Ines Duarte had not mentioned it and had been standing there holding the sheet.

"It has four questions and it has a box, and then at the bottom, under the box, in smaller type than the box, it has a fifth question, and the fifth question is not in a box, it is a line with a blank under it like a signature line."

"Say the question," said Ines Duarte.

"SINCE THE FIRST ISSUE OF THIS FORM WAS POSTED, HAS ANY PERSON OF READING AGE KEEPING AN ADDRESS AT THESE PREMISES BEEN ENTERED AS REFUSING AN ANSWER TO A COUNT."

Not one person in that room said anything for about four seconds. The oven went off behind them and did not come on again.

"Read the note under it," said Sun Meiling.

"There is no note."

"There is a note. It is in the same small type as the question and it is half the width of the sheet and you read the question and stopped." She put one finger, not touching the paper, an inch above the line. "WHERE THE ANSWER IS NOT GIVEN IT IS TAKEN FROM WHAT THE OFFICE HOLDS."

"They already know the answer," said Nadia Ferrous.

"They have known the answer since about ten past two on the twelfth, which is the exact moment that word went into a column on a wall in Cordwain." Sun Meiling did not take her finger off the air above the paper. "So this is not a question. A question is something you do not know. This is a line they have drawn on a form so that there is a place for the answer we all already know is coming."

"Then write no," said Beatrix Halm, from the doorway. "That is what the instruction says. If you cannot answer it, write no and your name."

"You cannot write no," said Ines Duarte. "Not to that one. It is not a matter of opinion and it is not a matter of nerve.

"A woman in this room was entered as refusing in a building in Cordwain four days ago. There is a paint mark on a frame in a muster room and a strip of card taped to the rail beneath it with four words on it.

"If this form is answered no, then this form is a document in which six women have stated that a thing did not happen. One of them knows it happened. And the person who would check was in that room."

She turned the sheet over and put a hand flat on it.

"There is an ordinary word for that and I would rather everybody in the room heard it from me than worked it out on their own."

Marguerite Vale said, "There is. And I am not going to say it either, because Ines is right and I have spent four months watching everybody in this house find out what happens when a woman who files nothing says something in writing."

"What do we do," said Runa Sato.

"That is the first question anybody at that table has asked in four months that I have been glad to hear," said Ines Duarte.

She straightened up and picked the sheet up and turned it to face herself.

"There is also something else on it, and I did not mention it because I wanted the room to have the fifth question first, and because the other thing is worse and slower and it has been there for twenty years and it will still be there in a fortnight.

"Down the right-hand margin, in a ruled space, with a heading over it. ACCOUNT — CLASSIFICATION ENTERED. And the Exchange's standard line against anything that does not carry one."

"No entitlement established," said Nadia Ferrous.

"No entitlement established. That is the fifth answer available to the fourth question and it is the one that was there on the seventh." Ines Duarte read down the margin and then stopped, and then went on reading, in a voice that had changed. "It is not the last one on the margin."

"There are four permitted answers printed in that space," she said, "and the fifth one is the one I do not know what to do with."

She read it out.

PREMISES HELD UNDER NO STANDING ENTERED. SEE THE SCHEDULE OF UNMEASURED ASSOCIATIONS, ITEM ELEVEN.

And then, printed small at the foot of the sheet, because forms in this city print everything they are going to rely on and nothing else, the schedule itself, in three lines.

ITEM ELEVEN. ASSOCIATIONS HOLDING NO PLATE AND NO AUDIT. PERSONS ENTERED IN THIS QUARTER: ELEVEN THOUSAND AND SOME HUNDRED. MEMBERSHIP ATTESTED. ATTESTATION NOT AUDITED. NO COMPOSITION RETURN HAS BEEN FILED BY ANY OF THEM AT ANY TIME.

The room was quiet in the way that a room is quiet when four people have all understood a sentence at slightly different speeds.

"They printed the number," said Sun Meiling.

"They printed the number," said Ines Duarte. "On a form about this address, in a margin, as the fifth thing an answer may be."

"Eleven thousand." Sun Meiling had gone back up two steps and was sitting down on them, which she did when a shape arrived. "Eleven thousand and some hundred, in one quarter, holding no plate and no audit, with membership attested and attestation not audited, and not one composition return in the history of this city.

"That is not a group of people. I want everybody in this room to hear me say that and hear it properly. A group of eleven thousand people would have returns. A group of eleven thousand people would have a hall, a membership list, a dispute about the minutes and a woman who objects to the minutes.

"That is a shape. It is the largest shape in this quarter that has never once been counted by anything, and it has been sitting here the whole time with the only claim in Ostara that not one office has ever tried to put a number on, which is that what you do when there is no witness to it is the only thing that counts."

"Then why is it on our form," said Beatrix Halm.

"Because four women answered the fourth question four different ways," said Ines Duarte. "None of them put a name in the box. A clerk with four unnameable standings for one address has nothing to write in the box, and a clerk with nothing to write in the box has to complete the return from the exhibit roll, and item four on that roll is a description of six lines a day in six hands.

"So an office looked down its own schedule and found a standing that is not a person and is not a company and does not have to be there at all, and it has printed it into the margin of a form as a thing these premises may be held under.

"And if this return goes in with the box empty, the Office does not have to leave it empty. That is the entire point of that line. For four months the blank at the foot of that box was the only thing in this city that was holding, and it was holding because not one clerk in Ostara could fill it in."

"How long has that line been in the schedule," said Runa Sato.

"Ines Duarte did not answer.

"Because I do not know," she said. "Because it is printed in three lines at the foot of a form about a house in Ferrier Street and it is the first time in my life I have seen it, and I have been reading this city's forms for eleven years."

She put the sheet down flat on the table.

"So here is where we are, and I am going to say it once.

"The first form asked four questions and had one box, and four women answered it over a weekend in four hands, and every one of them left the box empty, and the empty box was supposed to be the wall.

"This form asks five questions and has one box and prints the number eleven thousand in the margin, and the box is still empty, and there is now a printed standing in this city that a clerk may put in it without lying to anybody, and the standing is not a person and not a company and is made of eleven thousand people who have never been counted.

"If this goes back empty again, we are not protected. We are described. We are described as premises held under something we have never belonged to and cannot leave, by an office that does not need our agreement and does not need a name and does not need to send anybody to the door."

The oven had gone off and not one of them had noticed.

"The answer to the fifth question is yes," said Ines Duarte. "It went into a column in a room in Cordwain at ten past two on the twelfth. And the instruction on the wall of that landing says that if any one of us cannot answer a question on a paper, we write the word no and our name and post that as well.

"That rule was written by a woman standing on a stair on the tenth of this month and it is the best rule anybody in this house has ever produced, and it is going to fail on its first use, and I want it written down in the room that I said so before the six of you started."

Sun Meiling came down the two steps and took the sheet off the table and held it by the corner.

"It comes to me," she said. "It came to me on the thirteenth at half past ten and I wrote it on a doorframe and it has not changed since. I answer it, and I do not ask anybody else in this house what the answer is."

She put the sheet down in front of herself and uncapped a pen.

"I am going to write the four answers first, and then the fifth, and then I am going to put the pen down and I am going to go upstairs, and there are five other women in this house and every one of them has a copy of an instruction that says write your own part in your own hand and do not consult anybody.

"And I have not consulted anybody and I am not going to, and that has never once in four months been the thing that cost us the most."

She wrote for about six minutes and then put the pen down and went up the stairs without saying anything, and shut her door, and did not come down for the rest of the day.

The five of them stood there.

"Twenty-two minutes," said Ola Bright, who had a hand on the copybook. "She did not look up once and she did not stop and she did not put her hand over any of it. I want that written down as well, because in about a fortnight there will be a version of this evening in which one of us says she hesitated, and she did not, and I will know."

Marguerite Vale took a pencil and read the sheet upside down from where she was standing, which she could do.

"The four are correct," she said. "And the box is empty. And the fifth is answered."

She turned the sheet so the table could see it.

"She has written yes," Marguerite Vale said. "She has written yes, and she has put her own name under it, and she is right, and it is the first time in five months that anything has gone in that book that one of us actually wanted to put in."

"Does that settle the question," said Nadia Ferrous.

"It settles it for the Office, because the Office asked it only so that there would be a place to put the answer it already had." Marguerite Vale put the pencil down. "And every other one of us is now going to be asked the same question on the same sheet, on the same day, without knowing what she wrote, because she has just told us in front of six people that she is not going to ask us."

Runa Sato had picked up the pen.

She wrote her own part in her own hand in about ninety seconds, and she did not look at anybody while she did it, and at the bottom, under the fifth question, she wrote the word that was true and the date and nothing else, and put the pen down.

"Six answers for one address," said Ines Duarte, looking at the sheet. "Four of them correct. One of them the box empty. And two of them, in two different hands, saying two different things about a thing that happened on the twelfth of this month in a building neither of them was in.

"That is not an answer. That is this house."
