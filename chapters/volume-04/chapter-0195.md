# Nothing Said At All

The forms went round the table on the morning of the fifteenth and there were five of them because Doyle had said six lines and one of the six was the head's, and the head's line was a different paper and lived in a different building.

Each one was one side of a sheet. It wanted, in a ruled block: the member, in their own hand; what the member is; where that is evidenced; and then a line at the foot where the member signed.

Not what they stand for. Not a band. Not a plate. Not a number. What a member is, and the document behind it, because Doyle had said a standing is only enterable against a document and he was not going to be the man who entered five.

"You are asking each of us for the address of our own name," said Nadia Ferrous, turning hers over. "That is what this is. Not my standing. My name. The building. The door. Which register, which book, which counter, which file."

"Yes," said Ines Duarte.

"And on the seventh of this month Tessa wrote her name on a card at a counter in front of twenty-one people and said what she would answer for. And I have a household on page nine that goes to a counter once a week. And Marguerite has a standing in this city that predates all of us and does not sit in any document a clerk can read."

"Yes," said Ines Duarte.

"So three of the five of us can answer that form from something we put in a file in the last ten days on purpose, and it will be read in about nine years by a stranger."

"Two can," said Marguerite Vale. "And I will tell you the price of the other two before this room decides anything, and then I will answer for myself, because if I do not answer for myself then not one person in this kitchen is going to understand what is actually on that sheet."

The forms sat on the table.

"A standing that is evidenced by a thing we put in a file in the last ten days," Marguerite Vale said, "is a standing that will be read in nine years by a man who has never met us and will find it and will not find us, because the document says what it says and we are not in it.

"And a standing that cannot be evidenced is not enterable on the form. And a member whose standing is not enterable is a member the schedule holds. And a schedule that holds a member who cannot be evidenced is held."

"So the certificate does not issue," said Runa Sato.

"The certificate does not issue, and the sixty run, and this is the eleventh day and forty-nine of them are gone." Marguerite put her own form down with the pen across it. "I am going to say this in the middle of the morning because I am not going to say it at the end, where it would sound like an argument.

"Every one of us has, in the last ten days, written our own name into a document we chose to write it into, and we each did it for a reason that was ours and not his, and I would like the room to understand that not one of those five documents evidences a standing. They evidence a decision.

"There is a difference and it has taken me thirty years to learn to hear it and I am telling you now. What you have done is put yourselves in the record. What that form wants is the record saying what you are, and only an office can make the record say that, and an office will only say it if you can show it the source."

She looked round the table.

"So we have a form that wants a document, and five women who have written five documents, and neither of those is the other thing, and there is about four days between now and the twentieth in which to work out which is which."

Runa Sato had the form in front of her and had not picked up the pen.

"My standing is entered as at the seventh of this month, at a counter, in my own hand, with a date and an hour on it," she said. "Doyle said both dates will be on the sheet. So I can answer the form from a thing the Office already holds. I can answer it."

"You can answer it," said Ines Duarte.

Tessa Okonkwo had not picked hers up either.

"My standing is a chart," she said. "The chart went into a Wing file on the seventh of April. And then a woman read the front of it and sent a man with a warrant to my hatch, and I answered him at a counter, and I wrote a card at that counter, and yesterday a man took the hours out of my register." She turned her face away and then turned it back. "I can answer the form from the chart. I have a document."

"You have a document that puts a member of the Bureau and a building in one sentence," said Ines Duarte, "and you wrote it in daylight on purpose, and I would like you to notice that you just said it as though it were a problem with the form and not with the eight days."

Nadia Ferrous put her pen down.

"I cannot answer it," she said. "Page nine says a household and an address and a head and my name against the premises, and it does not say what I am. I run cars. That is what I am. And there is no document in this city that says I run cars, because the thing that says I run cars is a district lift service and it does not put itself in my file."

She said it flat.

"That is the whole of it. I have written myself into a book that goes to a counter once a week and I have done it because a page in a service file is the only thing that has ever gone anywhere on its own, and I have been waiting since the fourteenth of March for a document with my name and my decision in the same place, and I did not get it, because a service file records where I work and not that I chose it.

"And now the Office wants the second thing and I do not have the second thing, and I have made myself findable on purpose and findable is not the same as evidenced, and those are two different things and I am the one who has just found out."

The room sat with that.

Marguerite Vale took her own form and turned it round and wrote on it, in a small upright hand, about two minutes' worth.

"Here it is," she said. "I am a guild broker of nineteen years' standing. That is evidenced by the register of brokers, which is a book, which is in an office, and any clerk can be sent to it and it will say my name against it. I can answer the form and it will be true and it will be read in nine years by a man who goes and looks."

She set the pen down.

"And the consequence of that answer is that the standing of the standing household is my standing, and that has been the case since the seventh of this month when I said it out loud at this table, and every one of you heard me say it, and none of you asked me for it, and I have been waiting four days for somebody to ask me for it."

"Then we do not sign her," said Sun Meiling, from the fourth stair.

The table went quiet.

"We do not sign her," Sun Meiling said, "because the moment her name is on that sheet as evidenced, the schedule issues on the twenty-seventh of June bound to a rank-two broker, and that is not our household, that is a guild with a house attached. And I said on the sixth that I would not be written down and I meant it and I am not going to watch the other six be written down into a thing I would not be in."

"Sun," said Runa Sato.

"I have said no," said Sun Meiling. "I have said no four times now, on the second of May and on the sixth and on the thirteenth in a queue and now, and I am not saying it again, and I am saying it about her and not about me because it is her name and not mine and I am the only one at this table who is not going to be on it."

Marguerite Vale looked at the fourth stair for about four seconds.

"You are right," she said. "And I am going to do it anyway, and I am going to tell the room what that is, and then I am going to go and see a broker about it and I am not going to be thanked for it."

The room did not answer that.

"That is not the whole of it," she said, "and you all know it is not, so I will say it. If I do not sign, this schedule holds and the sixty run and the certificate does not issue and we go back to being six women and a man on a board and a word on a rail, and every one of us is exactly where we were on the first of this month.

"I am not signing it for you. I am signing it because it is the only instrument in this city that turns a door into a question. And I am signing it with my own name and my own register, and when the certificate issues on the twenty-seventh of June this household will be bound to me.

"And that is the price, and I have known the price since the seventh and I told you the price on the seventh and none of you asked me for it, and I have been carrying it round this kitchen for four days waiting for one of you to make me say it out loud."

"And we did not," said Ines Duarte.

"And you did not," Marguerite Vale said, "and that is the correct outcome and I want it noticed, because I did not arrange it and I would have been ashamed of either of us if you had."

Ines Duarte had her own form in front of her and had not touched it since it went round.

"You have all now told me what you can and cannot evidence," she said, "and I am going to say the thing that is left and then I am going to stop, and I am going to say it once.

"I cannot evidence anything either. Not this month, not in March, not since the fourteenth. There is no document in this city in which I am anything other than a person who files things, and the one thing I have filed, the composition return, names six women and a space for a head, and I did not write a word on it and I have said so to this room more than once.

"So I am going to leave it blank, and I want it recorded that I have been asked, and that I have not declined, and that the reason I am leaving it blank is not modesty and is not a position and is not a price, and I am not going to explain it to you, and I am not going to explain it to him, and I am not going to explain it to the form."

"Leave it," said Sun Meiling.

"I am going to leave it," Ines Duarte said. "And the schedule holds on my line as well as on Nadia's and on Marguerite's decision, and every day it holds is a day, and the certificate does not issue, and that is what happens when the only person at a table who can read a form correctly does not fill in her own."

She put the form face down on the table and squared it with the edge of her hand, which is what she did to everything, and which is why a form she has handled once looks handled.

"Take it in," she said. "I will write to it in a week or I will not, and either way it is going in a drawer with the rest of the week's post, and I am not going to say one word about it again."

The morning went on. Runa Sato and Nadia Ferrous and Tessa Okonkwo and Marguerite Vale wrote four forms in four hands over about an hour, and two of them were evidences and one of them was a decision and one of them was a building, and one of them was blank with a name on the corner.

At about half past ten Ines Duarte went up the sixty-one steps and did not come down for two hours, and when she came down she had her own rooms on Lantern Row in her head and she sat on the fourth stair and looked at the board on the landing wall for a while.

He came and stood at the eighth step.

"They have all filled theirs in," he said.

"They have all answered a form," she said, "and you have not asked me what I put on mine, and I want to tell you why you did not, and then I am going to stop."

He waited.

"You did not ask because you have known since the twenty-second of April not to ask me that," she said, "and because you made a rule of it once and kept it, and because a man who has kept that rule for twenty-four days does not break it on the day six women fill in five forms in a kitchen.

"That is why you did not ask, and it is the correct reason, and it is also the reason I am the only person in this house who has not been asked, and I have noticed that, and I am not going to turn it into a thing about either of us."

She got up off the step.

"You will sign on the nineteenth," she said. "In a room. With a clerk who writes down what is in front of the clerk. And when you do, the reason you are doing it is not the twenty-seventh of June and it is not six women, it is that you have known for twenty-four days that I am not going to say what happened to me, and you have decided to build a house around the space where I do not say it.

"I have read it. I have read every page of it, and I did not have to look at the review once, and I would like you to know that I know what that is worth."

He did not say anything, and she went in, and the kitchen door shut, and there were four forms on the table and one blank one face down.

Eighteen of the sixty are gone. Forty-two of them are left. Five days to the twentieth.
