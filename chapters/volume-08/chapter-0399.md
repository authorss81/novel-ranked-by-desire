# The Minute That Carries Her Initials

The last working day of a quarter is not a day that anything happens on.

It is a day that everything that has happened goes out on, at four in the afternoon, on the ordinary stream, from every desk in the city that keeps a book, and the room at the end of the passage off the second landing is a desk like any other desk in Ostara and it had a bundle of about nine sheets in it at about ten past three on the afternoon of the twenty-ninth of March.

He was on the bench in the passage with his bag on the floor, and Beatrix Halm had gone up the stair at about ten to three and had not sat down, and had said one sentence about a wall in the morning and nothing else.

## The Sheet

It was a quarterly return and it was the fourth one of the year and it went to four divisions on the ordinary stream, and the room's clerk put it on the table under the window face up so that anything in the corridor could read it, which is what the room does on the last working day of a quarter and not on any other day.

Nine ruled lines, one for each seat, and under the nine a tenth with a box on it, and under the ten, at the foot, two lines that the clerk writes herself and has never once been asked to print.

The first three said seated, and under them the clerk wrote the names, because on the last working day of a quarter the return carries names.

The fourth line did not say *vacant*.

It said, in a hand that was not a clerk's hand and was not the room's: *Marguerite Vale*. And under it, in the clerk's hand, in about four words, the reason it was not a seat: *present. No standing given.*

The fifth said recused on her own signature, the sixth said seal withdrawn on the twenty-third of December, and the seventh said not in the room since the twenty-third of December. The eighth and the ninth said seated and not present, and had said seated and not present on every return since the autumn.

And under the nine the clerk wrote what she writes at the foot of that return every week.

*The room stands at three.*

## The Transmission

Above the sheet, on a slip about four inches by two, was the transmission: what the return is going to four divisions on, who is producing what out of the return, and which division each part came out of.

There was one initial on it in a small level hand that goes back three inches at the top of the downstroke, and he had looked at that hand over a counter for eleven years, and it was not the hand of the woman who had written her name in the fourth line six days ago and it was not the hand of the chair.

It was the initial of the officer of the division that holds the fourth part, and an officer who produces a part initials the transmission above it, and that part has been in the counter-forensics of the second sub-level since the morning of the twenty-seventh of October, and its officer signed a minute about it in her own hand at about eleven o'clock on the twenty-eighth of March.

"Two initials," said the clerk, not looking up. "One of mine and one of the division. It goes out at four."

"How many copies."

"Four divisions and the copy is a copy. There is no other kind of copy in this room."

## What The Minute Said

The minute went out with the sheet. It was a quarter of a page and the clerk read it out in the passage, with the door open, in the flat voice she reads a requisition out in, and Lin Hao had heard it twice since the seventh of February and Beatrix Halm heard it for the first time.

It said that the requisition was not decided and was not carried and had not been received, and that it crosses into the quarter commencing the first of April at four in the afternoon on the thirtieth of March with everything written on it.

It said that the ground on the fourth part describes the address as it stood at about eleven on the morning of the twenty-seventh of October, and that the composition has since altered and that the room notes the alteration and does not act on it, which is the finding the chair put in the record on the seventeenth of March in her own words.

It said that the composition of the premises is seven from the thirteenth day of March and that the priced line of the sixteenth of September is seven, and that both figures stand in the same minute four lines apart and neither of them corrects the other.

And it said that the foot of the fourth of four sheets of plain paper of the twenty-ninth of December was produced on the twenty-second of March at the demand of the subject and entered as produced and not adopted, and the eight words went into the record in his own hand, in a room that had been told by its chair not to adopt them.

And then it came to the last three lines.

## The Three Lines

"The subject has attended every sitting at which this sheet has been read."

"The subject has put no question to this room since the sixth of February."

"The subject holds no standing in this room."

The clerk read them out and turned the page over and that was the whole of the minute.

"Read them again."

"I do not read the same line twice in the same minute," said the clerk, "and I will not do it for you, and you are not the only person in this corridor who could ask and the reason I am saying no out loud is that I have read about nine hundred of those minutes and there has never once been anything in one of them that anybody wanted read twice."

He stood in the passage and found that he could not get a breath in properly, which was not about the words. They were nine words each. It was that they were the only three lines in the whole of a quarter's paper that were about him, and that they were arranged in the order that makes a stranger certain of something.

A man who attended every sitting. A man who asked nothing for four months. A man with no standing.

In about four years a clerk in one of four divisions would read those three lines together and put a reason on them.

The reason would be that the man had been careful, because being careful is the only explanation that fits a person who turns up to everything and says nothing. It would be correct as a description. It would be a lie as a cause.

And no office in this city can put a second line under a minute once the page is in the book.

## What The Fourth Line Does

The clerk squared the bundle before she tied it, which she does at four every afternoon whether there are nine sheets in it or ninety.

"Four copies," she said. "One to each division. They arrive tomorrow morning and they are put on a shelf, and the shelf is the same shelf and there are four of them and nobody has ever had to look at this return before in their life."

"And the fourth line."

"The fourth line goes out as what is on it." She did not look up. "A name and four words saying why it is not a seat, and the four words are mine, and they are the correct four words, and I have entered a great many things in nineteen months that were not what anybody wanted."

"What happens to her."

"Nothing happens to her at four in the afternoon," said the clerk. "She is a guild broker and she is not on a roll and there is no division in this city that has to do anything about a broker's name in a line. She will find out that it went out in about a year, from a return, in a building she has never been inside." She put the bundle down on the table.

"That is the ordinary way a thing like that finds out about itself and it is worse than being asked."

## Two Sentences In A Passage

Wren Kallo came out of the room at about twenty past three with her coat on and stopped when she saw him standing at the end of the bench, and neither of them said anything for about four seconds.

"You initialled the transmission."

"I initial the transmission on every quarterly return that goes out with a part of mine in it," said Wren Kallo. "There have been four this year and there will not be a fifth until the thirtieth of April, and I am not permitted to say anything about it, and you are not permitted to ask me anything about it, and we have both had about eleven months of practice."

"I am not asking."

"I know you are not asking." She put her bag over her shoulder. "You have not asked me one question about that address since the sixth of December and you are not going to ask me one tonight, and I have noticed, and I am not going to reward it, and I want that said by both of us tonight."

"Noted."

"And the last thing, because you have been standing in that passage for about three hours looking at a foot of a sheet that you put into this room yourself on purpose on the twenty-second of March."

She did not lower her voice.

"You have not been careful. You have been doing arithmetic, and there is not one word on that sheet about arithmetic, and I have been in this room nineteen months and I have read about four hundred of those returns and I have never once seen one of them write down why a man said nothing."

The bundle went out at four.

It was a quarter of a page of minute and a sheet with nine lines on it and a fourth line that said *Marguerite Vale* in a hand that was not a clerk's, and it went to four divisions on the ordinary stream, and a broker who is not on a roll in this city and has never been on a roll in this city was in it four lines from the top of somebody else's name.

One day to the last working day of the quarter.

At six in the morning a boy with a paste brush and a list of numbers was going to come down at the north end of a square with a floor on a card, and the floor was going to be one higher than it had been since the thirtieth of December, and every hand under that plate had been given on a night in December against a composition of eight that had been seven since the morning of the thirteenth of March.
