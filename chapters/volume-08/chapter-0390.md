# The Notice Served At Four

Four gates in this city clear at four in the afternoon and everything a division has entered into a book that day goes out on them, and on the afternoon of the twentieth of March one thing went out about premises 34-6-118, and it was not a requisition, and nobody had asked for it.

It was a composition, at seven, with a date on it, in the ordinary way.

## What It Was

A return of a counter entry. A slip had been made out at nine forty-one on the morning of the thirteenth at a counter on the ground floor of the Vestry by a clerk who did not know then what she was doing to a man who was not in the building.

It had gone into the division that holds the list of persons keeping an address, and that division is required to send the composition of every address on its list back to that list, once, when it moves.

It is not a paper anybody can object to. There is no ground to strike and no fourth part and no ruled box that has to be filled, and there is no sitting, and there was no vote, and no room in this city has to do anything at all about it.

*Composition. Premises 34-6-118. Seven. From the thirteenth day of March.*

That is the whole content of it. It goes to the six desks in this city that keep one, and it goes to the names on the list, and it is signed for by whoever answers the door.

## Why Nine

Because a list is dated and made and it goes, and from the moment it has gone it is an afternoon that happened, and this city has no office anywhere that can reach back into an afternoon that happened.

The list of the thirtieth of October carries eight names. It is a list of persons keeping an address and not a list of addresses, and every name on it on that afternoon was somebody keeping premises 34-6-118.

It carried eight when it went out and it carries eight in the six divisions that keep a copy of it, and it will carry eight in four years, because a list is not a record of a house and a list cannot be corrected, withdrawn or unissued.

A list is a record of what was true on the afternoon it was made. The only instrument in this city that ever took a person off one was a woman at a counter who took herself off it on her own account and with no instrument at all, and what she took herself off was the list being made this month.

The one from the thirtieth of October went out at four on the afternoon of the twenty-ninth of October and it is out of her reach, and it has been since the counter at four o'clock on the afternoon of the thirteenth.

So eight notices went to eight names on a list from October, and seven of those eight slept at that address on the night of the twentieth, and the eighth gave the address up at a counter at nine forty-one on the thirteenth and is up the same stair in a room that is not that address, and she has not been at that door in a long time, which is not the same thing as having left the building.

Her envelope went under her own door that evening and she did not carry it to the table, and there was nothing for anybody to ask her about, because she had stood at that table on the fifteenth and said all of it, and this was a figure and it wanted nothing.

And a ninth went to the officer who keeps the address, in her own hand, because the officer who keeps an address is served the composition herself and not on a card pushed under a door.

Nine. All nine inside one building on Ferrier Street.

## Where It Went

He went down at about ten past four and stood at the far end of the arcade and watched it go into the cage at the Vestry, which is a grille with about forty hooks in it behind the doors and one key and the key is on the inside.

A clerk signed for it and wrote the hour in the column, and the hour was four, and there was nothing else in the column.

There were nine envelopes in the bundle and they were the same size and they were the same colour and they were not heavy.

He did not open one and he could not have opened one, because nine envelopes going to one address inside one building is not a thing a man may take an interest in from the far end of an arcade, and there are eight people in that building who would each have a different and correct opinion about a man who stood at a grille counting them.

## The One Nobody Had Asked For

And then there was the other thing, which was not on the bundle and did not come up the stairs.

A composition is served to the head of a household in her own hand at the address and at the post, and Sun Meiling had a post off a third court in a building where a man sits on the floor above hers, and the form had gone out of a division at four on the afternoon of the twentieth along with everything else, and it would arrive on a desk at about nine on the morning of the twenty-first.

Nobody at that table had asked for it, nobody in that building had asked for it, and the woman it was about had known about it since a keeper two floors under an Exchange had said her name out loud in front of a man on the fourteenth of March.

## The One That Is Going To Go To A Public Post

One of the six desks it went to is a wing off a third court, and that desk keeps a list of addresses and not a list of officers, and an officer who keeps an address at a public post is served at the post.

So there is a copy of a piece of paper, with the premises at the top of it, the figure seven, and the date of the thirteenth of March on it, that is going to be carried up two floors of a building off a third court and put down on a desk on the morning of the twenty-first. She will read it and put it in a drawer and say nothing to anybody, because there is nothing to say.

The same woman is handed a second thing on the morning of the thirtieth. That one is a printed sheet with a box to fill in and the address where the heading goes, and it renews a certificate she gave at nine in the morning on the thirtieth of November, in her own division, in the ordinary course of a job she does every quarter for as long as anybody has been keeping count.

And between the twenty-first and the thirtieth there is no point at which anybody asks her a single question.

## Nine Envelopes On Ferrier Street

They came in a bundle on the evening of the twentieth between about six and seven, brought up sixty-one steps by a young man on the ordinary delivery round, and there was nobody to sign for them because a signature for a bundle goes on the docket at the bottom of the street and the docket came up with them.

Beatrix Halm put the bundle on the table under the window on top of a piece of brown card with a man's name at the foot of it, and she did not notice that, and neither did anybody else.

Eight of them were addressed to eight names on a list from the thirtieth of October. They went along the hall and under eight doors and into eight rooms, and seven of those rooms belonged to people who keep that address tonight.

The eighth belonged to a woman who gave that address up on the thirteenth and has not been at that door in a long time, and she took hers in and put it face down on the shelf by the door and did not carry it to the table.

And one of them came back down the stairs.

Sun Meiling brought it down at about a quarter to seven and put it on the table face down, which she had not done with anything in five months, and stood there with her hand on it.

"It is her own," she said. "The one at the head. It has her name on it twice."

"Then it is the ninth," said Nadia Ferrous.

"It is the ninth." Sun Meiling did not lift her hand. "And it came up the same sixty-one steps as the other eight on the same delivery, and it was put in the same bundle by the same boy, and nobody in this city has the slightest idea that it is the only one of the nine that anybody has to do anything about."

Somebody at the table said *do not open it*.

"It is not a requisition," said Sun Meiling, "and there is nothing on it that wants anything. It is a figure. I have read it."

She took her hand off. "It is going in the drawer in my own room tonight, and I am not telling anybody in this kitchen what I am going to do with it, and there is going to be no argument about that, and I would like it entered that I said so in advance."

The table let her go.

## Ten Days

That was the whole of the afternoon of the twentieth of March and it took about four minutes.

There were ten days to the last working day of the quarter. On the thirtieth, a sheet at the end of a corridor goes down to a clerk under a new quarter's number, and a dispensary on the Vestry arcade is sent a return about a man held in the third sub-level of the Classification Wing, and a certificate lapses at a desk in a division off a third court.

Three things. None of them asked for. Two of them about the same door.

He walked up Ferrier Street at about ten past five with the back he has had since the autumn, and stopped at the top of it the way he has stopped at the top of it every evening for four months, and looked down at the wall.

Eighty days. Twelve weeks.

The card had come down on the thirtieth of December in a different hand on different paper and it had printed the floor, and the floor was 8,145 that morning and has not moved since, and no paint has gone over his figure anywhere in eighty days, because while he is on the floor he is in band and the wall has nothing to say about a man standing where the card says he stands.

It said nothing now, it had said nothing for eighty days, and it would say nothing for ten more, and the card would come down again on the thirtieth at six in the morning in the hand of a boy with a paste brush.

He went up the sixty-one and told the table none of it, and there was a piece of paper face down under the cloth on the table under the window with his own name at the foot of it, and eight women, and ten days.
