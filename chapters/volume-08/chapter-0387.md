# The Standing Time For A Sheet

A sheet is not read until its fourth part is in, and the fourth part of the eleventh requisition had gone into the space between the figure and the ruled box at the seventh minute of the morning of the twentieth of February, and the ninth minute is the standing time to read, to question and to decide, and this room has kept to that time for twenty years because the chair does not believe anything that has been skimmed.

The sheet was called for eight minutes to ten on the morning of the seventeenth of March.

He got to the corridor at eight minutes to ten.

That was not luck and it was not good planning. He had come off the Fifth Branch floor at a quarter to eight, because the Vestry clinic had wanted to talk to him about something that turned out to be nothing, and because a man who is served with a requisition and is not in the room when the sheet is read does not get it read, and that is not a rule anybody has ever had to enforce.

Anouk Ferreira was in the chair. One, two and three were in the room. Four said *vacant* on the sheet in front of her in a clerk's hand and had said it since the twelfth of February. Five was recused. Six and seven were not there and had not been since the twenty-third of December.

Three.

## The Room Before Nine Minutes

The room has a table under a window, nine chairs, and a small table at the door with a pen on it, and there is no clock in it, because the standing time is kept by a woman with a book and not by a man with a clock, and nobody ever wrote that down.

Fourteen chairs were in it. Three of the nine were seated and the other six had not been for four months, and one of the six was a recusal on a signature and one was a seal withdrawn on the twenty-third of December and one was a man who had not crossed the door since the same day, and there were two ordinary vacancies besides the fourth.

Lin Hao sat on the chair that is not one of the nine, the one with nothing in the side of it where a seal goes.

Nobody spoke to him for about four minutes. Beatrix Halm had called the sitting from the top step the previous morning and Runa Sato had co-signed it at a Field counter at about eleven, and the clerk had numbered it, and it was the thirteenth of these, and the room had never in twenty years heard the thirteenth called for on a ground.

"Thirteenth," said the clerk, turning her book round so that anybody coming through the door could read it. "Requisition. Subject: the account of thirty years standing at premises 34-6-118."

"Last time it was the eleventh," said the woman in the second chair. "And that was a month ago."

"It is the thirteenth and it is on the sheet and it is in the book," said the clerk. "The numbering is the room's and not mine."

## What Was Read Out

A clerk reads a requisition out. She does not hand it round and she does not summarise it and she does not do it nicely, and the reason she does not do it nicely is that in about four years the only person who will ever read the sheet in full is a stranger with a folder.

*First. The subject and the address.*

*Second. The account. Thirty years standing at premises 34-6-118.*

*Third. The figure. Two hundred and fourteen thousand grains, one movement, three years, sealed, source a held account in the third sub-level, file two thousand two hundred and four.*

*Fourth. The ground, which is the description a counter clerk made at a kitchen table on the morning of the twenty-seventh of October, taken against the head of the premises, with the hour entered and the division that holds it.*

He knew every word of that one. On the morning of the third of March eight women read four lines of it aloud over his own table, and the woman who had written it was a clerk called Imrie and the four words on it were his.

*Fifth. The box, ruled, empty.*

The chair put her pen down.

## The Ninth Minute

"Question," said Anouk Ferreira, at about four minutes.

Runa Sato had one and it was about the figure and it was about the order in which a figure is read, and it was a good one, and the chair answered it in a sentence, and it went into the minute.

Lin Hao had had about four minutes and had had one question available to him since the sixth of February and it was the same nineteen words it had been in February, because it was the sentence about the fourth part being the origin of the business, and he did not say it, and this time he had a better reason for not saying it, which was that striking the fourth part killed the sheet and a dead sheet is not carried and he wanted it carried.

"Anything from the subject."

"Nothing," said Lin Hao.

"Written?"

"Nothing written."

"Then the question is closed and we have five minutes and the sheet does not move," said the chair.

## The Seventh Minute, Again

"There is a minute in the middle of this morning," said Runa Sato, "and the room is going to get to it. Ground entered at the seventh minute of the twentieth of February. Two."

"Two is not a majority."

"I am not saying it is. I am saying the ground of this sheet arrived eleven days after the sheet was heard, and there is a form that shows which, and somebody in this room is going to have to say so out loud in about four minutes and I would like it said by a person and not by a clerk reading a line."

Nobody said so out loud.

"So be it," said Anouk Ferreira, and the clerk turned her book back.

## Eight On The Twenty-Seventh Of October

At about the sixth minute one of the three said the thing that nobody had said at that table in four months, and it was said by accident, the way it had been the fourth part on the sixth of February.

"It says eight."

"It says eight."

"The description says eight persons at a table at that address with a head at the head of them, and it says it as at the morning of the twenty-seventh of October, and it is the seventeenth of March." The woman at the third chair turned her sheet round. "There are seven."

The room went quiet in a way that Lin Hao had not heard in twenty years of rooms and had heard twice in five months.

"She is not the head and she is not a counter and she is not an office," said Runa Sato, from the second chair. "She is a woman who kept an address for six years and gave it up at a counter on the thirteenth and it came off the composition by one."

"And that is not in this room," said Anouk Ferreira, "and it is not going to be argued in this room, and it is going in the minute as an observation and nothing else. Write this: the fourth part describes the premises as at the morning of the twenty-seventh of October and the composition of the premises has since altered and the room notes the alteration and does not act on it."

## What A Description Cannot Do

"You are going to say that the fourth part is false," said Lin Hao.

"I am going to say that the fourth part is not false, and that it is a document, and that a document says what it said on the day it was made." The chair looked at him for the first time since he had come in.

"A description made at a date cannot be false. It can be old. There is a difference and the difference has been in this city since before any of us and I am not going to have it confused in this room in the nine minutes I have got."

"Nobody objected to the ground."

"Anybody may object to the ground," said Anouk Ferreira, "and nobody has, in four months, including the man who was served with it."

## The Ninth Minute

The clerk put the ninth minute in and said it out loud because that is what the room does.

"Ninth minute. Read, questioned, not decided. Three standing. Not carried."

"Not decided is available until the last working day of the quarter," said the chair, "which is the thirtieth, and anything still standing then goes across into the next quarter with everything written on it, and the administration of it crosses too."

Then she stopped, and read her own minute back, and read the ninth line of it again before she said the last thing.

"There is one thing on this sheet that was not on it on the twentieth of February and I want it in the record in my own words rather than the clerk's. On the sixth of February this holding could not be administered, because a live requisition is administered against a composition and the composition of that address disagreed with the figure the line is carried at by one, and a thing that disagrees with itself cannot be counted and cannot be administered."

"And on the thirteenth of March," said Runa Sato, "it can."

"It can," said the chair. "It has not been administered and it is not being administered this morning and this room administers nothing, and it is now administrable, and from the first of April it will be administered by an office that did not apply for it."

He had eight days.

He went out of the room at five minutes past ten and stood in the corridor with his back to the wall and stayed there longer than a man with nowhere to be stays.

Eight days. A return about a man in a room with no window would reach a dispensary on the Vestry arcade on the thirtieth. A certificate would lapse on the thirtieth. A requisition would cross into the next quarter on the thirtieth.

And a woman who cannot be sworn was going to be handed a printed form at her own post, in a building that has a man on its third floor, on the same morning as all three of the others, and had not been asked about any of it.
