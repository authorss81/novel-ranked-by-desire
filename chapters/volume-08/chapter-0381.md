# The Offer Made At The Kitchen Table

He came down the sixty-one at about twenty to seven on the morning of the eleventh of March with no sleep in him and an ache in his back that had got worse in the four hours he had spent lying on a floor, and he came down them because a man who is not in a kitchen by seven has not decided anything about it, and because there were nineteen days to the end of the quarter.

The kitchen was already warm and the tea was on and Nadia Ferrous was sitting at the head end in her coat with the hood down, which meant she had come in off the doors of the Vestry arcade and had not taken the coat off, which meant she was going out again.

She put her hand flat on the table and turned it over.

"The thirteenth," she said. "Two days. I am telling you and not asking you, and that is the difference and it is mine."

## What Became Of Forty-Eight Hours

He had asked for forty-eight hours at this table on the evening of the fourteenth of February, out loud, in front of eight women, and had told them he did not know what he was going to do with them.

The forty-eight hours ran out on the morning of the twentieth. On the morning of the twentieth she went to work and he went up a corridor to a room at the end of it, and nothing happened, and the arrangement died in the ordinary way that arrangements die in this city, which is that one of the two people stops mentioning it.

She said it in one sentence and she said it without any edge on it, because she was not there to score a point and had never once in five months been anywhere except a table with something to say.

"I am not bringing that up to make a point," she said. "I am bringing it up because a thing that waits for a man to give it a reason stops being a thing a woman does at a counter and becomes a thing two people are doing, and I have been at this table long enough to know which of those two is worth less."

## Why The Thirteenth

Then she put the arithmetic on the table, which is what she was for.

"A month is thirty days," said Nadia Ferrous. "The thirtieth is the last working day in it. The sheet that carries a line forward goes on the first of the month and it does not go on any day before that, and I am told by somebody at this table that if the composition of that address says what the line is priced at then it can go on, and if it does not say it then nothing goes on it and the line gets called again."

She put her thumb flat on the cloth.

"So it has to be seven before the end of this month, or on the morning it matters it is not seven at all. And I am not doing it on the thirtieth. I did the arithmetic about what a counter is like at four in the afternoon on the last working day of a quarter and I have decided against the last day of anything."

"Which leaves the thirteenth."

"The thirteenth I walk into a counter and say an address and go to work," she said. "Two days. And I have not asked this table about the thirteenth and this table is not going to be asked about the thirteenth, and I would like it noted that I have not asked, because I noticed last time that eight people deciding something makes it a different object entirely."

## What He Wanted And Did Not Say

What he wanted, and did not say, and would have paid a good deal for the right to say, was to be in the room.

He had spent eight years being a man who gives things away in the open and comes back thinner, and four hundred people in a quarter had worked out from that alone that he was some kind of Office, and there was no piece of paper anywhere in Ostara that said so.

Giving an address up at a counter is the oldest ordinary act in the city and it costs the woman who does it one thing, and it costs her exactly one thing for as long as she keeps a counter in front of her.

The second he asked her for it, it stopped being hers.

It became a man at a counter by proxy, with his face on it and his name nowhere on the paper. In about four years there would be a line in a counter book with a day and an hour on it, and everybody in that building would know whose morning it was, and eight women at that table would be able to say that is not what happened.

That was the reason. He had gone over it twice on the stairs.

It was not protection and it was not kindness and it was not even strategy, exactly.

It was that he had wanted for four months for somebody else to pay something and had been told in five different rooms by five different women that he could not arrange that, and the one thing that had come within reach of arranging it was a woman giving up the only thing she had, and if he put his hand on it then there would not be one square inch of this left that was anybody else's own.

## Agnete Lindqvist

"If you are in that room it is a man getting a woman to give up an address," said Agnete Lindqvist, from the end of the table by the window, without turning round. "If you are not in that room it is a woman at a counter. I have watched the difference happen from a chair by a window for eleven years and the difference is between a thing and an instrument, and an instrument is worth what somebody is prepared to spend on it."

"Which is not you."

"It is not me and it is not her and it is not any of us, and it is the first thing to arrive at that table in five months that nobody carried in here on his behalf, and I am saying it out loud so that nobody can say afterwards that it was arranged." She had not turned round. "That is all of it, and I would like it noted as coming from a woman who has not been outside."

## What Runa Sato Said About It

Runa Sato had come in at about a quarter to eight in her Field uniform and had eaten standing up the way she always did, and she put her cup down and did the thing she does, which is to put a price on a thing before anybody has decided whether to have it.

"I will say the queue, because that is what I am for and nobody has asked me and that has never once mattered." She put her hands flat. "A figure at an address is what an account is carried against and what a live paper gets administered against. A person giving an address up at a counter takes about ninety seconds and it costs the household one body."

"And it is worth it," said Nadia Ferrous.

"It is worth about four in the morning," said Runa Sato. "I have been pricing things at a Field counter for two years and that is what it comes to, and I have never once said out loud what I was doing up there in the second sub-level on the second of February because somebody had to do it and nobody else could."

The table stopped.

Runa Sato looked at the window.

"That was Wren's ground," she said. "I have just said it. It was hers and she never told us and I have known since the twentieth of February and I have been holding it since then, and I am tired of holding things about that table."

"Wren—"

"I am not asking her," said Runa Sato. "I am telling the table that I know, because a woman who has been holding a thing for a month in a house with eight people in it is not carrying a secret, she is carrying a debt."

## What The Table Decided

The table decided nothing, and it took about four minutes to decide nothing, and nobody was disappointed.

Eight people cannot decide a thing that has to be one person's ordinary act, and that had been said at this table once already, in the middle of February, and it was not going to be improved on.

What the table did do was the other thing, which was harder, and it took longer.

"Then he is not there," said Isolde Marchetti, about halfway through, and it was not a question and she did not look round while she said it.

No.

"And he does not stand outside the door."

He did not say that one either.

"Then he is not there," she said again, "and none of us is going to make that a favour to anybody, and Nadia is going to be annoyed about it for about a week and then she is going to be not annoyed about it and that is the correct order and I would like it to happen in that order."

Nadia Ferrous put her hood up.

"I am not going to be annoyed for a week," she said. "I am going to be exactly what I am going to be, and you can all watch that and it is going to be very boring."

## Afterwards

He got to the Fourth Branch at about ten to eight and did six floors before the office filled, and the wall said what it had said every morning since the card came down on the thirtieth of December, which was nothing at all, and which was seventy-one days of nothing now and counting, and a man on the floor of 8,145 is a man in band.

Beatrix Halm had gone down at half past six and come back up with her notebook under her arm and had sat on the other side of the table from him for about an hour without opening it.

"I am not writing the thirteenth," she said. "I have decided that on the step and I decided it before I knew what you were going to say, and I want you to hear it said out loud by me before I do not write it."

"That is your business."

"It is my business and I have never had a business before and I do not mind." She shut the book. "There is one thing I am going to ask for and then I am going to stop asking for anything for the rest of the month, so this is the one and it is cheap."

"Say it."

"If it goes wrong," said Beatrix Halm, "and a woman in an arcade says an address and a house of seven ends up in a piece of paper somewhere with her name off it and nothing on the other side of it, then somebody at this table is going to have to look at it. Not fix it. Look at it."

"That is you."

"It will be me," she said, "and I am putting it on the table now, in a kitchen, out loud, so that the version of it in four years is not a thing somebody decided on my own."

He went down to work.

There was going to be a fifth mark in this account on the morning of the first of April. That was not a guess and it was not his arithmetic: it came from a book on a chain, and a word against a printed rule, on the first of the month, as long as the thing waits at that rail and is neither paid off nor produced off the only door there is.

He knew which of the two figures had to move before the first of April and he knew it was not his, and on the thirteenth of March it was going to be done at a counter he was not going to be standing in, by a woman who had told him the date and had not told him why she had picked it.

That was the whole of the morning. He cleaned four washrooms on the fifth floor and thought about a counter.
