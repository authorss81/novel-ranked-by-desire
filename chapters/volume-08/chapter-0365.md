# The Box With Something In It

The room at the end of the corridor has nine seats and a table under a window and a clerk's desk at the near end with a book on it, and on the afternoon of the sixth of February there were seven people in the room and two of the chairs doing nothing at all.

One of them had stood empty since the first of November.

Runa Sato had a sheet of her own in front of her, which she had not brought, and she did the whole of it out loud in about four minutes and enjoyed it, and nobody in that room has ever enjoyed anything in the way she enjoyed that.

"Three of the four have been standing in since a fresh leaf of a quarter-book took this address on the second of October," she said. "The subject of it is the account. The holding is thirty years of a man, and the holding is a document now, and the document is one sheet of plain paper in his own hand with eight witness hands under it."

"It went into this room at five minutes to eleven on the fourteenth of January and it was served on the fifteenth and it went out on the stream at four in the afternoon on the nineteenth, and there is no standing anywhere in this city that could have held one of those back, and there was nobody to ask to hold it back."

"And the figure is seven."

"The figure is seven, and the figure is dated the sixteenth of September and it is initialled and not signed, and I have never seen a woman in this business price anything faster than that and I have never seen a man make one cheaper on purpose."

"Then it can be drafted," said a man in the seventh seat.

"It can be drafted today," said Runa Sato. "Two to call a sitting. Five to carry it. Nine minutes to read it, question it and decide it. It may be laid only before the last working day of the quarter. Three remain who would seal a fresh one and the chair will not."

She put her pencil down flat.

"And the fourth part of it is empty."

## The Fourth Part

"A requisition has four parts," said Runa Sato. "The subject, the holding, the figure, and the ground. Everybody in this room can recite the four and two of the three of you who are listening have been reciting them since October as though the fourth one were a formality, and it is the only one of the four that has to be a piece of paper."

"The ground is the reason it may be done."

"The ground is the reason it may be done, and a reason spoken out loud in a room is not a ground," said Runa Sato. "A ground is a document. It has to be a thing with a date on it that somebody else wrote, and it has to be in this room before the nine minutes are up."

"And if it is not in this room the sheet cannot be carried, and a sheet that is not carried takes nothing off anybody. Has anybody in this room got the slightest interest in a sheet that takes nothing off anybody?"

Nobody said anything.

"Which brings us to the part I have been waiting three weeks for somebody else to say, and I am going to say it and you are all going to hate it."

## It Cannot Be Him

"The ground cannot be the declaration of holding," said Runa Sato. "It cannot be the four sheets of the twenty-ninth of December either. He wrote those. A man is not ground for his own requisition."

"The fourth part of that sheet has to be a paper that a person other than him put his hand to. There are exactly three candidate documents in this city and I am going to run them, and then somebody is going to have to say the third one, and I would rather it was not me."

She turned the sheet round.

"The priced line of the sixteenth of September. No, because that is the figure and the figure is the third part, and you cannot put the third part in the fourth part and call it a reason."

"The minute of the twenty-sixth of December. No, because it is this room's own minute and a room cannot ground itself on what the room did at four in the afternoon on the twenty-sixth—"

"It was the twenty-sixth."

"—and because that minute says a requisition was voided, which is a finding against the ground and not a ground, and I have read it and it is four hands and it has the word requisition in it and any stranger with a folder will find it in about four years and I would like that stranger to find a sheet that is going somewhere and not a sheet that went nowhere in December."

She put the pencil down again.

"So that is two, and there is one, and it is the only one left, and it has been sitting in a drawer in a division on the other side of this city since the twenty-seventh of October and I am not going to say it."

## Wren Kallo

"There is one," said Wren Kallo.

She said it without moving, and she had been sitting in the second seat with her hands flat on the table the way she reads an instrument, and she did not look at anybody when she said it and she did not look at him afterwards either.

"It is in my division," she said. "It is the description those premises had put over them in the last week of October, made at a kitchen table against the head and not against the door, on his own asking, in front of eight of us, by a clerk called Imrie."

"The reason written on it is four words long and the clerk wrote the hour against them."

She turned her head about a quarter of the way and looked at a point on the wall above the window rather than at the table.

"The reason says *because it is cheaper*."

The room did not do anything at all. There is a sound a room makes when a sentence goes round it that is not silence and is not a voice, and it happened once, and then there was silence.

"It is the only paper in this city that describes those premises as having a head," said Wren Kallo. "And it is the only paper in this city that is older than the twelfth of January and was not written by him."

"And it is in my division. A ground is not drafted by a clerk. A ground is taken by a clerk from the division that holds the document, and the division that holds that document is the one I have been sitting in for eleven years."

## What It Costs

"Stop," said the chair.

Nobody else said anything for about eleven seconds and in those eleven seconds Lin Hao understood the whole of what had just happened in a room he had been inside four times.

A description made in a kitchen on Ferrier Street, by a clerk, at a table, in front of eight women, on the strength of four words a man said to her and the hour against them.

If that goes in as the fourth part of a requisition laid by nine people in a room at the end of a corridor, then for four years there is a document in a minute in this room that puts eight women and a clerk called Imrie inside the reason the account of thirty years is being taken, and every one of them will be asked about it by somebody who has never met any of them.

"That is the price of the sixth of February," said Anouk Ferreira, "and it is the only ground in this city and I am not going to stop anybody using it, and I am going to have it written down that I said so."

"We should not use it," said Agnete Lindqvist.

Nobody looked at her, because in that room she had not said anything since the twenty-ninth of December and had not been asked.

"There is a reason and there is a wrong reason," she said, "and a document that says the thing under classification is a person is the best paper any of us has ever had. If it is the ground then it stops being the paper that says you are a person and becomes the paper that says you are held, and those are two entirely different sentences, and I have spent eleven years in a building where I had to be careful about the difference between them."

## What A Room Does With A Number

"There is one more piece of the arithmetic before any of us says anything about the fourth part," said Runa Sato, "and it is the piece that everybody in a room forgets and that a clerk does not, and I have watched four good rooms forget it."

"Say the piece."

"Nine minutes," said Runa Sato. "Not nine minutes to read and nine minutes to decide and nine minutes for somebody who has not understood it. Nine minutes for the whole of it, and the whole of it includes a man who has not seen the sheet before he walks in, and that man is the only one at the table for whom it is not a repetition."

She put two fingers on the sheet.

"Three of you read this on the sixth. Two of you read a version of it in October and have not read the version that exists. He has read nothing at all, because nobody is permitted to give him a copy, and that is correct, because a man who is served with a requisition is served and not consulted, and if you want to consult him he is a different animal and you will not get him today."

"So we are nine minutes with a stranger in it."

"We are nine minutes with a stranger in it who does not have to understand the first part to understand the fourth," said Runa Sato, "because the fourth is a piece of paper and he will know the piece of paper. Everything above the fourth part is about an account at a door and every person at this table could describe that to him in four words, and the fourth part is the only one where being able to read is the whole of the job."

## The Sentence

He had not spoken since he came in.

"There is one more thing about a ground and nobody in this room has ever used it," he said. "I have been in this room four times and you have all taught me a great deal and nobody has ever once said this."

"Say it."

"A ground cannot be a sentence," he said. "A sentence is a thing a person says out loud and then nine people have a memory of and in four years there is nothing to put on a table. That is why a ground has to be a paper and not a voice. A paper can be asked for."

He put his hands flat on the table, which he had not done, and then took them off again.

"You can put a paper in a room," he said. "You cannot take one out of it."

The clerk had the fourth part of the sheet squared in front of her and had a pen and had not used it, and the room where nine people sit had gone quiet in the particular way a room goes quiet when everybody in it has understood the same sentence at the same moment and nobody wants to be the first one to move.

The clerk looked up at the chair.

"Do you want the ground written, chair."

"Not today," said Anouk Ferreira. "The ground is not going anywhere. It has been in that division since October. Leave the pen."
