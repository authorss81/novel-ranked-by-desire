# The Division That Would Use It

The second sub-level of the Classification Wing is a corridor with a stone floor and four doors in it, and three of the doors have a card in a holder with a division on it, and the fourth has a card in a holder and nothing in the holder, and has had nothing in it since Lin Hao first came up here in the autumn with a bad back and no idea what he was doing.

He stood in front of it on the afternoon of the twenty-fifth of March at about ten past four for about eleven minutes and did not knock.

Isolde Marchetti came up the stair behind him at her own pace and stood about six feet back, because she has never once in this house stood next to him at a door.

## What A Woman With A Door Knows

"There is one thing I am going to say out loud in this corridor and after that I am going to stand here and keep my mouth shut, and I have noticed in the last two days that keeping it shut is the only thing I am actually good at in this building."

"Say the thing."

"There is a rule about me that everybody in this house is polite enough not to say out loud, and I am going to say it out loud in a corridor outside a division so that it is once in a room where a stranger could hear it."

She did not raise her voice.

She never raised her voice.

"When I sing, a room that hears me agrees to what I sing. Not me. The room. It is not a thing I do to people. It is a thing that happens to a room."

"I know what it is."

"You have known what it is since the fourteenth of February, and in six weeks you have never once worked out what it means for a corridor."

She put her hand flat on the stone wall.

"This corridor has about fifty people through it on an ordinary afternoon and one of them is a man who writes down things that rooms agree to."

"If I sing one line in here then this corridor agrees to it, and it will go on agreeing to it after I have gone home, and there is no unagreeing it. In about four years a stranger will walk down it and find a thing a room believes about my husband, and it will not be me who said it."

"So do not sing in here."

"I am not going to sing in here," said Isolde Marchetti.

"I want you to understand how expensive that is. I do sums out loud in that kitchen. I have done sums out loud at that table every day since October and I have not been asked to stop and I would have stopped if anybody had asked. And I have walked up a stone stair in a building where I do not sing, and I want that on the record from somebody who was not asked."

## Eleven Minutes

"There is nothing to do here," he said. "That is the whole of the afternoon. I have no standing in this building, I have not got a folder, I am not a member of anything, and there is nobody inside that door who is going to come out for me."

"Then why did we come up."

"Because on the seventeenth of March a sheet was read for nine minutes in a room at the end of another corridor and the ground on it is a description made in this division on the morning of the twenty-seventh of October at a kitchen table in front of eight women, by a clerk called Imrie, on four words said by me." He put his hand flat on the wall opposite.

"And the division that holds that ground does not sit in this building and keeps no list of addresses and was handed nineteen minutes of somebody's shape on the first of January."

"You are going to stand outside it."

"I am going to stand outside it until half past four and then I am going to go and do my shift."

The corridor is about eleven feet wide, with one window at the far end of it and a bench that was put there some time before either of them was born and has not been sat on by anybody with a folder in it since.

About fifty people go through it on an ordinary afternoon. A man came down it at about a quarter past four with a tray of tea for a typing room and went through the door with nothing on it without slowing down, which told Lin Hao more about the standing of a corridor than any of the rest of the afternoon had.

That was the afternoon. Eleven minutes outside a door, and no instrument in this city by which a man who is not a member may ask a division a single question.

## What She Was Going To Have Done Instead

"I want to tell you the other version of this afternoon," said Isolde Marchetti, "because we are standing in a stone corridor outside a door that has nothing on it and I am not going to last until half past four without telling somebody."

"Go on."

"On the third of January, in this house, I said that you were the reason I was frightened and that you had not done one single thing to cause it." She put her hand flat on the wall on the other side of the door from his.

"And I have had eleven weeks to sit with that sentence and I have worked out that I can end it, and it is about nine seconds long, and it is the only thing I can do with it, and I cannot do it in here."

"Tell me what it is and I will not use it."

"You are not going to be in here when I do it," said Isolde Marchetti, "because the second I sing one line in this corridor the corridor agrees to it, and you would be standing on it, and a room that agrees to a thing about you is the last thing either of us wants on the morning of the thirtieth of March."

"So it waits."

"It waits until the first of April and it is the only thing I have got and I have been carrying it since the third of January and I am not going to put it on a stair in a building where a man writes down what rooms believe."

## The Question He Was Not Allowed To Ask

"Then ask me the one you cannot ask her."

He turned round.

"There is a rule," said Isolde Marchetti, "and the rule is that you may not put one single question to me about that address. You have kept it since the sixth of December and I have watched you keep it, and there is a thing I have wanted to say about it for four months and I have not said it because I am not allowed to ask either."

"Say whatever you like."

"I could say whatever I liked," said Isolde Marchetti, "and I am not going to, because a woman who has had a rule put on her and has then started collecting exceptions to it is a woman who has stopped being under anything."

"In six weeks you have not asked me one question."

"Not one of them."

"Seven weeks of a table every evening with eight women round it, and eight women asking you things, and not one person asking you anything. I would like you to understand that I noticed you doing that. I am not going to make a speech out of it at you in a corridor."

"That is not a speech."

"It is not a speech," she agreed. "It is a note. Do not put it in a book."

## What Came Out At Four

At about half past four a door further down the corridor opened and a woman came out with a bundle of about two inches of paper under her arm, and she was a counter clerk who had worked beside four people for eleven years and had not said one word to any of the four of them about the morning she had spent at a kitchen table.

She said good morning to him on the way past and did not stop walking, and Isolde Marchetti watched her go by and did not say anything at all about it.

He had about four seconds in which he could have stood in front of that woman and said a name, and he did not take them, because a man who says a name to a clerk on a corridor gets it entered, and he has had one document entered on him in this city in five months and it had been enough.

Then the bundle went up the stone stair, and it did not go up it in a hand that carried it to a counter on a floor of this building. It went up it the way a thing goes up a stair when it is going to a division with no floor in this building, on the ordinary stream, at four in the afternoon, about a set of premises on a street everybody in this city can walk down.

"You saw it," said Isolde Marchetti.

"I saw the wrapper."

"You saw the routing and the routing goes up and there is no floor up there, and there has not been since before either of us, and that is a thing you knew in January and did not know in January was going to be a piece of paper coming past us in this corridor on the twenty-fifth of March." She turned round.

"And a belief that was handed over in nineteen minutes on the first of January is being worked on this month by somebody who has never met me."

"That is not a thing I can stop by standing on a stair."

"No," said Isolde Marchetti. "It is a thing you can stop by never telling them which pair of hands, and I told them the pair of hands on the third of January in a kitchen in front of seven other women, because I do sums out loud."

They went down the stone stair together, and at the bottom of it she said, "There. That is what my gift is for and what it is against, in one afternoon, and you may have it for nothing because you did not ask me."

Five days to the last working day of the quarter, and a strip had gone up a stair about eight women at a kitchen table, and nobody in the building had asked anybody anything.
