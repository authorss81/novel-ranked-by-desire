# The Fifth Sheet Of Plain Paper

He had four sheets of plain paper on the table at eight o'clock on the morning of the twelfth of January and eight women watching him and no reason on earth to be frightened of a pen.

He was frightened of a pen.

It took him forty minutes. It is the fastest he has ever written anything that mattered and it is the only one he has ever written at his own table with the author of it sitting four feet away being read by eight people.

## What He Wrote

The paper was the same. Four sheets of plain paper, bought in the same shop on the same street on the same morning, because he had gone and bought them on the tenth and had not let anybody come with him.

He squared the first one and wrote at the top of it.

*I, Lin Hao, at premises 34-6-118, the head of those premises.*

Then he left a line and wrote the next thing.

*This is a declaration of holding.*

Then he left another line, because a thing of this kind with the headings run together looks like a form and he wanted it to look like a man.

*The account of thirty years standing at premises 34-6-118, being the residue of an unadministered account, is held by me. The figure on the line is seven. It was dated the sixteenth of September, initialled and not signed.*

Then the fourth line, which he wrote more slowly than the other three.

*I have not paid it. It has not been produced. There is no production off those premises and there never has been.*

And then at the foot of the page, because he was not going to write on the back of anything, his name and the date.

*Lin Hao. The twelfth of January.*

That was the whole of it. Four lines, a heading, a name and a date, on about a third of one sheet.

## Why There Is No Reason On It

He put the pen down and did not look up, because there were eight women in the room and he could feel what the room was doing.

"There is no reason clause."

"You have written that three times in two months and you have got very good at it," said Sun Meiling. "The description on the twenty-seventh of October. The four sheets on the twenty-ninth. This."

"Yes."

"Explain it to me as though I am a clerk at a counter who has never met you." She said it in the voice she used when she was giving him the chance to be right in front of witnesses. "Why is there nothing under the address."

"Because a clerk reads a reason clause in four seconds and a whole page in a year." He said it the way he had said it on the twenty-ninth of December, in the same words, because it had been true then and he had checked it since.

"There is one reason clause in this business with nobody's name on it, it is the only one anybody in this city wants, and I am not going to hand a room four seconds of the one thing I have never said out loud in an instrument that gets copied eight times and served at six desks."

"And if there is no reason, somebody in a room will write one."

"Somebody in a room will write one in about four years and it will be wrong, and I will be alive to be annoyed by it." He turned the pen over once. "That is the cheaper of the two."

## The Thing Nobody Did

Not one of them touched the paper.

That is the sentence the chapter of the twenty-sixth of November established and this is the second morning on which it has been true in front of the same eight people, and it was truer this time.

On the twenty-seventh of November he had been writing a description of his own house and four false slips had been offered and burned. This time the paper was one sheet and there was no false version of it anywhere in the city, because a false version would have to be written by somebody, and the moment somebody wrote it they would be on it.

Petra Ilyin had her arms crossed the whole way through and did not uncross them.

Two of the eight women cried.

He did not see it as a thing that happened at a table.

He saw one of them get up and go to the window and stand with her back to the room for about a minute, and he saw the other one sit absolutely still with her hand over her mouth and her eyes streaming and not making a sound, and he did not look at either of them for longer than he could help, because he had learned in November that looking at a woman who is crying in that house is a way of making it about him.

He did not know afterwards which one had gone to the window. He has never asked. He has been in that kitchen twice since, on the fourteenth and on the nineteenth, and he has watched both of them both times and he has never once been able to say which.

He signed it and put the pen in the tray and nobody said a word.

## The Difference Between Two Kinds Of Paper

At about half past nine he stood up and put his hand flat on the top sheet, not on the writing.

"I want to say what this is, because two women in this room are going to be asked about it in about four years and one of them is not going to be able to answer, and I would rather she had heard me say it."

He looked round the table.

"The four sheets of the twenty-ninth described me. This one does not describe anything. This one says that I hold it. That is the whole of the difference and it took me four years to get to the other side of it."

"Go on," said Isolde Marchetti.

"The description made me a person, so that a paper could not be laid on a door without landing on somebody. Four sheets and eight hands and I cannot be the address again in a document, and that was the right thing and I would do it again."

He lifted his hand off the paper.

"This makes me answerable. Every paper from today that touches that account has to carry my name at the top of it, and not because anybody made me and not because a clerk wrote it down, because I wrote it down at this table on the twelfth of January at about nine in the morning."

"That is the price," said Sun Meiling.

"It is not the price. The price was the twenty-ninth." He said it without any drama in it at all, because she had taught him in November that there is a difference between a price and a bill and that a man who cannot tell them apart will get them confused in front of eight people. "The price is gone. This is the bill. The bill is different, and the difference is that the bill has my name on it and the price had eight other women's."

## The Foot Of The Page

He got to the foot of the sheet at about ten past nine and stopped with the pen about an inch above it.

The first four had eight words at the foot of the last one. He had gone at that for about nine seconds on the twenty-ninth of December and had written them.

*I am the man at the top of this page and I will answer for it.*

There was a space at the bottom of this sheet about the same size.

He put the pen in the tray without writing anything in it.

Lin Hao, who is a man who cannot be requisitioned and who has had four years to think about the difference between a document that makes you findable and a document that makes you answer for it, left the foot of the fifth sheet of plain paper blank on purpose on the morning of the twelfth of January, in a kitchen on Ferrier Street, in front of eight women.

When Sun Meiling looked at the space and then at him, he said it out loud, because on the twenty-sixth of December eight women had watched him fail at an ordinary sentence and this time he was going to get the whole of it.

"A declaration is not an attestation," he said. "And there is nowhere on it for a man to say he will answer for it."

Four of them understood at once and two of them did not and one of those two was Beatrix Halm, who wrote it down anyway, which was the correct thing to do and the whole of what she was for.

Nadia Ferrous said, "That is mean."

"That is not mean. That is the only reason I can put my hand on it." He squared the sheet. "If I write those eight words at the foot of this, then this sheet becomes an attestation, and an attestation is a thing eight people sign under, and then this morning was the twenty-ninth of December again and nothing has happened since. I am not giving that room a second instrument that can be got at by anybody with a folder."

"I am giving it one thing. It names me and it does not say that anybody else has vouched for me, and in four years, when somebody asks who put this in the world, the answer is a man who wrote it at his own table and nobody else knows anything about it."

Nobody put their name under it. That was not a rule. There was no rule about it and there was no box for it, and the sheet went into his coat at about a quarter to ten with eight women watching him do it.

On the fourteenth of January it goes into the keeping of a room at the end of a corridor. He knows the hour. Five minutes to eleven.
