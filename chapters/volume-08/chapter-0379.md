# The Standing Of Nine People

A requisition that is laid and not carried does not lie still, and the thing that keeps it moving is the worst instrument in this city, because it is not dramatic and it is signed by nobody.

On the morning of the eighth of March the clerk at the second table had a printed sheet in front of her with nine ruled lines on it and a heading at the top, and the heading was the standing of the room, and the sheet was the first of its kind for that room, and it went out that afternoon in the ordinary way with a copy to every division holding a part of it.

He was let in at about ten minutes past ten and read the heading over her shoulder and then asked the only question worth asking in that building.

"How often."

"Weekly," said the clerk. "For as long as it is live. A live requisition is administered, and administration is not a decision and nobody votes on it and it takes about four seconds a week and it does not stop."

## Nine Lines

She read the nine out, because she read everything out, and she read them the way she reads a form at a counter to somebody who has not done it before.

One, two and three were in the room and could put a seal on a sheet.

Four said *vacant*, and had said *vacant* since the twelfth of February, because a man had laid a plain sheet on the table under a window on that morning and the clerk had entered it at eleven minutes to nine and it had gone out on the stream at four like every other paper that enters that room.

Five was recused on her own signature.

Six had withdrawn her own seal at thirteen minutes on the twenty-third of December.

Seven had not been in that room since the twenty-third of December.

Eight and nine were in the room and could put a seal on a sheet.

"That is three," said the clerk.

"That is three," said Anouk Ferreira, from the chair, without turning round. "It has been three since the first of November and it will be three on the thirtieth of March. Five of nine would carry it. Write three."

## The Box

But the sheet had a tenth line under the nine, and the tenth line was not a name, and he had read the first four lines of that form five times now and had never once been shown what was under them.

It was headed *composition of the holding*, and under it there was a space the width of four figures with a rule round it, and the rule was empty.

"A requisition names a holding," said the clerk. "A holding in this case is an account, and an account is carried at a composition, and the composition is certified at the address by the officer who keeps that address, in her own division, in writing. The last certificate at that address is the thirtieth of November. It is four months old."

"It is correct."

"It is correct and it is four months old," said the clerk, "and a live requisition cannot be administered against a certificate that old, because the whole point of administering it weekly is that a thing may have changed, and if the thing has changed and nobody has looked, then the return is a return on what was true in November and there is a person in this building in four years who will read it as a finding about today."

"So somebody has to certify the composition again."

"Somebody has to certify the composition again, and it has to be counter-signed at the address, because a composition certified at a distance by a division is a division's opinion about a house and not a count, and there has been exactly one instrument in this city for thirty years that turns an opinion into a count and it is a woman at an address writing a figure next to a rule." She squared the sheet. "I will write it out at eleven o'clock. It goes out at four."

## What He Could Not Ask For

"Chair."

"Say it fast."

"Recall it," said Lin Hao. "Do not send the tenth line. Send nine lines and a heading and let the certificate stand where it stood in November."

The clerk put the pen down across the book.

"I cannot do that and I would like you to understand that I am not refusing you."

"Chair—"

"You have said the word to me and I am going to tell you what it means and then it is finished." The clerk's voice did not change at all, because it never did.

"A subject served with a requisition may demand production. He may not demand a recall. That is not a rule this room made and it is not a rule I enforce; there is nothing here for me to enforce."

"A recall is a thing the chair may do once, and there is a book at the end of this corridor where the recalls are, and it has eleven pages in it and none of them are filled in since nineteen ninety-one."

He looked at the chair.

"I am not going to do it," said Anouk Ferreira. "Not because you asked and not because you did not."

"If I recall an administration of that sheet on the morning of the eighth of March then in about four years there is a minute in this room in which a subject asked this room to unsend its own paperwork, and that is the single worst document I have ever seen in twenty years and I have seen the one from the seventeenth of December, which came back with an instrument on it."

She had not moved.

"You have had five documents out of this room since December," she said. "You may ask for one more. You may not ask for one fewer."

## Eleven Streets

He left at about ten past eleven and he was at the north end of the square at twenty past one. The green card was up and the figure on it was his, and there was no paint over it anywhere, and he read the wall the way he has read it a hundred and forty times, and it said nothing about anybody.

The card had gone up on the thirtieth of December. This was the tenth week of that silence and it was the longest he had been a man on the floor in seven years.

Then he walked eleven streets in about nine minutes, with a back he has had since a washroom on the fourth floor went wrong in the autumn, and a back like that is not a thing a man walks eleven streets in nine minutes on. He got there at ten past four and he was the wrong side of it by a minute and a half and there was nothing on earth he could do about that.

The cage is behind a grille on the left of the doors and there are about forty hooks in it and every paper that goes into that building on the ordinary stream goes into that cage at four in the afternoon, and there is one door key and it is on the inside.

A clerk had signed for the bundle. There was a signature and an hour against it and the hour was four.

It was not the certificate. The certificate was in a different envelope and it was going to a public post at the end of a woman's name in a building off a third court, and there was a woman on a desk there who would put it in front of her and wait while she read it.

This was the Vestry's own copy of a return, going into a cage at four in the afternoon, in the ordinary way, because the Vestry is one of the six divisions that keeps a list and has always been one of the six.

He put his hand flat on the grille and the grille did not do anything, because a grille is a thing that opens from the other side and there was not one instrument in this city that would open it from this side.

He stood there about a minute and a half with the ache coming up out of his back the way it does, and then he went home and did not tell the table any of it.

## What Was In It

The tenth line had been entered at eleven o'clock and it had gone out at four with the rest of the sheet, and the certificate goes out to a division in a second sub-level and is countersigned at the address, and the countersignature is the part that matters.

A count that a division certifies about a house is a division's opinion. A count that a division certifies and a person at the house confirms, in writing, with a figure against a rule, is a composition, and a composition is what an account is carried at and what a requisition is administered against and what a called line is judged by in a cold room two floors under a sales floor.

There is no instrument in this city that is cheaper than that, and there is no instrument in this city that is harder to object to, and there is no person anywhere in Ostara who can stop it once it has been asked for, because a person keeping an address may state it.

And the countersignature is not going to be asked of the officer who keeps the address.

It is going to be asked at premises 34-6-118, of the person at the head of it, on a printed form with a rule in it and a box, and the form will be delivered to a public post at the end of her name in a building off a third court, and a clerk in that building will carry it to her desk and put it down in front of her and wait while she reads it.

Sun Meiling is not on a roll. She is on a list of persons keeping an address and there is no roll in this city she is on.

She has been under a permanent bar since the first of September, she cannot be sworn, and six weeks ago a form with a box on it had been put in front of her and she had answered it in four minutes of her own time and a word in capitals.

And the answer had gone into a book in a building with a man on the third floor of it.

There was a woman at a counter in the Vestry arcade who had offered an address on the fourteenth of February and had been asked for forty-eight hours and had given him until the morning of the twentieth, and the twentieth had come and gone, and she had not done it.

He had thought for four months that the cheapest thing in Ostara was a woman giving up an address on purpose.

It was a box on a printed sheet, and it was going to be delivered to a desk at about nine the next morning, and she was going to be asked a question about her own house by a clerk who had no idea what he was carrying.
