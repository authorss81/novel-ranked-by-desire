# The Sitting Called For The Eleventh

The room at the end of the corridor had nine chairs round a table under a window and a second table by the door with a pen on it, and at nine o'clock on the morning of the twentieth of February the woman at that second table turned her book round and left it face up, so that anybody coming through the door could read the first line of it before they got as far as the table.

*The eleventh. Requisition. Subject: the account of thirty years standing at premises 34-6-118.*

Nine minutes is the standing time to read a sheet, to question it and to decide it, and it is nine minutes because a room of nine has done this for twenty years and has not found that anybody could do it in less without deciding it badly, and Anouk Ferreira has never once let a room run over, in her own words, because a room that runs over is a room that has not read the thing.

He was let in at about five minutes to ten and he was put where a subject is put, which is at the near end on a chair that is not one of the nine and has no seal in the side of it.

## The Fourth Part Was Already Written

He had not been in the room since the sixth of February and the first thing he saw was that the fourth part of the sheet had a ground written on it.

Somebody had written it in a clerk's hand in the space between the figure and the ruled box on the fifth line, and the ruling of the box had been done last, which meant whoever entered the ground did it after the form was complete, which is the ordinary way and is what a clerk does all day.

It was not his name on it.

It was a description. A description of premises 34-6-118 made on the twenty-seventh of October at about eleven in the morning, against the head of the premises and not against the address, with the day on it and the hour on it and the division that held it.

He read it twice.

"How long has the fourth part been written."

"Since the second of February," said the clerk.

"Who brought it."

The clerk looked at the chair.

## Nobody Had Told Him

"The division that holds a description of those premises entered it on the second of February and the desk it was entered from is the counter-forensics desk," said the clerk, "and it came into this room on the stream that afternoon, and it was entered in the keeping book on the sixth, and it has been standing in my drawer since the sixth of February in the ordinary way, waiting for a sitting, because that is what a ground is for."

She did not look at anybody.

He looked down the table at the second seat and Wren Kallo was reading the sheet in front of her with her hands flat on it in the manner of a woman reading an instrument, and she had not looked up once since he had come into the room.

He had asked her about nothing on the sixth of February. He had not asked her about the address. He could not have asked her about the address on any day since the sixth of December, and there is a standing instrument that says so in one sentence, and she gave him that sentence once, on the sixth of December, and has not given it to him since.

And she had gone into a division on the second of February and found the only paper in Ostara that would stand in the fourth part of a requisition against a man she cannot be asked about, and had put it into the ordinary stream at four in the afternoon, and had entered it in a keeping book on the sixth, and had not told one single person.

"The description says the premises have a head," said Runa Sato, reading down the table. "That is the ground. A thing taken out of a head has to be taken out of something that has a head, or there is nothing to take it out of."

"That is the ground," said Anouk Ferreira. "Anybody may object to the ground."

## One Sentence

Nobody objected.

He sat at the near end on a chair that is not one of the nine, and the standing time is nine minutes, and there was one sentence available to him and he had known it was there since the sixth of February.

He was going to stand up and say that a ground cannot be the document that began the business, and that a description made on the twenty-seventh of October is the reason this sheet was ever drafted, and that a paper which is the origin of a requisition is not a reason for it, and that the fourth part is therefore empty and the sheet cannot be carried.

It is not even a difficult sentence. It is about nineteen words and it has been available to him since the sixth, and there are four people in this city who could have drafted it better, and one of them is at this table, and one of them is recused on her own signature, and one of them is in two rooms above a chandler's off Cordwain and does not come any more.

If he said it, the woman at the door would strike the fourth part. The nine minutes would run out on a sheet nobody at that table could carry, and nothing whatever would be taken off him or off anybody else, and the sheet would lie where it lay until the thirtieth and then go quietly across into the next quarter, where nobody would be in any hurry about it.

He looked at the description on the sheet in front of him.

It was a good piece of paper. That was the thing that made it impossible. A description of premises 34-6-118 made against the head of them rather than against the door, in a clerk's hand, on a kitchen table, at about eleven in the morning on the twenty-seventh of October, and it said that the thing at that address was a person, and it had been the only thing standing between him and a door ever since.

## What He Did

The clerk put the time in at the seventh minute.

"Ground entered at the seventh minute," she said. "That gives two."

Two is not a majority and the chair knew it and the room knew it and nobody said it.

The sheet went down in the middle of the table with the four parts written in and the fifth line ruled and the ruled box empty with a clerk's tick in the corner of it and nothing at all in it, because a tick is a thing somebody went round a room with a pen and checked, and he had checked it in December, and it had been empty then, and it was empty now, and it was never going to have anything in it at all.

"It goes into the quarter," said Anouk Ferreira. "Laid, called, served and heard. Not carried. Nothing has been taken off anybody and nothing will be taken off anybody before the thirtieth, and I am going to have that written in the minute in those words because somebody in this room is going to read this minute in about four years and I want them to read it in the right order."

"It is written," said the clerk.

## What It Cost

He came out of the room at about eleven minutes past ten with nothing in his hands and walked down the corridor and stood at the window at the end of it for a while.

The ground was in the minute now. A ground is in the minute and a minute is copied out and a copy is kept for four years, and the ground was a description made on the twenty-seventh of October at about eleven in the morning at a kitchen table in a building on Ferrier Street, in front of eight women, by a clerk called Imrie, on the strength of four words a man said to her and an hour she entered against them.

So the price of the twentieth of February was not his.

The price was that in about four years there will be a document in a room that says eight women were sitting at a table in their own house at eleven in the morning on the twenty-seventh of October while a clerk made a description of them, and that the reason given was *because it is cheaper*, and that the description is now the fourth part of a requisition that would take the account of thirty years out of the house they were sitting in.

And there is no version of the minute in which any of those eight women is a stranger to it.

One of them certified a composition at a desk on the thirtieth of November and signed it with her own name. One of them cannot be sworn and is line one on a page a man wrote in a kitchen. One of them has a notebook with five empty lines under five times in nine years.

And one of them has a room that agrees to what she says in it, and four offices in this city have asked her in writing for a thing she could put on a return, and she has never once given them one, because the city has no sheet for what she does and four offices have now spent eleven years trying to invent one.

And one of them put the ground in on the second of February and has not said one word about it since, and is sitting at that table now reading a sheet she has read before.

He had had one sentence.

He had had nineteen words and seven minutes and four people who could have said it better, and he had sat on a chair that is not one of the nine at the near end of a table and said nothing at all.

The last quiet he had was a sheet nobody in that room had got round to grounding, and he had spent every hour of it to buy a question.

## Why He Bought It

He stood at the end of that corridor and worked out why, because he wanted to be certain of it before he went down sixty-one steps and could not be certain afterwards.

He wanted a paper.

That was the whole of it, and it had been the whole of it since the sixth of February, when a woman read a sheet to him in a room under a window and said *a paper can be asked for*, and then said that you can put a paper in a room and you cannot take one out of it, and had been right.

He had sat there and understood that he was being taught the one thing nobody in this city had ever thought to teach anybody.

A ground cannot be a sentence. That is why a ground has to be a paper, and a paper is a thing that can be put in front of nine people at a table and produced at a later sitting by whoever asks for it, and asking for it is the ordinary right of a man who has been served.

Nobody in that room has ever asked for a ground. Not in twenty years, not on this account or any other, because in twenty years nobody in that room has ever wanted the paper their own sheet was standing on.

He had bought the right to ask for one piece of paper and he had paid for it with a description of his own house made at his own table by a clerk he had never spoken to, and he would have made the same trade again on the seventh minute of any morning in the city.

Then he went down to the sixty-one.

The description was in the second sub-level of the Classification Wing, in a division that has been served a copy of a declaration of holding at four in the afternoon on the nineteenth of January, and that has never sent anybody to say whether it opened it, and that he cannot ask, because the woman who signed the thing is a woman he put one question to on the sixth of December and received a standing answer for.
