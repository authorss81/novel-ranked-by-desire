# The Address In Her Own Hand

He was on the fifth floor of the Fourth Branch at nine o'clock in the morning of the thirteenth of March with a mop in his hand and a queue of about nine men in the washroom behind him waiting for the door, and he had been told in a doorway the previous afternoon where to be and he was here instead, and the distance between those two facts was about eleven hundred yards and four months.

He did the fifth floor. He did the fourth. He ate something standing up out of the staff kettle at about eleven because Beatrix Halm had left bread on the table with her hand on it and that is the whole of what that woman has ever communicated by post.

At about half past four he went up the sixty-one and Nadia Ferrous was at the table with her coat still on and a piece of paper in front of her, and it was face up, and it was not the shape of anything that came out of this house.

## Nine Forty-One

The ground floor of the Vestry has four counters along the wall under a window that does not open, and two of them belong to the clinic and the other two belong to the city, and the second of the city's two takes about nine hundred people a day past it whether anybody wants anything or not.

She had been in the queue for eleven minutes and had made a decision about the queue at about four minutes, which was to get to the front of it and say so in the ordinary voice, and which had worked, and she had then decided to stop at the second of the two and had gone to the first and come back.

The clerk had done the job about two years and she had a card on the wall with the rules printed on it in a frame, and she read the card and not the woman's face, because that is what the card is for.

"Premises."

"Thirty-four, dash, six, dash, one-one-eight. Ferrier Street."

"You keep that address."

"I have kept it for six years. I do not keep it any more."

The clerk wrote. Then she stopped and went up the card with her finger, which is a thing she had done perhaps four thousand times.

"You are not the head of the household."

"No."

"Then it goes on you and not on the premises, and it comes out of the composition of the address by one, and it does not stop anybody at that address from holding it themselves." She looked up for the first time. "It does not affect the head. The head is still the head."

"I understand."

"You will get a slip. It is a day and an hour and it is all of it and there is nothing else on it anywhere in this city." She wrote again. "Nine-forty-one."

"And it cannot be undone."

"It cannot be undone by you and it cannot be undone by anybody else and there is no instrument for it and there is no office you can go to." The clerk put the pen down. "It goes on the list at four this afternoon and the list goes out to every desk in the city that keeps one, and there are six desks and you are not on any of them after today."

"After today I am not on it."

"You may take it up again in four years and not one day before, and that is printed on the card and it is the same for everybody."

## What It Is On Paper

A slip about two inches by one, cream card, with a rule printed across it and a stamp at the left-hand end.

*Address given up. Premises 34-6-118. Nadia Ferrous. Thirteenth day of March. Nine forty-one.*

Five items. No figure of anything. Nothing on it about a composition, because a composition is not entered at a counter by a person giving up an address; the composition is a count, and the count is made by the woman who sleeps at the address and countersigned there, and the counter had simply taken one person off a list that a division somewhere keeps.

The only figure on it was the hour.

"Forty-one," said Nadia Ferrous. "That is the whole of it. I have thought about that slip for four months and it is a day and an hour and my name, and in four years the only question anybody can ask about it is what was happening in this building at nine forty-one on the thirteenth of March, and the answer to that question is that a woman got to the front of a queue."

She put it down on the table under the window where the papers went.

## A Hundred And Seventy-Seven Days

The table did the arithmetic, because that is what the table is for.

"The priced line is the sixteenth of September," said Nadia Ferrous. "That is seven, and it carries your initial and not my name, and it has been carried into fourteen places in four divisions and into books that went to a guild table on the morning of the sixteenth of November."

"Which was not my name either."

"No," she said. "The figure was brought to me on the sixteenth of September and initialled by the man who brought it on his way out of my rooms at about half past eleven in the morning and I have not said one word about that in five months and I am not going to start tonight."

Isolde Marchetti did the sum out loud, because she did sums out loud and had never once been thanked for it and had stopped expecting to be.

"Fourteen days to the end of September. Thirty for October. Thirty for November. Thirty for December. Thirty for January. Thirty for February. Thirteen more."

She put her finger on the cloth.

"One hundred and seventy-seven days," said Isolde Marchetti. "Two figures, disagreeing, for a hundred and seventy-seven days. And from an hour ago they are the same figure and neither of us has done anything clever. Nadia went to a counter."

## The Card On The Wall

The rule is printed on a card in a frame above the counter and it has been there longer than the clerk at it, and she can read it without looking at it, and she reads it out for him anyway because it is the first time in about two years that anybody has stood at that counter and asked her what the words mean.

*An address kept by a person ceases to be kept by that person on the day and hour entered at any counter in this city. It may be taken up again by that person after four years and not before.*

"None of that needs me," she said, "and none of that needs you, and I have read it out because you have been stood there looking at the frame for long enough and I did not want you doing that on your own."

"Thank you."

"Do not thank me. I am reading a card." She put her pen in the drawer. "Nine forty-one is written. It goes out at four. Go and do whatever it is you are going to do at four."

## What It Costs And Who Pays It

The list of the thirtieth of October had said eight, it went out of four doors on the afternoon it was made, and there is no office in this city that can withdraw it or call it wrong. Down the corridor, in the keeping of a room where nine people sit, a sheet of plain paper carries eight signatures under it and a gap two fingers wide.

Seven people now lived at premises 34-6-118.

"That is the bill," said Nadia Ferrous.

"It was the bill in February and it is the bill tonight and it has not got smaller because I have done it, and I would like it entered that it has not got smaller, because in about four years somebody is going to find a piece of paper that says eight and a counter that says seven and they are going to assume one of them is a lie, and the lie will be the counter, because a counter is cheap and a piece of paper is not."

"I am not going to be thanked for this," she said, "and I have told the table that in February, and I am telling it again because Beatrix is going to want to write it down and Beatrix must not write it down."

"I am not writing it," said Beatrix Halm.

## Eight Of Them

They read it in ones and twos between about half past five and seven in the evening and it is a slip about two inches by one and there is not much to take in.

Sun Meiling read it first and put it down in the same place and did not say anything, which from Sun Meiling is a whole speech and which Lin Hao had learned to read somewhere in October.

Beatrix Halm read it twice and did not open the notebook.

Petra Ilyin asked one question, which was whether the counter was a public counter or a private one, and Nadia Ferrous told her it was a public counter with a card in a frame and a woman who had read the card out to a stranger for nothing, and Petra Ilyin said *good* and nothing else.

Wren Kallo did not read it until about seven. She read it standing at the table with her hand flat on the cloth beside it and then she said, "That is an ordinary act," and then she said, "and it is the first ordinary act anybody at this table has done in five months," and then she sat down and did not look at it again.

Agnete Lindqvist did not come to the table at all. Beatrix Halm carried it up to her at about half past seven and brought it back and said that Agnete Lindqvist had read it and had given it back and had asked for it to be put on her own table rather than the common one, because of course she had.

Nobody said anything about the composition going to seven until Isolde Marchetti said it out loud at about a quarter to seven, and she said it as a sum and not as a feeling, and then Runa Sato did the sum back at her, and the two of them argued about it for about nine minutes and were both of them right.

## After

He read the slip on the table under the window at about a quarter to six on the evening of the thirteenth of March and it took him about four seconds, and he did not pick it up, because a piece of paper that has been on a table in a house since half past four has been looked at by everybody at that table and picking it up would have been a thing he did.

Premises 34-6-118 had stood at seven since the thirteenth of March and the priced line of the sixteenth of September said seven, and for about eight hours the two figures had been one figure.

Eighteen days to the last working day of the quarter.

He had been told in a doorway on the afternoon before that a carried line gets administered by somebody he did not appoint, in her own hand, in her own division, four times a year, and that he did not know which of the eight it was going to be, and he had had eight hours to guess and had guessed wrong twice and had been right once.

He went down the sixty-one at about ten past seven with no idea what to do with any of it, which was the state he had been in for five months, and which had never before been a state he was in about a piece of paper that was his own.
