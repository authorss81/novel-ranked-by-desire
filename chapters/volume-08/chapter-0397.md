# The Reason She Told It At Last

She came in off the doors at about half past six on the evening of the twenty-seventh of March with her coat still on and did not sit down, which is a thing Wren Kallo has done four times since October and never once for a reason she gave out loud.

Three days ago a woman had stood at a counter on the second floor and been made to read four words back twice.

"Sit down," said Lin Hao.

"I am going to stand for about a minute and then I am going to sit down, and the minute is not for you." She put her bag down against the leg of the table. "And I am going to say one thing first and then I am going to take my coat off, and if you have any of the three questions you have been not asking me since October in your mouth, this is the minute in which to put them away."

## Eighty-Seven Days

"I decided on the night of the thirtieth of December."

She said it flatly and without an explanation and the table went still in the particular way it goes still.

"You decided what."

"What I was going to have to do." She put her eyes on the cloth and not on anybody at it. "You know what I said to you that night. I said I was going to have to tell somebody and that I had not chosen anybody and that I had had since a quarter past six that morning and a card on a wall. That is the sentence. I have been carrying it since."

"Eighty-seven days," said Isolde Marchetti, from the end of the table, doing sums out loud because that is what she does with a number that has arrived in a room.

"Eighty-seven days," said Wren Kallo, "and I did it on the first of January, in nineteen minutes, and it is done, and it has not got any worse in eighty-seven days, and it got worse at about eleven o'clock on the twenty-fourth of March."

## The Woman She Was Talking About

"Mrs Imrie," said Nadia Ferrous.

"Mrs Imrie."

The kitchen was quiet.

"I gave them a shape," said Wren Kallo.

"That is the whole of what I handed over on the first of January and it is accurate and it is the only thing I gave anybody."

"Two documents about one set of eight women at one door, made inside a month by one pair of hands."

"One pair of hands."

"Eight women."

"One door."

"In that order, and in no other order, and I said the order out loud twice because I was told in nineteen minutes it would be read back to somebody in about four years by somebody in a hurry."

"That is the shape," said Lin Hao.

"That is the shape. And a shape with no hands on it is a rumour, and an office does not keep rumours, and it has spent two months and one belief turning the shape into a finding, and the only document that office can reach is the one made on the morning of the twenty-seventh of October at a kitchen table by a clerk called Imrie on four words I did not write and could not have predicted." She took one of her sleeves.

"And it has reached her. Not me. Her."

"You said on the third of January that an office cannot reach a woman on a list."

"I said it can only reach the building she is in," said Wren Kallo.

"And that was the cleverest thing I have ever said in my life and it was the sentence that did it. They wrote to the house. They cannot write to me, because I am an officer and an officer is served at a post, and so they wrote to the head of a household at premises 34-6-118 and required the person who made the entry to attend at the address the entry describes."

Nobody said anything at all.

"So it cost her," said Wren Kallo. "Not in nineteen minutes on the first of January. In eleven minutes on the twenty-fourth, at a counter on the second floor, where she read four words back to a man and then a question under them and then her own answer back, and went home, and is the woman who read it. I said that on the third of January to seven people in this kitchen and I was right and I have been carrying being right since."

## What She Is Not Going To Do

"You are not going to tell me who you told."

"No."

"You told me on the third of January that you were not going to tell me the office and you have kept that for eighty-four days."

"I have kept it for eighty-four days and I am going to keep it on the twenty-eighth of March as well."

She put her cup down on the table, not hard.

"I want the whole of this room to understand that the pair of facts have been sitting on this table nine times since January and that neither one of them has ever moved. I have never once been asked and you have never once asked. And in about four years one of the eight women at this table is going to say the wrong thing about it badly, and it is not going to be me."

She dropped her coat over the back of the single chair at the end of the table and sat down in it.

"You worked out on the nineteenth what a document with a name at the foot of it is," she said.

"You said it out loud in this room and then you went downstairs and wrote four lines on a docket with your name under them ninety seconds after promising a woman with no gift that you would write the floor nowhere. You have carried that for eight days and you have not mentioned it to one person, and I am not going to mention it either, and we are both going to pretend for about a minute that I have not just counted it out loud."

Sun Meiling put a cup down in front of Wren Kallo, and Sun Meiling did not look at her while she did it, and Wren Kallo did not look at Sun Meiling, and neither of them moved, and it was the second time in eighty-seven days that a thing had happened at that table which was going to be told to nobody.

## The Thing She Is Asking For

"Three days," said Lin Hao. "What happens in this room on the thirtieth is that a number I have never been able to write down goes out of the room at four in the afternoon, and a woman at a desk off a third court is given a printed sheet with a box on it, and I do not know one single thing about whether she will fill it in."

"She will fill it in," said Wren Kallo. "She will fill it in because she certifies a composition every quarter and has never once in eleven years failed to do it, and that is not a fault and I am not going to let anybody in this room call it one."

"Then you know what is on the sheet."

"I know what is on the sheet because the fourth block of a carry-forward is printed off a certificate and I have been reading what that certificate says since the morning of the fourteenth of March, and I know what the next three of those look like because they are the same form." She said it to the table and not to him, and then she said the last of it to him.

"And you have not asked me for any of it since the fourteenth of March and I want you to know that I noticed that as well."

## Eight O'Clock

The others went up about half past eight. Nadia Ferrous went last and did not say anything at the door, which is not like her, and he noticed it and she knew he noticed it and neither of them mentioned it.

"Are you going to say thank you for coming down sixty-one steps."

"No," said Wren Kallo. "I came down sixty-one steps and I have been in this room for two hours and there are eight women in this house who would have spoken to me in February and there are eight women in this house who have spoken to me tonight because of four words a man said at a table in October, and I have not had that for about nine years."

"You are not asking me for anything."

"I am not asking you for anything, and I am going to say the arrangement out loud so that you cannot pretend later that you did not know it."

She stood up and squared her bag on the table.

"There is a sheet I wrote on the thirtieth of December in a clerk's hand and it is in the bag on that chair and it has been in my bag for eighty-seven days. I am going to take it upstairs and I am going to cross out one of the five things on it and I am going to tell you which one and why before anything else happens tomorrow."

"What time."

"Eight," said Wren Kallo. "Not here. There is a table in this room with eight women's names at the foot of the eight words on it and a piece of paper with a man's name at the foot of that, and I am not doing it under either of them."

"Where."

"Your rooms," said Wren Kallo. "I have never been in them. I have stood on your landing eleven times since September and I have never once been past the door, and I have been curious about them for five months, and I am not going to tell you a single reason why, because there is no reason, and a woman who gives you a reason is selling you one."

She took her bag off the chair.

"It is not a reward and it is not a price and it is not for you," she said, from the bottom of the stair. "If you turn up at eight o'clock tomorrow believing that it is one of those two things, I will take the sheet back and we will not do it, and I would like you to decide tonight, on your own, upstairs, which of the three you can actually manage."

Eighty-seven days since the night of the thirtieth of December, three days to the last working day of the quarter, and about nine hours of the morning of the twenty-eighth that had no name on it at all.
