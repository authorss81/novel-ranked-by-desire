# The Page With One Direction

The docket was the kind that comes with a delivery of anything at all, stiff brown card, a printed box for an order number, and it had been on the table under the window since the sixteenth because somebody had opened tea on it.

He wrote on the back of it at about half past five in the morning of the nineteenth of March with a carpenter's pencil off the shelf by the door, standing up, in the kitchen, with nobody else in the house.

*Lin Wei. The second of a month. Three years ago. Seventh floor. Up.*

Three lines. Then he stood there for about two minutes with the pencil not put down.

Then he wrote a fourth line, because a piece of paper about a man in a room is a story until somebody is standing behind it, and there was one person in this city who had actually stood in front of the woman who wrote it and been told it was true.

Without a name under it, it was a rumour with a floor on it, and he had spent the whole of his working life understanding exactly what a rumour with a floor on it is worth.

*L. Hao. From the intake desk, Ninth Terrace, the eighteenth of March.*

He left it face down under the cloth, which is not hiding it, because eight women live under that roof and every one of them comes down that stair before seven.

## His Own Name

Beatrix Halm found it at about ten past six and read it and put it back down and went and got her coat and went to work, and she did not come back to it before she left, which from Beatrix Halm was a decision and not a failure to notice.

He had not expected to be asked about it and he was not, and by about half past seven in the evening everybody in that kitchen had read it and none of them had said anything to him about it, which was worse.

## One Direction

He said it to the table at about half past eight and he said it in one sentence because it was the only thing in it that was new.

"There are forty-one names in that book and every one of them went up. Not one page in twenty years records anybody coming down."

"That is a horrible thing to be told over your tea," said Isolde Marchetti.

"It is not a horror, it is a book keeping its own count." He put his cup down. "Forty-one people went up a stair and were not brought down and a woman wrote it down in a small hand because somebody has to. And nobody has ever come to read it. She said that in an empty hall as though it were a weather."

## What It Cost

Wren Kallo looked at it for about four seconds and then she said the one thing she was going to say about it and she said it standing, which is how she says the things she means.

"That is a document," she said. "It is not a note and it is not a story and it is not something a woman said to a man in a hall."

"It is a piece of paper with three facts on one side and a man's name on the other, and a document with a name on it can be produced, and it can be produced by anybody who finds it, and the only person in this city who can stop that happening is the person who put his name to it."

"I know what it is."

"You know what it is and you have written it on the back of a delivery docket in your own front room and put it under a cloth. In about four years there will be a stranger in a corridor with a folder who has got a name and a floor and a date, and he will be able to act on it."

"And in about four years there will be a stranger in a corridor with a folder who has got a piece of paper saying a man called Lin Hao put it there."

"Nobody outside this house knows it exists."

"Eight people know it exists," said Wren Kallo, "and one of them keeps a notebook and has never written anything in it, and one of them has a rule about things being asked and one of them put a description of this house into a live requisition in February and told nobody. This is not outside this house. This is the best-covered table in this city and that is exactly why it is a good place to lose a document."

## What He Kept

He did not go back up the walled road.

That was the third thing she had said and it was the real one, and there were eleven days to the last working day of the quarter and three things to keep, and he had broken exactly one of them, on purpose, on the morning after, in about ninety seconds with a carpenter's pencil.

He would break it again later in the month, and he had better get used to the fact that he does not know what day later in the month is any more.

He did not take it to Alma Quayle, who would have read it in about four seconds and put it in a drawer.

He did not take it to the third floor of a building off a third court, because in the first week of December he put one question to a woman there and received a standing answer to it in a sentence, and the standing answer covers this, and he has been extremely good at that for eight years and there is not one hour in the last eleven weeks that he has been less good at it than any of the others.

So it stayed on a kitchen table under a cloth on Ferrier Street with his name at the foot of it, and there were eleven days to the last working day of the quarter, and the seventh floor of a walled terrace was three years and a month away in a direction nothing in this city travels in.

He had a floor, a date and a direction and a piece of paper, and the woman who could have taken him there had given him a condition and taken it back, and he had broken one of her three and kept the other two and did not know yet which of the three was going to cost.
