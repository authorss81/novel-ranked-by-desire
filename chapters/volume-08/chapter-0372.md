# The Receipt That Names A Number

The description came back on the twenty-second of February, in the ordinary way, folded into a cover with a line across the flap and a clerk's signature on the corner of it, and the clerk at the second table read the outside of that cover out, and entered the ground in the fourth part with the date on it and the division that held it, and it went into the keeping book at eleven minutes past two in the afternoon.

Nothing was decided. There was no sitting, there was no vote and there were no nine minutes. A production is not a sitting and a room that has produced a paper has not done anything with it yet.

The holding came on the twenty-third.

## It Is Not A Sitting

He was let in at about ten minutes past ten and the chair told him what was about to happen in four sentences and then refused to explain any of them.

"The room has produced a ground," said Anouk Ferreira. "A ground that grounds nothing is a piece of paper, so the room is producing the holding, and whatever the holding stands on, in that order."

"It is a production. It is not a sitting, nothing will be decided this morning, and I will not have the word sitting used in this room today to make a piece of paper sound like a decision."

"How long does it take."

"It took the desk at Cordwain four minutes to find the address and about a month to find everything else," said the chair. "It has been gone since yesterday afternoon. What comes back will come back in the ordinary way, and you are not going to be in this room when it does, and neither am I, and the clerk will read it and enter it and that is the whole of the ceremony. There is no ceremony."

She looked at the fourth seat, which had been standing empty since the first of November and had a word against it in the keeping book since the twelfth of February.

"Four hours," she said. "Go down the corridor and come back at two, and do not stand at that window, because a man standing at a window at the end of this corridor for four hours is a thing somebody in this building will remember in about four years."

## What Came Back

At about ten past two the clerk put her hand flat on the book and did not turn round, and said one word, and the word was *receipt*.

"They have sent a receipt."

"What does that mean."

"It means they have not sent us a file and they are not going to." She turned round.

"No division in this city sends a file out of a sub-level and none ever has, and a division that did it would be doing a thing that has not been done since before the Veil."

"What a division sends, when a room at the end of a corridor asks it for the custody of a thing, is the receipt for the thing. That is the instrument. That is what has always come back."

He stood in the middle of a room with nine chairs in it and did not understand any of it.

"Read it out," he said.

"You are the subject. A subject may have a produced paper read to him and may not touch it. That is the rule and it is not a courtesy and it is not going to change because you asked nicely at a side table two days ago."

"Read it out."

## The Receipt

She read it out in the flat voice she kept for anything with a printing on it, and it took her about forty seconds, and he took it in at the time and understood about half of it in the corridor afterwards.

*Transfer receipt. One movement. Two hundred and fourteen thousand grains. Three years. Sealed.*

Then the second block, which is the part a receipt exists for.

*Source: held account, third sub-level. File two thousand two hundred and four.*

*Destination: file One, which is the first thing on the list and the last thing on the board.*

Then a line in smaller type that said the receipt was issued in answer to a production of the holding of the second part of a requisition in the eleventh sitting of that room, and gave the date, and gave the hour of the hour the paper had been asked for, which was not the hour it had come.

He stood very still.

"Say the figure again."

"No," said the clerk. "It is on the page and I read it once, and if you want it again you will want it four times this afternoon, and then you will have had me reading a receipt at you until I could do it with my eyes shut, and that is how a room loses the right to be trusted with anything."

"I want it again."

She read the figure again.

Two hundred and fourteen thousand grains. One movement. Three years.

## What Was Not On It

"Was there a name on it."

"There is not a name anywhere on that sheet," said the clerk. "There is no name on a transfer receipt. There never has been. A transfer receipt names a figure, a count of movements, a date, a seal and a file, and where a thing has gone. The person is not on it because the person is not the transaction. The person is the thing that happened."

"And the account it went into."

"File One." She said it the way a clerk says a heading. "It is at the foot of the list and it has been there three years and there is nothing under it. A file takes its number from what went into it, and there is a floor under one, and what sits on that floor is an account nobody has ever been able to describe well enough to give a second file to."

He put his hand on the back of the chair that was not one of the nine.

"The account I have declared in the second part of that sheet is the account of thirty years standing at premises 34-6-118."

"Yes," said the clerk.

"And it went there three years ago."

"Two hundred and fourteen thousand grains went there in one movement, three years back, and the account has stood at that address since the sixteenth of September, and that is this building's business and not mine." She turned the book round. "You asked me to read out the custody of the holding. That is the custody of the holding. I have entered it. Are you going to ask me anything else, because there is a queue behind you and one of the things in it is my own afternoon."

## The Part That Was Not About Him

He went out into the corridor and stood at the far end of it with his back to the door and read a receipt for the third time off the inside of his own head, which he had been doing since ten past two.

Two hundred and fourteen thousand grains. One movement. Three years ago, out of a room in this city with no window and a door that is not locked from inside, out of a man who had one use of his life and had used it.

He had known the figure and the movement and the bottom of the list for three years. He had worked all of it out himself, at a bench, off a sheet with a number on it, and he had never once been shown the paper.

What was new was that he had now seen it on a piece of paper with a number on it, produced on his own demand, entered in a keeping book at the end of a corridor by a woman with a pen, and due to go out on the stream at four that afternoon to six desks and to a keeping room that keeps four years.

His own name was not on it.

He went through the receipt four times looking for it and it was not there, and there was no place on it where a name could have gone, and that was not an oversight. A transfer receipt does not have a person on it. The person is the thing that happened.

Three years he had carried a belief that the account had come to him, and it had not come to him, and it had not been sent to him, and there was no version of the last three years in which he had been chosen.

He had been standing in the way of it.

## Why He Asked For The File

At about half past three he went back in and put one more request to the chair, and he had worked out how to word it on the stairs and he worded it in one sentence.

"I want the file the receipt was drawn out of."

Anouk Ferreira did not move for about four seconds.

"That is not the holding," she said. "That is beyond the holding, and I told you yesterday that the room would produce what the holding stands on, and a file in the third sub-level is not what a holding stands on. A receipt is what a holding stands on."

"I know."

"Then say why, because I am going to refuse it or I am not going to refuse it and I am not going to do either without knowing which of the two I am doing."

"Because a receipt is a piece of paper about a number," he said. "And in about four years somebody is going to open that file and get to the fourth part of a requisition and read the figure two hundred and fourteen thousand and come looking for the thing it went into."

"And there will be a receipt in the minute saying that the account it went into is the account standing at my address."

"And either there will be a file behind that receipt or there will not be, and if there is not then in four years a stranger reads a number and an address and concludes that a man took two hundred and fourteen thousand grains into his own account three years ago and hid it behind a declaration of holding in a room of nine."

She looked at him for a while.

"You have bought that right at a side table four days ago," she said. "And I am going to refuse it, and I want you to understand that I am refusing it because of you and not in spite of you."

"Chair—"

"No. You do not get to make me comfortable." She stood up out of the chair.

"I am not going to have a minute in this room in which a man served with a sheet was refused the paper that sheet stands on. In twenty years that has not happened and it is not going to start because a man at my side table found an argument in four minutes that I have not heard in eleven years."

"It goes out. It goes out at four this afternoon in the ordinary way, and the clerk will write it out, and you did not persuade me and I did not do it for you."

That was the last of it and she sat down again.

## No Version Of This Where He Walks Away

He stood in the corridor at ten past four with nothing in his hands, and a sheet had gone out of a gate on his behalf carrying a number he had not had eleven minutes earlier, and it was going to the third sub-level of the Classification Wing, and the third sub-level does not answer to him, and does not answer to a man at band six, and does not answer to anybody who is not the Council or is not a subject of the thing.

He had come up that stair on the thirteenth of December with four sheets of plain paper in his own hand and had laid them on a table under a window. He had come up it on the twentieth of February with seven minutes and nineteen words and had said nothing at all. On the twenty-first of February he had asked for a paper, because a paper can be asked for, and this morning he had understood what that meant.

A paper can be asked for, and asking for it is a machine, and the machine does not stop at the piece of paper you wanted.

At about half past four the sun came off the roofs on the hill and a wall in this city that had said nothing about anybody since the thirtieth of December went on saying nothing, and he went up the sixty-one steps to tell eight women that he had found out where his brother was, and he did not know whether he was going to say the number out loud or not.
