# The Cost Of A Ground In The Minute

It came to premises 34-6-118 at about ten in the morning on the third of March in the ordinary way, which is to say that it came to the person at the head of the household, who signed for it at the door with her own name in a book that keeps the hours, and then laid it face down on the table under the window and left it there for about four minutes while she made the tea.

Then she turned it over.

Eight of them read a copy of a minute of the twelfth sitting of a room at the end of a corridor, and the part of it that belonged to that kitchen was four lines long, and the whole of those four lines was a description of a table with eight women at it and a clerk called Imrie standing over them writing down that the premises had a head.

The reason on it was four words.

*The reason says because it is cheaper.*

## What The Four Words Are Worth

Nobody said anything for a while and Beatrix Halm did not open her notebook, which is the thing she has done twice in nine years.

"That is mine," said Nadia Ferrous.

"It is four words a man said to a clerk at my table on the morning of the twenty-seventh of October," said Lin Hao, "and I have not said one word to any of you about having said them, and I said them because I wanted it cheaper than a door and I did not think about anything else at all."

"You did not think about eight of us."

"I did not think about eight of us," he said, "because at the time the description was the only paper in this city that said the thing under classification was a person, and it was, and I wanted it."

"And it has been in the fourth part of a requisition since the seventh minute of the twentieth of February, and the reason it is in the fourth part is that nobody in that room grounded anything for four months and the woman who could did it without telling any of you."

He put his hands flat on the table and left them there.

"So it has been my sentence the whole time. I said four words at that table to a clerk and a requisition has been standing on them for three months and in about four years there is going to be a minute in a room with my name in it that says I said them, and not one of you has ever been asked about it and every one of you is in it."

## Wren Kallo

"That was mine too," said Wren Kallo, "and I am going to say one sentence about it and then I am not going to be asked anything, and I would like that arranged now rather than in four minutes."

The table stopped.

"The fourth part of that requisition is a description of this address made on the twenty-seventh of October. It has been in this house since that morning."

"It was in my division on the twenty-seventh of October and it was still in my division on the second of February, and on the second of February I went into a drawer and took it out and put it into a requisition. I did not ask anybody and I did not tell anybody."

"Nobody else on that page could lay a requisition and nobody in that room could lay a ground, and I was the person whose division holds the document, and I went and got it."

"You put a paper into a live requisition," said Isolde Marchetti, "and you did not tell the man it was about."

"It was in a live requisition for eleven days before it went into the fourth part and nobody struck it, including him, and he had a sentence available for seven minutes on the morning of the twentieth of February and he did not say it, and I have not asked him about that either and I am not going to."

She had her hands together on the table in the manner of a woman who has said the end of her sentence and knows it.

"I am not going to be asked who I told. It is not yours and it is not hers and it is not any of yours, and I said on the sixth of December what I would say, and I have said it, and this evening is finished."

## The Plain Sentence

The minute of the twentieth of February is a minute of production. A production is a copy, and copies go to every division that holds a part of the thing produced, in the ordinary way, and there are three of those divisions and one of them is the counter-forensics desk in the second sub-level of the Classification Wing.

That division has now got a copy of a minute that names a description it has been holding since October, and it has got it with the reason on it, and the reason is four words a man said to a clerk at a kitchen table, and the hour against them.

Mrs Imrie wrote those four words. She is a counter clerk in the second sub-level of the Classification Wing and she came out to Ferrier Street on the morning of the twenty-seventh of October because that is what she does, and she has worked beside four people for eleven years and has not said one word to any of them about the morning she spent at a kitchen table.

From the third of March there is a copy of a minute in that division that says what she wrote and why.

And the people who will ask her are the people she eats with.

## How Many Copies Of It There Are

There were four of it by the following morning, and the four were not in the same place and had not been made by the same hand.

One was in the keeping book at the end of a corridor, in a clerk's hand, in ink.

One was on the counter of the second sub-level of the Classification Wing in a drawer with a date against it, and the division that held the ground had its own copy because a division that holds a thing gets the paper when the thing is produced.

One was on the Vestry arcade's floor in a basket that came back empty at six, and the Vestry keeps its returns for a quarter because a clinic cannot answer for what it did not keep.

And one was in a kitchen on Ferrier Street, face up, under a cloth to keep the tea out of it.

Four copies in about twenty hours, made by four different sets of hands out of one minute, and nobody anywhere had been asked to make any of them and no office in this city would ever stop making a fifth.

## What The Eight Have To Decide


The eight had one thing to decide that evening and it took about nine minutes and it was not close.

Isolde Marchetti wanted to know whether they were going to do anything about a minute in which all eight of them were sitting at a table while a clerk described them as cheaper.

"I am going to say the whole of it," she said, "because I am the one of us that a division cannot serve and a form cannot hold, and if there is a thing to be said about a paper like this then it is better said by the woman that no office in this city can write a return about."

"A description that says a house has a head is the only paper any of us has ever had. It is standing in the fourth part of a requisition now, and it is going to be read in about four years by a stranger with a folder."

"And when he reads it he is going to work out that the man who made it cheap was standing in a kitchen with eight women who all hold posts and none of whom is a stranger to him. That is the sentence that is going to be in his head, and it is correct, and it is going to be true for four years."

"Who do you want to say it to."

"Not to anybody. I want to know whether eight people are going to be able to sit at this table in four years without one of them saying it first."

Sun Meiling put her cup down.

"Say it," she said. "Now. Once. And then it is said and it is not going to be said again, and it is going to be said badly by a stranger anyway, so it might as well be said properly by a woman who cannot be written down."

## Afterwards

He went down the sixty-one at about half past ten and came back up at about ten past eleven because he had forgotten something that was on the table, and the table was clear and the room was empty and there was a cup on the cloth where his had been.

There were three weeks to the thirtieth of the month, and a sheet that would go into the next quarter, and a copy of a minute in a division that had a clerk in it who was going to be asked four words by four people she knew.

He stood in a kitchen in the dark and worked out that the price of the twentieth of February was not going to be paid by him.

It was going to be paid on the third of March, by a woman at a counter on a floor two levels down who had written down what he said and gone back to work at about half past eleven that morning and would be at her counter again at six the next morning.
