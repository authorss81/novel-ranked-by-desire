# The Book With A Gap In The Line

The declaration of holding is one sheet of plain paper in his own hand, laid on the table under the window of a kitchen on Ferrier Street on the morning of the fourteenth of January at five minutes to eleven. It went out to eight desks on the fifteenth and out of the room at the end of a corridor on the ordinary stream at four on the nineteenth.

It is now in the keeping of the room where nine people sit, which means it is a document, which means it is not the household's any more.

There is another one in the same keeping, four sheets and sixteen days earlier, with the same name at the top of it and eight words at the foot of it instead of nothing at all, and the room at the end of the corridor does not distinguish between the two of them, which is the whole of what this afternoon is about.

Eight hands went under it.

Seven of those eight people live at premises 34-6-118.

Nadia Ferrous does not.

She came in off the doors a little after four on the fifteenth of March, before the tea, and put her bag against the wall and said she wanted to say one thing and then she would take her coat off.

"Say it."

"I want my name struck off it."

## What Cannot Be Struck

Wren Kallo put her cup down.

"A witness line is a list of the people who were in a room on a date," she said. "It is not a list of who lives at an address and it never has been and it cannot be made into one, because the day it becomes one is the day a document can be corrected by whoever is standing nearest to it, and a document you can correct is a document nobody has to sign."

She put it down the way she puts things down and the table let it lie there. There was a sheet at the end of a corridor with one hole in its line, two fingers wide, and the hole was where he should have been, and all eight of them had known that since January, and not one of them was going to bring it up to a woman who had come in off the doors with her coat on.

"Nadia," said Isolde Marchetti. "She has just told you that there is no instrument for it. Not that I would do it and not that I would not."

"I know what a witness line is. I have been counting people since the fifteenth of October." She had not taken her coat off. "Then it stays. And then in about four years somebody with a folder reads a sheet with eight names under it, and seven of us live at that address, and mine is on it and I do not, and he cannot tell which of those two facts is the wrong one."

"You cannot tell which one is wrong because both of them are true."

"That is what I am objecting to."

## The Count

Beatrix Halm had the notebook out, which she had not had out at that table since the morning of the third of March, and she was not writing in it.

She was looking at the sheet in her own head.

"Eight," she said. "In eight hands, and the order is Sun Meiling, Agnete Lindqvist, Beatrix Halm, Wren Kallo, Isolde Marchetti, Runa Sato, Petra Ilyin, Nadia Ferrous, and the gap is two fingers wide where the fourth seat is."

"Read the gap out."

"The gap is between the third name and the fourth and it is where a member of the room signs and he has not signed because he cannot, and it has been there since the twenty-ninth of December and it was there on the fourteenth of January." Beatrix Halm shut the book. "Nine things on the line. Eight of them are hands and one of them is nothing."

"Nine," said Lin Hao.

"Nine."

## The Arithmetic Of It

Isolde Marchetti did it in about four sentences and she did not enjoy it.

"The composition was eight on the fourteenth of January and it has been seven since the morning of the thirteenth of March, and the sheet does not know that, and it is never going to know that, and every copy of it in four divisions is going to carry the fourteenth of January on it for the rest of this decade."

"Could we lay a second one."

"No." She said it fast. "A second declaration is a new holding and a new sheet and a new minute, and the first one does not go anywhere, it is superseded, and superseded is not the same as withdrawn, and in about four years there are two documents in that room saying two different things about one kitchen and one of them is old."

"So the eight names stay."

"The eight names stay and the composition says seven and that is the whole of it." Isolde Marchetti put her hands flat on the table. "That is what happens when something true stops being current. It is not a lie. It is an old true thing, and there is no office in this city that can tell the difference between an old true thing and a false one, and there never will be, and every clerk who ever lives will make the same guess."

## What Nadia Ferrous Said About It

She took her coat off then, and put it over the back of a chair, and sat down, which was the moment at which it stopped being an objection and became a thing that was going to happen.

"That is the worst of it," she said, "and I want it said at this table because nobody else is going to say it and I have been carrying it since the day before yesterday morning."

She put her finger on the cloth where the sheet was not, because the sheet was in a room at the end of a corridor and had been for two months.

"I went to a counter at nine forty-one on the morning of the thirteenth of March to take myself off every paper in this city that has got that address at the head of it. That was the whole point of it. There are six desks that keep lists and I have been on the list of the thirtieth of October for six years and I gave the address up so that the next list is not."

"And you are on a witness line," said Lin Hao.

"I am on a witness line under a piece of paper with my name at the top of it," she said, "and it goes into that room on the stream at four in the afternoon and it is copied out by a clerk every quarter for four years, and there is a woman's name on it and a date and a document, and if you were a stranger with a folder in four years you would not look at the composition at all. You would look at the line."

She stopped and put her hands together.

"I gave up an address to get out of a list and I am going to spend four years under a document I gave up the address to get out of," said Nadia Ferrous. "That is the bill. I would like it in the book if anybody is writing anything down."

## What The Gap Is Worth Tonight

"Somebody is going to ask about the gap," said Runa Sato. "They have asked about the gap twice since the twenty-ninth of December and both times it was a man from the booking office who wanted a clean line."

"What did you tell them."

"That the gap is where a member of the room signs and he has not signed because he cannot." Runa Sato turned her cup round. "That is true and it is the whole truth and it has stopped being the whole truth since the thirteenth of March, and the difference is that there is now a second thing that could fill a gap in a line and it is not a member of this room."

She let that stand.

"So in four years there is a stranger with a folder," said Runa Sato, "and he counts eight hands and one hole, and he does not know that a hole is a man who lost his standing on the first of November and he does not know that eight hands was the composition of a house in January and seven is the composition of it now. He has two gaps in front of him and he will pick the one that looks more like carelessness."

Beatrix Halm put her hand flat on the notebook and said the thing she had decided to say and had not said.

"I am going to write one line about this," she said, "and I have had six empty ones since the sixth of February and I have not filled one of them, and I am going to fill one now and it is going to say *thirteenth, counter, nine forty-one* and nothing else, no name, no address, no figure. Is that allowed."

Nobody answered her for about four seconds.

"That is allowed," said Nadia Ferrous. "Put the hour in and nothing else."

## The Foot Of The Other Sheet

There is one other thing and it is not on that sheet. It is on the foot of the other one, under the line, in his own hand, and it is the only part of either of them that he wrote for himself.

He had written it on the twenty-ninth of December at a table under a window at about eleven at night and he had not read it since except on the day he wrote it, and he had never once given any thought to the word answer.

On the twelfth of January he had come down to the foot of a fifth sheet with the pen an inch above it and put it in the tray, because a declaration is not an attestation, and he had said that out loud in his own kitchen in front of eight women and Nadia Ferrous had called it mean.

"Read it to me," he said.

Beatrix Halm did not open the notebook.

"You have just asked me to read a document out of the keeping of a room I have never been in," she said. "And neither have you, except on the day you laid it on a table. Which means neither of us can read it, and neither of us is going to find out what is on the foot of it until somebody holds it up."

"I wrote it."

"You wrote it and you are the only person in this city who knows what is on it and you have not said it out loud in eleven weeks."

He looked at the table for about a second and then he said it out loud, flatly, in his own kitchen, to eight women, the whole of it, in one breath.

Then he said, "I am going to answer for it and I do not know how, and I have had since the twenty-ninth of December to work out how and the answer is that I have not found one, and I would like it noticed that I have said that in a room and not in a corridor."

"Notice it," said Isolde Marchetti. "Nobody is going to do anything with it. I am going to say one thing and then this is finished."

"Eight of us are going to be sitting at this table in four years, and one of us is going to say the wrong thing, and it is going to be said badly, and it is going to be said by somebody with a folder who has never been in a kitchen and it is going to be about him and there is nothing any of us can do about it."

Nadia Ferrous got up and put the kettle on.
