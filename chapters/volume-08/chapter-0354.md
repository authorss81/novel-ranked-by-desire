# Six Of The Eight Have A Post

Cordwain is a counter with a hall behind it and the hall is where they put a man who has to wait, and at half past ten on the eighth of January there were six people in it and one of them was him, and there was no reason in the world why Lin Hao should be at the Field counter at Cordwain on a morning when the Fourth Branch had a full complement of hands.

Runa Sato came out from the back at about eleven and did not come out to him. She came out to the counter, because the counter is where the Field counter takes people, and she stood on the other side of it with a bundle of returns under her arm.

"You have never come to this counter to talk to me."

"No."

"Four years." She set the bundle down. "You come to the Yard at six. You come to Cordwain at about eleven when there is a man being taken somewhere and you stand at the back with your hands down. This is the first time you have come in as a person."

"I have a question."

"Everybody at this counter has a question. That is not a differentiator." But she did not put the bundle down a second time, and she did not call the next one, and about a minute went past in which a counter in this city did not turn over. "Say it."

## The Count

"How many officers in this city could serve a paper on me without coming through my own front door."

Runa Sato did not answer him at once, which was the most frightening thing that had happened to him all year, because Runa Sato answers arithmetic before anybody has finished asking it.

"Then there is no number," she said. "There is a list. Give me the list."

"The eight at the address."

"Six of them."

He waited for the rest of it and she did not give it to him for about eleven seconds, which is about three times as long as it takes.

"Six of the eight at premises 34-6-118 hold a public post, and a post in this city is an address you can serve a paper at." She held up the bundle and did not open it. "Field. That is me, and that counter, and I am not going to pretend I am not part of the answer."

"Counter-forensics. That is Wren Kallo, and that division holds the description of those premises, which means that division serves anything laid against them, and it is the division that serves a requisition on a described subject in the ordinary way."

"The Vestry, where Nadia Ferrous works the doors of the arcade and nine hundred people a day go up and down them. The guild. The Church. Veil-Research."

"That is six."

"That is six, and I have had them in my head since the sixteenth of October and I have never once written them down, because a list of six names in a Field notebook is a list of six names, and a list of six names at that address is a piece of paper about eight women that nobody has ordered."

"It has been ordered. It was made on the twenty-seventh and it went out on the thirtieth at four."

"It was ordered by a man who did not know he was ordering it." She put the bundle down. "Lin Hao. A description of premises carries the public post of anybody at that address who holds one. That is the rule."

"It has been the rule since the sixteenth of October, when Wren Kallo asked Alma Quayle the wrong question in a kitchen at four in the afternoon and was told no, kindly, by a woman who has not been unkind to anybody in this business in thirty years. It does not carry work. It does not carry a salary."

"It carries one thing, and it is that when a paper is laid against those premises, every person at that address with a post gets served at that post."

"And you found that out on the twenty-eighth of October."

"On the twenty-eighth of October at about eleven in the morning, at this counter, with a fair copy in front of me and a clerk behind me who had not read it." She looked at him without any softening in it at all. "And you have known for seventy days."

"The twenty-eighth of October to the eighth of January is seventy days, and seventy days is ten weeks, and for ten weeks you have sat at a table on Ferrier Street with six of the people I have just read out to you and said nothing."

"Yes."

"Say why."

"Because I did not work out what to do with it."

"That is the first honest thing anybody has said at this counter about that address." She turned round and shouted for a clerk and did not say what for, and the clerk came and she gave him a number and he went away again. "There is a reason you did not work it out and I am going to give it to you and then I am never going to give it to you again."

"You did not work it out because the answer was the count and the count is the size of the box."

## Five And One

"Five of the six sit inside the machine," she said. "That is what you are actually asking and you have been standing here for twenty minutes not asking it. Field, counter-forensics, the Vestry, the guild, the Church. Every requisition, every description, every correction, every standing order and every sheet of every kind that has ever come at a house with people in it goes through one of those five rooms or it does not go anywhere at all."

"A paper laid against you would not have to find you. It would have to find five desks, and there is a woman sleeping in your building at every one of them, and the woman who signed a description of your house against the head of it in October has already put a witness line under it, and she will go on carrying the consequences in a book of her own for four years without ever once saying so."

"And the sixth."

"And the sixth does not sit in the machine." Runa Sato put both hands flat on the counter. "Veil-Research. Second floor, iron stair, off a court you could not find again with a map."

"That wing answers a departmental consult about a transfer that carried no name, and Sun Meiling has been in that room for three years, and there is a man on the third floor of that building who has been there nine years and who signed the answer to one such consult in four lines three years ago."

The hall behind him had four people in it and none of them were listening, and he had the very strong impression that he had been standing in a public place for twenty minutes saying things that could not be unsaid.

"That is five of six inside the machine," said Runa Sato, "and the sixth is the only one of the eight who is standing anywhere near the paper that is actually about you. And you did not know the count."

"I did not know the count."

"Nobody has ever counted."

## The Standing Order

"And now the thing I have not said at this counter in a month."

She took her hands off the wood.

"There is a standing order on premises 34-6-118. Field division. Applied for on the twenty-seventh in my own name, at about four in the afternoon, by me, on the day you had a description made against the head of your own house in a kitchen in front of eight women and a clerk. Reason box: blank. It is blank on purpose and it will stay blank while I am alive."

"You never told me you put it in."

"You never asked and I would have said no." She said it as flatly as she said everything at this counter. "You wrote me a note on the thirtieth of October saying that for the life of that account I was not to ask you for a release of it, and that is the only paper in my life with your handwriting on it that has never once been filed anywhere, and I am the only person alive who knows you wrote it,"

"and there is no figure against it and it is not a debt and I am never going to invoice it and if anybody in this division asks me why there is a Field man watching a house of eight women I am going to say the reason box is blank because I did not want to write down why."

"That is not a licence you need from me."

"It is not, and I have not taken it as one, and the difference between those two sentences is the difference between a standing order and a warrant and I have known it since I was twenty-one." She picked the bundle up. "I will be there on the day somebody comes at that door, and I will be there for the life of the account, and neither of us is ever going to discuss it at a table on Ferrier Street."

## The Seventh

He was about to leave when she said it, with her back to him and a bundle of returns under her arm and about four clerks waiting behind her.

"Eight women. Six posts in divisions."

"Seven." He did not know where it had come from. "Six, and Beatrix, whose is a tram office and not a division and which would answer a summons the same morning."

"Seven. Yes." Runa Sato turned round. "So that is one, and it is me, and I have been standing here for half an hour putting it off."

The counter behind her went on being a counter.

"Her posting is not in any division in this city at all," said Runa Sato. "It is not in the Bureau and it is not in the Church and it is not in the Vant and it does not sit on a roll that any office keeps. She is a woman at the Bellhouse and what a woman at the Bellhouse has is a room that agrees to what she says in it."

"Eight of you have worked that out between you about eleven times since October and none of you has ever counted it, because none of you has ever counted anything."

"What does that do."

"It does nothing. That is what I have been trying to say for about nine minutes." She put the bundle under her arm properly. "It does nothing at a counter. A paper cannot be served on it. A description cannot carry it. A standing order cannot name it. There is no clerk in this city who could write down what that woman does on an ordinary night and there is no office in this city that would know what to do with the sheet if he did."

She looked at him for about two seconds.

"And the only reason clause that has ever been attached to those premises is a thing that cannot be put on paper, and it has been sitting in a division since the nineteenth of October on top of a description of the premises, and there is nobody in this city who could put an author to it."

## What He Took Up The Hill

He walked up the hill from Cordwain at about twenty past eleven with the number six sitting in him where a number ought not to be.

He had spent seven years refusing to be a person in any room he was in.

On the twenty-seventh of October a woman had written down, at his own kitchen table, at his own asking, the reason *because it is cheaper*, and from that morning eight women had been individually reachable for the first time in this book, and he had known it for seventy days, and he had told nobody, and the reason he had told nobody was that the count was the size of the box and he could not make the box smaller.

Six of them inside the machine. Five of those six at a desk.

Seven of them on a roll somewhere, counting the tram.

And the eighth one was a woman who made a room agree to her, and there is no office in Ostara that has ever taken a statement out of a room.

He got to Ferrier Street at about twelve and there were eight women at the table and a good many of them were going to find out that afternoon that the man they had fed since November had spent ten weeks counting them and had told none of them, and he had about two hours to decide whether he was going to say so before one of them worked it out on her own.

Isolde Marchetti, who was at the far end, had put her hands flat on the table about a minute before he came in, and had taken them off again, and had not said anything.
