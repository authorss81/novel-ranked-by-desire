# A Composition Of Seven

He went down behind the plate office on the morning of the fourteenth of March, a quarter of an hour short of the hour the valuation is made, the way a man goes down when the stair is the only door and the short way at the top of it is a counter somebody looks up at.

It was the fifth morning in eleven weeks he had done it at that hour, because ten to nine is when the valuation is made and it is the only hour a man carrying two figures for one door can stand at a rail without being asked about.

[Ledger. One hundred and forty-two lines. Nine turned, and the ninth is still himself.]

"There are still two figures for one door," a piece of paper had told him in writing back in September, and there were not, and he was going down the stair anyway.

The book was open on the board and Solveig Rask had the pen on its chain over her wrist, and Hanneke Ruis was at the rail with a cup she had not drunk out of.

"The composition of premises 34-6-118 went to seven yesterday morning," said Lin Hao, "at a counter, in the ordinary way, at nine forty-one."

## What Four Marks Buys

"Four marks," said Hanneke Ruis.

"Four."

"Four is two calls and two extra, and the rule says two, and it has no opinion about the other two and I am not going to give it one." She did not turn round. "Once is waited at. Twice is the point the rule was written for, and after twice the room that keeps the book can put the line on a quarter without anybody drafting a thing first. That is the whole of the power and you have known it since the first of January."

"You told me the other half of it on the first of February and it is the reason I came down this stair in the cold."

"Yes, and I said the whole of it in one sentence, as usual, and you have spent eleven weeks waiting for me to improve it." She squared the book. "Seven and seven. Say the two figures."

"Seven is the composition of that address since yesterday morning. Seven is the priced line of the sixteenth of September, initialled and not signed, and the initial is mine, and I have never told a living soul that until the fourteenth of February at a kitchen table in front of eight women."

"Then it goes on the sheet."

## The Sheet That Goes On The First

What came out of a drawer at about ten past nine was not a requisition and not a valuation and not anything that had ever been in that room before, and it was about the size of a playing card and it had four ruled blocks on it and a heading at the top.

*Carry-forward. Address. Quarter commencing the first.*

"The first," said Hanneke Ruis. "The first of April. That is the sheet it goes on and not today, and today is the day it is drafted, and a sheet that is drafted on the fourteenth of March is carried on the first of April and is administered from the first of April, and it is administered for as long as the account is open."

"Which is not printed on it."

"Which is not printed on it," she said, "because an account of thirty years has no term printed against it anywhere in this city and the one thing that closes one is payment, and the price of paying is seven and the books that would take seven sat down to seven on the morning of the sixteenth of November and have not been opened since."

"So on the morning of the first of April instead of a fifth mark, there is a line carried."

"On the morning of the first of April instead of a fifth mark, there is a line carried," said Hanneke Ruis, "and I write that in the same hand and under the same printed rule as the other four."

"In about four years somebody takes the plate off the chain and scrapes it and makes a new one, and the new one will say eight, and eight is not a thing a stranger can read as anything except a reason."

## The Fourth Block

She wrote the first three blocks herself, and they took about nine seconds each.

*Figure carried. Seven. Being the composition of the address.*

*Priced line. Seven. Sixteenth day of September. Initialled. Not signed.*

*Composition certified at. Premises 34-6-118. Certificate of the thirtieth day of November.*

And then she stopped, and did not put the pen down, and did not write in the fourth block for about as long as it takes a man to understand something.

"Go on," said Lin Hao.

"I am going to," said Hanneke Ruis. "I want to say the rule out loud first, because in about four years somebody is going to read this sheet and I want there to be no question about which of us put the name in the fourth block. You did not."

"I did not. It was already there when the form came out of the drawer, because it is printed off the certificate and a form cannot be drafted without a certificate, and the certificate of the composition of that address is the thirtieth of November."

## What A Keeper Is Not

"You have a face on you," said Solveig Rask from the end of the board, without stopping, because Solveig Rask had been at a book on a pen on a chain for longer than Lin Hao had been alive. "You have had a face on you since I put the book down and it is not a keeper's business and I am saying so in front of a witness, for the record, because there is a woman on that sheet who did not ask any of us to do this."

"Take it out again," said Lin Hao.

"Even if I wanted to. And I would not want to." She turned a page. "Four blocks and the fourth one is a name and a name is the only part of any form in this building that cannot be got at afterwards. Every figure can be got at. Every hour can be got at. A name goes on and it stays on and that is the entire difference between a piece of paper and a person."

## The Name In The Fourth Block

"Sun Meiling," said Hanneke Ruis.

The stair went on somewhere above them with somebody's boots on it and then stopped.

"The woman who sleeps at that address, certifying in her own division, in writing, at nine on the morning of the thirtieth of November, that the composition of premises 34-6-118 is eight."

"She did not know," said Lin Hao.

"She knew what she was doing," said Hanneke Ruis. "She certified a count. That is her job, the count was right, and she signed it where she works, and there is nothing on that certificate about anything except a house with eight people in it."

She wrote it.

"And now say this with me," she said, "because it is the second half of the form and it is the half that is going to cost that woman something: a composition is certified for the quarter it is certified in."

"So it lapses."

"It lapses at the end of March. It is certified for the quarter it is certified in and it is certified in no quarter longer than a quarter, and the reason for that is thirty years old and nobody remembers it, and it means that the composition of premises 34-6-118 has to be certified again on the first day of every quarter by the officer who keeps that address, on a printed sheet, in her own hand, with her own name at the head of it, countersigned at the address."

## Four Times A Year

"Four times a year," said Hanneke Ruis. "The first of January, the first of April, the first of July, the first of October, and I have written the four of those dates on the back of a quarterly return in this building more times than I could count until about the eleventh of March when it stopped being a thing I did."

"And it goes on for as long as the account is open."

"It goes on for as long as the account is open, and it does not stop on the first of April, and it does not stop in four years, and there is no provision anywhere in this form or in any other form in this city for an account being closed other than by payment." She put the pen down. "She is not appointed. She was appointed in November by doing her job. That is the whole of it and it is worse."

"And nobody has asked her."

"Nobody has asked her and she does not know," said Hanneke Ruis, "and I would have said she knew, because the woman certifies a composition every quarter and reads a return every quarter and does not read anybody's, and that is a woman doing a job and not reading it, and I have met about four of them in twenty years and every one of them was doing the job and not reading it."

## The Thirtieth Of March

Then she squared the four blocks and put the sheet under the chain with the other three, and said the last thing, and it was the only thing she said all morning that she had not written anywhere.

"On the thirtieth of March this year, that certificate lapses."

"Yes."

"On the morning of the thirtieth of March, at a counter at about nine, a woman off a third court is handed a printed sheet with a box on it to fill in, and at the top of it where the heading goes is the address, and there is nothing else on the whole of it about anybody."

He stood at that rail with a number he could not write down in his head and another number that was going to be on somebody else's form in sixteen days.

## What He Did Not Do

He went up the stair at about twenty to ten and took the tram and walked the rest, and he did not tell her.

He stood in that kitchen at about half past eleven in the morning of the fourteenth of March with five women in it and Sun Meiling at the far side of it with her back to the light, reading the four lines of a thing that had come back off a desk, and he had about nine seconds in which he could have said it, and there was no version of saying it in which he was asking her a question rather than handing her a bill.

Because if he asked her she would say yes.

She had said yes to him at a table since the fifteenth of October, in five different rooms, about six different things, and she had never once made him pay for it, and she would have said yes to a form about her own house in about four seconds and gone back to her desk and never mentioned it again.

And an agreed thing is a thing she can be produced against.

He has never asked her for anything. In five months she has given him six answers and he has asked her for five words, and one of those five words was her brother's name, and she gave it.

So the form will arrive at her desk unasked, from a division that holds it, in her own division's ordinary stream, with the premises at the head of it and a box that has to be filled on the first day of a quarter, and there will be no minute anywhere in this city that says he asked her.

He went down to the sixty-one at about half past twelve and did not tell the table.

The composition of premises 34-6-118 was seven as of the thirteenth of March and the account of thirty years standing at that address would be administered out of a woman's own hand four times a year until she or the account stopped existing, and he had known what it cost for about two hours and had spent one of them not saying so.
