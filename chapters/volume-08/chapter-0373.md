# A Name He Is Not Looking For

He came up that stair at about half past eight on the morning of the twenty-fourth of February with one question, which was how long a production from the Classification Wing takes, and he got the answer out of the clerk at the second table in about four seconds and then stood there for another nine minutes for no reason he was ever able to give anybody.

The book was open in front of her. Books on that side table are open in the ordinary course of the morning and nobody has ever made a rule about it, and she turned it a half inch to reach the pen, and the page she turned to was the twenty-third.

## The Page For The Twenty-Third

There were two entries on it, both in her own hand, both in the form that entry takes in a keeping book: what came out, from which division, at which hour.

The first was the ground.

The second was the holding, and it was longer, because the holding had gone to two divisions instead of one, and because the second one was not a division that keeps a list of anything and had to be written out in full rather than abbreviated the way the first one was.

He read it standing up. He did not ask permission and she did not offer any, and both of them understood in the course of about four seconds that a man who is served may have a keeping book shown to him, and that nobody in this building has ever shown one to him, and that it had happened because he had asked a question about a production four seconds earlier and had not asked for the book.

The second division was named in full. It did not sit in the Wing. It did not keep a list of addresses. And it had been sent a strip about those premises in October.

He had known since the third of January that a division like that existed. He had got it out of Wren Kallo's mouth at a kitchen table in about four sentences, on the third of January, in the middle of a morning when eight women were working out what a room of nine could not say, and he had not been able to find it since.

It was in a book at the end of a corridor in four words with a date against them.

He stood there and let that happen, because he had spent three months looking for an office on a wall in a directory of offices, and the thing he had been looking for had been written down eleven feet from where he was standing since the twentieth of February by a woman with no reason in the world to notice that she had handed a man the name of a division.

## The Strip And The Production

That office had been sent a strip about those premises in October. A strip is what a division receives when somebody has decided that a thing it does not hold needs looking at, and it carries a subject and nothing else, and it is not a document and it is not kept and it does not come back.

That office had got a strip in October and a production on the twenty-second of February about an account standing at an address. And a division that does not hold a list of addresses cannot answer a question about an address by itself, and has to go and find one.

It goes to a division that does hold one. And there are six of those in this city, and one of them is a counter in a second sub-level, and one of them keeps the record of every person at that address with a day and an hour against it and has kept that record since the third of October.

So a division that keeps no list of addresses was about to go and ask a division that keeps nothing else, about an account at premises 34-6-118, on the strength of a production a room at the end of a corridor sent out on a subject's demand.

He could not stop that either, and he could not see it, and there was no clerk in this building who would be able to tell him afterwards whether it had happened, because nobody writes down a division asking a division a question.


## The Part He Was Not Looking For

The second entry had a destination under it, and the destination was not a division either, and that is what made him stop reading.

A production is a copy going somewhere. A division can be written short. An address cannot, because an address is not a division and a clerk cannot abbreviate a place a person sleeps.

*Premises 34-6-118. Service to the person at the head.*

Six words and a line under them and the word *eight* against the line, because that is how a keeping book records a composition without recording the list.

He read the six words twice.

Service at that address goes to the person at the top of that household, because that is the arrangement that has been in place at that address since the third of October and is on a list in a division off a third court.

And the person at the top of that household has a name on the first line of a page a man wrote in a kitchen on the twelfth of January, and she signed for a copy of that page at her own desk at nine in the morning on the fifteenth.

And she is under a permanent bar and is never going to be sworn, and she works two floors below a man on the third floor of the same building who came down her corridor once on the fourth of February and asked her for her name.

He had opened his brother's file.

And the first name the file put in front of him was the woman he had gone up sixty-one steps for since September.

## Why He Did Not Stop It

He stood at the second table with his hands on the edge of it for a while.

There is one thing a subject may do about a production and there is one thing a subject may not do, and the difference between them is the whole of what being served means.

He may demand one. He may not demand the other, because the other is a recall, and a recall is a thing the chair of a room may do once in twenty years and has never done.

And every person at that address would be entitled to be told that it had been done, and that is why it is not a thing anybody does.

If he had gone in and asked for it, he would have been asking a room at the end of a corridor to unsend a paper about the account of thirty years to a woman who cannot be sworn and who cannot ask for a copy of anything that is served on her.

And she would have found out. Not in a week. That morning. Because she is line one, and line one takes service, and a woman who takes service finds out at the moment it is put in her hands.

He would have called her in front of a room and told her that he had seen a number on a piece of paper, and there is not one instrument in this city that can keep a number from the woman whose duty it is to know what is being served on her own house.

He did not ask for the recall.

That was the whole of the decision and he made it at a side table in about nine seconds and he would make it again, and he was going to have to look at her across a kitchen table that evening at about half past six and not say a word about it, and he knew that at about half past eight in the morning with the sun not yet on the roofs.

"Anything else," said the clerk.

"No."

"You have been standing at my table for nine minutes," said the clerk, "and the ninth minute is the one where somebody in this building starts writing it down. Go up the hill."

He went up the hill.

At about half past six that evening he came up the sixty-one steps for the second time, and eight women were at the table, and Nadia Ferrous had not sat down yet.

Sun Meiling passed him the plate without looking at him and said nothing at all to him for about four seconds longer than she had ever said nothing at all to him before.

"You have read a paper," she said.

"I have had a paper read to me."

"That is not the same and you know it is not the same." She put the plate down. "I am not going to ask you what it was, and I want that understood at that table rather than worked out." She took her cup. "It is the fifth time since September that I have not asked you something. I keep the count, and the count is mine."

He put his hands in his lap where she could see them do it.

"It is yours," he said.

"It is," said Sun Meiling, "and I am going back to my dinner."
