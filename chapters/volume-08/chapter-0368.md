# The Address Somebody Offers To Give Up

Nadia Ferrous had been cutting bread for about a minute without eating any of it, and she stopped, and laid the knife across the loaf, and every woman at that table stopped what she was doing, because in this house that is what the knife means and it has meant it since the twenty-seventh of November.

"I have been doing it since the second of February," she said. "I have had it in a drawer for twelve days and I am not going to apologise for the twelve days, and if anybody at this table wants to help me then this is the moment to get up and leave, because you cannot half do this."

Nobody got up.

"I work the doors of the arcade at the Vestry and nine hundred people a day go up and down past me and I have been counting them since the fifteenth of October, and I am not the cleverest woman at this table and I am the one who counts."

## The Arithmetic

"Eight is the composition of premises 34-6-118. It was certified on the thirtieth of November, at nine in the morning, by the woman who sleeps at that address, in her own division, in writing. Seven is the price, and the price was set on the sixteenth of September, and it carries an initial and no name on it, and three people at this table have told me that both figures are true and I am not going to be the one who argues with them."

She turned the knife over and put it the other way down.

"A person keeping an address may go into any counter in this city and give that address up. Not the house. The address."

"It requires no instrument and no permission and no witness and no holder, and it is dated at the counter, and the clerk enters a number and the composition comes down by one. That is not rumour and it is not something Wren got from a girl at a counter. It is four lines out of the head of classification going out on the ordinary stream at four in the afternoon on the twenty-sixth of January."

"They are in a division, and everybody at this table has read them except the two of them who have not been allowed to."

"Eight becomes seven."

"Eight becomes seven. And seven is what it is priced at. And on the morning of the first of February a keeper stood in a cold room and told this man that a carry-forward sheet carries the figure a line is carried at, and that she is not going to move a figure across a quarter that disagrees with itself."

Nadia Ferrous turned her palms down on the table and left them there.

"So the composition says seven, and the priced line says seven, and the two figures are one figure, and that account goes on the quarter's carry-forward sheet and it is carried, and it does that every quarter after this one without anybody drafting anything and without anybody putting their name under it."

"And it does not generate a valuation again."

"It does not generate a valuation again. Not on the first of March. Not on the first of April. Not on any first of the month for as long as there is a figure that matches the one it is priced at, and that is the end of it, and it is the end of it because of one sentence a woman said in a sub-level a fortnight ago."

## The Price

"Now the bill," said Nadia Ferrous. "The list of the thirtieth of October says eight. A list is made and dated and it issues at four in the afternoon, and once it has issued there is no office in this city that can pull it back, cross it out or say it was wrong."

"So there will be a list going out of this city for the next four years that says there are eight people at that address, and there will be a counter somewhere saying seven, and both of them will be true, and nobody will be able to say which one is the lie."

"And the witness line."

"The witness line has eight names on it," said Nadia Ferrous. "Eight hands under a sheet a man laid on a table in a room on the fourteenth of January."

"If I go into a counter on the fifteenth then the composition of that address is seven, and there is a document in a room at the end of a corridor with eight signatures on it and seven people living in the house. That is not a mistake anybody made by accident. That is a thing I did on purpose with my eyes open."

She looked round the table.

"It is my name that comes off. It is not line one and it is not the head of the house. It is mine and I have thought about it for twelve days and I am not being brave and I would like nobody in this room to say that I am, because I am being arithmetic."

## One Thing About A Counter

"I am going to say one thing about a counter," said Wren Kallo, "and it is not about him, and I am not going to be asked anything else this evening."

The table waited.

"Giving up an address is the oldest ordinary act in this city, and there has been a place for it since before I came to that desk. It is not a form anybody has invented for this and nobody has to invent one now."

"What you get is a line at a counter with a day on it and an hour on it, and the day and the hour are the entire content of it. In about four years the only question anybody will ask about it is what was happening in that building on that day at that hour."

She put her hands together on the table.

"That is all I have. I have not gone to find out whether anything has been opened and I am not going to, and that is not a thing anybody at this table is entitled to ask me about."

Nobody asked her anything about it.

## Say It Out Loud

"Say the address out loud," said Sun Meiling. "Not to him. To the room. Say premises 34-6-118 and say that you are going to give that address up at a counter on the fifteenth, and then everybody in this room knows it, and then it is a thing eight people decided instead of a thing one woman did at a counter."

Nadia Ferrous looked at her.

"It cannot be eight people deciding. It has to be one woman at a counter saying I do not live there any more, on a day, with her name on it, because the second it is eight people deciding it stops being ordinary and it becomes a household acting, and a household acting is an instrument and an instrument needs a reason and we are not going to write a reason for it because there is not one."

Sun Meiling sat back.

"Then say it anyway so that they know."

## What He Did Not Say

He had been sitting at that table since about half past six and he had not said one word, and eight women had watched him not say it for about nine minutes, which was the fourth time since the third of January that they had watched him do it and the first time that it had cost one of them something.

What was in his mouth was *don't*. One syllable, in four languages, in every register a man has ever had for telling a woman that she is not going to do a thing, and he had been on the point of it three times and had not said it, and the reason he had not said it was not patience.

The reason was that there is no instrument in this city that can stop her, and there never has been, and a man who says *don't* to a woman who is about to walk into a counter in the ordinary way is not protecting her from anything.

He is putting himself between her and an act she is entitled to do. Every man who has ever done that has told himself it was for her sake, and not one of them has ever been wrong about that in the way that mattered.

## Forty-Eight Hours

"Nadia."

"Don't." She put a hand flat over the knife. "I know what your face is doing and I have known what your face is doing since November and I am not going to be argued with by a man who cannot say it."

"I am not asking you not to."

"Then say what you are asking for."

"I am asking you for forty-eight hours," said Lin Hao. "Until the morning of the twentieth. And I am telling you at this table that I do not know what I am going to do with them, because I do not, and I am asking you anyway."

Nadia Ferrous sat with that for a moment.

"Forty-eight hours is not nothing," she said. "Do not think about them. If you think about them you will find a reason, and the reason will be good, and then I am going to come down those stairs on the morning of the fifteenth and you are going to have spent forty-eight hours finding a good reason and you are going to have to say it out loud at this table and I am going to hear it."

"The fifteenth, then. I will say it out loud."

"That is the deal and I want it in front of eight people." She picked the knife up and turned it back the right way round. "And do not thank me for it. Gratitude in this kitchen is a bill and I will not pay one for something I chose to bring, and the last man who thanked me for offering did it for nine days and then he asked me for the receipt."

## From The Ground Floor

Beatrix Halm had gone down at about half past nine for a glass of water and had come back up with a folded piece of paper in her hand and an expression on her face that Lin Hao had only seen twice before.

"That was on the mat at the bottom of the stair," she said. "It was not there when I went down. It is not signed and it is not addressed and it came up sixty-one steps while eight of us were sitting at a table and nobody heard anybody."

He read it standing up.

*A thing I have not said out loud in eleven years. The sixteenth, seven in the evening, the Vestry. Bring nothing.*

"She has not been outside this building since the autumn before last," said Sun Meiling. "She said so at that table in December and she said it as a fact and not as a complaint."

"It is the sixteenth," said Beatrix Halm. "That is two days. And I am going to write the time down and I am going to write *sixteen* under it, and for the first time in nine years I am going to write what a thing is."

She did not write it, because she was not allowed to and because she had said so.

She wrote *seven o'clock, Vestry, Agnete* and nothing else, and shut the book, and went to bed, and left him standing in a kitchen with eight women in it and a piece of paper in his hand and forty-eight hours that had been asked for and not yet started.
