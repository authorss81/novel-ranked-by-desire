# The Sentence She Sent In October

The second sub-level of the Classification Wing has a waiting bench and a counter and a smell of hot paper that does not go away, and anybody served may sit on the bench and be attended to in the ordinary way, which is the whole of what he used on the morning of the twenty-sixth of January.

He had sat on that bench in November, and on it in October at nine in the morning, with a description of his own house made against the head of it at a kitchen table, and a reason given in four words by a clerk called Imrie with the hour against it.

*Because it is cheaper.*

She was at the counter at about a quarter to eleven with a bundle, and she said good morning to him the way a clerk says good morning to a man who has a file, and that is all she has ever said to him and all she ever will.

He sat down and waited for about forty minutes and read nothing, because there was nothing on the bench in this building.

## Ninety-Seven Days

Alma Quayle came out of her room at about eleven with a sheet in her hand and stopped when she saw him standing up.

"Mr Lin Hao."

He had not been expecting her to know his name in that room and he had not been careful about it.

"I have a page," he said. "I would like to know what it does."

"That is not a question I am able to take." She did not move toward him and she did not move away. "I am recused on my own signature and the recusal was minuted before anybody voted, and everything I say in this sub-level from today is said as a witness and not as a seal, and a thing said as a witness is a thing that is said out loud in a room."

"Then I am asking you as a witness."

"You are asking a woman who put her name on a description of your own house against the head of it on the twenty-seventh of October, and there is no version of asking me that makes it anything other than what it is." She looked at the sheet in her hand and did not put it down. "Sit down."

"I will tell you what I can stand next to and you may not write it down, and I will tell you why I am telling you at all in about a minute."

He sat down on the bench.

"Ninety-seven days," said Alma Quayle. "I drafted that on the nineteenth of October at eleven forty in the morning, on four lines, in my own hand, with your address four lines under my name. I read it once. I did not send it."

"Why not."

"Because it was true and because it was ordinary, and because a thing that is true and ordinary is not a document, and because I am the head of classification and everything I put out of this sub-level under my own hand is a finding." She said it evenly and she did not put any weight on it at all.

"And because I had wanted for three months to be the person who has to say what a thing is while somebody with an interest in the answer is standing in front of me, and I could not have both, and I chose the chair in the end, and it has been in a division for ninety-seven days with your address on it, and there is no instrument in this city that stops a thing that has been in a division for ninety-seven days."

## Four Lines

She read them out, standing in the sub-level, in the voice she used for a finding, and she did not ask him whether he wanted to hear them, because she had worked out thirty years ago that a man in that room always wants to hear them and that wanting to hear is not the same as being able to act.

*An account carried at a composition is reached through that composition and through nothing else in this city.*

*A composition may be lowered by any person keeping that address, at any counter, at any hour, by giving the address up.*

*Giving an address up is an ordinary act. It requires no instrument, no permission, no witness and no holder. It is dated at the counter and the clerk enters the number.*

*The priced line of the sixteenth of September says seven. The composition of the thirtieth of November says eight. Both are true and neither corrects the other, and the second is not a mistake.*

Four lines. She had had them since the nineteenth of October and they were the answer to four months of everything the room at the end of a corridor had tried to do.

She folded the sheet along the fold it already had.

"You have been building a door," said Alma Quayle. "You put a page of your own into that room on the fourteenth of January with your name at the top of it, and it has been served, and it is correct, and it is the first piece of paper about those premises that has ever had a person in it. And it is a good door."

"I have been in this business thirty years and I would not have thought of it and I would not have permitted it."

"And this."

"This is the way the house comes apart." She said it without any softening. "It is not a document. There is no reason clause on it. There is nobody's name on it except mine and mine is only on it because I drafted it. Any woman in that kitchen may walk into any counter in this city and give up premises 34-6-118, and the address will stop, and the composition will drop by one, and the priced line of the sixteenth of September will become correct."

## The Whole Of It

He did not say anything for a while, and she let him, because she had been the woman in a room waiting for somebody to say nothing for thirty years.

"Say what you have understood," said Alma Quayle, "because you have understood it and you are not going to, and I am not going to stand in a sub-level until eleven forty."

"When a person gives an address up, the composition goes down by one. Eight becomes seven. And the priced line says seven."

"Correct."

"And there is no production off those premises."

"There has never been a production off those premises," she said. "That is why the line is called at every valuation and will be called on the first of February and the first of March until somebody pays it or somebody produces, and nobody can pay it at seven into books that went to a guild table on the sixteenth of November, and nobody can produce off a door that has never had anything produced off it."

"And if the composition drops to seven, then the account is carried at a composition of seven, and a carried line at a valuation may be carried forward on a quarter's sheet without anybody drafting an instrument."

He understood it in the ordinary way and then he understood it a second time, which was the reason she had said it in a room rather than on a bench.

"Not if anybody sees it happen."

"Not unless somebody sees it happen," said Alma Quayle.

"There is no discretion in it and there is no way to object to it and there is nobody to object to it, because the person giving the address up is a person keeping it, and a person keeping an address may stop keeping it at any counter on any working day at any hour, and every office in this city will write it down for her because it is ordinary,"

"and it is not a false statement and it is not an offence and it is not even a mistake."

## One Word

She put the sheet down on the counter and squared it.

"I am going to ask the room to lift my recusal."

"Why."

"Because in about four years somebody is going to hold a hearing on that file, and on that file there is a description I signed, a composition a different woman signed, a page you wrote, and a referred reason that no clerk in this city can reproduce in writing, and if it is heard there is going to be somebody in that room who has to say what those premises are and what they hold." She looked at him.

"I am the only person alive who is going to know, and I have spent thirty years arranging to be the person who has to say what a thing is while somebody with an interest in the answer is standing in front of me, and I have arranged it for thirty years and I am not going to get it, because I recused myself in the first week of December on a signature that put my whole name at the top of the thing."

"When did you write."

"The twenty-second. I wrote it on the twenty-second and it went out on the ordinary stream at four yesterday afternoon and it came back this morning on the same stream at about half past nine with one word on it in the chair's hand, and the word is *no*, and it is not a refusal of anything except the lifting, and it is the first time in eleven years that room has answered a woman of mine in one word."

She did not show him the word. She put the sheet face down on the counter, and Mrs Imrie came along the counter and Alma Quayle pushed the four lines across the wood towards her and said, in the ordinary voice of a head of classification doing an ordinary thing in front of a man she is recused about.

"Enter this in the standing papers in my own hand and send it out on the stream this afternoon. No division is to be asked to do anything with it. It is a finding about method and it is not about the subject."

"Yes, head."

"And put the time against it."

"Yes, head."

Alma Quayle turned round and walked back into her room and the door closed behind her, and a woman who has been the head of classification for a long time closed a door behind her at half past eleven on the morning of the twenty-sixth of January because she could not stand in a corridor with a man in it any longer than that.

## The Man Who Bought A Door

He sat on the bench for another twenty minutes.

For four months he had been trying to keep the account at an address with eight people in it, and he had done it with a laundry and a gate and a yard and a lift and a room, one at a time, nine seconds each, and then with a room where nine people sit, and then with a kitchen table and eight hands and a page with his own name at the top of it.

Every one of those had been about keeping the number of persons at that address at eight.

Not one of them had ever been about whether the address was there.

A woman who was in his own kitchen would go to a counter and give up premises 34-6-118 and it would be dated and the composition would drop and the priced line of the sixteenth of September would finally be right, and a called line could then be carried on a quarter's sheet by the room that holds the book, and the quiet he had been bought on the first of January for the price of a torn slip of paper would go on for ever.

And the list of the thirtieth of October would still say eight, because a list is made and dated and issues at four in the afternoon and cannot be corrected, withdrawn or unissued.

He had spent three months making a household visible and the cheapest way to take it apart was a woman at a counter saying four words.

He had known Alma Quayle for four months and had refused her once with an instrument on the seventeenth of December, and the thing she had just done was to hand him, in a sub-level, in her own hand, in ninety-seven days, the cheapest instrument in Ostara against everything he had done since October — and she had done it because she was right, and because she could not send it for three months, and because a person who is diminished by being right is still right.

## Up The Sixty-One

He went back up the hill and stopped at the north end of the square at about twenty past twelve, because a wall in this city is read whole and he had not read one since the fifth of January.

There was nothing on it.

Not *out of band*, not his figure, not two words about a man in band six. The green card was up and the number on it was eight thousand one hundred and forty-five and about nine people were at the rail and none of them were looking at him.

Twenty-six days, four short of a month, from the thirtieth of December. The first week was the fifth of January and this was the fourth.

For four weeks the only sentence he had been given about his own safety was a wall that said nothing at all about anybody, and he had been telling himself since the fifth that it meant a man in band.

On the twenty-sixth of January he stood in front of it and let himself have the other reading. A wall in this city runs out of things to say about a man who has stopped being interesting, and it will have plenty to say again the day he goes under, and it will say it inside a week.

Then he went up the sixty-one steps.

Sun Meiling had not said one word about the third floor since the sixteenth.

Four days ran out on the twentieth. It was the twenty-sixth. He had waited ten days for a sound from a building off a third court and there had been no sound at all, and nobody had come up his stairs, and nobody had sent anything down them, and the one woman in that house with the fewest defences had gone completely quiet about the one thing she had told him she was going to do.

A woman who has stopped announcing that she is going to do a thing is a different thing from a woman who never said she was.

He hung his coat on the nail and set his shoes square under it, and stood in the hall with four lines in his head about a counter in this city where any woman at that address could put an address down and walk out, and the only sound in the building was Agnete Lindqvist on the ground floor telling somebody through a door that the oiling of the lock would do, in a voice that has not been raised in eleven years.
