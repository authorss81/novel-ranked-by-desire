# The Thing He Is Going To Write

He told them about the count on the eighth of January at about one o'clock in the afternoon, standing up, with his coat still on.

Six of the eight hold a post. Five of those six are inside the machine. He had known for seventy days and had said nothing, and he said it in about ninety seconds and sat down, and eight women looked at him, and nobody made anything of it at all.

That was the price of saying it and it was cheaper than he had expected and it was the reason the evening of the tenth got as far as it did.

It was about eleven when he came in from the branch, and the kitchen had been full since about half past nine, and he stood at the head of the table in his coat and said it.

"I want to say the thing I could not get out in the right order on the twenty-sixth of December."

Sun Meiling put her glasses off.

## The Sentence

"It is this. The sheet they served at this table on the sixth of December has four parts. The subject. The holding. The figure. The ground. The fourth of the four is the ground, and the chair called it the best ground this city has had since the war."

"And under the four parts, on a fifth line that is not a part and was only a line, there is a ruled box the width of a hand with *held by* over it, and there is a clerk's tick in the corner of it and nothing in the box."

"The tick is against the held-by line and it is the only tick on the whole of the four parts. It is the only tick on that sheet. Somebody went round that room with a pen and checked every part and then ticked the corner of the one that was empty."

"And the only person alive who can put anything into that box is the man it is about."

The kitchen had the kind of quiet in it that eight women make when they have decided not to be helpful, and it is a different quiet from any other quiet in this city, and he had last heard it on the twenty-sixth of December.

He had hoped, going in, that somebody would break it.

Nadia Ferrous had a knife and a loaf and she put the knife down flat, which was how she did things when she wanted to be listened to, and everybody waited.

"An empty box does not carry anything," she said. "I know you have not asked me, because nobody at this table is going to be asked anything, because we have all worked out what would happen if one of us answered. But an empty box does not carry anything. Not on its own. That is a true thing and I would like it said in this kitchen once so that nobody can use it later."

"Said."

"I am not going to say anything else," said Nadia Ferrous, "and I am going to cut the bread, and the reason I am going to cut the bread is that if I sit here another minute I am going to start helping."

## Why They Do Not Help

Sun Meiling spoke without taking her glasses off.

"They will not help you because helping you would be the same as writing it for you."

"I know."

"You have known it since the twenty-seventh of November and you have been waiting ten weeks for a room full of women to do a thing that would have taken you eleven minutes." She put the glasses on. "And they are not going to, and you are not going to be angry with them, and I am going to say why in one sentence and then not again."

"Anybody who puts a hand to this has her name on it, and there have been enough hands on paper about you in this house already this year."

"Isolde."

"I am not doing it," said Isolde Marchetti.

"I did not ask."

"You were about to." She had her hands in her lap and she had not taken them out, which in that room was a thing she did when she was holding the room rather than standing in it. "You were going to ask one of us for the wording, because you have had that sentence in your mouth since November and you have never once been able to get it out in the order it wants to be in."

"You want one of us to say the middle part for you so that you can say the outside parts yourself."

"Yes."

"No." She said it without any heat at all. "And I want to say why, and it is not noble."

"If one of us gives you a sentence, then in four years there is a document in a room and a document in four hands, and the four hands will be the same four hands, and somebody with a folder will ask the women in this kitchen what they were doing on the tenth of January, and one of you will remember, and that one will be the one who remembered it kindly."

Nobody argued with her, because she was right, and because she was the only woman at that table whose work could not be written down, which Runa Sato had told him at a counter on the eighth of January and which he had not yet managed to say out loud in a room.

## The Door He Has Just Built

"Now say the part that makes it a decision," said Runa Sato.

He had not wanted to say it. He had come in at eleven to say four sentences and get through them, and the fifth sentence was the one that could not be got out of the way afterwards.

"A requisition cannot be laid at all without that box filled."

He heard somebody draw in at the end of the table.

"That is the whole of it. There is no other door into that business. Any person in this city may write a sheet against me, any office may table it, any two members of that room may call a sitting for it, and it will go nowhere at the ninth minute, every time, for as long as the box is empty. The box is the only instrument in this whole business that does not care whether five people want anything."

He set his hands flat on the table.

"So I am going to fill it. And when I have filled it, that room will have the only sheet they have ever had that names a person, and they will be able to lay it, and five of them will not have to want it, and the fifth seal does not have to be found by anybody who is willing to look."

"I have spent the last two months finding out who in this house would put their name under a page of mine."

"And I have just built that room a door, and I am the man who has to walk through it."

## What Eight Women Did With That

It went round the table, and he let it, because on the twenty-sixth of December he had let nine seconds go and that had been correct.

Runa Sato said the arithmetic, because Runa Sato always said the arithmetic: two to call a sitting, five to carry, and three left who would seal a fresh one and a chair who made an offer on the seventeenth and will not make it again.

Petra Ilyin said the only thing she had ever said in that kitchen about his handwriting, which was that in four years whoever wrote under this carried the name and the cost was paid by whoever wrote, and that on the twenty-ninth of December eight of them had signed for somebody else and had not known what they were agreeing to.

Beatrix Halm wrote the time and wrote nothing under it.

Agnete Lindqvist, who has not been outside one building in eleven years and who is the only answer in this city to being examined, said that she would not be able to help him with this either, and that she had said so about six other things, and that this was the first one where she had wanted to.

And then Nadia Ferrous put the loaf down and said: "When."

"The twelfth. In this room. At about eight in the morning, with the eight of you, on plain paper, in my own hand, the way I did the four sheets on the twenty-ninth."

"The twelfth," said Beatrix Halm, writing it.

## The Second Question

He was going to say goodnight.

He had got the three parts out in the right order for the first time in six weeks — what it was, where it was going, and who would hold it — and the kitchen had heard all three and had refused to touch any of them, and he was standing up, and Sun Meiling said:

"Lin Hao."

He stopped.

"The first time I asked you a question about this was on the twenty-sixth of December and I asked you for three things in an order, and you could not do it, and eight women watched you fail and none of them helped, and I told you the reason and it was the reason then."

She had her hands in front of her and she was not using her hands for anything.

"You have done it in the right order tonight. You have said what it is, where it goes and who holds it. That is the whole of the first question and I am not going to ask it again for the rest of the year."

"So that is the first question, and it is finished, and I am not going to take it back."

"Now here is the second one, and it is the only other one I am going to ask you about this until it is done, and I would like you to answer it quickly, because I am tired and I have been carrying a thing since the first of January."

"Ask."

"Who signs it."

The kitchen had a clock in it and the clock was the only thing that made a sound for about four seconds.

"Me."

"I know," said Sun Meiling. "I am not asking about the paper."
