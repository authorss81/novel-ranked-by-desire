# A Copy Goes Up The Iron Stair

The ordinary stream leaves this building at four in the afternoon and has left it at four in the afternoon every working day since the city had an ordinary stream, and it goes out with a manifest under the bundle string, and the manifest is not a document anybody may read.

He knew that. He had known it since the sixteenth of October, when a description had gone out at four and he had gone down to the gate at ten past four to see which way the boy went, and had learned in about nine seconds that a man who watches a bundle go out of a gate learns nothing at all.

He spent the morning of the nineteenth of January doing it anyway.

## What Cannot Be Found Out

Petra Ilyin came out of the hall at about eleven with the day's returns under her arm and did not stop, and he walked beside her for forty yards.

"You have been standing in that hall since a quarter to eleven."

"I have."

"You are waiting for the four o'clock."

"I am waiting for the four o'clock."

She stopped.

"Every person named on a thing laid into a room gets served with it. That was read out at the desk on the fourteenth in front of the room, and there is no discretion in it anywhere in this city and nobody is going to be given one in January." She shifted the bundle. "They were served on the fifteenth. At their desks."

"Nine in the morning, one of them, in this building, in a sub-level, by a clerk with a book of times. You know that because somebody told you the same thing at that table on the fourteenth, and it was me, and I told you because I would have had to serve it."

"I know."

"So what you are standing in that hall for is the four o'clock, and the four o'clock is not a service." Petra Ilyin put the bundle down on a windowsill, which she did not do. "The four o'clock is the stream. Eight copies go out on the stream to the eight addresses inside this building, because that is where eight of the people named sleep, and that has been true since the twenty-ninth of December and it is true today."

"And six go out to six divisions, because six of the eight of you hold a post and this one goes where the post is. That is not a service either. That is a bundle of six going to a counter in a building where somebody has a stamp and a list, and there is no name of the person it is for on the outside of it, because that is the entire purpose of the stream."

"Then it is untraceable."

"It is completely traceable," said Petra Ilyin. "It is traceable by everybody in six divisions today and by anybody with a folder in about four years."

"What you cannot do is stand at a gate at ten past four and find out which of the six has opened it, and that is the only thing anybody in this city thinks you are doing down there, and you have been doing it at four o'clock on the fifteenth of October and on the twenty-ninth of December and you are going to do it again this afternoon,"

"and I would like you to understand that a man who watches a gate is a man who expects to be watched back."

## The Nine Days

On the sixteenth of January he had gone up the iron stair off a third court and had come down it at about eleven, and there had been eight women at his own table that night and nobody had said one word about where he had been, because there is no rule in this house about where a man goes and there has never been and everybody at that table found it easier not to make one.

He had not gone up it again.

He stood in the hall of a building on Ferrier Street for about forty minutes in the middle of the day and did the arithmetic he had been not doing for nine days.

The copy had been served at her desk at about nine on the morning of the fifteenth. She had told him that herself on the sixteenth, in a room with two hundred sheets on the wall, before she had said anything else, because she has spent three years arranging to be the one who says things.

So she had it.

And he could not ask her a single question about what she had done with it, because the one woman he is forbidden to ask anything at all is a different woman in a different building, and there is no instrument anywhere in this city that stops him asking Sun Meiling whether she has read a piece of paper addressed to her.

That was the thing he had understood on the night of the sixteenth and had not been able to put anywhere in eight days.

He was not allowed to ask Wren Kallo.

He was not forbidden to ask Sun Meiling, and he had not asked her, and the reason was not permission. The reason was that a woman who is named first on a page a man wrote at his own table is not a person he gets to interrogate for the rest of his life, and there is no version of this in which he is.

## Ten Past Four

The stream went out at four.

He was at the bottom of the sixty-one with his coat on when the runner came out of the gate with the bundle string over his shoulder, and it was two bundles and not one, and the second one was thinner.

He did not follow him. He had followed a bundle once, in October, and had learned nothing, and he had also spent seven years learning exactly how conspicuous a man is when he walks behind another man in a street.

At ten past four he was at the bottom of the third court.

The iron stair went up the side of a building that is not on any map he has walked in four years, and there are eight doors on the corridor at the top of it, and a runner went up those stairs at about a quarter past four with a bundle of one sheet under his arm, and put it on a table on the second floor.

Lin Hao was standing in the street at the foot of it and did not go up, and there was no reason on earth why he should not have gone up, and he stood there for about four minutes.

A copy of a document he had written at his own kitchen table on the twelfth of January, in his own hand, with no reason clause on it, signed by him and witnessed by nobody at all, was lying on a table on the second floor of a building off a third court.

Six of the eight of them had taken one at a desk on the fifteenth.

Seven of them had it in the building they worked in, because a counter-forensics desk serves six divisions and Wren Kallo had been sitting on six copies of it since the middle of that afternoon and had never taken one herself.

## The Division That Wanted A Reason

Ninety days.

The account at premises 34-6-118 was referred for classification on the nineteenth of October, and it was referred with three parts, and the third part was a reason clause, and the reason clause was a song made in public on the fifteenth that cannot be written down, and nobody has been paid for it.

Ninety-four days ago Isolde Marchetti had stood in a room and nine hundred people had carried something out of it for nothing.

Ninety days of a division wanting a reason it could not write down, and the wanting had gone from a want to an appetite, because a division that cannot write a reason down cannot finish a file, and a division that cannot finish a file is a division that is asked about one in four years' time by somebody who has never met any of them.

And at about ten past four on the afternoon of the nineteenth of January, in the ordinary way, with no discretion exercised and none available, a division that had been holding that file for ninety days was served a document.

One sheet of plain paper.

A name at the top of it.

A figure, a holding, a date, and a signature, and nothing else whatever, because there is no reason clause on it anywhere and there never was going to be one.

There is nothing on the face of that service that anybody could object to.

## What It Bought

He walked back up the hill in the dark and did the sum, because there was nothing else to do with the rest of the evening and the sum was short.

He had spent seven years refusing to be findable. He had refused a laundry, a gate, a yard, a lift and a room, one at a time, in about nine seconds each, and the sum of it had been a man who could not be served.

Since the twenty-seventh of October he had bought legibility at the cheapest price anybody has ever found: by making other people write his name down.

On the fourteenth of January he had stopped paying other people and had written it himself, and this afternoon a runner had gone up an iron stair off a third court with the result in his hand.

Four of the six divisions are run by women who sleep in his building.

One of the six is a wing where a woman who is line one on the page works on the second floor and where a man who has been in the building for nine years has a window on the yard and has not come down that corridor in three years, and the copy that went up the iron stair on the fifteenth had nothing whatever to do with him.

And one of the six is the division that holds the description, and it has been served, and he cannot ask whether it has opened it, and he cannot ask the woman who signed the description whether she has read what was served on her own division, and there is nobody else in this city who is permitted to tell him.

That was the specific shame of the nineteenth of January. Nothing else had happened to him in four months that was as small, and it was the first time anybody had gone out looking for a piece of paper in order not to be the subject of it.

The ledger had gone to a hundred and twenty-eight lines in nineteen days, because he had kept his round at the Fourth Branch and branch attendants score in aggregate, and nine of those lines were turned, and the ninth of them was still him, and there was nothing spent on any of them.

## Ten Past Four On The Sixty-One

Beatrix Halm was up the top of the stair with the notebook open and the pencil down and the expression of a woman writing something she has no right to know.

"I do not know what it was."

"You do not need to know what it was."

"I have written the time down anyway, because I write the time down against everything you do, and there was nothing to write under it, and this is the fourth time in nine years that has happened." She wrote. "Six forty-one. Ten past four."

"That is three blanks."

"It is four blanks and I have started counting them, which is a thing I did not intend to start doing." She shut the book and got up. "And I am going to say the thing I have been sitting on this step for an hour, and then I am going to bed, and you are not going to like it."

"Say it."

"Two months ago you came up this stair and told me a card at the north end of the square said eight thousand one hundred and forty-five, and I wrote it down, and you were proud of it in a way you have not been proud of anything since June." Beatrix Halm did not raise her voice, because she never raised her voice, and because a woman who can feel forty thousand people can feel one of them shouting in her own building three streets away.

"Today a man came down this street at ten past four with a piece of paper in his hand, and I know that because you came up those steps at a quarter past and stood there for a minute before you said anything, and a man does not stand on his own stairs unless something has happened to him that he cannot put in a book."

"I put it in a book. On the twelfth. In my own hand."

"Yes." She went past him to the door. "And that is the first time in nine years I have found out that a thing I could do was the thing you were doing all along."
