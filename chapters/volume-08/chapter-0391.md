# The Account And The Quarter

The muster room off Cordwain has a bench along one wall, a table at the far end of it, and a lamp that is off for twenty-three hours a day. Runa Sato had it on at about a quarter to eight on the morning of the twenty-first of March and had been in the room since seven, which is a thing she has done about four mornings in ten since October and has never explained to anybody.

He came in with a bag over his shoulder and put it on the bench and she looked at the bag and then at the clock on the wall.

"Nine days."

"Nine days."

"Sit down. I have done this about nine times since the autumn for nine different people and I am not going to do it twice for the same man, so you are going to let me get through it before you ask me anything."

## The Arithmetic

"A quarter is ninety days," she said. "It does not matter when you start it. It is ninety days and then the paper is shut and a new one is opened, and that has been true in this city since before anybody now working was born."

"A quarter closes on its last working day, and in this one that is the thirtieth of March."

"It is the last working day of the month and the last working day of the quarter, and the card goes up on that morning at six, and I have watched it go up four times."

She turned the cup round on the table.

"Your sheet was laid on the twentieth of February. It was called and served and heard on the same day and it has been read twice since, once on the seventeenth of March and once before that, and it has not been decided on either occasion."

"So it lies in this quarter."

"It lies in this quarter and it has never been in another one, and it goes into the one that starts on the first of April at four o'clock in the afternoon of the thirtieth, and it goes in with everything written on it, and the ninth minute goes with it, and the ground goes with it, and the figure goes with it, and the hour anybody entered against a ground in a kitchen goes with it."

"Who does that."

"A clerk with a book. Not the chair. The chair does not move a sheet."

## What Actually Crosses

"And here is the half of it that nobody tells a subject, because a subject does not need it." She put both hands flat on the table. "The sheet does not cross. The paper stays where the paper is, in the keeping of that room, in a folder, and it will be there in four years and it will have a reference on the spine. What crosses is the standing."

"The standing of the room."

"The standing of the room. Three."

"Three," said Lin Hao.

"Three, and it has been three since the sixth of February, and it will be three at six in the morning on the thirtieth of March, and that is the number the next quarter opens on. Not five. Three." She let that sit for about as long as it needed. "And I want you to hear the rest of it in the right order, because you will not get a chance to hear it in the wrong one."

"Go on."

"You cannot withdraw it. You may demand production and you may demand a ground and you may put nineteen words into a room and be refused, and a man who has been served with a sheet may not take that sheet off a room's list. There is no paper for it and there has never been one, and the reason for it is twenty years old and nobody down there remembers it either."

He had thought about that for a long time in February and it had not been the sentence he expected.

"You do not want it withdrawn."

"I want you to have said yes out loud in a room with a lamp on it," said Runa Sato, "so that when it is somebody else's afternoon one day you and I are both talking about the same morning."

## The Administration

"And from the first of April it is administered weekly, and administration is not a decision, and everybody in every office in this city confuses the two and calls the confusion progress."

"Administered by whom."

"By whoever holds the fourth part of it, and that is a division outside this building altogether — one that keeps no address list of its own and has had a strip about Ferrier Street on its desk since October." She looked at him. "I am not going to say that office has a name in this room, because you did not give me that name either and I have spent four months being extremely good at it."

"You did."

"And the administration runs at four on the afternoon of a day in the week and it does not go out to a vote and it does not go out to a room. It goes out to a desk. And a desk that receives a thing once a week for a quarter will, at about the eleventh week, have a person attached to it who has never met you and whose name is on the top of the file."

## What She Paid For It

"That is the machine," said Runa Sato. "Now I am going to tell you what my half of it has cost, because you sat on that bench on the eleventh of March and told me I had been carrying a fact since the twentieth of February, and you were right, and you were right in front of eight women, which is the single most expensive place in this city to be right in."

She put her cup down.

"On the twenty-second of February I had a request for it in writing from a man in an office I have never been inside, and it was not rude and it was not a summons."

"It was a form."

"Somebody had gone back to what I said out loud on the eleventh and asked the office to have it in writing from the person who said it, and the office wrote to me, and I answered it, and my answer is in a file in that building and it is dated and it has my hand on it and it says that the ground was laid in a division that does not sit in the Wing."

"That is not much."

"That is almost nothing, and it took me a week and a half to write, and I did not write it."

She turned the cup round again.

"I went and asked for it to be put through a man who owes me something and he put it through a woman who owes him something worse, and the price of that was a favour I was going to keep until the autumn. It is gone. It went on the second of March and it is not coming back and I have not told anybody at your table and I am not going to."

"Runa—"

"You asked me at that bench on the eleventh and I am answering you now because there are nine days and because in about four years a stranger with a folder will read the two of those documents next to each other and will want to know why a Field officer of rank six is mixed into the fourth part of a requisition about a kitchen." She put her hand flat on the table.

"Because I would not sit at that table and be quiet about it one more week, and because that is not a thing I am going to be able to do in April."

## The Figure On The Card

Then she said the thing she had been sitting on since about ten past seven, and she said it standing up, which she did not often do in a room this size.

"Now the part I have been not saying, and I am saying it because you have nine days and because you are going to find out on the thirtieth whether I told you or not."

"The card."

"The card comes down at six on the last working day of a quarter and it is put up by a different office in a different hand on different paper, and it prints two things. It prints the floor. And it prints the standing figure at every premises that has one, and it prints it at the composition of that premises at the hour it is read."

"How many."

"Eight thousand one hundred and thirty-one," said Runa Sato.

## Fourteen

Nothing happened in that room for about four seconds and the clock on the wall went past the minute.

"That is fifteen under what the floor has said since the thirtieth of December," she said, "and it is fourteen under what it said at six that morning, and the fourteen is the number you were carried on the thirtieth of December by eight hands on four sheets of plain paper, and the eight hands attested eight persons at that table on the fourteenth of January, and the composition of that address has been seven since the ninth hour and forty-one minutes on the morning of the thirteenth of March."

"They attested eight."

"They attested eight, and there are seven, and a card that prints a figure at the composition cannot print a figure held by eight attestations of eight persons."

She put her hand out flat.

"It is not that the paper is wrong. Every sheet of it is in a room at the end of a corridor and every one of them says what it said on the day it was made and none of it can be corrected. And the figure goes back to what those premises held before anybody put their hand to anything at all, because a plate only stands up while there is something standing it up."

He did not say anything for about four seconds.

"The card printed eight thousand one hundred and thirty-one on the twenty-ninth of November," said Runa Sato, "and I read it in the dark at a quarter to six on the thirtieth of December, and it stood at that number for a month while eight people did nothing whatever, and then eight women put their names under yours and moved it fourteen points in a single day, and that is the whole of what four sheets of paper have been worth to that address for ninety days."

"And on the thirtieth of March it is worth nothing again."

"On the thirtieth of March it is worth nothing again and nobody will have done anything wrong and there is no instrument for putting it back and no office that can be asked why."

## The Wall

He stood up off the bench and picked his bag up and did not put it on.

"Beatrix Halm is under the north end of that wall at ten to six," said Runa Sato, "and she will have the figure written down twice before she has finished saying it, and she will have written the two words under it in brackets with the shade named beside them, because that is how she writes a wall when a wall changes its manners."

"She has been writing it since the thirtieth of July."

"She has been writing it for nine years and you have been the only thing on it for five of them."

He went out of the muster room at about ten past eight with nine days to the last working day of the quarter, and the whole of the volume's arithmetic in his head, and one figure he could not get out of it by doing sums.
