# The Man Who Was In The Fourth Seat

The fourth line of the weekly return had said *vacant* since the twelfth of February and there was a reason for it that four divisions could look up and only one man could not, and on the morning of the twenty-fourth of March he came and found him at the foot of the sixty-one steps with a bad back and no umbrella.

Bertram Sallow had put a dated entry in a keeping book in February declaring the seat vacant, in his own hand, with the date against it, which is a thing this book has not seen a man do before.

"I have come about the fourth line," he said.

"So have I. Third in three weeks."

"Not for that." He stood at the bottom of the steps in the rain for about four seconds before he said it. "I am not asking you for anything. I want you to come and stand in a corridor tomorrow while a woman reads a line out of a book, and I am telling you that it is going to be the worst four minutes of the year of either of us and that I would rather there were two people there."

## The Quarterly Re-Read

There is a quarterly re-read in that room and it happens on the first working day of a quarter, and it is not a sitting and it has no ninth minute and nobody votes on it. A clerk goes down the book and reads each member's standing at that member's own entry, in order, in the ordinary voice.

On the first of March the fourth entry read at a woman who had withdrawn her own seal on the twenty-third of December, and on the fourth of February it read at a man who had not been in the room since the twenty-third of December.

And on the first of February the clerk came to the fourth entry and read the whole of it, including the part underneath the standing, which is the part that says what a member may not do, and she read it out in a room with eight people in it.

*A member of this room is not the officer who certifies a composition at any address.*

"There is no second line under it," said Bertram Sallow. "It is one line and it has been in that book since before either of us and it is not new and it is not aimed at anybody. And I had certified the composition of a house in Cordwain on the thirtieth of January because the woman who keeps that address has stood at that counter for twenty-six years and she asked me, and I did it, and it was correct, and I signed it."

"You are the officer who certifies a composition at Cordwain."

"I was, for one quarter, at the end of January, and no one asked me to be and no one sent me and I did it because a woman I have known for nine years asked me in a doorway." He put his hands in his pockets.

"On the first of February a clerk read it out in a room, and after she had read it she said *the fourth is vacant*, and wrote it in the book, and that was the twelfth of February and there has been a word in that room every week since."

## What A Chair Cannot Do

"You could have asked the chair to strike the entry."

"I could have asked the chair to strike about four words out of a book," said Bertram Sallow.

"And the chair cannot. A standing is not hers. She has never had a member's standing in her hand and neither has the clerk, and there is nothing in this city a chair can put one back on, and there is not one on which the man can put his own back. That is the single difference between this room and a division, and a division could have me back inside a week."

"Then who."

"The Council." He said it the way a man says a month. "Nine people who are not this room and meet in another one, and they do not sit until the first of April."

"Six days."

"Six days, and then the first of April, and then they sit, and then it is a matter for nine people who will want a day and a half with it, and I have not spent nine years being short of nothing."

## What He Says Now

"You have been short four months," said Lin Hao.

"I have been short since the twelfth of February and I have been at a desk in Cordwain doing another division's work since the fourteenth, because an office will not give a man back a standing and will happily give him the work." He said it without heat.

"And I am going to tell you the thing that is in that room, because you are the only man in this city who cannot do anything about it and that is the only kind of man I have anything to say to this month."

"Say it."

"It has been one short for six weeks and it has carried nothing and decided nothing and administered nothing."

She turned the cup round. "Every week the clerk writes *the room stands at three* at the foot of it, and every week that three goes into a copy that goes to four divisions. In about four years somebody with a folder is going to read twenty-six returns in a row that all say three, and he is not going to work out that the fourth line had a name on it until the fourth year."

"Somebody put a name in it yesterday morning."

Bertram Sallow looked at him.

"Marguerite Vale," said Lin Hao. "She wrote her own name in the fourth line of a return in a room at the end of a corridor at about nine yesterday morning, on her own account, and I was in a washroom on the fifth floor of the Fourth Branch and she told me about it on a table with four chairs on it."

"And that is not a seat."

"That is not a seat."

"Then somebody with a folder in four years is going to read a guild broker's name in the fourth line of a return about a requisition against eight women at a kitchen table," said Bertram Sallow, "and he is going to get there eventually, and he is going to be right, and I could not stop it, and it is the best news I have had since the twelfth of February and I would like to be recorded as having said that."

## What It Is Worth

"And the other thing is that I cannot ask you to do anything about it either," he said, before Lin Hao had decided whether he was going to.

"You can ask."

"You can say no."

"He turned round and looked up sixty-one steps at a kitchen window with the light on in it."

"I have been in that building on the twentieth of February and on the eighteenth of March."

"Nobody at that table looked at me."

"Not once."

Eight women have been living under that roof since October and not one of them has ever turned round at a man standing in their hall.

"I have been a member of a room that can hold a requisition for four months and I cannot get the attention of a woman at a kitchen table," said Bertram Sallow, "and both of those are because of the same thing. It is not your fault and it is not mine and it is the whole of what a standing is."

"Then what did you come up the steps for."

"To tell you that it is not coming back before the first of April, and that there are nine people in another building who will have a day and a half with it."

He put his hands back in his pockets.

"And if I were you I would spend the six days I have got on something that is not this, and I would like it written down that a man who has been short for six weeks told a man with a standing to go and spend his last week somewhere else."

## Alma Quayle Reads It Out

Alma Quayle read the entry out in the corridor off the second landing at about ten in the morning, in her own building, in the voice she uses for anything she means to be on the record, and Bertram Sallow stood at the far end of it and did not come closer than about nine feet.

That was the arrangement and neither of them argued about it. A member whose standing has been read as vacant is not in the room when it is read, and Alma Quayle had ruled that on the fourteenth of February in one sentence and had not improved on it since.

"Bertram Sallow, fourth. Standing held. No seal. Certified a composition at Cordwain at the end of January, and an officer who certifies a composition may not stand in this room, and the standing is vacant from the first of February and remains vacant until the Council sits."

She read it twice, the way a clerk reads, and put the book down and wrote nothing on the face of it.

Lin Hao was in the passage and Alma Quayle looked at him for about four seconds.

"I can do that for him," she said. "Because he is not you."

"I know."

"Do you." She squared the book. "I put my own signature under a recusal on this building's paper on the fifth of March and a clerk wrote it into a minute, and there is not one thing in this city that will take me off that, and the only person who can is a man who is not in my division and has not asked and will not."

"You told him that on the fifth."

"I told a man who was not you, in a corridor, with a clerk writing, which is the only form any of this comes in." She closed the book. "It has been six weeks and I have not walked into that room once, and it is not a punishment, and I want you to hear that from me once, because in about four years the whole of what I did will be described by a man as having thrown something away."

Six days to the last working day of the quarter, and a room that was going to be three when it crossed, and the only door into four was nine people in another building who did not sit until the day after it crossed.
