# The Four Lines He Was Never Shown

The index came back on the twenty-sixth of February at about twenty past two in the afternoon, folded into a cover with a line across the flap, because an index is the only part of a file that a division will let out of a sub-level and nobody in this city has ever thought to ask it for one.

The clerk read the wrapper out. It said what had been asked for, from which sub-level, on which date, and it said that the thing sent in answer was an index of a held account and that the account was not moving.

A file does not leave the Classification Wing. An index is not a file.

## Two Lines Of It Out Of Twelve

There were twelve lines on the index and they were three years old, and every one of them was a thing that had gone into a room and had not come out.

He read them off the clerk's mouth one at a time because he asked her to and because that was faster than looking.

The first line was a departmental consult, three years back, headed with a file and underneath the file with a number, and the number was one digit.

The second line was the draft of an answer to it. Four lines. Initialled in the margin against the fourth.

The third line was the reply. Four lines. Signed by the responsible officer.

And then eight lines of nothing at all in three years, which is the part of an index that nobody ever reads out, because eight lines of nothing is the most ordinary thing in the world and is not a finding and is not a decision and is not anybody's fault.

He stood in the middle of that room and understood that he had been looking at a gap for three years and had never once called it a gap.

## The Third Line

"I want the reply," he said.

"You do not get the reply."

"It is on the index."

"It is on the index the way a name is on a form," said the clerk. "The index says a thing happened. It does not let you have the thing. That is the difference between a list and a file and it is the only difference there has ever been."

"Then I demand it."

"You may demand it," she said, "and I am going to write it out, and it will go out at four this afternoon, and it will come back on the twenty-eighth of this month or the first of March, and it will be read out in this room and entered in the minute, and it will stay in this room. You understand that last part?"

"That it stays in this room."

"That it stays in this room," said the clerk, "and that you do not take it out of it, and that nine people will have read it and you will have had it read to you once. That is what production is. Nobody in this building has ever produced anything for anybody and I have read the rule about it four times in eleven years and I have never once seen it done."

He stood there.

"That is the ordinary right I bought."

"It is," she said. "It is not a right to a copy. There is no instrument in this city that gives you a copy of what is produced in this room, and there never has been, and if there were one then the four of you who are on the list of eight would have had to learn a completely different city from the one they live in. Ask again on the twenty-eighth."

He did not ask again on the twenty-eighth.

## The Twenty-Eighth

The four lines came back on the twenty-eighth of February at about eleven minutes past two in the afternoon and the clerk read them out in a corridor, standing, with the door open behind her, because a production is read out where it is produced and not in the room.

They were four lines. She read them slowly, the way she reads anything that came out of a sub-level, with a pause at each full stop that made them sound older than they were.

*A transfer of this shape cannot be answered from the receiving end. This house holds no figure and no composition and nothing that can be put a question.*

*The question as headed asks about a destination. A destination is not a question. It is an address, and this house does not hold addresses.*

*The matter is therefore filed and not answered, and no division is instructed.*

*The responsible officer asks to be left off the distribution of anything further upon it.*

Then she stopped and looked at him.

"That is all four lines," she said. "Say something, because you have gone a colour and I have read out four lines at a great many counters in this building and nobody has ever gone a colour over any of them."

## What They Do Not Say

He said nothing for a while, and it was not the silence he had used four times that year at a kitchen table. It was the silence of a man who has been holding a thing for three years and has just been shown that it was never the thing he thought it was.

He had believed, for three years, that those four lines were the answer.

He had built an entire life on the belief. He had gone up sixty-one steps to eight women on the strength of it.

He had stood in a hall as a boy with a number in his head and a shape he could not put down, and he had carried it as a question about a figure for as long as he had been in this city, and at some point inside the last year he had understood that it was not a question, it was a receipt.

And he had been right about that, and being right about that had been the whole of what he had.

The four lines did not say what the account was.

The four lines said that the office could not answer, and had stopped, and had asked to be let go.

It was the same four lines. They had been in a drawer or a file or a wing for three years and they had never once contained a single word about the account.

## The Second Line Was About The Receipt In His Hand

*A destination is not a question. It is an address, and this house does not hold addresses.*

He stood in that corridor and understood that a man had written that sentence three years ago and had been right about it, and that on the twenty-third of February a clerk at the second table in this room had read him the answer to it.

The receipt had come back into this building five days ago. It had come back because the chair would not carry a requisition on a paper that grounds nothing, and it had come back with a destination on it, and the destination was a file at the foot of a list.

And the reason a division had sent a receipt and not a file was that files do not go out and receipts do.

Four lines written by a man on a research floor three years ago had decided the shape of the instrument that came back for the holding of a requisition laid five days ago. He had been living inside the consequences of them for five days without knowing that they were consequences.

"You said it did not come out of the first sub-level."

"It did not."

"It came out of the third," said the clerk. "Every receipt for a held account comes out of the third, and every one of them says where the account is administered and what it was administered at, and that has been the shape of the form since before either of us was in a building."

She squared the keeping book and did not look up.

"You have read a thing today that is older than this room and you have been standing in my corridor as though it were about him. There has not been a word about him in it for three years."

## The Last Of The Four


The last of them was the one that had been doing the work.

A man who asks to be left off the distribution is not declining a question. He is declining the next twenty of them.

And a division that has removed a man from the distribution of a file does not hand the file to the next man, because the next man was the man who asked, so the file stays exactly where it is.

And a file that nobody is on the distribution of is a file that is looked at about once every four years by a person whose entire job on the morning of that day is to turn to a page.

And that was the whole of three years. Not a conspiracy and not a cover-up and not a man protecting anybody. An officer who could not answer a question asked in a corridor to be removed from the list, was removed, and went on working on a floor thirty feet above a woman who drafted answers to a transfer carrying no name, and neither of them ever knew.

"He did not stop because he had written something down," said Lin Hao.

"No," said the clerk.

"He stopped because he had written that he could not."

"That is what four lines are," said the clerk. "They are what a man writes when he has run out of things he is allowed to say and has not run out of ways to say them."

## What It Costs To Have Asked

She entered it in the minute at twenty minutes past two in the afternoon and it went into the keeping book and it cannot be taken out of that book, and in about four years it will be copied out with everything else in it, and a stranger with a folder will read four lines written by an officer of a research house three years ago saying that the office could not answer a question about a transfer that carried no name.

And the stranger will want to know whose transfer it was.

There is nothing in those four lines that says. That is what an unanswerable question looks like in a file. It is a gap with a signature at the top of it.

He stood in that corridor for a long time after she had gone back into the room, and what he could not get out of his head was not the four lines.

It was the second line of the index, read out in a room at ten past nine on the morning of the twenty-sixth and not understood then and understood now.

*Draft of an answer to the consult of three years back. Four lines. Initialled in the margin against the fourth.*

Above the four lines of Perrin Sowe, on the same sheet, in a different hand and in a smaller one, there were three characters and a full stop.
