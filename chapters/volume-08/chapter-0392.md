# The Clause He Wrote For Himself

On the afternoon of the fifteenth of March he said a thing out loud at his own kitchen table, with eight women round it, and Beatrix Halm told him he had said it in a room and not in a corridor, and that she had liked the room.

Seven days later he went and said it in a corridor.

The room where nine people sit is at the end of a passage off the second landing, and the passage is about eleven feet wide and has a window at one end and a bench against the other, and the bench has a crack in it in the shape of the city. Beatrix Halm was sitting on the bench when he came up the stair with a folder under his arm that he had not opened and had not intended to open.

"You have got the wrong folder."

"I know which folder I have got."

"You have brought the folder you use for the branch at the end of a week," said Beatrix Halm. "You have brought it because it is the one that is always in the bag and you have not brought it into this building at the end of a week since January." She got up off the bench. "I am going to stand at the window, because it is the only part of this corridor where a person is not in the room."

## What He Came For

A subject who has been served with a requisition may demand production of the paper the fourth part is grounded on, and the chair may add the holding to that demand and must produce what the holding stands on. He had used that on the twenty-first of February and it had brought back a ground, a receipt, an index and four lines, and he had none of them in his hands, because a production is read out where it is produced and stays in the room.

He came back to it on the twenty-second of March for about nine words on the foot of the last of four sheets of plain paper, and he had thought about it for seven days, and the reason he had thought about it for seven days is that he already knew what it would do.

"A production is read out where it is produced," he said. "It goes into the minute. A clerk copies the minute out, the copy is kept for four years, and on the thirtieth the whole of what is on that sheet goes out of this room into four divisions at four in the afternoon."

"Yes."

"And there is no copy, so I cannot have read them in a corridor and kept them."

"No."

## The Clerk And The Foot

The clerk's name is not in this chapter. She had been in the room nineteen months and she read the requisition out on the seventeenth of March at eight minutes to ten and had numbered it the thirteenth of those the room's clerk has ever numbered, and she turned a folder round on the table under the window and did not look at him at any point.

There were four sheets of plain paper in it and they had been in that keeping since five minutes to eleven on the evening of the twenty-ninth of December, and eight of the nine had signed underneath them on the same night and on the fourteenth of January, and there was a gap in the line two fingers wide.

She read the head of the first one, which is his name and an address, and then she stopped and turned to the fourth and went down to the foot of it and read it out in the flat voice of a woman reading a printed line, twice, because the first time she had said the middle of it wrong.

*I am the man at the top of this page*, and then the rest of it, which is that I will answer for it, and there is nothing else on the paper, and it is in his own hand, and there is a date at the head of it and the date is the twenty-ninth of December.

Eight words. He had said in a kitchen on the fifteenth of March that he was going to answer for it and did not know how, and he had said that in a room in front of eight women and had asked for it to be noticed, and Isolde Marchetti had noticed it.

The room had got it eleven days late and it had got it from a clerk.

## What The Chair Would Not Do

"That is entered," said Anouk Ferreira, from the chair, in about four seconds.

"That is entered as produced at the demand of the subject," said the clerk.

"Not as adopted."

"Not as adopted, chair."

The chair turned the sheet round so that the foot was facing the room and looked at the eight words for about as long as a woman looks at a line of print that belongs to a man who is not in the room.

"I will not have that carried in as a minute of this room," said Anouk Ferreira.

"This room has not adopted eight words written in a kitchen at eleven at night by a man who lost his standing on the first of November. A record that a subject demanded production of the foot of a sheet is a record that a subject demanded it. That is all it is, and it is entered, and the difference between that and what you would like it to say is the entire content of this building."

"Then what does it go out as."

"It goes out as produced at the demand of the subject, entered in the ordinary course, on the twenty-second of March, with the minute." She put her pen down. "On the thirtieth of March it goes out with everything else on that sheet, to four divisions, at four in the afternoon, and there is not one of those four divisions that will ever know the difference between what you would have liked and what you got, because they will not be looking at it."

## What He Had Come For

The clerk waited with her hand flat on the fourth sheet.

"What I came for is this," he said, "and I am saying it before you go on, because you have read it out already and I would rather it went in the record that I asked."

"That is already entered. You asked."

"Then let me say it and let it go in twice."

Nobody stopped him and nobody encouraged him, and that was the room.

"I did not come to hear eight words I wrote at a table at eleven at night," said Lin Hao.

"I have said them out loud twice since the day I wrote them. I came because they are the only part of anything I have put into this building that is in my own hand at the bottom instead of the top, and a hand at the bottom is the part a stranger looks at, and I wanted to be in a room where somebody had to look at it while a woman was saying it out loud."

"Entered," said the clerk.

"Not the reason. The demand."

"The demand," said the clerk.

## In The Corridor With The Door Open

He came out into the passage and Beatrix Halm was at the window with her back to it and her notebook shut in her hands.

"You said it in a corridor."

"I said it in a corridor."

"I know," said Beatrix Halm. "I could hear the clerk through a door that has been painted four times and I have been sitting on that bench since a quarter past two."

She did not open the notebook. She turned it over once in her hands and put it under her arm.

"I have not written it down," she said. "I want you to understand that I have wanted to write it down every day since the fifteenth, and that is the first thing I have ever wanted to write in this book that is not a figure, and I have not done it. Here is the reason and then I am finished with the subject for this month."

"Tell me."

"Because on the morning of the nineteenth of March a man wrote three lines on the back of a goods docket in pencil in this city and then wrote a fourth line under them with his own name on it, and Wren Kallo told him what that is."

She did not look at him.

"A piece of paper with a name on it can be produced by anybody who finds it. And I have been sitting on a bench outside a room with a pencil in my pocket since the fifteenth of March and I have been able to hear myself not writing eight words on a page."

"That is not the same thing."

"It is exactly the same thing," said Beatrix Halm, "and it is the only rule I have got, and I am not going to be the woman in this house who puts the foot of a sheet into a notebook when the man who wrote it could not put three facts into a pencil without his name under them, and you know that, because you did not do it either until ninety seconds after you had promised a woman with no gift that you would not."

## Eight Days

He went down the stair at about twenty past four with nothing in his hands, which is the ordinary result of a production and which he has now had four times.

There is a keeper of a counting room two floors under an Exchange who answers questions from him in writing, and on the nineteenth of February she wrote that he was not to send her a third question in that week. He had thought about going down there at the end of the week and about what he would ask, and on the morning in question he worked out that he had no third question that was not an argument dressed up as one.

So there was no third question, and there was a corridor, and a sheet.

The minute goes out on the ordinary stream at four in the afternoon.

A clerk copies it out, the copy is kept for four years, and by the time the thirtieth of March is out of the morning it is in four divisions, and in about four years a stranger with a folder will read a line that says the man at the top of the page asked a room to read the foot of his own page out loud, and will not know whether a room thought better of the eight words than he did.

Eight days.

Beatrix Halm read a wall at a quarter to six every morning for nine years and had written his plate under it twice each time since the fifteenth of November, and on the thirtieth of March she was going to write 8,131 under the floor of 8,146 and the two words under that, and the eight words he had put in a corridor were going out of this building at four in the afternoon whether the woman holding the notebook liked it or not.
