# She Enters It As Given

He had been in that room three times with a sentence in his coat and once with a sheet of nothing, and on the thirtieth of August he came in at about ten with neither.

Two thousand three hundred and sixty-two was the twenty-third of August and the thirtieth is day two thousand three hundred and sixty-nine, and two thousand three hundred and sixty-nine less two thousand three hundred and sixty-two is seven, and thirty less twenty-three is seven because a month is thirty days and has no thirty-first.

## Three Things With Commas In Them

He had thought about it for seven days and he had not once come to the end of a sentence.

What he had come to instead was a list, and he had written it out on the back of his own hand in pencil, which is what he has been writing on since the seventh of April, and it had come out of him in about four minutes in the first week and he had not been able to improve it since.

It was not a sentence. That was the whole of his cleverness and he had known that it was the whole of it on the first day and had spent a week hoping it was enough.

*Between the ninth of January and the sixteenth of August, four hundred and nine pieces of paper. Each a copy of the foot of the twenty-fourth sheet, each with writing in the two inches. None of them is a deed.*

He put the back of his hand on the table rather than a sheet of paper, because a sheet would have been a thing she would have to refuse.

Wenna Raut read it without touching it, which is what she does, and she read it twice.

She had one question a morning. She had had it since about nine and she did not spend it on the list, and he understood why about two seconds later than she understood it.

“It goes in the book,” she said.

“Yes.”

“It does not go in the column.”

“No.”

“I am going to tell you why I have not spent my question,” said Wenna Raut, “and then I am going to go back to my column. If I ask you whether you want it in the column you will say yes or no and either way you have asked me a question in a room where I do not take them.”

“You have come in here with three things with commas in them and that is the first thing since the ninth of January that was not a sentence, and I am going to enter it as it is and I am not going to make a speech about it.”

“Enter it.”

“I am going to enter it.”

## Four Minutes And A Read-Back

It took her four minutes and she did it in the order it was given to her, which is the whole of what a clerk is.

A date. The ninth of January.

A number. Four hundred and nine, of pieces of paper.

And then the third of the three, which went into the same field as the first two because there was nowhere else on the form for a thing that is not a claim, and which she entered exactly as she was given it and without a stop in the middle of it.

*None of them is a deed.*

She wrote the last four words in the same pen and at the same speed as everything else and did not underline them, and then she squared the book to the edge of the table and read it back to him in the order it was in.

“That is in the Register,” she said.

“That is in the Register of this office and it is in my hand and it is dated to day and it has a number against it and a sentence against the number.”

“It is the first thing that has gone into that column in this building since before I came to it, and I have entered it exactly as you gave it to me and I have not improved one word of it and nobody is going to be able to say in four years that anybody in this room added anything.”

“That is what I asked for.”

“No,” said Wenna Raut. “That is what you got. You asked for a holding in June and I gave you a drawer. What you have got is an entry, and an entry cannot be taken back, and you knew that on the seventh of April in a kitchen off Ferrier Street when a woman told you a number in this city can be a number somebody made up on purpose, and you have been managing what is in a drawer ever since.”

She went back to her column and did not say anything else about it for about nine minutes, and he stood there and did not go out.

## The Sentence That Stopped Being True

He was not proud of what he felt and he was not going to be able to put a name on it later, and it was not triumph and it was not the thing he had gone in expecting.

The second of the four sentences had been at the foot of that page since before this office existed. It says, in other words, that where the Register has nothing in it then nobody has been given anything.

For six years it had been the one sentence this office had that nobody in this city had ever been able to argue with, and Yusuf Adeyemi had said in this room in February that it was the truest thing on the sheet, and about nine people a week read down to it and had read it about a person who went to a counter and was refused.

At ten past eleven on the thirtieth of August it stopped being true.

Not of the city. It was still true of every person who had ever gone to a counter in this city and been refused and gone home with nothing, and it was going to go out at four on the thirtieth of September with that meaning intact and about nine hundred people would read it.

False of this office. Of this building. Of the thing that prints it.

Four hundred and nine pieces of paper had been given to it in seven months and it had entered a receipt for them and it had a bag at the front door with eleven more in it on an ordinary morning, and it was now a body in a city that had been given something, and it could never once again say in its own hand that it had not.

He had spent two years and one sentence trying to stop a city believing something nobody could check, and what he had done on the thirtieth of August was make the truest sentence on the most widely read page in Ostara untrue about the only building in Ostara that printed it.

Nobody had asked him to.

## Four Things Said At The End Of The Table

Yusuf Adeyemi had been at the end of the table with his coat on since about a quarter to eleven and had read the back of Lin Hao’s hand upside down without moving his head, which he can do and has done for eleven months.

“I am going to say four things and not one of them is about whether you were right.”

“All right.”

“The first is that the Register is not empty and there has not been a day since this office opened that the Register was not empty, and you have put three lines in a column that had two, and one of the three is yours,” said Yusuf Adeyemi.

“The second is that there is no form anywhere between this room and the works department on which an office may say a fact about itself, and there has not been one in six years, and you have not asked for one and you are not going to get one.”

He held up two fingers.

“The third is that an entry can be made and an entry cannot be taken back, and that is not a rule in this city, it is the way paper is, and I told you it in the seventh month of a year I am not going to name and you have now proved it.”

“And the fourth.”

“The fourth is that an entry with nothing at the foot of it is a receipt, and a receipt is about paper,” said Yusuf Adeyemi. “It is the safest kind of entry there is and it does you no harm at all, and I want you to sit with that for a minute before you go down the stair.”

“In about four years a man is going to stand at a counter and ask what this office ever did with four hundred and nine pieces of paper, and the answer will be on that line in a clerk’s hand, and it will be *we were given them, here is the number, none of them is a deed*, and that will be true.”

“It will be the truest answer anybody in this city has ever been given about anything, and not one person in this city will be able to point at it and say whose idea it was.”

He turned the pencil over between two fingers, which is a thing he does with a pencil.

“You will want to put a name on it one day,” he said. “There is no form for that either. Do come and see me before you do it.”

*Two thousand three hundred and sixty-nine days. Less two thousand three hundred and sixty-two, the twenty-third of August of the seventh year, is seven, and thirty less twenty-three is seven because a month runs out at its thirtieth.*

*Less the thirtieth of March of the seventh year, two thousand two hundred and nineteen, is a hundred and fifty days, which is five months exactly, and the twenty-fifth sheet went up on a rail at four on that morning with eight hundred and fifty-two at the head of it and two lines in the second column.*

*Less the thirtieth of December of the sixth year, two thousand one hundred and twenty-nine, is two hundred and forty days, which is eight months exactly. That is the twenty-fourth sheet and about four hundred kitchens in this city have the foot of it written out in a hand that is not the office’s.*

## What The Third Of Four Sentences Lets A Person Do

He went down and along at about half past eleven and he stood in the passage off Cordwain where a sheet has been lying out on a rail since a March in a year nobody behind that counter mentions.

There was a man at the counter who had a pencil in his hand and a book with about thirty names in it, and he had a copy of his own face up on the counter which he takes off the rail in the morning and puts back on it before he goes, and he did not put anything down when Lin Hao came along.

“Second of the fifth month of the year before this one,” the man at the desk said. “The woman who came to me with a bundle and wanted a receipt for having left it. You have not been down here since the twelfth of May.”

“I have not.”

“You have had a face on you since May that I have not seen before,” said the man at the desk, “and I have got about four minutes before the next of them, because a woman with a bundle comes about twice a week now and she has started to come at eleven, and I am not going to spend my morning on a man who has not asked me anything.”

He squared the pencil against the edge of the counter.

“Offices are not the only thing in this city with a drawer in it,” he said.

He said it without emphasis, and went back to his book, and about eleven minutes later he looked up and said, without any preamble at all:

“Say that on a wall at ten to six and about forty people will have it by six and not one of them will have a number in front of it.”
