# A Copy In A Demand

Seven days had gone since a man in a delivery coat had told him at a wall end that nine hundred people had done the thing he does alone, and had asked him to find out what it was called, and since then he had not been able to stop asking the only question that had an answer in it.

Two thousand two hundred and seventy-five was the twenty-sixth of May and the third of June is day two thousand two hundred and eighty-two, and two thousand two hundred and eighty-two less two thousand two hundred and seventy-five is seven, and thirty less twenty-six is four, and four added to three is seven by the month count.

Has a copy ever been used on anything.

He had been assuming for four months that about four hundred people were making them and waiting for him. There was another possibility and it had been sitting in the room off a third court in a drawer since the first of February.

## A Demand Is Where You Put What You Cannot Put Anywhere Else

No sheet of paper in Ostara may be put before a magistrate by a landlord except a demand. Not a claim. Not an account. Not a return. A demand says pay, or here is what happens instead, and it is the one thing a landlord is permitted to hand across a counter and point at.

Which means a demand is where a person puts the things they cannot put anywhere else.

And the bottom of the office’s standing sheet is the only printed paper in this city that says what the office records, and it goes out at four on the last working day of a quarter to about two thousand counters, and the people who get all the way to the bottom of it in this city number about nine in a week.

Somebody in this city had taken the bottom of it and put it where a landlord’s paper goes.

## Eleven In The Morning At A Counter Off A Third Court

Somebody knocked on the front door at about eleven, which has never happened. That door stands on a court with three other doors on it, and about two thousand people go past the front of it every working day, and not one of them has ever knocked on it or on any of the other three.

The man who came in had a clerk’s coat on and a badge and a bad manner that was not unkind, and he looked about as old as the building he was standing in, and he set the sheet down on the side of the counter by the door and straightened the edge of it with two fingers, and stopped there, in the doorway.

“From a counter off this court,” he said. “I am not serving anything. I want you to know that and I want it in your own book if you have one. That has been left and I have brought it back because the address on it is this building.”

“Why has it been left.”

“Because the address on it is this building,” said the clerk. “A thing gets left where it is sent. I have walked it back from the counter and I have not put it into anybody’s hands and I have not read it, and I do not know whose hand it is in and neither do you.”

He went out and shut the door.

## What Was At The Head Of It

At the head of it, in a clerk’s hand, in the ordinary form, was the name and the address of the office of the Registrar of Unrecorded Merit, in a room off a third court.

Under the address it said something about a tenancy and a sum and a period, and the sum was not large, and the period was about nine months.

And in the middle of it, pasted on, about four inches by two, was the foot of a printed sheet.

Four sentences at the top of the paste in the office’s own hand. Six lines under those, and the fourth of the six run through end to end, with a name written under the stroke. And below all of that, in pencil, in an ordinary kitchen pencil, in about six inches of nothing at the bottom of the paste, about nine words in a hand that is not the office’s.

He read it four times.

The nine words said that a woman had lived in two rooms off a street in this city for six years and had paid nothing for the water and had had it on and off eleven times and had been given a date and an hour for every one of the eleven times by an office off a court, and that there was no figure anywhere on it, and that the office had never sent anybody to look.

He knew what he was looking at. It was a copy.

It was the foot of the twenty-fourth sheet written out by hand and then written on, and it had been in a pocket in this city since about the ninth of January, and it had come back that morning to the front door of a building with no door onto a street, attached to a landlord’s demand. A person cannot get a receipt for anything, and that was the only paper in the city that said an office had ever heard of her.

## The Second Sentence He Wrote And What It Now Describes

The second of the four at the foot of that sheet says, in other words, that where the Register is empty then nobody has been handed anything.

He wrote it. It has been in this building’s hand since before this building opened and it goes out at four on the thirtieth of every quarter, and about nine people a week read it to the bottom of.

It was written about the people it was meant for, which is somebody who turned up at a counter, got turned down, and went home without a paper of any kind.

From this morning there is a sheet of paper in a room off a third court that says the office was handed something, on the twenty-first of May, by a woman who cannot read, in a set of rooms off a street where she had been turned off and on eleven times.

## A Person Who Could Not Read It

He read the four sentences on the paste and the six lines and the strike and the name, and he read the nine words underneath, and there was not one figure anywhere on the whole sheet except the sum the landlord wanted.

He could not tell whether the woman who wrote the nine words could read the four sentences.

That was the part of it that took him about nine seconds standing in that room. If she could read, then she had taken four sentences off a printed sheet and put them in the middle of a landlord’s demand as an argument, and she had chosen them, and that was an act.

If she could not read, then she had carried a piece of paper to a counter off a court and had it pasted into a demand because somebody who could read told her that the office had written down what had been done to her, and she had no way of knowing that the office could not say so back, and she had put the whole of the office’s case in front of a magistrate with nothing behind it at all.

Both of those were in the room with him and he could not get rid of either one, and there is no instrument in this city that can tell him which of them he is looking at, and he is not going to build one in an afternoon.

## The Room With A Table In It

He went down at about half past twelve with the sheet in a folder.

There is a counter off the same court and behind it a room with a table in it, and the table has about nine feet of nothing round it, and a clerk in the room writes at the far end of it.

He was not permitted into the room and no one refused him. The clerk at the door said the room was not open until the sitting and that there was no list and that a man who was not on anything could wait at the counter, and he waited at the counter for about an hour and a quarter.

Nobody asked him anything. That was notable. He had come down a stair with a spent chest expecting to be asked one question, because every man behind a public counter in this city has one and may ask it once, and the man at the door had not asked it and had said nothing at all about having one.

## A Thing That Has Been Named And Not Been Told

It is not the hearing. There is no hearing this month and there may not be one, and he does not know and cannot ask, and there is no list anywhere that he is on and no paper has been served on this office by anybody.

And that is the whole of what is wrong with it.

The office is named at the top of a demand. Not as a witness and not as a party and not because anybody put it there. Because the address at the head of every page the office prints is this building, and a woman with a pencil could not write the office’s address and could read it off her own copy, and pasted the foot of the page under a landlord’s demand because it was the only paper she had that said anybody had ever heard of her.

Somebody in this city has used his sentence to make a demand stick.

And nobody has told the office. A thing that has been named in a demand and has not been told is a thing that will be told, on a morning it did not choose, by a clerk at a counter with one question, in a room with a table in it.

By then there will be about four hundred and nine pieces of pencil paper in a drawer, and a line in a column headed carried by with a date on it and nothing whatever else on it, and a clerk who entered it exactly as given.

*Two thousand two hundred and eighty-two days. Less two thousand two hundred and seventy-five, the twenty-sixth of May of the seventh year, is seven, and thirty less twenty-six is four, and four added to three is seven by the month count.*

*Less the ninth of November of the sixth year, two thousand and seventy-eight, is two hundred and four days, which is six months with twenty-four days after them. A shutter at the far end of a yard has had nothing at the foot of it for all of them.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is five hundred and sixty-four days, which is eighteen months with twenty-four days after them. It is the third of four sentences at the foot of a sheet, and about four hundred copies of that foot are in about four hundred pockets in this city, and one of them has been pasted into the only piece of paper a landlord is allowed to put in front of a magistrate, and the office is named at the head of it, and nobody pasted the office there.*
