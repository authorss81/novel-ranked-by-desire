# The Sentence She Cites

Six days had gone since he stood in a lane at ten to six and heard a man in a delivery coat say he wanted a sheet and would not ask for it.

Two thousand two hundred was the eleventh of March and the seventeenth is day two thousand two hundred and six, and two thousand two hundred and six less two thousand two hundred is six, and seventeen less eleven is six by the month count.

The branch office off a third court was quiet at about two in the afternoon, with the counter closed and the passage empty, and Ines Duarte was in the audit room with the door shut, working through the branch returns with her cuffs fastened and her sleeves to the wrist, the way she had worked since the first of October of the third year without either of them saying anything about it.

Lin Hao stood outside the door a while before he knocked, because what he meant to ask could only be asked once, and in a room with a shut door in it, and not in a corridor, since a sentence said in a corridor at two in the afternoon is a sentence a corridor has heard.

She opened the door and looked at him without surprise and let him in and shut it again behind him.

## What He Asks

“I want to ask whether a copy is evidence,” he said, without sitting, because sitting would have made it a visit and it was not a visit.

She did not answer at once, but went back to her table and set her pencil aside, not flat, but aside, the way a woman sets a thing down when she will take it up again inside a minute, and folded her hands.

Her fingers were dark at the tips of the first two on the right hand, the way they had been eleven years, soap never lifting it, and there was grey at her left temple with fine lines at it, and neither mark was anything but hers, and he had never once lied to her and she had never once lied to him.

“You will not answer me in a corridor and you will not answer me for free,” he said. “I know that. I am asking here, once, because you are the only instrument in Ostara that can check a slate, and if you say it is not then everything next is already decided and it is better to know it here.”

She studied him a long moment, and her face was grave and unguarded, and what she felt was not triumph and not pity, but something older, the regard of a woman who has audited a branch three years and has said nothing about it because saying it would have been a claim.

“I carry one question since the seventh of March of the fifth year,” she said. “It is about a man and not about a sentence, and I have not asked it and I do not ask it here. And I am not the instrument. I am the woman who holds it, and the distinction is the whole of my standing.”

He nodded, because he knew it, and waited, because waiting was the only thing he could do in that room, and his chest ached dully with the cold he always carried there now.

## Her Answer

She picked up the copy he had laid on the table, a copy of the foot of the twenty-fourth sheet in a hand not the office’s, with a date and a place in two inches of nothing, and held it without reading it, because she had read four hundred like it and did not need to read this one.

“A copy is a thing somebody put together on purpose,” she said. “That is my answer, and it is the only instrument I have.”

Then she said, in her own mouth, clearly, without raising her voice, the sentence he had written to free a woman in number four from having to prove a thing, with a man’s name under it, as it stood at the foot of that sheet since the ninth of November of the fifth year.

“A figure in Ostara may be a figure somebody made up on purpose. This standing sheet is printed by the office itself. Emil Brack.”

He could not answer it.

It was his sentence, and he had written it to stop a city believing a claim it could not check, and she had turned it round without cruelty and without pleasure, because it was true, and because it was the sentence that let her refuse him, and there was nothing behind it to argue with.

He understood it all at once, plainly.

He had spent four months believing the sentence at the foot was a warning to the city and it was a shield, and the woman holding it up to him was the one person who had never once lied to him.

She certified the sheet. She said nothing at all about the copies, and the nothing at all was a position, and he felt grief come up in him, quiet and complete, because losing an argument in his own office to a woman who loved him was the correct cost of having written it.

“I have read it four hundred times,” she said, more gently. “You have read it out loud to two men in two rooms since the ninth of November, you and a man in a delivery coat, and neither of you read it. That is the difference between reading and saying.”

He went out past the counter into the cold afternoon with nothing in his hands and everything in his chest, and what he wanted was to unsay what he had written, and he knew as he wanted it that unsaying is not an entry any clerk can make.

*Two thousand two hundred and six days. Less two thousand two hundred, the eleventh of March of year seven, is six. Seventeen less eleven is six by the month count, because a month is thirty days and has no thirty-first.*
