# An Entry Cannot Be Taken Back

There was a thing he had been carrying since the twelfth of September and he had written it out nine times and he had understood the whole of it at about eleven o’clock that morning, which was four hours before he went down the stair with it.

Two thousand four hundred and thirteen was the fourteenth of October and the twenty-first is day two thousand four hundred and twenty, and two thousand four hundred and twenty less two thousand four hundred and thirteen is seven, and twenty-one less fourteen is seven by the month count.

## Nine Times On The Back Of His Hand

It was not a sentence. He had got that right on the first day and it had taken him nine versions to stop trying to make it one.

*Between the ninth of January and the sixteenth of August four hundred and nine pieces of paper were given to this office, each a copy of the foot of the twenty-fourth sheet, each with writing in the two inches. None of them is a deed. None carries an hour. None carries a name. This office has entered the receipt of them and not one claim.*

And under it, in his own hand, because a clerk takes what she is handed word for word and is not allowed to improve it, and is not allowed to improve a name either:

*Lin Hao.*

The first eight versions had the name in and he took it out four times. The reason he put it in the ninth was not courage.

It was that a name in an office hand and a man’s name in his own hand are two different instruments, and a receipt with nobody against it is a receipt about paper, and he had known that on the thirtieth of August and had heard it said out loud at the end of that table and had gone away and thought about it for fifty-one days.

There was a last line under that and he had been carrying it for three days and he had not shown it to anybody.

*The drawer they are in is not to be opened.*

## What He Asks For

He asked for nothing. That is the whole of it and it is the only part of it that is true in a way he can live with.

He did not ask the office to read them. He did not ask for a column and he did not ask for a book and he did not ask for a woman to come to a laundry and be told anything, and he did not ask for the nine people who have stopped coming through a door since the middle of August to be given back.

He went in at about ten past three with the back of his hand out and he put it on the table face up in front of Wenna Raut and said, “Enter that,” and she said, “Say the date first,” and he said, “The twenty-first of October,” and then he read the rest of it out at the speed of a man reading a docket.

“Four hundred and nine pieces of paper and none of them is a deed.”

“That is in the Register already,” said Wenna Raut. “I entered it on the thirtieth of August at about ten past eleven and it is in my hand and it has a number against it and it is in the Register in front of me and it has been in there for fifty-one days. I am not permitted to enter it a second time and you are not permitted to make me.”

“I am not asking you to enter it again. I am asking you to enter what I have given you, and it is a different thing, and it has a name at the foot of it, and it has one sentence in it that is not on a form.”

## The One Question She Had Not Spent

Wenna Raut put her pen down.

She had one question a morning and she had had it since about nine, and in fifty-one days she had spent two of them, and this was the third and it was the last one she would have today.

She looked at the back of his hand for about nine seconds.

“Which of those four hundred people is going to be told.”

That was it. She said it in the ordinary way and she did not ask him to sit down and she did not ask him to explain, and she had one question a morning and that was the shape of it.

He did not answer for about a minute.

“No one,” he said.

“Then you have given me a receipt of four hundred and nine pieces of paper that this office has, and a name against it, and an instruction about a drawer, and no name against any of the four hundred and nine, and nobody in this city is going to be told any of it.”

“That is correct.”

“I am going to enter it exactly as given and I will not improve one word of it and I will not be asked to hurry,” said Wenna Raut, “and I would like you to understand before I start that this is the fourth time somebody has brought something into this room that I am able to enter and not able to do one single thing with.”

“The other three were a man with nine words and a drawer and two boxes of paper. In four years this office will hold four hundred and nine claims it cannot check and a man’s name against them, and that the man’s name will be the only part of it anybody can be got at.”

She picked the pen up.

“Ask me the other thing,” she said. “You have been standing there for about a minute with your hand on the table and you have not said the sentence with the drawer in it.”

“I am not saying it.”

“You came in here with nine versions of it on the back of your hand and you have read eight and a half of them and the half that is left has a drawer in it,” said Wenna Raut, “and I am not permitted to improve it and I am not permitted to leave it out, and if you do not say it then what I enter will not be what you brought in here and that is the one thing this table has never done in eleven years.”

He said it.

“That drawer is not to be opened, by anybody, on any ground, until somebody in this city builds a form that says otherwise.”

## What It Closes And What It Costs

She entered it in four minutes and read all of it back in the order it was in, and the name was in it, and his name was the last thing on the page.

Yusuf Adeyemi said four things and none of them was about whether he was right, and he had not moved from the far end of that room since about half past two and had not taken his coat off.

“The first is that the second of the four sentences has been at the foot of that page since before this office existed and that where the Register has nothing then nobody has been given anything, and that this office is the only thing in Ostara that has ever printed it, and that as of a quarter past three this afternoon it can never mean itself again.”

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

“The second is that the third line of that column is now dated, in my hand, and it carries a man’s name under it, and that a man’s name under an entry in this city is the difference between a receipt and a claim, and that nobody in this building has ever put a name under one before and that I did not know whether it was permitted until about four minutes ago.”

He held up two fingers.

“The third is that you have just instructed this office not to open a drawer, and that you are the only person this office may ask, and that you have spent the only thing you had on a request that nothing happens in it.”

“And the fourth.”

“The fourth is that there are about four hundred people in this city who will find out in about four years that the only copy of what they wrote is a line in a drawer in a building with no door onto a street,” said Yusuf Adeyemi, “and that the man who put it there cannot open it, and that he cannot open it because he instructed this office in writing nine minutes ago not to, and there is no form anywhere for undoing an instruction either.”

He put the pen down.

“I am not going to raise my voice about it and I would be permitted to. I want you to notice that I did not.”

*Two thousand four hundred and twenty days. Less two thousand and seventy-eight, the ninth of November of the sixth year, is three hundred and forty-two days, which is eleven months with twelve days after them. A shutter at the far end of a yard off the Vestry has been bare for every one of them and about two thousand people a day go past a pale rectangle.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and nine, the thirtieth of June of the seventh year, is a hundred and eleven days, which is three months with twenty-one days after them, and three lines have been in the second column for all of them where there were two, and about nine people a week read down to that part of it and can read it.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is seven hundred and two days, which is twenty-three months with twelve days after them. It is the morning he wrote the four sentences in his own hand and put a name under them, and about four hundred kitchens in this city have the foot of that page written out in a hand that is not his.*

*Neither of the two figures above is the one that stands in the last footer of the twenty-fourth sheet, which was measured from that other November and was right then.*

## Nine O’Clock Down The Stair

He went down and along at about five because that is what he does.

Nobody in that building thanked him. Nobody in that building said his name except the one clerk, and she said it because the name was in the entry and not because of anything else, and she had entered nine hundred and forty of those since she came to that room and had not improved one of them and had been thanked about nine times and every one of those nine was about a bundle that arrived early.

He had not moved his rank and he was not going to and he had not bought anything and nobody had offered him anything. He is rank one on a branch night shift, four nights and three off, eleven at night until half past five, and the head of that page has not moved in ten quarters and did not move this afternoon.

The twenty-eighth sheet goes up at four on the thirtieth of December.

And he stood at the bottom of a stair off a third court at about five past five with his hands in his pockets and worked out, standing there, the thing he had done and the thing it could not be undone from.

He had asked for nothing. He had given a room a number and a date and four words about what none of the four hundred and nine is, and he had put his own name under it, and he had instructed that the drawer be shut.

The fourth of the six lines that he struck through on the twenty-third of November with one stroke from the first letter to the last is in that drawer four hundred and nine times in somebody else’s pencil.

It is not the striking. He could not strike a pencil. He had built a page that could be argued with by anybody, and about four hundred people had done it, and every one of them had used it to say something about a day and a place and how long a thing went on, and not one of the four hundred and nine had ever been asked to say anything about the fourth of the six lines, and not one of them could have.

An entry can be made. An entry cannot be taken back.

He had used up the single resource this office is allowed to spend, and the resource is him, on an instruction that cannot hurt a soul, and the office had done it, and about four hundred people in four years are going to find out that their handwriting is in a building with no door onto a street and that the man who put it there signed a piece of paper saying it would stay shut.

He walked the length of a street and got nothing for dinner and did not want anything, and it was not the worst morning of his working life, because the worst one had been about nine years and it was in a cold store in a room with a rack on it and it belongs to a woman who took something out of him in nine minutes and has never once said so and never will.

It was the heaviest thing he had done in seven years and it was a piece of paper with a name at the bottom of it, and it was done in four minutes by a woman who may only enter, and it cannot be taken back by anybody, including him.

Nobody was thanked, and no door in that city is on a street, and the fourth line he struck off his own page is written out four hundred and nine times in the handwriting of people who have never met him and cannot read.

There is nothing in Ostara that can open that drawer except him, and he has instructed in writing that it is not to be opened.
