# A Finding About Paper

Seven days had gone since a man had stood in a corridor and asked him a question with no preamble at all about two rooms off a street, and since then the answer to it had been sitting in the middle of everything he did, and the answer was never.

Two thousand three hundred and twenty-two was the thirteenth of July and the twentieth is day two thousand three hundred and twenty-nine. The subtraction gives seven, and so does the month: twenty less thirteen.

## The Same Counter Off A Different Matter

He was at the counter off the third court at about ten on the twentieth, which is a quarter past the hour at which a man who came here in June had waited for about an hour and a quarter and had not been asked a single question.

The man behind the counter was not that one.

He had a folder and a sheet inside it and the sheet was the sheet, which had been off the rail in the passage off Cordwain since the thirtieth of June and had been produced in a room with a table in it by a man who was entitled to produce it.

“I am drawing a finding,” he said. “I have one question. Every man behind a counter entitled to draw one has exactly one, and it may be asked once, and I have had it since the sixth of July and I have not asked it.”

“Ask it.”

“I am going to, in a moment, and I am telling you first so that you cannot prepare, and so that you cannot say afterwards that you had no notice.”

“That is generous,” said Lin Hao.

“It is the practice,” said the clerk, “and I have never had the practice before.”

## The One Question

He put the sheet down in front of him and squared it to the edge of the counter and then did not touch it again.

“Has anybody in this city been given anything by this office.”

It was not about the four lines at the foot. It was not about the sum. It was not about the water or the nine months or the woman who cannot read, and it was not a question about any particular person at all.

It was the largest question anybody in this city had put to that office in six years, and it came from a man with a folder and no authority whatever except that he had a pen.

Lin Hao stood at that counter for about four seconds and understood the whole of his position in it.

The answer that was true was no.

About ninety pieces of pencil paper had come through the front door of that building since the thirtieth of March and every one of them had been handed to him, personally, at the counter in the morning, by the people who had made them, and he had put them in a drawer himself.

An office is not given a thing. A building with a door that opens on a court is not given a thing. And that sheet has said, four times a year in its own hand for six years, that where the Register has nothing then nobody has been handed anything, and the Register had nothing, because nothing had been entered, because there is no form anywhere in this city for holding a sheet of paper.

So the true answer was no, and he could have said it in four words, and it would have gone up in a finding against his own office and it would have been the easiest thing he had done in six years.

And it would also have been the answer of a man in a uniform coat speaking in the mouth of a body he does not run.

## The Answer He Could Have Given

“There is a second answer,” he said, “and it is the one I would rather give, and I am going to describe it to you so that you can rule on it.”

The clerk wrote nothing and inclined his head about half an inch.

“Ninety pieces of pencil paper came into that building since the thirtieth of March,” said Lin Hao. “Every one of them is a copy of the foot of a printed page with something written under the four sentences in a pencil by whoever made it. About nine hundred people in this city can read them. Nobody made them on the office’s account, nobody sent them, and nobody has ever been asked to make one.”

“That is a claim about persons.”

“It is a claim on about four hundred people,” said Lin Hao. “And a claim on a person must be entered against that person, and the entry has to carry a name, and not one of those ninety pieces has a name against it anywhere in this city. Nor do the four hundred others.”

“If I say that sentence in this room it goes into a finding as a statement of an officer of this office about four hundred people who did not write it, and the first one of them to go wrong will be a woman who cannot read.”

He put his thumb on the edge of the counter.

“And if I say no, then the finding says that this office has been given nothing in six years, and that is what its own sheet says four times a year, and it is true, and it is true because I asked for a drawer in March and there is no form for a holding in this city and about ninety pieces of paper are lying in it entered as nothing.”

“You are not able to answer my question.”

“I am the only person this office may ask,” said Lin Hao, “and I am not the office. If I answer that, the answer is a man’s answer and it is entered against a body as though it were the body’s, and the body has a clerk who enters what she is given and cannot improve one word of it, and in about four years somebody is going to read that finding out loud in a room and say that the office has said so.”

## What He Handed Over Instead

The clerk looked at him for about two seconds and then opened the folder and turned it round.

“All right,” he said. “Enter it exactly as given and nothing improved.”

He wrote for about four minutes, and he wrote it in the order it had been given him, which is the whole of what a clerk does.

That a question was put to the office by a person entitled to ask it, in the terms *has anybody in this city been given anything by this office.*

That the question was not answered, and the reason, given by the only person the office may ask, was that a true answer was a statement about persons and no form in this city carries a name against those persons, and that an untrue answer would be the second of four sentences at the foot of the office’s own sheet, which is true of the Register and not of a drawer.

And then he entered the drawer.

Sheets of pencil paper, ninety or thereabouts, taken in since the thirtieth of March of the seventh year, entered as nothing, there being no form in this city for holding anything, and no form that makes holding anything other than nothing.

“I cannot improve any word of that,” said the clerk. “It is a receipt and not a claim, and it is the only part of it that anybody can check, and I would like you to understand that I have just entered into a finding that the office is holding ninety pieces of pencil paper and cannot do one single thing with them.”

“I understand it.”

“Do you,” said the clerk. “Because in about four years somebody will read this out, and the first sentence anybody will take hold of is not the question and it is not the answer you would have given. It is ninety pieces of paper.”

“Yes,” said Lin Hao. “That is the one I would take hold of.”

He thought about it on the stair and he got it wrong twice on the way up and right once.

Right was this.

A woman who cannot read has carried the same sheet to the same table in a laundry for six months and eleven days, and her own pencil is across the bottom of it, and she paid for that pencil herself out of what she had. She has asked a woman with a pencil whether it is right. The woman with a pencil cannot tell her. And a man in a rank-one plate has told a magistrate’s clerk that this office cannot do one single thing with any of it.

That is the cost of the morning, and it was not the cost he had gone in expecting.

He had gone in expecting a rule and he had got one, and the rule was that he is not permitted to speak in the mouth of a body he does not run, and it is a good rule and Yusuf Adeyemi had it printed before he had.

What he had not gone in expecting was that a rule this good, applied honestly, in a room with a pen in it, ends with the words *ninety pieces of pencil paper* going onto a page that will outlive every person in the four hundred kitchens.

Before this morning that sentence existed in a drawer in a room with no door onto a street, where nobody could reach it.

He had gone up four months wanting that office to have something. He had gone in this morning wanting it to have something it could answer with.

*Two thousand three hundred and twenty-nine days. Less two thousand three hundred and twenty-two, the thirteenth of July of the seventh year, is seven, and twenty less thirteen is seven by the month count.*

*Less the thirtieth of March of the seventh year, two thousand two hundred and nineteen, is one hundred and ten days, which is three months with twenty days after them, and a drawer in a room off a third court has had ninety-odd pieces of pencil paper in it for all of them, entered as nothing, and on the twentieth of this month a man with a pen entered the drawer itself.*

*Less the twenty-seventh of December of the sixth year, two thousand one hundred and twenty-six, is two hundred and three days, which is six months with twenty-three days after them, and a form saying take down to course and make good has been on a board by a kerb since the seventh of April, with two ruled squares at the foot of it and nothing in either.*

## The Thing That Has Closed

He went back up the stair and along two floors and into the room off a third court at about eleven. The drawer under the table was shut and he could not see into it from where he stood, and there was now a finding in a building on another side of this city that said, in a magistrate’s clerk’s own hand, that this office had been asked one question and had not answered it.

That was the option that closed.

For six years this office had been able to say, four times a year, out loud, in other words, that nothing had ever been handed to it, and it had meant it, and about nine people in this city got to the last two inches of it in an ordinary week. It has now been asked, once, by a man with a right to ask, in the plainest words anybody could use, and it said nothing back.

He had been ready to say nothing. He had a rule for saying nothing and it was one of the best rules in this city and Yusuf Adeyemi had printed it in the office’s own hand on the first page of it.

He had not been ready for the nothing to be written down.
