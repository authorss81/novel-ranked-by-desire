# Paper Costs Money

Seven days had gone since a woman auditor cited his own sentence against him in a room with a shut door in it and he could not answer it.

Two thousand two hundred and six was the seventeenth of March and the twenty-fourth is day two thousand two hundred and thirteen, and two thousand two hundred and thirteen less two thousand two hundred and six is seven, and twenty-four less seventeen is seven by the month count.

The counting house above a street off Cordwain was warm at about two in the afternoon, with ledgers along every wall and a clerk’s desk by the window, and Solveig Rask sat at the desk with her hands folded, twenty-six years in that trade behind her, keeper of the real books of the house of Vant, and a book of her own that had been open since the sixteenth of September and written off on the second of May of the sixth year.

Lin Hao had come to find out what four hundred pieces of paper cost, because he had never once paid for anything and was about to find out that the first thing he had wanted to do four months was a thing with a price on it.

He wanted it plainly, and he said so, because saying it plainly was the only honesty he had left that week.

## A Real Book

“I cannot tell you what the copies cost in my own words out loud,” said Solveig Rask, before he asked, because she had known why he came from the moment he came through the door.

“A real book is written in the hand of the person who carries the debt and the carrier may not read her own entry out to another person, and if she does the entry is void. I will not have it written down, and there is a broker above me who will price anything and who profits nowhere a person loses.”

She said it without apology and without pride, the way people state a rule that has bound them longer than he has been alive, and he accepted it, because there was nothing else to do with a rule like that.

“What can you tell me,” he said.

“That about ninety copies need about ninety pieces of paper,” she said. “That paper is bought by weight by nine wholesalers. That the number of people in Ostara who bought paper in a given week is a figure a division reads twice and keeps in two books four feet apart, and the round book and the desk book disagree about it, as they do about everything, and the difference is seventeen in one quarter.”

He stared at her.

Cold anger came up in him, at an institution for being right, which was the last thing he expected from that winter, and it was correct anger, and it did not help, because correctness is not an instrument and cannot be spent.

“The entry written off on the second of May is not reopened,” she said, seeing his face. “What I say now is about paper and about a figure nobody can reconcile. Paper is a thing that gets bought by people who are counted, and people who are counted are people a landlord may find.”

He understood her perfectly.

He also understood that stopping the copies cost nine people about four hours in a week each and he was not paying it and neither was anybody else, and that wanting them to stop was a want he would not say out loud, because saying it would have made him the kind of man who asks a laundry to stop writing.

## The Figure Against The Counter

She took him to the back of the counting house and showed him, without reading it out, the wholesaler’s book open on a stand, with a figure against a counter and no name against it, and the counter had not moved seven years.

He heard the same sentence in a stone yard nine streets away and did not know yet that it was the same sentence, because a figure that can be added to and cannot be taken from is the same wherever it stands, and nobody in Ostara opens such a book.

He went out into the cold afternoon with anger still in his chest, clean and useless, and what he wanted was for paper to be free, and he knew as he wanted it that paper costs money and money is a thing with a price, and the first honest want he had owned in weeks was also the cheapest.

Pieces of paper were counted as pieces of paper and never as claims and never as people, and ninety of them would need buying, and buying them would put names in a book, and names in a book were the very thing Perla Quist would not give him.

He walked home slowly, because his chest hurt going up the hill, slower than in the spring and slower every week, and Tessa’s words from November walked with him, that nobody in Ostara could put it back, that she had looked, and that looking was the whole of what she was for.

He was angry at a building rather than at a person for the first time that winter, and it was the correct anger, and it changed nothing.

*Two thousand two hundred and thirteen days. Less two thousand two hundred and six, the seventeenth of March of year seven, is seven. Twenty-four less seventeen is seven by the month count, because a month is thirty days and has no thirty-first.*

*Pieces of paper are counted as pieces of paper here and never as claims and never as people.*
