# A Docket Nine Streets Away

Seven days had gone since a man behind a counter under a window had told him that a figure with nothing against it is not a mistake, and since then he had been unable to stop thinking about the word *before*.

Two thousand two hundred and sixty-one was the twelfth of May and the nineteenth is day two thousand two hundred and sixty-eight, and two thousand two hundred and sixty-eight less two thousand two hundred and sixty-one is seven, and nineteen less twelve is seven by the month count.

There is one piece of paper in this city that says a thing left a place and went somewhere, and it is about as big as a sheet of paper folded once, and there are about eleven thousand yards’ worth of them in this city and nobody has ever been refused anything by one of them, because not one of them has ever been asked for anything.

## Two In The Afternoon At A Bench

He went back at two with nothing in his hands, which is a thing he has done about nine times in nine years and never once with a purpose like this one.

Sander Vole had a docket out on the bench behind the counter and was copying a figure from one page onto another with a straight edge laid across, and the dust off about nine hundred feet of open ground comes in through the front and lies on everything in that building at about the same rate.

“You have come back about the figure.”

“No,” said Lin Hao. “I have come about a docket.”

Sander Vole put the straight edge down.

“A docket is not a figure.”

“It is not.”

“A docket is a bit of paper that says a block left this yard and went to a place, and there is one for every barrow that goes out of that gate, and there were about four thousand of them on the seventh of April and there have been about nine hundred since, and I file them in a box under the counter because a man who is sent a block he did not order will send it back if he can say where it came from.”

“Then I would like the docket for a course of dressed stone.”

“You would like the docket for a course of dressed stone that went to that wall.”

“Yes.”

“You have said a great deal this month for a man who cannot give you a figure.”

## A Field On It For The Thing Cut In The Face

He got up from the bench and went and lifted about nine inches of dockets out of a box under the counter and carried them over and put them down in front of Lin Hao.

“They are in order of the gate and they are in the hand of the man who carried them and not in the hand of the man who cut them,” he said, “and that is not a rule anybody made, it is just how a yard works, because the man who cuts a block does not know where it is going and the man who carries it does.”

He went through about four inches of them.

“This one is a course.”

It was a sheet of paper folded once and it had four things on it in a hard upright hand: a place, a thing, a number of pieces, and a line that ran the width of the paper with two words against the end of it.

The line had a heading. It said, in the same upright hand, *Marks or letters cut in face.*

And against it, in the same upright hand, at the end of the line, in an ordinary working pen, there were two words.

They were the two words at the foot of the wall.

Lin Hao did not touch the paper.

## Before

“They were cut before it went,” said Sander Vole. “That is what that line is for. A mason who cuts anything into the face of a block writes it on the docket so that whoever gets it knows it is there and does not send it back for it.”

“And that is why they are still there.”

“That is why they are still there,” said Sander Vole. “Nobody has been trying to keep them for thirty years. A man wrote a sentence on a bit of paper at a bench so that a wall would not send his block back, and he got it wrong and the wall did not send it back anyway, and there has been no wall in this city that could hold a sentence off a block for thirty years. The block was the only thing that could. That is all that is.”

He put his thumb on the line and did not turn the docket over.

“There is no figure on it,” he said. “There is never a figure on a docket. A figure goes in the book and a docket is a piece of paper that a man can carry, and the last man who carried this one died before the man who took the book off him did.”

## What He Is Not Going To Say

“And who cut them.”

“I am not going to tell you.”

“You know.”

“I know it was not the man who carried it and I know it was not the man who cut it, because if the man who cut it had cut it then the man who carried it would not have had to write it on the docket to stop a wall sending it back, he would have said so to the man who cut it and it would have been in the book,” said Sander Vole.
“That is as far as I have got and I have been at it for two hours and I am going to stop now, and you are going to let me stop, and I am telling you that I am going to stop before you ask me the next thing.”

“All right.”

“I have said the whole of what I have got and I have not said who, and I am not going to, and it is not because it is a matter of the yard.”

He squared the docket to the edge of the bench.

“And it has taken you two weeks and about nine streets and a man who has been standing in front of that wall since he was small. And the two words are on a piece of paper in a box under a counter in this yard and it has been there the whole time, and it got there because a man wanted a block not to be sent back, and not one person in this city arranged any part of it.”
“You have been standing at that corner reading four inches of stone for six years and you have known who cut them for about two weeks,” he said.

## The Date He Did Not Take

There was a date at the head of that docket in the same upright hand, and he looked at it, and he did not write it down and he did not ask for it to be read out.

That was deliberate and it cost him something, because a date is the only thing on that paper he could have carried to somebody.

It is older than the standing sheet. It is older than the office and older than the corridor with the nine hundred lines on it and older than the account of the yard by a long way, and there is nothing in this city that a date that old can be set against except how many years a wall has stood, and that wall has just been taken down to the course and made good and there is nobody left who remembers what was on it.

So the date is the one thing about those two words that he is not going to put in a book, and he is going to have to go on not knowing it, and that is the price of the other eleven inches of paper in front of him.

“You have read it,” said Sander Vole.

“I have read it.”

“You are not going to write it down.”

“Not this morning.”

“Write it down,” said Sander Vole. “I am telling you to write it down, because I have had a figure on a line in my book for nineteen years that nobody has ever come to look at, and I have found out in the last nine days that the reason nobody came to look at it is not that it is a secret. It is that nobody has ever had a reason. You have got a reason. Write the date down.”

He wrote it on the back of his own hand with a pencil.

Then he stood there for a second.

“No,” he said. “I did not do that. I have stood at this counter for nine years and I have never once written down something for a man in a coat because he asked me to, and I am not starting because a man who carried a docket told me to, and if you go up that counter tomorrow and say that I told you to do it then I will take it back and I will take back everything I have said to you.”

He wiped the pencil on his coat and put it away.

## The Box Under The Counter

He put the docket back in the box himself, because Sander Vole made him, and it went in about nine inches from the top, in a hand that was not his, at a place in about four thousand dockets that had no mark on it.

It has been in that box for thirty years.

There is a wall nine streets from this yard that has been taken down to the course and made good and there is a new joint at the bottom of nine feet of fresh render and underneath the joint four inches of dressed stone with two words cut all the way through the face of it, and paint will go over the six inches above the joint and stop dead, because paint will not hold on dressed stone, and that is the entire mechanism.

And in a box under a counter in this yard there is a piece of paper about the size of a sheet folded once with a line on it for the marks or letters cut in the face of the block, and two words against that line, and a date at the head of it, and no figure anywhere on it.

The book on the counter has a figure against that course of dressed stone with no name against it. It has not moved in seven years. Nobody has ever opened that book at that page.

A counter figure can be added to and cannot be taken from.

*Two thousand two hundred and sixty-eight days. Less two thousand two hundred and sixty-one, the twelfth of May of the seventh year, is seven, and nineteen less twelve is seven by the month count.*

*Less the ninth of November of the sixth year, two thousand and seventy-eight, is one hundred and ninety days, which is six months with ten days after them. A shutter at the far end of a yard has had nothing at the foot of it for all of them.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is five hundred and fifty days, which is eighteen months with ten days after them. It is the third of four sentences at the foot of a sheet, and there are about four hundred pieces of pencil paper in this city with the foot of it copied out on the top of them, and a wall will not hold a sentence and a block of stone will, and there is no column anywhere in this city for a word on a stone except one on a piece of paper in a box under a counter nine streets away that has not been asked for in thirty years.*
