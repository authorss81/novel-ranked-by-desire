# A Figure With No Name Against It

Seven days had gone since a man who had carried a figure for nineteen years had told him that a figure can be added to and cannot be taken from, and that nobody had ever opened that book at that page, and since then he had not been able to put it down.

Two thousand two hundred and fifty-four was the fifth of May and the twelfth is day two thousand two hundred and sixty-one, and two thousand two hundred and sixty-one less two thousand two hundred and fifty-four is seven, and twelve less five is seven by the month count.

He went back to the yard first, at about ten, because he had left something out of the morning before and wanted it said in front of the man who had said the first one.

## Two Of Them In One City

“About how long has that figure been on that line.”

“Seven years,” said Sander Vole, without looking up. “It was there when I took the book off the man before me and he told me it had been there when he took it off the man before him, and I have never asked either of them when it started and neither of them is alive to ask now, so it is seven years of me and an unknown amount before me.”

“Can it be moved.”

“No.”

“Has anybody ever put a name against it.”

“Never once.”

“Has anybody ever opened the ledger at that page to find out.”

“Not once,” said Sander Vole.
“Not in nineteen years and not, as far as I know, before that, and I have thought about that more in the last nine days than in the previous nineteen years. A man in a white coat came and asked me the question and I did not have an answer, and a thing I did not have an answer to on the first of April is a thing I thought about every day in between.”

## The Desk Under The Window At Half Past Three

That desk was where it has always been, in a passage off Cordwain, with the window on the wall behind it and the rail along the front of the counter, and there is still a sheet lying out on that rail where it has lain since a March in a year nobody behind that counter mentions. A hundred times a month somebody puts a palm on one corner of it to get past.

He had stood behind that counter for about nine years with a pencil, a book with roughly thirty names written in it, and not one form for anything at all. He had had a single question available to him about the sheet on the rail since the twenty-third of December and he had already used it, badly, on a number, deliberately, and said out loud that he was going to use it that way before he did.

He was not going to get a second question out of that man in a year, and Lin Hao had known it since the eighth of February and had arranged the morning so that it did not matter.

“You have come about a yard,” said the man at the desk.

“I have.”

“You have been down there and you have come back and you have got a face on you that I have not seen on you before,” said the man at the desk. “I have been here about nine years and I have seen a good many faces come out of that direction and yours is not one of them. What is it.”

“I want to know what a figure with nothing against it is called.”

## The Fact He Was Given Without Being Asked

The man at the desk put the pencil down and turned the ledger round so the top of it showed.

“The second of the second month of a year I do not name. A woman came to this counter with a bundle and wanted a receipt for having left it. There is no receipt in this city for that and there never has been. I told her so and she went away. I wrote down the date and the street she came off and one word against it. I have never found out what the word meant, and she has never come back and nobody has come back about it.”
“There,” he said.

He put his thumb on the line.

“Seven years,” said the man at the desk. “That is the only one I have got and I have been looking for a second one since you walked in about ninety seconds ago, because you have got a yard face on you and there is only one thing anybody ever comes back from that yard about.”

He turned the ledger back round.

“A figure with nothing against it is not a mistake,” he said. “A mistake gets corrected. That one has never been corrected in seven years and nobody came to correct it, and that is the only thing anybody has ever said about it and it is the only thing I know that is true about it.”

He said it before he was asked. He said it standing up in the middle of a working afternoon, with the counter being walked round a hundred times a month by people going past, and he had said it because a man had walked in with a face, and not because he wanted anything.

## He Said Why First
“I am not going to give you that ledger. I am not going to give you thirty names, and I am not going to give you a street and a date off the second of the second month, and if you ask me for any of it I will tell you no and I will not enjoy telling you no and I will not remember that I did it.”

“I am going to tell you something and I am telling you before you have asked me for it so that you cannot say afterwards that I offered it,” said the man at the desk.

“Nine years at this counter and about two hundred thousand people have gone past the end of it and not one of them has ever asked what is in my ledger, and that is not modesty. It is that there is nothing in my ledger anybody could use, and a thing nobody can use is not worth stealing, and that is why nobody has ever tried, and that is why I have never had to decide what I would do.”
“All right.”

“I will also tell you that nobody has ever asked me for it,” he said.

He picked the pencil up and then put it down again.

“And I have been right about that for nine years,” he said, “and I have been wrong about it once, about four months ago. The once was you standing at this counter asking whether I had a column for it, and I told you I had not got a column for anything.”

“That was the first time in nine years that anybody had made me look at the inside of my own book and I did not enjoy it, and I have not enjoyed it since, and I am not going to pretend to you that I have.”

## The Thing He Wanted And Could Not Have

He wanted it.

He wanted it standing at that counter in a passage off Cordwain at about half past three on the twelfth of May, and he wanted it the way a man wants a thing he has no right to, which is to say entirely and without any shape at all.

He wanted one figure of his own with nothing against it.

Not a figure anybody could check. Not a figure that proved anything about anybody who could be found and cross-examined about it. A figure on a line in a book in a room, with nothing whatever after it, that had been there for seven years and had not moved and had never been wrong because it had never been tested, and that no clerk in this city was permitted to enter and that no magistrate could ask about and that could not be taken from him.

He had about ninety pieces of pencil paper in a drawer and he had been told to the effect that he had asked for them, and the drawer was in a building with no door onto a street and there was not one entry against one of them, and no form exists anywhere in this city for a holding, and there is not one person in Ostara entitled to ask him when he intends to open the drawer and what he intends to do with what is in it.

And a man at a desk under a window had been doing with a pencil and a book what the office was built to do, for nothing, for nine years, and had never once been asked to justify it.

There was no arrangement in this city that put a rank-one plate and a nine-year ledger on the same shelf. He had looked for it for about nine minutes and it was not there.

## Two Books

He went back down the street at about four and stood across from the gate for a while and watched the barrows go out.

There are two books in this city that hold a figure with nothing against it. One is in a yard nine streets from a works department and it has been there seven years and nobody has ever opened it at that page. The other is on a counter in a passage off Cordwain and it has a date and a street and one word against it on the second of the second month of a year nobody names, and that one has never been corrected either.

The two of them are about nine streets apart. They are in two trades. Neither of them is the account, and neither of them can be drawn against or allocated or closed, and about nine hundred people in this city could read a figure off either page in about four seconds and not one person alive has ever needed to.

And the yard has never heard of the desk, and the desk has never heard of the yard, and neither of the two men who hold them has ever been asked by anybody about the other, and he was the first, and he wanted them joined together more than he has wanted anything since a broker on a floor above a counting house told him what paper cost.

*Two thousand two hundred and sixty-one days. Less two thousand two hundred and fifty-four, the fifth of May of the seventh year, is seven, and twelve less five is seven by the month count.*

*Less the ninth of November of the sixth year, two thousand and seventy-eight, is one hundred and eighty-three days, which is six months with three days after them. A shutter at the far end of a yard has had nothing at the foot of it for all of them, and a chaplain has stood on steps in front of it six times and not once has it made a difference.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is five hundred and forty-three days, which is eighteen months with three days after them. It is the third of four sentences at the foot of a sheet, and two books in this city now hold a figure with nothing against it, and neither of them has ever been opened at its page, and a figure can be added to and cannot be taken from.*

*Nine hundred feet of open ground and nine streets and about thirty-one years of a man writing down what he is told to write down. That is the whole of the distance between two figures with nothing against them in this city, and there is no instrument in Ostara that measures it and no clerk who may be given it.*
