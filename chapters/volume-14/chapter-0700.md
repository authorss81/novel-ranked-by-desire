# The Twenty-Eighth Sheet

A sheet goes out at four on the thirtieth of December and it cannot come back off the rail, and he had used that twice and had been right both times by about four hours, and on this one the thing that could not be undone went into a drawer at about twenty to three and he carried it in himself.

Two thousand four hundred and eighty-three was the twenty-fourth of December and the thirtieth is day two thousand four hundred and eighty-nine, and two thousand four hundred and eighty-nine less two thousand four hundred and eighty-three is six, and thirty less twenty-four is six by the month count.

## Half Past Two, On The Last Working Day Of The Year

It came out of the drawer at half past two and it was squared against the wood inside about four seconds, which is faster than it used to be and which nobody in that room remarked on.

Eight hundred and fifty-two at the head of it. It has stood at that since the thirtieth of March of the fifth year and it is unmoved across twelve sheets.

About four hundred and nineteen accounts under the head, every one of them somebody’s account of a street dug in the cold, with a day against it and an hour against the day and no figure and no name on any of them.

Three lines in the second column, headed carried by. One of the three has been in there since the twelfth of September and it carries a date and a number and four words and a man’s name, and under the name a sentence in a clerk’s hand saying that a drawer is not to be opened, and the other two are older than this office.

Four sentences at the foot, two of them older than this office and two of them set on a morning in November by a man with a delivery coat’s name under one of them and not his own.

And under the four, since the twenty-third of November: six lines, the fourth of the six struck through from the first letter to the last, a name under the striking, and two inches of nothing.

“Twenty-eighth,” said Wenna Raut.

“Twenty-eighth,” said Lin Hao, who had said it twenty-seven times before.

“The head has not moved and is not going to,” she said. “There is no permission and there is nobody in this city entitled to give one. I say that out loud once a quarter in this room and I am saying it with a clerk’s hand on a page that is about to be folded, and this is the last time I will say it in this year.”

She signed for it. She folded it into eight and she carried it down two floors herself, because that is what happens to it every quarter and nobody makes a ceremony of it, and at four it went face up on a rail in a passage off Cordwain.

## Twenty-Five Things Into A Room

He went back up afterwards and got the bundle off the table where it had been since the twenty-eighth of October.

Twenty-five things. The twenty-third of them had a sheet of paper in it with nothing written on the paper.

The last one had never been opened by anybody. The twenty-fifth was a piece of pencil paper about the size of a receipt with a date on it and nothing else, and it had come through the front door in a bag and had been entered as nothing, and it was the first thing anybody in this city had ever handed to this office that was not a copy of its own page.

He carried it in and put it on the boards under the window beside the two cardboard boxes, and the boxes had four hundred and nine sheets of rag paper in them and had been on the floor since the thirtieth of August because a table in that room is for entering things and nothing has ever stood on one that was not going to be entered.

Then he shut the drawer.

He shut it himself, with his own hand, at about eleven minutes to three on the last working day of the year, having instructed this office in writing on the twenty-first of October that it was not to be opened, by anybody, on any ground, until somebody in this city built a form that said otherwise.

There is no form anywhere for undoing an instruction. He looked for one on the eighteenth, and what he found was not a form and not a way round one, and what he did not find was anything anybody had ever made, because nobody had ever had to.

Nobody else in that city has ever been able to ask that office anything, and he has been able to do it since the sixth of February, and what he spent it on was a drawer.

“Nothing goes in that drawer,” said Wenna Raut, without looking up, “and I am not the person who decides that. You are, and you did it in a clerk’s hand with your name at the bottom of it nine weeks ago, and I entered it exactly and I did not improve one word of it and nobody was thanked.”

## What Has Not Moved In Fifty Chapters

He went down the stair and along at about ten past four.

He is rank one, on a branch night shift, and not one hour, plate, room or standing of his has moved in the last ten months. Nobody offered him anything. He bought nothing. The plate is a white one with his name on it and no figure cut into it anywhere.

A branch office off a third court. Three floors of washrooms, a stairwell and a bin room. Eleven at night until half past five, four nights and three off, on a rota the size of a folded hand with four names on it, and he asked for that shift.

The head of that page has not moved once in fifty chapters and it did not move this afternoon at half past two. Nobody in this city holds a permission that would move it. About nine people a week get down to the last two inches of it and can read every line.

And the second of the four sentences went out at four this afternoon, in the office’s own hand, saying that where the Register has nothing in it then nobody has been given anything, and it is true of every person in this city who has ever gone to a counter and been refused, and it is false of the only building in Ostara that prints it, and there is no form anywhere between that room and the works department on which a building may say a fact about itself.

## Eleven At The Rail

There were eleven at the rail at four minutes past four and there have never been more than four, and the man at the counter under the window had his own copy face up on the wood and read the foot of it out to the whole of them without being asked, the way he reads it to about nine people a week. Nobody pays him for it and nobody has ever paid him for it and there is nobody alive he could name who has thanked him for it.

The ninth of the eleven was a man who had been coming since the second month and had never once got to the second of the four, and asked whether they had started, and was told the true version, and said thank you to a man behind a counter.

Nobody thanked anybody. That was the arrangement and it had been the arrangement for about nine years and both of the men in it had made it on purpose.

*Two thousand four hundred and eighty-nine days. Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is ninety days, which is three months exactly, and the twenty-seventh sheet went up at four on that afternoon and this one has gone up at four this afternoon, and the head has stood at eight hundred and fifty-two across twelve of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and nine, the thirtieth of June, is a hundred and eighty days, which is six months exactly. Less two thousand two hundred and nineteen, the thirtieth of March, is two hundred and seventy days, which is nine months exactly.*

*Less two thousand and seventy-eight, the ninth of November of the sixth year, is four hundred and eleven days, which is thirteen months with twenty-one days after them, and a shutter at the far end of a yard off the Vestry has been bare for every one of them, and about two thousand people a day slow down at a pale rectangle.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is seven hundred and seventy-one days, which is twenty-five months with twenty-one days after them, and it is the morning he wrote four sentences in his own hand and put a man’s name under one of them, and about four hundred kitchens in this city have the foot of that page written out in a hand that is not his.*

*Four hundred and eleven is also the figure that stands in the last footer of the twenty-fourth sheet, where it was measured from that other November and was right then. One figure, two printings, two anchors, and a chapter that names which of them it means.*

## The Corner

He went up the hill at about half past five because it was the last working day of the year and there was nowhere else in the city he had to be.

Two hundred and forty people came past that corner between five and half past five. About a hundred stopped at a board carrying a month’s form for an address, with two ruled squares at the foot of it and nothing whatever in either. Nine of the two hundred went down at the foot of the wall.

Under the fresh render at that corner there is a course of dressed stone about the depth of two fingers, and it has been under a wall for longer than anybody alive has been reading it. Two words are cut into it. They go right through the face. The one on the left is deeper than the one on the right, because a man cutting stone leans into the cut and a man writing does not.

Paint will not take on a face a mason has dressed, and that is the whole of why they have survived, and nobody has been keeping them safe for thirty years. Nobody has needed to.

There is no board to put anything on, and nothing to read on the one that is there, and about two hundred people a day stand at a place where neither of those things is available, and about nine of them a week get down low enough to read two words.

Nobody in this city has ever asked who cut them.

A man on a bench wrote them on a docket the size of a folded sheet, so that a wall would not send his block back, and the block was the only thing in the city that could hold a sentence.

The paper came out of a box under a counter in a yard nine streets from a works department on the seventeenth, and it is in the inside coat of a rank-one officer, and he has not read the date on it and he is not going to.

About four thousand of them are still under that counter, and one of them is the docket for whoever took the block away, and the man who keeps them told him not to come back for the rest of it.
