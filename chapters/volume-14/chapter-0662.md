# Four Inches Of Dressed Stone

Seven days had gone since a hoarding about sixty feet long had gone up along the foot of a wall and had put four inches of dressed stone behind painted boards with a slot under them, and he had been getting up in the middle of the night about that.

Two thousand two hundred and twenty-six was the seventh of April and the fourteenth is day two thousand two hundred and thirty-three, and two thousand two hundred and thirty-three less two thousand two hundred and twenty-six is seven, and fourteen less seven is seven by the month count.

He got to the bottom of it at about ten in the morning, and the bottom of it was open, and he stood there for about four minutes without doing anything at all with either hand.

## The Scaffold Came Down On One Side

They had taken the hoarding down off about forty feet of it overnight and put it across the other twenty, and about nine feet of what had been a wall on the seventh of April was bare on the pavement side by about ten o’clock.

It was not beautiful. It was the colour of wet ash and it had holes in it where the render had been off the brick in places for years, and the mortar between them was a different colour from the mortar between the others, because a wall made good by about six different men over thirty years is a wall with six different men in it.

Underneath it, at the foot of it, running the whole length of that about forty feet, was four inches of dressed stone.

He had looked at it through a slot in a hoarding eight days ago.

He had looked at it through the mouth of a hole in a mortar joint in the middle of the night in a year he does not name, with a lamp, and had got the words out and not understood where the lamp was.

## Sixty-Two Of The Month’s Forms

They came out of the wall in about nine minutes because the wall was open and they were stacked inside it, one a month since the first year, in a pile about half an inch deep at the front and about a foot deep at the back.

Sixty-two of them. Every one of them had two ruled squares at the foot and both of the squares on every one of them were empty.

The boy took them out and stacked them on the pavement in the order they came out and he did not open any of them and he did not read any of them, and he had never read any of them, and he had put up sixty-three of them over six years and he had never once looked at the bottom of a different one.

“Do you know what is on them.”

“No,” said the boy. “There is nothing on the top half of one of those that has ever been about anything, and there has never been a person in this city who could put anything at the bottom of one, and that is why I have never opened one.”

He squared the stack with the side of his boot.

“There is sixty-three of those going up on that board tonight,” he said, “and I am not going to write in either box, and I have not been asked about either box by anybody in six years, and I have stopped.”

## Four Inches

Below the lowest form was the stone.

Dressed stone. It is a different colour from everything else in that wall and it is smoother and it is the only part of the wall that was ever meant to be looked at, because a course of dressed stone at the foot of a wall is put there so that the wall has a bottom that is not a place where water gets in.

The coat of paint goes over six inches of render and stops dead at the top of the stone, every month, for six years, and the reason it stops is that paint will not hold on dressed stone.

That is the whole of why two words are still there. Not a rule, and not a decision, and not anybody’s care. A coat of paint will not hold on a hard face, so for six years a man’s brush has run out of something to grab and stopped, and the words underneath have gone on being there whether anybody meant them to or not.

He had read that wall for six years as a thing that survived.

It had survived the way a stain survives.

## The Left-Hand Word Is Deeper

Two words cut into the four inches. Both of them go clean through the face of it, and the left-hand one is the deeper of the two.

He put his fingers in and he could tell that with his fingers before he had anything else to tell him.

A man cutting stone is leaning on it. A man writing is not leaning on it. The left-hand word had been taken out with about a pound and a half behind it and the right-hand one had been taken out with about half that, and somebody going across the face from left to right gets tired, and the reason the left-hand word is deeper is that it was done first and it was done by a man in a hurry who had a job on the second of that.

He has been asked by nobody when. He was not going to say. He had nothing, and he had been carrying nothing since the twenty-seventh of December when a man behind a counter told him he had been holding a piece of paper upside down for nine weeks.

The two words are not a sentence that anybody in this city has any other copy of. There is one rubbing in the inside of a boy’s coat and there are about nine people in this city who have been in a room where it was read out.

## About Four Hundred Of Them

They came to about ten and stopped.

They stood in a loose line about four feet off the wall and looked at four inches of stone at the bottom of nine feet of bare grey render, and not one of them got to the foot of it for about nine minutes, because the two words are cut in a hand about two inches high and you have to be close to it and you have to know what you are looking for before you know where to put your face.

Then somebody said one word and about forty of them got there at once.

They stayed about an hour.

About four hundred people saw them for the first time that morning, which is not a figure and is not a claim and is what a man counts when he is standing on the wrong side of a road doing it again. About four hundred more can read them, and those two numbers are not the same number and it is not the same people.

He wrote neither of them down.

## The Woman With The Broom

She came at about half past eleven and went along the four inches with a short-handled broom and the bristles were about as long as her hand, and she did the whole length of the bare part of it and then came back and did it again, and the reason she does it is that it is under a wall and nobody spits there.

He had about four seconds in which he could have asked her.

He has been carrying the wanting to ask that question since the thirtieth of December, when he worked out that she was the only person who went to that stone every single morning and had therefore been the only person in this city who could say whether anybody ever came to it at night. Nine years he had wanted to know. Eight days he had had a hoarding in the way.

He stood nine feet off and did not ask her, and it was the hardest thing he did that morning and it took him about four seconds and there is nobody in this city who would have known it had happened.

She did not look at him. She has never once looked at him.

He wants it very much more than he wanted it a fortnight ago. He wanted it badly enough on the seventh of April that he got up in the middle of the night about it, and that wanting is a thing he is not going to put on a form and there is no column for it anywhere in this city.

## No Column Anywhere In This City For It

At about one he went down to the end of the bare forty feet where the scaffolding was still up and there were two men on it with a hawk and a float.

They were going to put render back on the wall at some point in the next nine days. That was the whole of it. When the new render is on, the stone will be the bottom four inches of a wall again with a joint over it, and every coat of paint from that morning on will come up over the joint and give out at it, and the words will be there, and the wall will look like a wall.

He stood under the scaffold and asked the man on it a question, which was how a person finds out where a block of stone comes from.

The man on the scaffold looked down at him for about four seconds.

“Not off me,” he said. “I have been on this wall nine days and I have not been under it once, and I do not know what is under it, and there is nothing on the job sheet about it.”

He went back to his float.

About two hundred a day pass that corner and maybe one in a hundred of them ever stoops far enough to read what is down at the foot of it. That morning about four hundred people stooped. Not one of them went away with any way of finding out where a block of dressed stone comes from.

There is a works schedule nine streets away with about nine hundred lines on it and three columns on every line and none of them is about a word on a stone. There is a month’s form with two boxes at the foot of it and both of them empty. There is a painting contract in another building that says nothing and has said nothing for nine years.

And four hundred people in this city can now read two words cut into the bottom of a wall by somebody they will never find, and there is nowhere to write down the one question that would find them.

*Two thousand two hundred and thirty-three days. Less two thousand two hundred and twenty-six, the seventh of April of the seventh year, is seven, and fourteen less seven is seven by the month count.*

*Less the ninth of November of the sixth year, two thousand and seventy-eight, is one hundred and fifty-five days, which is five months with five days after them, and a shutter at the far end of a yard has had nothing at the foot of it for all of them.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is five hundred and fifteen days, which is seventeen months with five days after them. It is the third of four sentences at the foot of a sheet, and it is the reason copies of that foot are being written out longhand in pencil at kitchen tables all over this city, and not one person making one could have told you where a block of stone comes from.*
