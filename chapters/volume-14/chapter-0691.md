# A Bundle Of Twenty-Five

Seven days had gone since he had instructed an office in writing not to open a drawer, and in every one of the seven days he had gone down a stair off a third court and up it again with the piece of paper in his coat, and it had not got any lighter.

Two thousand four hundred and twenty was the twenty-first of October and the twenty-eighth is day two thousand four hundred and twenty-seven, and two thousand four hundred and twenty-seven less two thousand four hundred and twenty is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven by the month count.

## The Drawer Is Shut

It was shut. That was the whole of what his instruction had done and it had done it in about nine seconds on the afternoon of the twenty-first.

He is the only person that office may ask.

He has been the only person that office may ask since the sixth of February and there is no second person in Ostara who could ask it, and nobody in that building is going to stop him. He was told a long time ago by a man at the end of that table that this city contains no instrument capable of telling a man he may not do a thing he has come in there to do.

And there is no form anywhere in this city for undoing an instruction.

That is the sentence he had gone down with on the twenty-first and it had been true then and it was true now, and the seven days had not improved it by one minute of anything.

## Twenty-Four Things On A Table

The bundle had been on the table since a year ago and it had twenty-four things in it.

He had never been told what any of them was. That was not modesty. Nobody in that building had told him and nobody was obliged to tell him and he was not entitled to be told, and the twenty-third of them had a sheet of paper inside it with nothing written on the paper, and the last one had never been opened by anybody including the man who brought it.

What he knew about the bundle was four things and he had got them over about eleven years.

It was in a room with no door onto a street. Nobody was required to open any of it. It was not entered against anything. And it was the only thing in this city he had ever built by hand out of things that had not been asked for.

## The Twenty-Fifth Thing

The bag came at about ten on the twenty-eighth, the way it has come every morning but the day the office is shut, and it had four sheets in it.

Three of them were the foot of the standing sheet in somebody’s pencil with something written in the two inches, which is the ordinary traffic and which comes in about eleven a week and goes into the drawer entered as nothing.

The fourth was not that.

It was a piece of pencil paper about the size of a receipt and it had one thing on it and the one thing was a date.

No hour. No place. No lane, no months, no figure, no name, and none of the four sentences and none of the six lines and no striking and nothing in the two inches, because there were no two inches on it. It was the size of a thumb and it was not a copy of anything at all.

Wenna Raut read it where it lay, the way she reads everything, and then went over it a second time.

“This is not a return,” she said.

“No.”

“It is not a claim and it is not a demand and it is not a notice and there is no form in this city that this piece of paper is an instance of,” said Wenna Raut. “I cannot enter it.”

“Enter it as nothing.”

“I am going to enter it as nothing,” she said, “and it is going in the drawer with the rest of the nothing, and you are going to stand there and work out for yourself that in about four years the only record in Ostara that a person gave that office something on a particular day is a line in a drawer in a building nobody can walk into from a street, and that the line will not say what they gave or why.”

He looked at it for about nine seconds.

“Why did somebody give us a date.”

“Because somebody gave us a date,” said Wenna Raut. “That is not a question this office has ever been asked and I am not going to improve it. Take it to the table and put it on the bundle and do not tell me what is under it.”

## Two Rooms Where Nobody Asked Him

He heard about both of them in the same week and he was in neither, and that is the shape of the whole of the last four months.

Emil Brack read them out at a wall end at ten to six on four mornings of the last seven, in his own words and not the office’s, and about forty people stood there for it, and the ninth of the nine on the last of those mornings was about a street that has no water on it and he said the reason for it before he wrote the first of the figures and rang it in the margin.

Nobody asked him to read them. He has never once asked anybody for anything in four years at that end of that wall and he did not ask for this, and he did not tell anybody he was doing it, and he refused a thing that was offered him for it, which was that a man in a white plate say at that end of that lane that a man in a delivery coat was the mouth of it.

That would have taken about four seconds and it would have made every one of those nine figures his.

And Agnete Lindqvist said them on the first of the month from the steps in a yard off the Vestry, once, in one month, in her own words, with about eleven thousand people in it, and it was the only thing she had said on those steps in sixteen months that anybody could repeat back to her afterwards, and there is no second month for her and she did not look for one.

Nobody asked her to. She is entitled to say one thing a month on those steps and she has not missed one in about sixteen months, and what she did with it in the ninth month was put four sentences of a printed page into a yard, and about nine hundred of those eleven thousand can read what she said and about four hundred of them are people she has never met.

He was not there. He was at a counter off Cordwain at ten to six on the first of the month with about nine people a week going past him, and he heard it on the second, from the man at the desk under the window, who had heard it in the yard and had got the third of the four slightly wrong, which is the ordinary rate and is wrong about forty times an hour in that yard.

Neither of them was thanked.

Neither of them was thanked by anybody, and neither of them said they wanted to be, and he had arranged in advance, on a hill in the summer, that there would not be a moment in which anybody did.

## What Is Not On The Table

He went down and along at about half past five carrying nothing, which is how he has gone everywhere since the summer, and he stood at the corner of a lane where a man squares about twelve books against a wall at six.

Brack looked up and said good evening to him for the first time in four years and went straight back to the ninth figure, and did not say it twice, and did not look at him again.

That was all that happened and it took about four seconds and he stood there afterwards for about a minute trying to work out what he had come down for.

## The Twenty-Fifth Thing Is Not The First

He worked it out on the walk back and it took him two streets.

The twenty-fifth thing in that bundle is not the first of anything.

The drawer has been shut for seven days.

In it are about four hundred and nine pieces of paper in two cardboard boxes on the floor of a room off a third court, and a bundle of twenty-four things that nobody is obliged to open.

Since the thirtieth of August there has been an entry in a clerk’s hand in the second column of a sheet, and one of the three lines in that column carries his name and a sentence saying that the drawer is not to be opened.

And now there is a piece of pencil paper with a date on it and nothing else, and it is the first thing anybody in this city has handed to that office that is not a copy of the office’s own page.

It is the first thing that came in that was not about him.

*Two thousand four hundred and twenty-seven days. Less two thousand and seventy-eight, the ninth of November of the sixth year, is three hundred and forty-nine days, which is eleven months with nineteen days after them, and a shutter at the far end of a yard off the Vestry has been bare for every one of them.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is seven hundred and nine days, which is twenty-three months with nineteen days after them.*

*It is the third of four sentences at the foot of a sheet. A man in a delivery coat has said it out loud in his own words at a wall end about six days a week for four years, and a chaplain said it once this month from steps in a yard, and neither of them was asked and neither of them is thanked.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is twenty-eight days, and the twenty-seventh sheet has been face up on a rail in a passage off Cordwain for all of them with three lines in the second column of it and eight hundred and fifty-two at the head.*

He went up the stair at about ten past six with a piece of pencil paper the size of a receipt in his coat and he did not open the drawer and he is not going to, and there is no form anywhere in this city for undoing an instruction, and the drawer has about four hundred and nine pieces of paper and a bundle of twenty-four things in it and nobody in that building is obliged to open one item of either.
