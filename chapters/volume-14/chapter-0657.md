# About Nine Houses

Seven days had gone since a woman in a laundry gave him a copy with a number of months in it in charcoal and asked him whether that was where such a thing was supposed to go.

Two thousand one hundred and ninety-three was the fourth of March and the eleventh is day two thousand two hundred, and two thousand two hundred less two thousand one hundred and ninety-three is seven, and eleven less four is seven by the month count.

He spent the morning going from room to room, a kitchen at about seven with washing on a line across it, a barbers at about eleven with the chairs empty and the mirrors covered, a laundry again after noon, trying to see with his own eyes whether about nine houses was nine houses, and he could not count them, because counting them would have been an instrument and there was no instrument.

Nine was not a figure. Nine was what a man said when he had been in three rooms and heard the same thing in each, and what he heard in each was that copies of two words were being made in pencil at home, at tables, and nobody ordered it.

He felt envy, close to joy, watching a woman fold a copy into a pocket with care, and under both the cold thing, because the woman with the charcoal was doing more of the work than his office did.

## The Boy Refuses A Second Time

At about three he went to the corner where the boy was pasting up nothing, because the sixty-third form was already up and the next was not due, and asked him for the sheet, and the boy refused him, on the grounds he gave in December, which were that a rail is read once by about nine people in a week and a corner is a place a thing is said.

Lin Hao did not press him, because pressing him would have restaged what was already decided, and the boy was not to be given an age and was not to be asked twice, and he accepted the refusal with a nod, because a refusal honestly given is a thing a man can carry.

“You are right about the month and the boxes,” he said, “and wrong about nothing, and nobody has asked you anything, and that is the whole of it.”

The boy looked at him a long moment, surprised into stillness, and then went back to cleaning his brush, and neither of them said more, because more would have been a second conversation and neither wanted one.

## Ten To Six In The Lane

At about ten to six he went and stood at a wall end in the lane off Cordwain, where Emil Brack had the slate set up on end and nine or ten people round it in the cold, with twelve books stacked beside it and the date and the weather against every line and a reason in the margin against every ringed one.

Brack read about nine figures a morning, in other words, without skipping and without explaining, and the people listened the way people listen to a man who checks a figure out loud every morning before he writes one.

Afterward Lin Hao stood with him while the crowd thinned, and Brack was right about every fact and wanted nothing, and that was plain from the way he handled his books, squaring them without pride.

“The rate has not changed and is not going to,” said Brack. “I have said nine about two thousand times since the thirteenth of July, and I have never once been able to check whether any of the nine is true. That is the whole of my standing. I read what I can and I say what I cannot.”

Lin Hao felt the envy again, cleaner this time, because here was the only person in Ostara who did the work out loud, and he was doing it without a sheet and without a clerk and without a drawer.

“I want a sheet,” said Brack suddenly, without looking at him. “I have wanted one since the ninth of November and I have not asked you for it and I am not going to, and if I asked you for it you would be able to give me one and that is the reason I am not going to.”

He said it first, before Lin Hao could offer, and Lin Hao did not offer, because offering would have diminished them both, and Brack was not to be thanked and not to be defeated and not to be wrong, and this winter was not going to defeat him.

A woman came back up the lane with a copy in her hand and asked Brack whether a figure on it was true, and he said he did not know, and she said that was the first person who had told her that, and she folded the copy away with something like relief on her face.

Lin Hao walked home in the dark with that in his ears, and what he wanted, plainly and without disguise, was to be the kind of man people asked that of, and he knew as he wanted it that he was not, because his office could not say it did not know without entering the saying.

He was wanted nowhere that evening, and the cold was honest, and he carried it home like a companion.

*Two thousand two hundred days. Less two thousand one hundred and ninety-three, the fourth of March of year seven, is seven. Eleven less four is seven by the month count, because a month is thirty days and has no thirty-first.*
