# He Says No And Means It

He had nine days to make the answer into a sentence and he did not manage it, and on the morning of the third of December he went to the counter with a list of eleven things instead, which is what nine days produces when the thing wanted is a sentence.

Two thousand four hundred and fifty-five was the twenty-sixth of November and the third day of the next month is day two thousand four hundred and sixty-two, and two thousand four hundred and sixty-two less two thousand four hundred and fifty-five is seven, and thirty less twenty-six is four and three days together are seven by the month count.

## Four Minutes Past Ten

He came in at four minutes past ten and the clerk had the folder open and the sheet inside it and the date at the top of it in his own hand, and he did not say good morning.

“Eleven things,” said Lin Hao. “And then the answer. You have not asked me for the eleven things and I am going to give them to you, and you may enter them or you may not, and I am not going to be offended either way, and I am not going to be asked a question about them because I know what your one question is.”

“All right,” said the clerk, and took the pen out, and did not write anything yet.

“I am going to say them slowly because I have had nine days and you have not, and you are going to enter them in the order I say them or not at all, and that is the whole of what I have.”

“Go on.”

“The office was opened on the first of February of the first year and it has a counter that opens when the building opens and there is no street door on it anywhere in the city.”

“The second.”

“It has never sent a man to anybody. Not once. There is no form on which it may and there has not been one in six years.”

“The third.”

“It has never asked anybody to come to it. About ninety pieces of paper came through that front door between the thirtieth of March and the ninth of January, and every one of them was brought by the person who made it, and about eleven a week come and the clerk takes them out of a bag and enters every one of them as nothing.”

“The fourth.”

“It has never had anything sent to it about anybody else’s handwriting. There is no column on any form in it for a piece of paper and there has never been one, and in six years not one person has been asked by it what anybody else wrote.”

He stopped there for about four seconds.

“That is the whole of it in four,” he said. “The other seven are the same fact four more times with dates on and I am not going to waste a clerk’s morning.”

## No

He squared the sheet to the edge of the counter.

“Has this office, at any time since it was opened, put a question to a person in this city about a thing that somebody else had written down.”

“No,” said Lin Hao.

He did not add one word to it. He had thought about adding about four things to it on the stairs and every one of them had been a way of making a bare answer sound like a man defending himself in front of a room, and he had taken them all out.

The clerk wrote one word and a full stop and then put the pen down about an inch from the sheet.

“I am going to tell you what happens to that word,” he said, “and then I am going to ask you the thing I am entitled to ask. It is the same one and I have had it since the fifteenth of September and I am entitled to put it whether or not you like the answer.”

“Ask it.”

He turned the sheet round and put a finger on the line and did not say it again, because he was too exact a clerk to read one sentence out of a folder twice, and he looked at Lin Hao over the top of his own finger until the answer came.

“No,” said Lin Hao, in the same four seconds he had said it the first time.

“You understand what it says.”

“Yes,” he said. “It says that in six years this office has never once asked anybody in this city a question about a thing anybody else wrote down, and that about four hundred and nineteen people who came to a counter and were refused had nobody in this building behind them, and that the reason is not a rule anybody made and it is not a decision anybody took. It is that there is no form.”

“You put the reason in and not the answer.”

“The reason is true and it is in your hands in four lines and you may leave it out, and I am not permitted to improve anything and I am not asking you to improve anything.”

The clerk entered the four lines in the order they were given. He wrote for about two minutes and then he read all of it back in the order it stood, which is what a clerk does, and the last thing he read was the word.

## A Name He Offered And Did Not Insist On

“You may put a name against it,” said Lin Hao.

“There is one. It is mine. It is under a line in the second column of the standing sheet of that office since the twenty-first of October, in a clerk’s hand, with a sentence under it saying that a drawer is not to be opened, and I gave that instruction in the only form this office can be given anything in, and I would like this one to have the same.”

The clerk put the pen down.

“No,” he said.

“I am not asking you to improve it. I am giving you the name.”

“You are giving me the name and I am not permitted to enter a name that was not in the answer,” said the clerk.

“I have told you that I do not improve. That is not a courtesy to you.”

“A finding is read out in a room by a man who has not been in this building, and if your name is on it then the first thing anybody will take hold of is not the four lines and not the word, it is your name, and in about four years a person who has been asking a question of this office for nine years will stop asking it and start asking you.”

“About four hundred people are already going to ask me.”

“Then four hundred people will ask you,” said the clerk, “and they will ask you because they worked out on their own that you are the only door, and they do not need a finding to tell them so. If I put your name on this then in about four years there will be a sheet with your name at the foot of it saying that this office has never asked anybody anything.”

“There is not one form anywhere in this city that says what a name at the foot of that means.”

He turned the sheet round so it faced Lin Hao.

“Read it,” he said.

He read it. It was four lines and a word. The four lines were the things he had said and the word was *no*, and there was nothing on it anywhere that said who had said the four lines, and there was nothing on it anywhere that said who had answered the question.

“It is correct,” he said.

“It is exactly as given,” said the clerk, “and I did not improve one word of it, and I have not been asked to hurry about it.”

## The Question He Was Not Allowed To Ask

He had a second question and he was not allowed to ask it.

He was not allowed to ask it because he had used his one in July and used it in November and there is nobody in this city entitled to put a third to him on this, including him.

The question was whether the finding is read out in a room.

It was the only question in this city he had ever wanted to ask and it is not about him, it is about four hundred people who have written something down and cannot find out whether anybody has read it, and it was on the tip of his tongue at about four minutes past ten in the morning, and he did not ask it and he is not going to ask it.

“The fourth of December,” said the clerk, “and I am drawing it then and I am not telling you what is in it and you are not entitled to know.”

“All right.”

“I will tell you one thing that is not in the file and is mine,” said the clerk.

“Four lines and a word, and the word is the last thing anybody will remember and the four lines are the whole of it. In about four years a man will read the word out in a room and about nine people will say out loud that he has missed the point, inside four minutes, and he will be right, and that is the instrument working.”

He shut the folder.

“I did not put that in,” he said. “I am not permitted to put anything in that was not given to me, including that.”

## The Filing

He went down the stair and along and he was aware, the whole way, that he had come in with eleven things and gone out with a bare word and had been told he could not have his name on either of them.

In about four years there will be a sheet in a building on another side of this city with four lines on it and one word at the end, and the four lines will say that this office has never sent a man to anybody and has never asked anybody to come to it and has never had anything sent to it about anybody else’s handwriting, and the word will be *no*.

And about four hundred and nine pieces of paper will be in a drawer in a building off a third court that has been shut since the twenty-first of October by written instruction, and the office will have entered the receipt of them and not one claim, and there will be a name in the second column under a line saying the drawer is not to be opened.

And the man who gave that instruction is the only person that office may ask.

And there is no form anywhere in this city for undoing an instruction, and there is no form anywhere in this city on which a body may be asked a question by itself, and the finding will say so in one word at four minutes past ten on the fourth of December and not one person in this city will be told.

*Two thousand four hundred and sixty-two days. Less two thousand three hundred and twenty-nine, the twentieth of July of the seventh year, is a hundred and thirty-three days, which is four months with thirteen days after them, and a finding in that building has had two questions in it since the summer and has had no answer to the first of them.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is sixty-three days, which is two months with three days after them, and the twenty-seventh sheet has been face up on a rail in a passage off Cordwain for all of them with three lines in the second column and eight hundred and fifty-two at the head.*

*Less two thousand and ninety-two, the twenty-third of November of the sixth year, is three hundred and seventy days, which is twelve months with ten days after them, and a drawer has been shut since the twenty-first of October under a name and a sentence in a clerk’s hand.*

*Less the ninth of November of the sixth year, two thousand and seventy-eight, is three hundred and eighty-four days, which is twelve months with twenty-four days after them, and less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is seven hundred and forty-four days, which is twenty-four months with twenty-four days after them.*

*The answer entered on the third of this month is the word no, and it is entered without a name on it, and it is the only one of those four entries that has no name against it.*
