# The Question On The File

The second folder was not the first folder and the man behind the counter was the same man, and that turned out to be the whole of what he had expected and none of what he got.

Two thousand four hundred and forty-eight was the nineteenth of November and the twenty-sixth is day two thousand four hundred and fifty-five, and two thousand four hundred and fifty-five less two thousand four hundred and forty-eight is seven, and twenty-six less nineteen is seven by the month count.

## A Second Folder And A Second Question

He was at that counter at about ten in the morning with the twenty-seventh sheet still face up on a rail in a passage off Cordwain and about two hundred and eighty people a day going past the corner of it, and the man at the counter had a folder and a sheet inside it and the sheet was not the sheet.

“This is a different matter,” said the clerk.

“All right.”

“It is the same building and the same counter and it is the same finding as far as the landlord is concerned and it is a different question as far as you are concerned,” said the clerk.

“Every man behind a counter entitled to draw a finding has one question for it. It may be asked once. I asked the one for July on the twentieth of July and it is in that folder and it has not been answered and it is not going to be improved by anybody.”

“I did not answer it.”

“You described the reason you did not answer it and I entered it exactly,” said the clerk, “and I have thought about that answer every day since, and there is a second question on this one and I am entitled to it in exactly the same way, and I have had it since the middle of September.”

## A Question Dated The Fifteenth Of September

He turned the folder round and did not open the flap, and there was a sheet in it with a date at the top of it in his own upright hand and one line under the date.

The fifteenth of September.

That was six weeks before he had gone into the room off a third court at a quarter past three with nine versions of it on the back of his hand, and about two months before a name went under a receipt in a clerk’s hand in the second column of a page, and a sentence went under it saying that a drawer was not to be opened.

“I wrote that down on the fifteenth of September,” said the clerk, “and I have carried it about in this folder for ten weeks and I have not asked it and I am telling you the date so that you cannot say afterwards that you had no notice, and I am telling you the date because you are going to notice what it is, and I would rather you noticed it here than in about four years.”

“I am noticing it.”

“The fifteenth of September is six weeks before you put a name under a line in a column,” said the clerk. “I had a question then and you had not done anything that could be answered. That is not a difficulty with the question. It is the reason I have had it since then and not since October.”

## The Question

He squared the sheet to the edge of the counter and did not touch it again.

“Has this office, at any time since it was opened, put a question to a person in this city about a thing that somebody else had written down.”

It was not about the four lines at the foot. It was not about the landlord and it was not about the water and the nine months and the woman who cannot read, and it was not about the second of the four sentences either, though every person who has ever read that sheet has thought it was.

It was the smallest question anybody in this city had put to that office and it was the only one of the four in ten months that he could not answer in nine seconds.

Because the answer was no, and it had been no since the first of February of the first year.

That office had never sent a man to anybody.

It had never had a form on which a person was asked anything.

It had never gone to a counter and stood at it and wanted to know what a person had written.

It had never asked a woman who could not read whether the pencil at the bottom of her paper was hers, and it had never asked a man at a wall end whether one of his nine figures was true, and it had never asked a boy with a paste brush what the two boxes at the foot of a form were for.

It had been handed things. That was the entire of its dealings with about eleven thousand people for six years and the second of the four sentences at the foot of its own sheet had been true of every one of them.

## What He Could Not Do With It

He stood at that counter for about a minute and a half and the clerk waited with the pen not lifted.

The answer that was true was no, and four words, and it would have gone into a finding in a building on another side of this city and in about four years somebody would read it out in a room and it would be the truest thing anybody in this city had ever been given about itself.

And the reason he could not say it was not the reason he had said it in July, which was that a true answer was a statement about persons.

The reason was simpler and he got it about a minute in and it took him the rest of the day.

The answer was no because he had never asked, and he had never asked because there is no form on which this office may put a question to a person about a thing somebody else wrote, and there has not been one in six years, and he had never once gone looking for one, and Yusuf Adeyemi had told him in February that there was not one and he had believed him.

He was not the office. He was the only person the office might ask. And what he had spent nine years learning to do was to be handed things.

“I am not going to answer it now,” he said.

“I am not going to ask you again,” said the clerk.

“That is not a favour and it is not patience.”

“It is that a question may be asked once and if it is not answered on the day it is put then it is not answered, and there is nobody in this city entitled to put it to you again, including you, and I have written on the sheet in front of me that it was put and not answered, which is what I did in July and which is the only reason that folder is worth anything to anybody.”

He did not write it there and then. He put the sheet into the folder and squared the folder to the edge of the counter and shut it.

“Come back on the third of December,” he said. “I am drawing this on the fourth and I will take an answer at four minutes past ten on the third or not at all, and if you bring me an answer in the middle I will enter it exactly as given and I will not improve it and I will not be asked to hurry.”

## The Passage Off Cordwain, At Twenty Past Eleven

He went down and along and got as far as the passage before it went into him, and about a hundred people a month put a palm on the corner of that counter to get past it, and the sheet is face up on the rail at the height of a man’s chest.

He stood there for about half an hour and let four people go past, and about nine in a week get to the bottom of it, and every one of the four read the second of the four sentences and believed it, because it says in other words that where the Register has nothing in it then nobody has been given anything, and for six years that sentence was true of this office as well as of every person who came to a counter and went home with nothing.

He has been the only person that office may ask since the sixth of February and in seven months he has gone into four rooms and been asked nothing in any of them, and he had filed that under the same heading as a woman with a pencil refusing him a book.

He had not filed it under the heading it belonged under, which was that this office has never sent a man to anybody and never had anything sent to it about anybody else’s handwriting, and that a clerk with a folder has just written the whole of that into a finding in four lines, and that about four hundred people in this city have spent seven months carrying pencil paper up four flights to a door that has never sent anybody back to them.

A counter figure can be added to and cannot be taken from. He had heard that in a yard nine streets from a works department about four years ago and had thought nothing of it, and it turned out to be the whole of his position.

## Down A Stair Off A Third Court

He went on and got as far as the second court and then stopped in it for about four minutes, which he has not done in this city in about two years.

There are four hundred and nineteen accounts in the office’s Register and about nine hundred people who can read every one of them and about eleven thousand people in a yard who can put a wrong sentence right out loud inside four minutes.

And in six years not one person in this city has ever been asked a question by that office, because there is no form on which one may be asked, and there is no second person who may ask it, and he is the only one and he has never once gone and done it.

He had wanted, since the seventh of October, to be asked something.

He had gone to four desks on the fifth of November and none of the four wanted anything off him, and on the nineteenth of November he had been told by a clerk with a folder that in six years not one person has been asked a question by the only body in this city that prints a page about nine hundred people can read.

That was the same fact twice and it cost something the second time that it had not cost the first.

*Two thousand four hundred and fifty-five days. Less two thousand three hundred and twenty-nine, the twentieth of July of the seventh year, is a hundred and twenty-six days, which is four months with six days after them, and a finding in this building has had one question in it since that morning and has had no answer to it.*

*Less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of September of the seventh year, is fifty-six days, which is one month with twenty-six days after them, and the twenty-seventh sheet has been face up on a rail in a passage off Cordwain for all of them.*

*Less two thousand and seventy-eight, the ninth of November of the sixth year, is three hundred and seventy-seven days, which is twelve months with seventeen days after them, and a shutter at the far end of a yard off the Vestry has been bare for every one of them.*

*Less the ninth of November of the fifth year, one thousand seven hundred and eighteen, is seven hundred and thirty-seven days, which is twenty-four months with seventeen days after them. It is the third of four sentences at the foot of a sheet, and not one person in this city has been asked a question by this office in six years, and there is no form anywhere on which one may be asked.*
