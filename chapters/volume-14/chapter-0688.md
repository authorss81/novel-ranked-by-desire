# The Sixty-Fourth Form

He went up the hill at about ten in the morning for the fourth time since the twenty-fourth of July and the thing he went up for had changed on the thirtieth of September without asking him.

*Two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine was the thirtieth of September and the seventh day of October is day two thousand four hundred and six, and two thousand four hundred and six less two thousand three hundred and ninety-nine is seven, and the thirtieth stops the month and seven days on from it is the seventh day of the next one, and there is no thirty-first in this city to stop it earlier.*

## The Boy Was Right About The Month

There were two sheets on the board by the kerb and there had been two on it since the seventh of April, and one of them was a works form for a wall whose top nine feet came off in the fourth week of the fourth month and whose site hut is due to be pulled down with the rest of it, and the other of them was this month’s form for an address.

At the foot of the month’s form there were two ruled squares, one on the left and one on the right, and there was nothing written in either of them and there had not been anything written in either of them on any month’s form since the eighth of October of an earlier year.

The boy said in the summer that he would put the sixty-fourth up in the ninth month of this year.

He had come at about half past nine and he had put it up.

Two hundred people a day stop at that corner and about nine of them a week get low enough to read four inches of dressed stone at the bottom of nine feet of new render. He was there at about ten with the jar of paste on his shoulder, working up from the bottom of the render in a band about eighteen inches wide, the same colour as the rest of it, and stopping dead where the new joint was.

He does not go at the four inches. He has never gone at them: a coat of paint will not stay on stone that has been worked by a mason, and that face has been worked by a mason, and nobody has ever printed a word of that anywhere, and the form on the board asks for nothing of him at the bottom of a wall.

Nobody has asked him anything about either square in about a year and a half. Nobody asked him anything on the seventh of October either.

He was right about the month, and he was right about the two squares, and in seven years of putting them up he has not been wrong about anything, and there is not one instrument in this city that can put a figure in either of them, and he said none of that to anybody, and Lin Hao had come up the hill to say something to him and did not.

He stood four feet off with his hands in his pockets for about forty minutes, and about two hundred people came past and about nine of them got low enough to read the two words cut through the face of the stone at the bottom of the wall, and not one of the two hundred looked at the four inches at all.

They looked at the board.

He had worked that out in July and had told himself it was an answer. It is not an answer. It is that the four inches have become the least-read thing at a place that about two hundred people a day stop at, because the board is at the height of a man’s chest and a man does not have to go down on his knees to get to a piece of paper.

*Two thousand four hundred and six days. Less the twenty-seventh of December of the sixth year, two thousand one hundred and twenty-six, is two hundred and eighty days, which is nine months with ten days after them, and a form saying that the ranks wall comes down to course and make good has been on a board by a kerb since the seventh of April with nothing whatever in either of the two ruled squares at the foot of it.*

*Less the first of February of the seventh year, two thousand one hundred and sixty, is two hundred and forty-six days, which is eight months with six days after them, and the first thing that happened in this year was a piece of pencil paper coming through a front door, and about eleven a week have come through it since, and none of them has been entered against.*

## A Woman With Eleven Sheets

She came up at about twenty past ten with a bundle against her chest and eleven sheets of rag paper in the top of it, and she had been up and down that hill about four times already that week, which he knew because he had been counting.

She stopped at the board and read it, all of it, including the two squares, in about nine seconds. Then she came down the four or five steps of that kerb and went across the road and stood about four feet off him and looked at his coat.

“You are the one whose name is at the bottom.”

“Yes.”

“I have heard there is a drawer,” she said.

“There is a drawer.”

“Not that there is a drawer,” she said. “There is a drawer. A woman in a laundry with a pencil said there is a drawer and she said it to nine people in a week and I am the fourth one of the nine who has come up here to find out whether it is worth carrying eleven sheets four flights.”

He did not say anything for about nine seconds and in those nine seconds he understood the whole shape of what he had done in March and August and on the twelfth of September, and it took him about four of them to get it.

“No,” he said.

“No you cannot ask it.”

“No I cannot ask it,” he said. “I am not the office. I am the only person the office may ask, and asking is all I may do, and I have been the only person it may ask since the sixth of February, and none of that means a woman can walk four floors with eleven sheets in her arms and be told something.”

The hill went on.

“Then that is a thing I can be told at the bottom of the hill instead of the top,” she said. “You have not told me anything and you have not told me nothing, and that is worth the walk down, and I am not going to say thank you for it because you have not done anything.”

“I have not done anything.”

“You have not done anything this morning,” she said, “and I have been up this hill four times this week and two women have told me you did something in June, and I would like to know whether that is the same thing or a different one, and if it is a different one I would like to know now while I am standing here and not in four years.”

He put his hands in his pockets.

That was the moment.

 It was the first time in seven years that a person had stopped at that corner and put a question to him, and it was not the question he had spent four months waiting for, and it was a question he had no standing to answer, because the only thing he was permitted in that city was to ask, and there was nothing in the world he could ask for on behalf of a woman with eleven sheets that would be any use to her.

And he could.

That was the other half and it went through him at about the same speed as the first one. He could ask.

 He was the only person that office may ask and there was no second person in Ostara who could ask, and nobody in that building was going to stop him, and Yusuf Adeyemi had told him in the seventh month of a year neither of them names that there was nobody in this city who could tell him that he was not allowed to do a thing he had come in there to do.

He could ask it to hold eleven sheets from a woman off a hill. He could not do it on the back of an envelope to her.

That was the whole of what was in it. He could put a thing on the same piece of paper as a name, and it would be as official as the four sentences at the foot of his own page, and about four hundred people in this city had spent seven months finding out that an official thing which is not entered is not an official thing at all.

He did not say it.

She watched him not say it for about four seconds and then she nodded as though it had been a form with her name at the top of it and she had filled it in and handed it back.

“All right,” she said. “I have carried them up four times and I can carry them four times more. My sister had a bundle like that in the fourth year and nobody ever gave her a receipt and she stopped talking about it and I have not been able to get her to start again, and that is the only reason I have eleven sheets.”

She went back up the steps and read the board again and went down the hill on the other side.

*Two thousand four hundred and six days. Less the thirtieth of June of the seventh year, two thousand three hundred and nine, is ninety-seven days, which is three months with seven days after them, and the twenty-seventh sheet has been face up on a rail in a passage off Cordwain for all of them with three lines in the second column of it and eight hundred and fifty-two at the head.*

## Four Inches At The Bottom Of Nine Feet

He stayed until about half past eleven and about two hundred and forty people came past.

About nine of them got low enough to make out the two words at the bottom of the wall, and one of the nine was a girl with her bag in her hand who went down on one knee on the kerb, read them, and said something out loud that he did not hear and did not ask about, and got up again and went on.

Two hundred and forty people came past and about ninety read the board, and about nine read the form under it, and nine knelt at the foot of a wall that has been standing for more than thirty years, and not one person did both.

He had come up that hill for four months waiting to be asked something by somebody and he had been asked something by somebody, 

The question had been a good one and completely fair and he had answered it correctly, and the answer was that the only thing he could do was to go and ask a room to hold eleven sheets.

He has been able to do that since the sixth of February and he did not do it, and the reason is that about four hundred people in this city have found out that a holding is not an entry, and a person who is told the truth about that cannot be given anything else.

He came down the hill at about half past eleven and there were about forty people at that end of a lane off Cordwain and a man with a slate on end and twelve books squared beside it, and the man read nine figures out loud and said the reason for every one of them before he wrote it, and did not look up, and did not say good evening, and had not said good evening to him for four years.
