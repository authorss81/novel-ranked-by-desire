# The Spike With Nine Hundred On It

Seven days had gone since a nine-word sentence had gone back into a coat instead of into a book, and the office had done nothing at all, which was correct.

Two thousand one hundred and eighty was the twenty-first of February and the twenty-eighth is day two thousand one hundred and eighty-seven, and two thousand one hundred and eighty-seven less two thousand one hundred and eighty is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven by the month count.

It was an ordinary morning of an ordinary month.

## About Nine Thousand People Go Under It

About nine thousand people go under that wall in a day.

The reason is that it is an ordinary morning of an ordinary month and the trams go under it, and there has never been anything else about it, and it has stood there for more than thirty years and it is not going to stop because a man in a rank-one coat has started looking at the bottom of it.

He stood at about nine feet off at about ten in the morning and let about nine hundred of them go past in the time he stood there.

None of them looked up. That is what a wall is for. A wall is not read by the people who go under it; it is read by the two hundred a day who stop at the corner at the far end of it, and those two hundred are a different set and they are not the same two hundred twice.

## Sixty-One Days On A Spike

The form went in at a counter nine streets away on the twenty-seventh of December under a number the size of a thumbnail, and it has been on a spike ever since.

Sixty-one days.

It has a number against it the size of a thumbnail and the number means that somebody once wrote down an address, and nobody has moved it, because moving it is not in anybody’s job.

Nobody paints that wall. The wall belongs to the works department and the painting is somebody else’s contract in another building nine streets the other way, and in nine years neither of those two offices has written to the other one, and there is no arrangement anywhere in this city that would make them.

He had walked to that counter on the twenty-seventh of December and stood in a queue behind a barrow of broken paving and been handed a piece of paper about the size of a lease, and had carried it in a coat for sixty-one days and never once turned it over.

The man at the counter came out of the building while he was standing at the kerb, because the man at the counter has been watching for somebody to turn that piece of paper over for about nine weeks.

“You have had it in your coat since December,” said the man at the counter.

“I have.”

“Nine weeks.”

“Sixty-one days.”

“Six weeks and about five days,” said the man at the counter, “and I have had about nine hundred and forty of these and every one of them is a wall, and I have never seen a man carry one that long and never once look at the back of it.”

“It was upside down.”

“That is not a reason, it is a method,” said the man at the counter, “and you have been carrying the answer to a question you have been asking that building for about two hundred and seventy-nine days, and you worked that out on a kerb this morning in front of me, and I would have told you for nothing if you had asked me in December.”

He went back in before Lin Hao could say anything about it.

He turned it over at the kerb by the board.

The form has a single ruled square printed at the bottom of it, and the square is where the day the work was finished goes.

That day has not arrived.

He had been carrying the answer since the twenty-first of February of an earlier year.

## About Nine Hundred Lines

The schedule hung in a corridor off the second floor of that building where anybody who wanted to could stand and read it, and there was a line printed under the last of the nine hundred lines saying so, and the line has said so longer than the man at the counter has been on the job.

He got up the two flights in four and had to stop on the landing, and he stood on the landing for a while and then went the rest of it, and that is what he does now and there is not one instrument in this city that puts it back.

Nine hundred lines, and every one of them has three things on it and no more: where, what, and when it was finished.

The ranks wall was on it in about four minutes, because he has walked past the foot of it for six years and he knows what the address says.

What it wanted done to that wall was four words and a full stop. The little square at the end of the line for finishing it was empty, and it is empty on eight hundred and eighty-nine of the nine hundred lines, and the eleven that are filled in are filled in with dates from before the wall was built.

There is not one column on that schedule about what is on a wall. There is one about what is to be done to one, and one about when it was finished, and that is the whole of the instrument.

He stood in a corridor reading a wall for about four minutes and then he went down two flights in four and stood at the bottom of them on the step.

## What The Boy Has Noticed

The boy was at the corner with a paste brush in his fist and the sixty-third form was already up on the board by the kerb and had been up since about ten past four the previous day.

There are two ruled squares at the foot of that form and both of them are empty.

“They have been past twice,” said the boy.

“Who.”

“Two men with a thing on a stick that goes out when you push a button,” said the boy. “They stood where you are standing and they looked up at it for about four minutes each and they did not stop and they did not write anything down.”

“What was on the stick.”

“A mark they made on the render and then took off again,” said the boy. “It went up about four feet and it came down again and it went up higher and it came down again. It is the only thing anybody has ever done at that wall in about four years and it was two men in coats getting wet.”

He wiped the brush on his coat.

“They did not ask me anything,” he said, “and they did not ask you either, and they would not have, and there is nothing on a form about four inches of stone at the foot of anything, so they had nothing to ask me and I had nothing to tell them and we were all three of us doing our work.”

“That is right.”

“It is right and it is in the spring,” said the boy. “And nobody has ever asked me anything about those two boxes and nobody is going to, and I have stopped waiting, and you are the only person who has ever asked me about them and you did not ask me, you stood there.”

## Two Thousand People A Day Slow Down At Nothing

The shutter at the far end of that yard has had nothing on it for a hundred and nine days.

It is not cleaned. It is bare. Three boards came off it on the ninth of November in about nine hours and the paint underneath has gone the colour of weak tea where the last one hung, and that rectangle has been there since before the month turned.

About two thousand people a day come down that path and cannot get by without slowing for it.

They slow down. There is nothing there. They go on.

He stood at about nine feet off it at about half past eleven and watched four of them do it and not one of them stop and not one of them say anything, and a man came down the path behind him with a child on his shoulders and looked at the pale rectangle and did not ask.

He has watched that corner since the thirtieth of September and he had a rag in his hand at the foot of it and it is the only thing he has ever built by standing still.

## What He Wants There

He wanted a field on the schedule for what was on the wall.

That was the whole of the want, and he stood on a wet step with it and it was not available anywhere, and he had spent about nine years finding that out about one thing after another.

He wanted a form with a line on it for two words cut into a stone.

There is no such line. Not on the works schedule and not on the Bureau contract and not on the month’s form the boy paints, and a man making good a wall takes the render off and puts render on and has never in the history of this city been asked about the stone at the foot of one.

And there is a third thing he wanted and he did not let himself finish it, which was that he wanted somebody to have already noticed, and there was not one person, and he had known there would not be.

## The Schedule Comes Down On The First

The schedule comes down on the first of the month and goes up again on the last working day of it, and the twenty-eighth is not the last working day of this month, so it would have been there for two more days.

He looked at the line for the ranks wall once more before he went.

*Take down to course and make good.*

Nine hundred walls on that corridor and not one of them had ever been asked what was at the foot of it, and the reason was not a decision anybody had made. It was that there was nowhere to write the answer down, and a man who has nowhere to write an answer down does not ask the question, and that is the whole of it and it has been the whole of it for longer than he has been alive.

At about half past eleven he walked back to the corner and the shutter at the far end of the yard was bare.

About two thousand people a day slow down at nothing, and they have been doing it for a hundred and nine days, and not one of them has walked into a counter about it.

And at this corner there are about two hundred people a day, and about two of them a week get to the foot of anything, and there is nothing at the foot of it to read, and there has been nothing at the foot of it since a month in an earlier year that the boy has carried in his head since he was small.

At that corner, in pencil, in about nine houses a week, somebody is now writing something in two inches of nothing.

And nobody in this city has ever given a piece of paper away at that corner.

*Two thousand one hundred and eighty-seven days. Less two thousand one hundred and eighty, the twenty-first of February of the seventh year, is seven, and twenty-eight less twenty-one is seven by the month count.*

*Less the twenty-seventh of December of the sixth year, two thousand one hundred and twenty-six, is sixty-one days, which is two months with one day after them, and a form saying take down to course and make good has been on a spike nine streets away for all of them, under a number the size of a thumbnail.*

*Less the ninth of November of the sixth year, two thousand and seventy-eight, is one hundred and nine days, which is three months with nineteen days after them, and a shutter at the far end of a yard has had nothing at the foot of it for all of them.*
