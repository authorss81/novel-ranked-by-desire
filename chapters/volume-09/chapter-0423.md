# The Envelope He Would Have To Open

The envelope was on the shelf at the head of the stair, under a chandler's bill and a coil of tarred string, and it had premises 34-6-118 written across the face of it in a clerk's hand and nothing whatever under the address.

Isolde Marchetti found it at about ten in the morning on the eleventh of May because she was doing what she does, which is to say a thing out loud twice while she does it, and the shelf is the first thing anybody in that house looks at.

"Right," she said. "That is nine. Shelf is eight, shelf has been eight since I came in, and that is nine, and I do not know whose it is and I am not going to open it in front of a stove."

She put it down on the table instead and put her hand flat on it, which is what she does with a sum she has got wrong.

The kitchen had four women in it by about ten and a half and the fifth came in while the coffee was going.

Agnete Lindqvist was at the end nearest the door in the grey she wears at that hour, Petra Ilyin had a docket under her arm and her sleeves pushed up, and Nadia Ferrous still had her jacket on because she had come in off a car.

Beatrix Halm was standing with her back to the table in the position she takes when there are four people talking, because there have been four people talking in that room for two years and she cannot hear one of them.

"You have found the service," said Runa Sato, from the doorway, with her coat off one arm.

"How do you know about it."

"Because a service on a described subject comes to the head of the premises and not to the door, and the description of those premises was made against the head of them in October, and so the service comes against the head, and there is only one head in this building."

She hung the coat on the hook. "It went on that shelf on the morning of the twentieth of February at about the same time it went into a room at the end of a corridor, and nobody has opened it, and I have known that since the seventh of May and I did not think it was worth a kitchen."

"It is worth a kitchen," said Agnete Lindqvist.

## What A Service On A House Is

"Take it up," said Runa Sato, "and do not open it yet."

Nadia Ferrous picked it up at the bottom edge with two fingers and turned it over. The back of it was blank, which is the correct thing, and two of them looked at each other about it.

"There is no name," said Nadia Ferrous.

"There is no name," said Runa Sato, "and there is going to be no name. It is not a mistake and it is not rudeness and it is nobody in a division being careless with a person's privacy."

"A service on a described subject goes against the head of the premises. It does not know anybody. It knows the description, the description was made against the head, so the service is made against the head, and it comes up a stair and goes on a shelf in a hall and is done."

"And if somebody opens it."

"Then the person who opens it has taken the service." She put her cup down. "Not the sheet. The service. Those are two different animals and you will not enjoy the difference between them. Being handed a service is being handed the thing a subject is given at the moment he is served, and once it is in your hands this city has nothing at all for putting it back down on the shelf, and nobody at this table has ever wanted to pick one up."

"Then why has it sat there since the twentieth of February."

"Because it was put there on the morning of the twentieth of February by a runner doing the ordinary thing, and no one in this house thought the ordinary thing was for them."

Runa Sato looked at the envelope on the table. "The requisition is eighty-one days old. It was laid on the twentieth of February, read for nine minutes at ten o'clock, and the chair wrote four words about it that are in a minute now, and one of the four is *served*. That word is why this is on that shelf and not in a drawer."

"It is the only thing that has come to the head of this stair," said Petra Ilyin. She turned her docket over and put it down on the table face down, which she does when she is going to talk about an instrument. "Nine volumes. Four sheets, a page, a finding, a list, a declaration of holding, a priced line, a witness line and a minute. Every one of them was put in a room. This one came here."

## Eight

"Then somebody opens it," said Nadia Ferrous, "and it is me, and I am the one who works a lift and I am the one who is not on any post in this city that a division can serve a paper at, and there is no division of mine to have a service in it, and that has been worth exactly nothing until this morning."

She did not put her hand on it.

"Nadia," said Agnete Lindqvist.

"I have priced it. It is not expensive. It is the opposite of expensive." She took her jacket off at last and put it over the back of a chair. "Eight."

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"Read the fifth line," said Runa Sato. "It is not under the address. Turn it so the writing is the right way up and read the fifth line and do not read it out loud, and then put it down."

Nadia Ferrous turned it. It took her about two seconds.

Then she put it face down on the table with both hands flat on either side of it and did not say a word, and Beatrix Halm, who cannot hear a person at that table and has not been able to for two years, came round the table and looked at the face of it upside down and then went and got her notebook.

"That is five lines," she said, writing. "It is a printed form with five lines on it and somebody has filled them in by hand."

"Read it to me," said Lin Hao.

She wrote a little longer and then turned the notebook round.

*Laid. Twentieth day of February. Heard. Same day. Room at the end of the corridor off the second landing. Not carried. Composition of the premises at the date of service: eight.*

The kitchen was very quiet, and the reason it was quiet was not the number.

## The Two Sevens

"That is the twenty-eighth of April," said Runa Sato. "I know it, because I have been in that office for nine years and a composition is administered weekly against the composition, and the figure has been seven every week since the first of April, and before the first of April it was eight, and there is a line in that book that goes back and the last figure on it before the first of April is eight."

"Nine forty-one on the thirteenth of March," said Nadia Ferrous.

"You have said that out loud at this table once already and I am not going to have you say it again this morning."

Runa Sato's voice was entirely level. "You are not going to say it again because you told us on the third of May and because I have carried the number seven in my head since about eleven o'clock that morning and there is nothing in that number that is yours and there is nothing in it that is mine and there is a great deal in it that is his."

"That is not fair," said Agnete Lindqvist.

"It is completely fair. It is nine forty-one on the thirteenth of March and it is a figure and I have never once in nine years met a figure I could not put a person behind, and that is my whole gift and my whole trade, and I have just done it to the two people in this room who least deserve it."

## What He Broke

That was when he put his hand on it.

"Stop," said Nadia Ferrous. "Do not. If you open that, you have taken the service."

"The sheet stays in the room," said Runa Sato. "She is right about taking a service and she is wrong about what is in it. It is not the sheet."

"A man who is served is served and not consulted and he is not handed a copy, and that is correct, and the copy is in the room and stays there. What comes down a stair is the notice. Five lines."

"It is the only paper about that requisition that has ever existed outside that corridor, and it has been on that shelf under a bill from a chandler for eighty-one days."

"You are a member of the room that carries it," said Petra Ilyin.

"I am."

"Then you are the one person in this city who is not allowed to open it, and I am telling you that as a woman who has been under an instrument for eleven years and knows exactly what a person is when they touch one."

She had not moved from the table. "You would be a member of the room, and you would be the subject of the paper, and you would be holding the notice of the service of your own sheet. There is no line anywhere in this city that permits that, and if there were one I would know it, because that is the trade I am in."

Lin Hao broke the wax.

It took him about four seconds with a knife he did not have to look for, and the noise it made was very small, and four women at a kitchen table in Ferrier Street watched a man open his own service in front of them, and none of them said anything at all while he did it.

## The Fifth Line

He read five lines and then he put it down on the table face up, which was the opposite of what Nadia Ferrous had done to it, and nobody turned it back over.

"Eight," he said.

"Eight," said Runa Sato.

"The requisition has been administered weekly since the first of April against the composition of that premises. The composition has been seven since the thirteenth of March. And on the morning of the twentieth of February a runner from a division put a piece of paper on the shelf at the head of our stair with the number of people in this house on it, and put it there because a description of this house was made against the head of it in October, and went away again."

"That is what a service is," said Runa Sato. "It is not a letter and it is not a notice of a decision and it does not take anything off anybody. It is a piece of paper that says where a subject is and how many people are keeping the address, and it was true on the twentieth of February and it has been false since the thirteenth of March."

"Somebody should have told us."

"Somebody should have told us, and there was nobody to tell us, and the reason there was nobody to tell us is that there was nothing to be told."

"The service happened on the twentieth of February and a service does not wait for a composition to change. A composition is read when it is administered, and it is administered on a fixed day by a person, and the person who administered it that morning read it and wrote down what it was. That was eight, and eight was correct."

She put both hands flat on the table.

"That is what the fifth is," she said. "Not a seal and not a vote. A sheet of paper that comes down a stair onto a shelf in this house because somebody made a description of this house against its head in October and wrote down how many of us there were, and it does not know that four of us are officers of the holding, and one of us put a space in a line on purpose, and one of us cannot be sworn at all."

## What It Closed

"You have opened it in a kitchen with four women in it," said Petra Ilyin.

"Yes."

"Then you are not a stranger to that service any more, and you were this morning, and there is no instrument in this city that puts a man back to being a stranger." She picked her docket up off the table.

"I will price it, because it is my trade. A sheet in a room is a sheet in a room. The moment two people in this city have read the service it stops being the room's paper and becomes a fact about a house. In four years somebody is going to want to know who else has seen the service of that requisition, and there are five of you, and it is not going to be five in four years."

"So the cost is on the eight."

"The cost is on whoever you did not think about when you put your hand on it."

She stopped at the door. "You did it in front of four women because you did not want to do it in front of nobody. That is the arithmetic of it and it is the whole of it, and every time a thing like this happens in this house it is the same arithmetic, and it has been the same arithmetic since the twenty-ninth of December, and I have watched eight women do it four times."

She went out.

Nadia Ferrous picked the envelope up off the table, folded it once, and put it in the drawer where the string was, which is where a thing goes when a person has decided it is going to be looked at again.

"You do not have to put it in the drawer," said Lin Hao.

"I know you do not," said Nadia Ferrous. "Eight is my bill and I said so at this table eight days ago and I am not going to say it again this morning."

"I have just put a piece of paper in a drawer that says the number of us, and in four years one of us is going to have to say out loud what is on it. It is going to be me. It is always me."

"I would rather have it in a drawer than on a shelf where a child could get at it."

## The Question The Fifth Line Asked

Beatrix Halm wrote for about eleven seconds more and then shut the notebook and put the pencil in her coat pocket and came back and stood at the table with the other four.

"It is on the shelf under a bill," she said, loud, for everybody, the way she says things.

"Yes."

"And it went on there on the twentieth of February and that form gives me nowhere at all to put the difference and it has been in the drawer since this morning."

"There is not."

"Then somebody has to write down that it was eight and now it is seven." She said it as though it were a sum and not a question, because that is the only way she has ever been able to say a thing like it. "That is what I do. That is the whole of what I have ever done. Somebody hands me a number and I write down what the number was and what time it was and I am not in it."

"You are not in it," said Runa Sato.

"I have never been in it."

Runa Sato looked at the drawer where the envelope had gone, and then at him, and then at the two of them together.

"Four times a year," she said. "On a printed sheet with one box on it, a woman signs her own name at the head, writes down how many people are keeping that address, and countersigns at the address. And it is good for the quarter it was done in and no quarter beyond that one."

"She did it on the thirtieth of November. She did it again on the thirtieth of December, and again on the thirtieth of January, and again on the thirtieth of February. And then on the morning of the thirtieth of March a small printed form arrived down at that division with a single box on it, and she put a figure in it in about four minutes and carried on with the rest of the morning."

The kitchen did not stop and nobody spoke for a while.

"That woman is not on the roll," said Agnete Lindqvist.

"That woman is not on the roll and cannot be sworn and has a bar on being reached that she put on herself, and she has certified that address five times since the thirtieth of November," said Runa Sato, "and he found out on the nineteenth of April and he has not told her a single word of it, and I am not going to do it for him in a kitchen either."
