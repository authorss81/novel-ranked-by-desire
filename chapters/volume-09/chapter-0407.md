# The Arithmetic Of Eight Hands

The muster room off Cordwain is a long low building with a stove in it and forty folding chairs and a floor that has had four hundred pairs of boots on it since the war, and at four in the morning on the twelfth of April there was one lamp on and Runa Sato sitting on the end of a table with a mug of something she was not drinking and a sheet of paper she had ruled herself.

She had ruled it herself because she did not trust a book.

"Sit down," she said. "You have about an hour before the shift and I am going to give it to you in the wrong order twice and you are going to write it down, because the second time is the one that matters and the first time is the one you will remember."

"Which is the second one."

"That is exactly the wrong question." She put the mug down. "It is half past three in the morning and you have not asked me one single question in eight months that I have been able to enjoy."

## Six Of The Eight

"Six of the eight at that table hold a post a division can serve a paper at."

She wrote them down as she said them, in her own hand, in a column, with nothing to the right of them.

"Field. That is me. Counter-forensics, second sub-level, which is Wren Kallo and there is another name in that division I am not going to say. The Ferrier line, which is Beatrix Halm, and a signal operator is a division whether she is on a tram or not, and a tram gets stopped in the middle of a street with everybody in it looking out of a window."

"The guild, which is Petra Ilyin, and a guild round gets served at a guild door in about four seconds. The Church of the Quiet Number, which is Agnete Lindqvist and eleven thousand people with no ranks and no register."

"And the sixth."

"Veil-Research. Second floor, iron stair, off a court with no name on it that a stranger could carry in his head." She put the pen down on that one. "And that one is not in a building. That one shares a building with a man who has been on the floor above it since before you had a plate, and he has not set foot in her corridor since the start of February, and you put nine names on a roll on the second."

"Sun Meiling is not on the roll."

"Sun Meiling is the reason the sixth one is worth saying out loud." Runa Sato looked at him. "There is not one of these six that a division cannot reach. There are two at that table with no post at all and one of them is a lift between two floors."

"And the two?"

"Nadia Ferrous and Isolde Marchetti. Nadia's is a lift on Ferrier Street that a man can stand in, and Isolde's is a room in Bellhouse four nights a week with nine hundred people in it, and there is not one door in this city that either of those two can be served at."

She squared the sheet.

"You have six a division can put a paper into the hand of and two it can only find a building for, and you knew most of that, and you came up here at four in the morning because you want it from the woman who prices it."

## The Instrument

"Now the part you have not asked about," said Runa Sato. "I am going to give it to you once. In a hundred words. Then I am going to shut up about it."

"A witness line is a list of the people who were in a room on a date. That is all it is. It is not a list of who lives at an address and it cannot be made into one, and the day somebody tries to make it into one is the day a document in this city can be corrected by whoever happens to be standing nearest to it."

He had not said anything.

"A name on a witness line cannot be struck. Not by the person who wrote it, not by the person whose name it is, not by anybody in this city, in any quarter, ever."

She tapped the sheet. "Superseded is not withdrawn, and I have been watching you work that out for about four months."

"Nadia Ferrous gave up an address at a counter on the morning of the thirteenth of March, because she wanted her name off every paper that carries that address anywhere on it."

"She is still the eighth name under a sheet in a room at the end of a corridor, and she cannot be got off it. She told me so herself at a counter in February, and she said it the way a woman says the price of something."

"So it is the same instrument."

"It is the same instrument and you know the price of it to the grain, because you set it." Runa Sato put both hands flat on the table. "That is why you are awake."

## The Turn

He bought a line like this in January.

He bought it on purpose.

He paid for it with eight women in a minute.

He is about to buy the same line again, and this time it is the only door he has.

"That is not a discovery," said Runa Sato. "That is arithmetic and you did it at that table in about four minutes in the middle of January and you went to bed pleased with yourself. Go on."

"The floor has gone up fourteen points in one day and down fifteen on a card. Eight hands under a page."

"Three attestations move a plate," she said. "You have never once scored a grain yourself. You have done a great deal and nothing has ever scored and you have known that for five volumes, and every figure you have ever had came from somebody else writing something, and you have spent four months on a kitchen table taking that very seriously."

She picked the pen up and drew a line under the column.

"That is what the eight are. They are not witnesses that you were there. They are the eight people whose names the only instrument in this business runs on."

## The Price Is Not Their Price

"I am going to have to certify something," said Runa Sato. "That is the part I have been not saying since about two o'clock in the morning."

"At a composition."

"At the address, in the ordinary way, at the top of the sheet with my name on it and nothing above that name." She wrote the field on the sheet. "A consignment is witnessed on the day it is lodged and at the address of the holding, and the address of the holding is a kitchen on Ferrier Street, and if a person is going in the room to certify the composition of a premises they go as the person who certifies compositions."

"Then the Field division has you."

"The Field division has me on paper against a man, and it has had me on paper against a man since the twentieth of October, and the difference is that this one will have a figure on it." She capped the pen. "I have decided that I will do it and I want you to understand that I decided it about four minutes before you knocked and I did not decide it because you asked me at a table on the fourteenth."

"What is it going to cost you."

"My file, in about two years, to a stranger in an office who is not cruel and is not anybody you have ever met and will simply be working." She stood up. "That is the honest price and it is mine and not theirs."

He got up as well, because there was one left and he wanted it answered in the room.

"Runa. What does it cost to ask eight people in one room."

She looked at him for about four seconds.

"Nothing," said Runa Sato. "That is the whole of the answer and you have had it wrong for eight volumes, and I am going to give it to you in one sentence and then I am going to go and hand a report to a man who does not care."

She picked up her coat.

"The cost is not theirs. It is yours, and it is that you will never be able to say you did not know."
