# The House That Is Nine People

Eight women were at the kitchen table at Ferrier Street at half past eight on the evening of the twelfth of June, and the ninth name on the roll was not at that table and had never been at it, and Isolde Marchetti counted them out loud the way she sums, because somebody had to say it.

"It is one hundred and twelve days since the twentieth of February," he said from the doorway, because the date had to be said somewhere. "Ten from twenty leaves ten, with thirty and thirty and thirty and twelve makes one hundred and twelve."

"Eighteen days to the thirtieth," said Runa Sato. "Thirty taken from twelve leaves eighteen. That is the quarter."

## Eight At One Table

Agnete Lindqvist had her coat on over her shoulders, back from the room she has left twice in eleven years and will have to leave four more times before the quarter ends. Runa Sato had her ruled sheet folded in her pocket. Nadia Ferrous sat with her hands round a cup. Wren Kallo had a pen in her fingers and said nothing.

Isolde Marchetti had chalk on her sleeve. Beatrix Halm had her hand flat on the wood so she could feel the talk through it. Petra Ilyin stood at the stove. Sun Meiling sat at the end with her folder against the chair.

"The house is nine people," said Isolde Marchetti. "Six of us sleep under this roof and are sworn. Two of us sleep under it and are not. One of us is sworn and sleeps elsewhere. That is the arithmetic and it has been the arithmetic since the second of April."

"The six who sleep here and are sworn," said Petra Ilyin, "are Beatrix, Nadia, Runa, Isolde, Agnete and me."

"The two who sleep here and are not," said Runa Sato, looking at Sun Meiling and then at Wren Kallo, "are the officer of the composition and the officer of the finding. Neither can be sworn. That is not news."

## What The Seventh Signed

Nadia Ferrous put her cup down.

"I took us to seven at nine forty-one on the thirteenth of March," she said. "By myself. At a counter. In nine minutes. The paper says seven and eight of us sleep under this roof. I have said my bill out loud at this table and I am saying it again because the twelfth of June needs it."

"You are the reason for the rest of your life," said Isolde Marchetti, not unkindly. "Nobody is ever going to ask you about it and you are not going to want them to."

"And the seven who signed on the eighteenth of April are seven officers of the holding," said Runa Sato. "Six of us heard that sentence that night and nodded. The space in the middle of the line is the only thing standing between any of them and a room of five, and it is hers."

She nodded at Sun Meiling, who did not look up.

Wren Kallo said the old sentence about a cut space into her pen, quietly, and no one at the table sought an explanation, because it has been in this house since the eighteenth of April and belongs to the woman at the end of the table.

## The Ninth Who Is Not Here

"What about the ninth," said Beatrix Halm, her hand flat, feeling the words. "Say her."

"The ninth is Ines Duarte," he said. "On Lantern Row. On her own plate. She signed the roll on the second of April in person without arguing. She is not at this table. No paper is read in her rooms again, not to her and not by me."

"And the other two," said Agnete Lindqvist. "Say them too, so the house hears it whole."

"Tessa Okonkwo in her own rooms over the cold store. Marguerite Vale in the smaller rooms at the Vant. Both signed on their own paper from their own plates. Both bound. Neither under this roof."

Petra Ilyin turned from the stove.

"Then the house that is nine people has never been in one room," she said. "Not on the second of April. Not tonight. Eight at this table and one elsewhere. That is the shape of it and it is not going to change before the thirtieth."

Sun Meiling stood up then. She had said nothing the whole evening, at the end of the table with her folder, and now she spoke, once.

"I certified on the twenty-ninth of May knowing what I am," she said. "I am the officer of this holding by a certificate I fill in four minutes. On the thirtieth of June there will be no certificate and a keeper will write my name from memory. I am not asking any of you to carry that. I am telling you so that when it arrives you know where it came from."

Nobody answered for about eleven seconds. Beatrix Halm felt the silence through the wood and kept her hand where it was.

Then Runa Sato said, "Noted," and the table breathed again.

## What Holding Means

Petra Ilyin put a pot on the table and sat down with them, which she rarely does before the tea is made, and the talk turned to the small things because the large one had been said.

"The thirtieth is eighteen days away," said Agnete Lindqvist. "The book I put the line in closes on the day before the first. It went in on the first of June. It cannot be taken out. Eleven thousand people walk past that book every quarter and most of them never open it, and that is the whole of its safety."

"And the return goes out at four every week until then," said Runa Sato. "Nine lines and a tenth with a box. The seventh stands at five. Beatrix writes on the back of it what no office can ask her for, and the clerk keeps it, and once it is in no hand in this city gets it back out."

Beatrix Halm lifted her hand off the wood and put it round her cup, and smiled at nothing.

"I will be at the north end every morning," she said. "Nine years. Hands cold round the pencil. On the thirtieth the card comes down at six and I will write a name down and put the time against it."

Wren Kallo capped her pen. Sun Meiling picked up her folder. Isolde Marchetti began to sum under her breath, because nobody agrees with arithmetic, and the table agreed with nothing, the way it has every evening for nine months.

He stayed in the doorway with eighteen days in the quarter and a requisition one hundred and twelve days old administered against the composition of the address they were sitting in, and he understood, the way he had understood nothing else that evening, that the household he had asked for on the fourteenth of April was holding, and that holding was not the same as shelter.
