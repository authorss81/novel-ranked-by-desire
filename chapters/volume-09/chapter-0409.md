# The Movement That Cannot Be Undone

The public post off a third court is on the ground floor of a building that has a name on a plate nobody reads, and above it on the second floor there is a division with a woman in it who has been putting boxes in for eleven years, and above her on the third floor there is a man with a window on a yard who has been doing work he has been doing for nine years.

He had never lodged anything in his life. He had been served with a great many things and had demanded a production of one of them once, and he had not got a single sheet away from that room at the end of a corridor in about nine months.

The counter opened at eight and he got there at ten minutes to nine with about nine other people in front of him.

## The Boy With The Paste Brush

The boy behind the counter was the one who put a card on a wall at six in the morning on the thirtieth of March with a paste brush and a list of numbers, and he was the same age and he had the same hands, and he was not told what he was holding and did not ask.

That was the entire safety of it. There was no instrument, no permission, no witness and no holder, and a public post takes all four in the ordinary way for nothing, and nobody at a public post in this city is required to know what a person has brought.

"The same office that set the card," said the boy, when Lin Hao had put his plate on the stone. "Different paper. It goes in the out-tray, same as the renewal."

"Same as the renewal."

"Renewals run to about four inches by two with a box on them. You have not got a box."

"I have not got a box."

"Then I do not know what it is, and I am going to have to look at it, and looking at it is not the same as knowing what it is."

The boy turned the sheet over once and looked at the back of it, which was blank, and put it in the out-tray.

"That goes down the chain at four in the afternoon on the ordinary stream. You will get nothing back, because there is no copy and no receipt, and anybody who tells you there is a receipt is talking about a different instrument altogether."

## One Movement

He had written it at about half past five that morning at the table in his own rooms, on one sheet of plain paper, in his own hand, and it took him about ninety seconds, because there is very little to write.

It said what he was lodging. It said that the whole of it was lodged and not a part of it. It said the date, which was the fifteenth of April, and the place, which was a public post off a third court, and it said that the address at which the holding is kept is premises 34-6-118 and that the witnessing is at that address on the day.

It did not say why. There is no place on that instrument for why.

His hands were not steady and they were steady by the time the pen was on the paper, which he noticed and did not think about.

The boy read it. That is the whole of what a clerk does at a public post: read the thing, check the name against the name on the plate, and enter it.

"Lin Hao."

"Yes."

"Your plate is fifteen under a floor that came down on the thirtieth of March." He said it the way a boy says a thing he has been told to say. "Are you aware of that."

"I am."

"You get one of these or you do not. Once you have got it there is no office in this city that can take it off you, including the one you are standing in front of, and there is a second thing I have to say, and once it is said I am finished talking for this morning."

"Say it."

"A lodged citizen is held. It means a file and a room of his own and an administration every quarter, and a medical return like anybody else's, and standing that is not his to spend while it stands." He looked up. "He keeps his name and his trade. He can be arrested in the ordinary way. He cannot spend."

"I understand."

"Do you."

"I do."

He put it in the out-tray. The whole movement took about eleven minutes including the queue, and there was no sound made about it, and nobody in the hall looked up.

## Noon, And A Book At The End Of A Corridor

At twelve o'clock he was in the corridor outside the room with an empty out-tray going out at four and nothing in his hands, and the clerk read to him from the keeping book because he was a member now, and he had been a member since about ten past nine that morning, and the first thing a member is shown is the book.

"How many in the room."

"Five."

"How many before this morning."

"Three since the twelfth of February. Four since the first of April." She turned the page back a quarter of an inch. "And before the twelfth of February, three for about four years."

"And this morning."

"Entered in the book at about ten past nine this morning, under the fifth line, out of a book in the keeping." She put her finger on it. "A consignment is entered in this room on the day it is lodged and it is not entered out on any day. That is all of it, and there is nothing for it on the page after this one."

## Five

He stood in a corridor of about forty feet and did the sum in about nine seconds, and it is not a complicated sum, and he had known it since the sixth of February.

A requisition carried needs five.

The room had stood at three through twenty-six weekly returns in a row. It had stood at three on the twentieth of February when that sheet was laid on that table. It had stood at three on the seventeenth of March when it was read for nine minutes and not decided. It had stood at three on the twenty-third of December, and it had stood at three on the morning of the thirtieth of March when the standing of three crossed a quarter boundary with everything written on it.

It had stood at four since the first of April, and the fourth was Bertram Sallow's, and the Council had put it back at eleven minutes to ten on the morning of the first.

And at about ten past nine on the morning of the fifteenth of April it stood at five, and the fifth was a man who had walked into a hall off a third court with one sheet of plain paper and given it to a boy with the same hands as the boy who paints cards on walls, and who had got nothing for it and could not get it back.

He had spent eight volumes building one thing. A requisition that could not be carried because a room could not reach five.

He had just supplied the fifth himself.

## What He Had Done And Why

The alternative had been a fifth chosen by somebody else, in a room he could not enter, against a holding he had declared in his own hand at his own table on the evening of the fourteenth of April.

That was the whole of the decision and he had made it in about four seconds at ten to nine that morning, standing at a rail, and there had not been any drama in it at all.

"He will want to know what it means for the requisition," said the clerk.

"A requisition carried needs five."

"That is not an answer, that is the rule."

"That is the whole of what I can put in a book about it, and a member does not get a longer answer than a stranger."

He took his hands out of his coat.

"The sheet of the twentieth of February has not been carried, has not been received and has not been decided, and as of about an hour and a half ago this morning it has been capable of being carried for the first time since the twenty-third of December."

"The reason is standing at the end of a corridor, and it is not anything to do with the sheet."

The clerk wrote one line in a book, which is what she does.

Then she wrote under it a word, because there was a space under the fifth line and there had never been anything in that space in nineteen years.

She wrote it without asking, without looking it up and without knowing what it was. Neither did he. Neither, at that hour, did anybody else in Ostara.

Under the fifth line of the keeping entry of the fifteenth of April, at about two minutes to one in the afternoon, in the hand of the clerk of that room, there is one word.

## The Address Of The Holding

He went down the iron stair at about twenty past one and the light on the second floor was on and the light on the third floor was on, and nobody came down either corridor while he was in the building, which is what the third floor has done since the fourth of February and what the second floor has never once done in nine years.

And there was a piece of paper going down the ordinary chain at four that afternoon, on different paper from the card, in a different hand, and attached to it was a line, and the line was to be witnessed on the day it was lodged, at the address of the holding.

The address of the holding was premises 34-6-118.

Which is a building with sixty-one steps in it and a kitchen at the top of them, and eight women in it, and on the evening of the eighteenth of April that kitchen was going to be the only place in Ostara where a line could be witnessed, and seven of the eight at that address were going to be asked to sign it in front of each other.
