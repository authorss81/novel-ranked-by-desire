# The Hand That Did Not Sign

The side room off the third court on the second floor had two hundred picture hooks on the wall and two hundred sheets on them, and every sheet was a thing that had come into Veil-Research and been copied and been put up, and none of them was hers.

She got in at about a quarter to seven in the morning on the nineteenth of April because she had not slept much and because the corridor had eight doors along it and there was no light behind any of them, which on an ordinary night is the ordinary condition of that corridor and on that night was not.

She put her bag down and did not take her coat off and stood in the middle of the room with her hands at her sides.

She was going to work out what she had been the officer of, and she had been not asking that question since about four o'clock the previous afternoon, and she had three hours.

## What A Gap Tells A Woman Who Sees Shapes

A woman who has spent three years seeing the same shape in different data does not read a document. She looks at where the parts are not.

Seven names on a line. A space two fingers wide in the middle of them.

That is the entire content of the sheet on the kitchen table at Ferrier Street and it is a complete description of a shape, and the shape has been sitting in front of her eyes since about six o'clock the previous evening while she said one sentence and went down a stair.

Nobody gets put in the middle of a witness line. A witness line is a list of the people who were in a room on a date, in the order they arrived or the order they were written down, and a space in the middle of one is not a place for a person.

A space in the middle of one is a hole where a seat should be, and a room that has a hole where a seat should be has a standing that is one short, and she had known about that hole since the twelfth of February.

So somebody at that address had been asked for a name on a line about a holding kept there, and had not given it, and had put the not-giving into the document itself in a place where a person could be looked for.

She stood in the middle of the room and found that she was not frightened, which she had expected to be, and that she was something she had not been able to name in about three years.

She had been the cheapest person in this city for five months.

## The Order It Has To Be Done In

She got her coat off at seven and sat down and did it in the order it has to be done in, because a fact said in the wrong order sounds like a story and she had watched a great many people make that mistake in this room.

One. A thirty-year account is carried forward on a quarter's sheet, and it has been called four times — the first of December, the first of January, the first of February, the first of March. There was never going to be a fifth mark, because a carried line and a call are two different animals and only people who have never had to write either one confuse them.

Two. A quarter's sheet has four ruled blocks and the fourth block is printed off a certificate of the composition of the premises.

Three. The certificate is of the thirtieth of November, at nine in the morning.

Four. The account cannot be closed, because the only thing that closes an account is payment, and payment was impossible on the morning of the sixteenth of November and there has been no movement since.

She stopped there for about a minute.

And five, said out loud in an empty room to nobody: the certificate is in my hand.

## Four Minutes And A Box

"It is in my hand," said Sun Meiling. "I wrote it on the thirtieth of November at nine in the morning because that is when a composition is certified in my division, and a composition is certified for the quarter it is certified in and in no quarter longer, and I certified it because there were seven persons keeping that address and a certificate says how many, and that is a thing I do four times a year."

She put both hands flat on the desk.

"So I certified the composition of the house I sleep in, in my own division, in my own hand, under my own name, at the head of it, on the thirtieth of November."

"Again on the thirtieth of December. Again on the thirtieth of January. Again on the thirtieth of February."

"And then somebody drafted a carry-forward sheet in the middle of March, and it went on the first of April, and everything after that is administered from the first."

"And on the morning of the thirtieth of March a small printed form came to my desk with a single box on it, and I filled the box in in about four minutes, and put it in the out-tray, and carried on with the rest of the morning."

She did not say the next part for about half a minute.

"And nobody had put it to me."

## It Is Not About Her

The room where this cannot reach her is the room she has been building for three years.

She cannot be sworn, so she is not on a roll, so no roll in this city has her name on it. She cannot be served a requisition. She declined an attendance in her own building at four minutes to twelve on the twenty-sixth of January and the refusal was entered in a book in that building, in her own hand, at the head of it.

None of it helps. Not one grain of it helps.

All of it is about whether a person can be found. The certificate is not about her. It is not about a woman on the second floor of a building with a docket folder against her back for eleven years. It is about the composition of a set of premises, and it is in her hand, and the woman whose hand it is is the officer of the holding that the premises are answerable for.

"An officer of a holding is a person the room can serve," she said.

She had said a version of that sentence in a kitchen the night before with seven signed names on a sheet in front of her, and she had said it to stop a man asking her to be reachable, and she had been entirely right.

And there was a second person at that address who had been reachable for five months and had not known it, and she was the second person.

## Four Lines She Has Never Read

Four lines carry her initials on the sheet above them. They sit in a minute kept at the far end of a corridor in a building she has never once walked into.

She has never read them and she is not going to. Since the twentieth of February she has carried the answer to those four lines up sixty-one steps to a man who did not ask her for it once, and she gave it standing up in a corridor with the door open, and she wrote nothing down.

There is a row of twenty-two stitches in a pocket of the coat she has been carrying since February, sewn there with something pencil-shaped and left. That is paper and not a body and it is hers, and she put her thumb against it through the cloth while she sat there, and that was all she did.

On the floor above hers, a man looks out at a yard and goes on doing what he has done for nine years.

He came down her corridor once, on the fourth of February, for a box. He stood in the corridor so that they were both standing, and he gave her an answer in four sentences, and he wrote nothing down, and she has never asked him anything since and he has never offered.

## Sixty-One Steps

She got up at about ten past eight and went and stood in front of the wall of two hundred sheets and looked at all of it, which takes about nine seconds, and then she put her hand out and took one of them off its hook.

It was a copy of a departmental paper about a transfer that carries no name in it anywhere, and she had initialled a draft of it against the fourth line, and the draft is filed under a number and not under a woman.

She turned it over.

"I am going to go down sixty-one steps and read a minute," she said to the room, "and it is going to be a minute and not a file, because a file is on a different level and I am not going up there and I am not going to be asked to."

She put the sheet back on its hook and squared it, which is a thing she has done two hundred and some times.

"I am not taking anybody."

She put her coat on and stopped at the door with her hand on it, and the corridor was still dark along all eight of its doors, and she did not look at any of them.
