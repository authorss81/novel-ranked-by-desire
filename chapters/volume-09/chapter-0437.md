# The Third Sub-Level

The doctor keeps a surgery on the ground floor of the building above the third sub-level, and on the morning of the seventh of June she was at the rail outside it with a bag in her hand, and he was there because she had told the clinic he might come and she had not told him to stay away.

"It is one hundred and eight days since the twentieth of February," he said. "Ten from twenty leaves ten, with thirty and thirty and thirty and seven makes one hundred and eight."

"Twenty-three days to the thirtieth," said the doctor. "Thirty taken from seven leaves twenty-three. I have walked this corridor every working day for three years and I count it the same way."

## Down The Stair

She did not take him to a room. She took him down the stair past the first sub-level and the second, where the counter-forensics division keeps its minute with his premises at the head of it, and stopped at the landing above the third.

"A held account is administered on the last working day of the last month of its quarter," she said. "The thirtieth of December. The thirtieth of March. The thirtieth of June. A quarter each time."

"I know."

"A lodged citizen is held," she said, "and what is held is administered like anything else held out of one account — one fixed day, one clinic for the return." She shifted the bag to her other hand. "On the thirtieth of June this building sends two returns about one matter to one clinic, and neither was asked for by anybody."

They went down the last flight. The corridor at the bottom had no window and three doors on each side, none of them locked from inside, and a man came out of the far door with a cup in his hand and nodded to the doctor and went back in.

## The Corridor

"This is the corridor," said the doctor. "You are not being put inside one. I do not lock it and I do not unlock it. I walk a man down to his own door and I walk him back up again."

"Whose door."

"Yours is the second on the left." She did not point. "It has been yours since the fifteenth of April at about ten past nine. You have never seen it and you are seeing the corridor now and not the room, because a man who is held is administered and not imprisoned, and there is a difference and this building keeps it."

He stood in the corridor with the cold coming up through the floor and looked at the second door on the left. It was painted the same shade as the others and had no name on it and no number he could read.

"It is one hundred and eight days since the sheet was laid," he said, "and the administration is twenty-three days away."

"It is the way a held account is administered," said the doctor. "On a fixed day. With a medical return to the same clinic. The tenth line of the last one about the man two doors along is in a different hand and says the man is held, three years, a quarter shorter than the last, and that I do not know what that means."

"You promised to say nothing else."

"I promised to say nothing else and I have kept it for five months." She started back up the stair and he followed, because there was nothing in the corridor to stay for. "On the thirtieth of June I will be in and out of the clinic twice. The second time I will be about four floors away on a corridor I have walked every working day for three years."

## What She Is Angry At

At the top of the stair the doctor put the bag down and faced him.

"I am angry," she said, plainly, as though she were giving a dose. "Not at you. At the shape of it. Two returns in one quarter about one matter and nowhere on either return to write that they are two. Tessa Okonkwo is going to write it on the outside of the file in her own hand before either is read, and it will cost her, and you do not get to hear the figure."

"I know she will."

"Then go and be held somewhere else until the thirtieth." The doctor picked up the bag. "You have seen the corridor. You have not been inside the door. That is the whole of what this morning was for, and I am not writing it down, and you may put it in whatever file you have got and check the date on it."

## The Man Two Doors Along

At the top of the stair she stopped and set the bag down between her feet, as though she had decided something on the way up.

"You did not ask about the man two doors along," she said. "Every person I bring down here asks, sooner or later. You did not."

"He has been held for three years," he said. "A quarter shorter than the last. The tenth line of the December return is in a different hand and says you do not know what that means. It is the most honest sentence in this building. I am not going to spend it by asking."

The doctor picked up the bag again.

"Good." She started toward the surgery door and stopped. "On the thirtieth of March his return was administered the way yours will be. A doctor, a room, a fixed day. Nobody sent for his either."

"And on the thirtieth of June there are two."

"On the thirtieth of June there are two." She looked at him directly for the first time that morning. "Tessa Okonkwo receives them. She has ruled the date since the morning of the twenty-fifth of May and she has been looking at it for thirteen days. She will read both and hold what is in them, and that is the whole of her evening, and she has told nobody that either except you."

He nodded. His hands were cold and he put them in his pockets, and he was glad of the anger in her voice, because it was the first true feeling anybody in a white coat had shown him since March, and it meant the fixed day was a day with people in it and not only a date painted on a board.
