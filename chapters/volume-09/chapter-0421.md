# The Week It Could Be Carried

She brought the sheet on the evening of the seventh of May and she had ruled it herself and she had not written anything on it, which on the fourth evening of May had been her way of saying she was listening harder than anybody at the table and on the seventh of May was her way of saying she was about to make everybody do arithmetic.

"Nobody stand up," said Runa Sato. "I have thought about it and I am asking you in advance, because the moment anybody stands up in this kitchen this evening we will do something stupid and we will all be kind about it and it will cost one of us something she did not have to spend."

"That is a strong thing to say in a room with eight people in it."

"It is an arithmetic remark and there are eight people in it." She put the sheet flat. "Twenty-three days of May. Thirty days of June. Fifty-three."

## Seventy-Seven

"Seventy-seven days," she said. "It was laid on the twentieth of February. There are ten days left in February after the twentieth, and thirty in March, and thirty in April, and seven of May, and that is seventy-seven, and it is two months and seventeen days and it is not three months."

"Who has been calling it three months."

"Nobody at this table and four people I work with." She tapped the sheet. "It was read for nine minutes on the seventeenth of March and not decided. It went across the quarter boundary on the thirtieth of March carrying everything that had been written on it. Since the first of April it has been administered weekly against the composition of that premises, and the composition is seven."

"It needs five."

"It needs five." She looked round the table. "The room stood at three through twenty-six weekly returns in a row, and it stood at three on the day in February that sheet was laid on that table."

"It went to four at eleven minutes to ten on the first of April, and to five at about ten past nine on the fifteenth, and the fifth is him, and he did that himself at a public post off a third court in a queue with a pen in about eleven minutes."

"Ten past nine on the morning of the fifteenth of April," she said, "was the hour it became capable of being carried. The rest of the fifteenth is fifteen days. April is thirty. And then the first of May, the second, the third, the fourth, the fifth, the sixth, and yesterday."

## Twenty-Two Days And Fifty-Three

"Twenty-two."

"Twenty-two days on which it could have been carried and was not carried." She put the pen down. "And twenty-two days is not the number that matters. The number that matters is that there are fifty-three days left in the quarter and the number of days it has been capable for is already at a bit under half of them, and in about eleven days it will be half, and there is not one thing anybody at this table has done on any of the twenty-two."

Nobody said anything.

"Not one act. Not a letter, not a query, not a form, not a visit to the corridor at the end of the second landing except his." She said it without any edge at all.

"And I want it said in a kitchen by a woman who prices things that this is not the same as anybody not wanting it carried. There is a difference between a room of five that will not carry a sheet and eight people at a kitchen table who cannot make a room of five do anything, and the second one is legal in every direction."

"I asked you on the fourth evening not to get up and I am now asking whether anybody wants it carried."

## The Question She Is Not Allowed To Ask

Nobody answered for about eleven seconds.

"Right," said Runa Sato. "Now I am going to explain why that was a waste of eleven seconds and I am going to do it once. Eight women in a kitchen are not a room of five."

"A room of five is a book in a keeping and a bench at the end of forty feet of corridor and a chair and four lines with a box at the bottom of the sheet."

"I cannot ask that room a question and neither can any of you and neither can he, and that is not a rule anybody made, it is a rule that is the shape of the thing, and I have looked for an instrument for asking it for about eight years and there is not one."

"Then what is there," said Agnete Lindqvist.

"There is the sheet going down an ordinary chain to an ordinary address, once, on a day, and a room that is competent to act on it deciding to act." She squared the sheet. "And I have not got one of those and I have looked for one in about four minutes of thinking and the honest answer is that I have not found one and I am a woman who finds them."

"Sit down."

"I have not stood up," said Runa Sato, and everybody looked, and she had not, and that was the point she had made with the first sentence of the evening and had made it twice.

"You are not certifying on the twenty-eighth," said Wren Kallo.

"No."

## What The Fifth Was Entered On

"And the fifth is in the book."

"The fifth is in the book and the book does not care." She put the sheet away. "I want that said before anybody starts arranging the two of them."

"I said in a loading bay on the twenty-seventh that I was going to go and put my name at the head of a composition of this address and I have not gone and I am not going, and the reason is a woman at this table told me on the first of May what the figure on that certificate is, and I am not signing it."

"And none of that has anything to do with the book at the end of a corridor. The fifth went into that book on the fifteenth of April at about ten past nine in the morning, and a consignment is entered in that room on the day it is lodged and it is not entered out on any day, and my name was never going to be the reason it went in there."

"So the composition of this address has not been certified at this address."

"It has not, and it is not going to be, and I have told the Field office I am not attending and I gave them no reason." She looked at Nadia Ferrous for about a second and a half and then did not look at her again. "That is the arrangement and it is mine and it is not a favour to anybody in this room and I will not be thanked for it tonight or in four years."

She got the sheet out again at about ten o'clock, which nobody had expected.

"Now the last one and it is the reason I came tonight and everything else has been about four minutes' work to get round to it."

"The Council does not sit again until the thirtieth of June."

## Fifty-Three Days In Which Nothing Can Be Changed

She said it and put her hand flat on the paper.

"I checked it. It is not in a thing anybody would publish. It is entered on a calendar at the beginning of the quarter for the quarter, and a Council sitting is entered four months ahead of it, and the entry for this quarter is the thirtieth of June, and that is the last working day of the quarter, and that is fifty-three days from tonight."

The kitchen did not stop and nobody spoke for about half a minute.

"Which means," said Runa Sato, "that for fifty-three days there is nobody in this city who can restore a standing, or take one away, or lift a recusal, or send for the four people who have not sat since December. There is a room of five at the end of a corridor and a sheet that five can carry, and the number of seats in that room cannot be changed by anybody who is not one of the five, and every one of the five has a reason."

"They have all got a reason," said Isolde Marchetti.

"They have all got a reason and every one of those reasons is good and I have listened to about nine of them in the last twenty minutes." Runa Sato stood up at last, and it was at the end, and it was to go and find her coat. "So there it is. Seventy-seven days old. Capable for twenty-two of them. Fifty-three days left in the quarter. Nobody in this city can change the number of seats in that room until the thirtieth of June."

She got her coat on at the table.

"Nobody stands up," she said. "And nobody is going to do anything about it tonight, and I have not come here to be answered. I have come here because in about nine weeks somebody is going to go through a list and find that a room stood at five for fifty-three days with a live sheet in its keeping, and the list will be written by a stranger, and he will not be cruel and he will simply be working."

She stopped at the door with her hand on it.

"And when he reads that line, the five names under it are going to be four people who had a reason and one man who is the man the sheet is about. And that is going to read as a decision. And there is not one thing any of you can put in a book tonight to say that it was fifty-three days of everybody being right."
