# The Standing Order She Will Keep

The loading bay off Cordwain has a bench in it under an awning that stops the rain and does not stop the noise, and at about twenty past five on the evening of the twenty-seventh of April Runa Sato had a sheet of paper she had ruled herself and about four minutes.

"Right," she said. "You have told me a number on a corner. I am going to price something, and it is going to be me, and you are going to hold the paper."

He held the paper.

"You are not writing it."

"I am not writing it. There is a woman in this city who could be asked for it and she is not you."

## Three Things And A Figure

"First." She put one finger on the ruled sheet, which had nothing on it. "I am going to certify the composition of premises 34-6-118. In person. At the address. In my own hand, with my name at the head of it and nothing above that name, because that is what a person who goes in to certify a composition is, and because a consignment is witnessed at the address of the holding and the holding is kept there."

"Next."

"Field division then has me on paper against a man. It has had me on paper against a man since October without a figure on it, and I have survived eight years of that on the strength of the reason box being blank, and from about the middle of May it has a figure on it, and the figure is not a grain of anything, it is the standing of a room that stands at five."

"And that is the price."

"That is one third of the price and it is the cheapest third." She put a second finger down. "Second. There is a standing order on that premises. Field division. Applied for on the twenty-seventh of October at about four in the afternoon, in my own name, by me, on the same day you had a description of your own house made against the head of it in a kitchen in front of eight women and a clerk."

"You have told me that."

"I have told you that twice and you have never once written it down, which is the only reason we are still talking." She squared the sheet. "So the woman who put a Field man outside that house for the life of the account is going to put her own name at the head of the document that says who is inside it. Two acts in two capacities, six months apart, same premises, same woman, and neither one undoes the other and neither one is the other's price."

## Seven

"Now the third and it is the one you did not know about."

She turned the ruled sheet round and pushed it back to him.

"Seven."

"What."

"The composition of premises 34-6-118 is seven." She said it flatly.

"It has stood at seven since the thirteenth of March, at about nine forty-one, when a woman went to a municipal counter on the ground floor of the Vestry and handed back an address she had been keeping for six years. Four years before anybody can touch it again."

"She did it on her own account and she was not the head of the household, so the head was unaffected and the count went down by one, and the address at the head of every paper in this city went from eight to seven at nine forty-one in the morning."

"She told me it cost me the floor of my band ninety days later."

"It cost her the only thing she had and it cost you a floor." Runa Sato did not raise her voice. "And what I am going to sign on the twenty-eighth of this month, if the clerk at that counter is in, is seven."

"There are eight people under that roof tonight," she said. "Seven of the eight women sleep there and you sleep there, and Sun Meiling is up the same stair at a door that is not that address, and eight is what a person would count if you counted heads."

## Seven Is A Bill And Not A Count

"And the document says seven."

"The document says seven and the priced line of the sixteenth of September says seven with your initial on it and not your signature, and the two of them agree with each other, and a composition that agrees with its own priced line can be counted." She put her hand flat on the bench.

"And that is the whole reason Nadia Ferrous's eight-year bill is a bill and not a piece of luck. It took one woman one counter one morning and it did what she wanted it to do. Her name went off every paper with that address at the head of it."

"And then she signed a line."

"And then she signed a line on the eighteenth of April with her name in it, on a consignment of a holding kept at that address, after being told the price out loud." Runa Sato's jaw moved once.

"I am not saying she should not have. She should sign whatever she wants and she has told three years of kitchen that she is not to be managed. I am saying that I am the one who is going to sign the seven, and I am going to sign it in my own hand, and I am going to sign a figure that is not a count."

"What is the price of signing a figure that is not a count."

## The Price, Given And Not Given

"I do not know," said Runa Sato, "and that is the honest answer and I have given you three of them this evening and the fourth one is free."

"Then what did you come out here to do."

"I came out here to write it down and I am not going to write it down." She took the ruled sheet off him and folded it once and put it in her coat.

"There is the price. It is on a piece of paper for about four minutes and it is going in my coat and it is going home and it is going in a drawer, and it is not going anywhere, because a thing that is priced and not invoiced has not happened to anybody."

"Every office that could receive it would have to take my name as well, and my name is on the sheet I am going to sign, and I am not putting my name twice in the same quarter for the same address."

"You could have said the price and kept it."

"I did say the price. You are holding the paper I ruled it on and you cannot read a word on it, which is exactly as designed."

"There is a thing you have never done with me and I want to say it once and then never again," she said.

"What."

## The Two Dates

"You have never once put the order next to the note."

"There is no order on me. The order is yours."

"The order is mine and the note is yours and they are six days apart and you have never once, in eight months, put them in the same breath, and you have been extremely careful about it. I noticed in about October and I have not raised it, because raising it would have been asking." She turned round on the bench.

"The twenty-seventh is mine. I filled in a form at about four in the afternoon with my own name on it and I left the reason box empty on purpose and it stays empty while I am alive. The thirtieth is a note in your own handwriting that has never been filed anywhere in this city, and no living soul has ever seen it but the two of us, and that will keep being true until one of us is not."

"They are not the same thing."

"They are not the same thing and that is the entire difference between a standing order and a promise and it is the only thing I have got that you cannot spend." She looked at him. "And you are not going to be thanked. Not tonight and not in four years and not at the end of it. If you thank me for that order I will walk off this bench and go and be somewhere else for a while."

"I was not going to thank you for it."

"You were thinking about it. I could see you thinking about it and you are going to do it about four months from now in a kitchen with eight people in it and I am telling you now what my face will be doing."

He got up off the bench about two minutes before she expected him to, which she noted.

"Can anybody else sign that certificate."

## Who Else Could Do It

"There are two other women in this city who could put a name at the head of a composition of those premises and I am not going to say either of their names in this bay at half past five on the evening of the twenty-seventh of April." She got her coat closed.

"One of them signed the certificate of the thirtieth of November in her own division in her own hand four times a year for a quarter at a time and does not know that anybody in this city has worked out what that makes her, and she is not on a roll and cannot be put on one, and she works two floors under a man who has sat at the same post for nine years and has not been in her corridor since the fourth of February."

"Sun Meiling."

"And I am not going to say it again." She turned up her collar. "And you are not going to ask her, because you have already decided that in a kitchen and you have never taken it back, and I have read the four minutes of that from the other side of a table."

"And the other one."

"There is no other one. That is what I have been telling you for four minutes and you have been waiting for me to get to it." Runa Sato got about four paces away and stopped. "It is me or it is nobody, and it is me, and the reason it is me is that it has to be a woman with a post and she is the only woman with a post who is not already an officer of that holding."

She went off up the yard.

He stood in the loading bay with a bench under an awning and a figure of seven in his head that was not going to be correct on the twenty-eighth of this month, and a woman in a coat who had just told him that the reason she had put a Field man outside his house for six months was that the box in the form had been left empty on purpose by a person who did not want to write down why.

The roll at the Ninth Terrace was entered against the household's name at 8,131 on the second of April and cannot be put back down, and the certificate he was going to be behind on the twenty-eighth was going to say seven, and both of those figures were on paper, and neither of them was going to be corrected by anybody, ever, and one of them had been put there by a woman at a counter in nine minutes because she wanted to be off a list.
