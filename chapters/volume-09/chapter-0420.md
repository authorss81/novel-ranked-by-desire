# The Clerk Who Reads A Gap Aloud

The witness line came up the chain on the fifth of May and was in the keeping book before noon on the sixth, and nothing happened to it for about four hours, and then at about four in the afternoon the clerk of the room at the end of a corridor off the second landing turned up the page and read the whole of it into a minute, because that is what she does with an entry that carries names.

He was in the corridor. He was allowed to be in the corridor.

"The minute has to have it read into it," she said, "or the book has got a thing in it that nobody saw come in, and I have been doing this for eleven years and I have never once had that."

"How many times."

"Twice, always. Once for the book and once for the minute." She had the entry open. "It takes about ninety seconds and nobody has ever wanted to hear it and I am not asking you to."

She read it.

"Consignment, lodged at a public post off a third court on the morning of the fifteenth of April. Witnessed on the day it is lodged at the address of the holding, which is premises 34-6-118."

She went down the line.

"Agnete Lindqvist. Runa Sato. Nadia Ferrous. Wren Kallo. Isolde Marchetti. Beatrix Halm. Petra Ilyin."

Then she stopped.

## A Space Is Not A Hole

"There is a space," she said.

She did not say the word *vacant*. He noticed that and she noticed him noticing it.

"A space is not a hole," said the clerk. "A hole is where paper is missing. That is a space where a person would stand and does not. There is a difference and it is the whole of it as far as I am concerned, and I have been the clerk of this room for eleven years and I have signed my name under six figures in that time and not one of them was mine."

"Who was in the room."

"That is the question you may not put." She said it without heat. "You asked me on the second afternoon what a member may ask this room and I told you the answer and the answer has not changed since. A member may be shown the book."

"A member may not ask that room to do anything, and a member may not put a question about a name or a space to the clerk of this room."

"And if you want to know why, it is because the room has never once been asked anything by one of the nine who sit in it, and it is not going to begin on the sixth of May because you have been a member three weeks."

"I was not going to ask her."

"I know you were not going to ask her. Everybody who comes up this corridor with a space in it in front of them says they are not going to ask, and I have never once known one of them to mean it as anything other than a way of saying that they have already worked it out."

She put her finger on the paper.

"And here is what nobody in this building understands about it, and you can take it or leave it, and I have said it to exactly four people in eleven years and two of them were clerks. A space is a decision. I know that."

"Everybody in this house knows that and it got said at a table on the eighteenth of April and it is true and it does not help me one single bit at four o'clock on the afternoon of the sixth, because I have got to write down what is in front of me and I cannot write down that a person decided, because a clerk does not write down what a person decided. A clerk writes down what the paper says."

"Is there a box under it."

"There is a box under it and it is the same box as under the fifth line." She turned the sheet a quarter of an inch.

## The Rule About The Space

"This sheet came down the chain with a line on it and a space in the middle of it. There is a line under the line and the space under the space, and the space under the space has been on sheets like this for nineteen years and has never had anything in it, and the one under the fifth line had never had anything in it either until about two minutes to one on the afternoon of the fifteenth of April, when I put something in it."

"You told me you did not know what it was."

"I have looked for it in about two hundred and sixty entries in the index at the back of that book and I have had it in my coat pocket for about four seconds a day since the fifteenth. It is neither of the two things that book has names for, and the index has never heard of it." She squared the sheet.

"I am going to write it again. I know how that sounds and I have asked myself about it for three weeks and the answer is that the alternative is a box with nothing in it on a document about a room that stands at five, and I would have to initial that as well."

She read it the second time for the minute, exactly as she had the first, and it is a thing that has happened in that corridor perhaps four hundred times and it was the first time in this city that a gap in a line had been read out loud by anybody.

"Agnete Lindqvist. Runa Sato. Nadia Ferrous. Wren Kallo. Isolde Marchetti. Beatrix Halm. Petra Ilyin."

## She Reads It The Second Time

Then the space.

And then she said the word.

She said it exactly as she had said it to him a week earlier in that corridor when he had asked her what it said, in the same voice, at the same speed, and it was the same word.

He did not ask what it meant.

He had asked her once, on the twenty-first, and she had told him, and he had said *I do not know*, and she had said that that was the first honest thing anybody had said to her about that word since she wrote it, and neither of them had learned anything since.

She wrote it under the space.

"There," she said.

"That is the same word."

## Two Of Them, In Two Rooms, In The Same Hand

"Yes."

"In the same hand."

"Yes, and it is going to be in the same hand for the rest of my working life, and it is in two books about eleven hundred feet apart in this city, one in a corridor off a second landing and one on a ground floor four floors up that has a number on its door and nothing on a plate." She put the pen down and did not cap it.

"And the first one has been there since the fifteenth of April and the second one has been there since about four o'clock this afternoon, and neither of them can be read by anybody, including the two of us."

"You do not know what it means."

"I know what a clerk writes into a space that has been left empty for nineteen years by somebody who was not given an instruction. That is the whole of what I know, and it turned out to be enough to do it twice." She looked at the line.

"There is no index for it because it is not a figure and it is not a rank. There is nothing for it at the back of this book. If you asked me in four years what the word means I am going to have to tell you that I do not know, and I am going to have been wrong to be certain of it twice."

He stood in a corridor of about forty feet for a while after that.

There were four things he could have done and he did none of them and it took him about nine seconds to work out why, and the reason was the same in all four cases.

## What Is Not Going To Happen

He could not ask her. A clerk does not answer questions about a word, and she has told him so in as many words.

He could not ask the room. A room has never been asked for anything by one of the nine people who sit in it.

He could not ask Sun Meiling. He gave her his word at the top of that stair in December that he would put it to her once and never twice, and he has kept it, and he is not going to break it in forty feet of corridor merely because he has started to want to.

And he could not ask Runa Sato, and the reason he could not ask Runa Sato was that a woman who prices things would price it, and the price of that word was the whole of what he had spent the last two days putting into two people's heads, and he was not going to hand a fourth head a thing that had already cost him the only two he could find.

A requisition laid on the twentieth of February was seventy-six days old at about a quarter to five on the evening of the sixth of May, and a room of five can carry it and has not carried it, and it is administered weekly against the composition of premises 34-6-118 and the composition is seven, and the seventh is not a fact anybody in this corridor has any business checking.

The clerk signed the minute at about five minutes to five and squared the book and put it back into the keeping, and the page went in at the back where a page goes when it is a page about a person, and not at the front where a standing goes.

## Seventy-Six Days And A Word In Two Books

"Nobody will read that word for four years," he said.

"Or nine." She was shutting the book. "And when one of them does, there will be another one under the space, because that is what I am for, and nobody in this city has ever yet had to correct a clerk."

"Has anybody ever asked you about it."

"Nobody has ever asked me about anything in eleven years that I did not put there myself." She locked the keeping. "That is not a complaint either. It is the arrangement, and it is a good one, and it is the reason this room has a book that anybody in Ostara would trust."
