# The Standing To Answer For Himself

The clerk of the room at the end of the corridor had a slip on the second table under the window on the morning of the thirteenth of June, and it was not the one from her drawer, and she turned it face down when he came in.

"It is one hundred and thirteen days since the twentieth of February," he said. "Ten from twenty leaves ten, with thirty and thirty and thirty and thirteen makes one hundred and thirteen."

"Seventeen days to the thirtieth," said the clerk. "Thirty taken from thirteen leaves seventeen. You have seventeen days to decide what you are going to do with what I am going to tell you."

## Called

"The requisition is called," said the clerk. "Not carried. Not received. Not decided. Called. That is a different animal and you know the difference, because a service and a minute are different animals and an entry and a carrying are different ones again."

"When."

"On the thirtieth. At the sitting. The list for the sitting is made in this building out of this room, and the sheet of the twentieth of February will be on it, because a sheet in the keeping of a room with five standing in it cannot be left off a list that carries standings."

He sat down on the bench because his knees told him to.

"Answering it means standing in that room as a member," said the clerk. "With a seal you cannot refuse to use. The fifth went into the book on the fifteenth of April under the fifth line. A requisition carried needs five. A requisition answered needs the man it is about to stand up in the room that holds it."

"I cannot ask that room for anything."

"You cannot." She did not soften it. "On the ninth of May at about ten past five you worked out that you can make it need something, and that every time you do a woman pays out of her own future, and you decided never to use it. On the thirteenth of May a woman guessed it and you neither confirmed nor denied it. Nothing in this tells you to use it."

## What Answering Is Not

"Answering is not carrying it," he said. "And it is not deciding it."

"Answering is standing up when it is called and saying whose holding it is and whose ground it is." The clerk looked out of the window. "The holding is yours. The ground is your own description of your own premises, made over it on the morning of the twenty-seventh of October at about eleven against the head, by a clerk called Imrie, on four words said by you in a kitchen."

"Because it is cheaper."

"Do not say the four words in this corridor." Her voice was sharp for the first time in nine weeks. "Mrs Imrie may not be asked anything and she is not in your ten days. The description stands. A man who has lodged his whole standing cannot be administered against a composition he is not keeping. That is the only answer that can be given, and it is yours to give, and it is not a carrying and not a decision against you."

He put his hands on his knees to stop them moving.

"If I stand, the minute of the carrying will carry five seals, one of them mine. It names the premises four times. It goes out at four to seven desks. Six of them have a woman from my address at them."

"That is what the fifteenth of May said." The clerk turned the slip face up. It was blank. "I have nothing to give you. That is the point. The room asks for nothing. You bring the standing. The standing is the consignment you lodged at ten minutes to nine on the fifteenth of April with a pen, in a queue, with no witness, and which is entered in the book on the day it was lodged and is not entered out on any day."

## Seventeen Days

He stood up. The corridor was forty feet long and the bench had never been offered to him and he had sat on it anyway for two months.

"On the thirtieth the clerk writes up her book when the room sits," he said.

"On the thirtieth I write up my book when the room sits," said the clerk. "The sitting is entered a quarter ahead. The entry for this quarter is painted on a board at the end of the corridor under nine lines of daily runs. The calendar is the only door and it opens on a list."

"I swore never to make it need something."

"Then do not make it need something." She picked up the blank slip and put it in the drawer with the other one. "Stand up when it is called. That is not making. That is answering. There is a difference and you know the difference the way you know a service from a minute."

## The Seal He Cannot Refuse

"The fifth carries a seal," said the clerk. "You have had it since the fifteenth of April at about ten past nine. You have never used it. On the thirtieth, when the sheet is called, you will stand in that room as a member and the seal will be used because the minute of the carrying carries five, one of them yours, and a member does not refuse his own seal when the room sits."

"I have refused everything else."

"You refused Alma Quayle on the thirteenth of May." The clerk nodded. "A sentence out loud in front of me. The only thing you can give anybody. You were right. Lifting a recusal in the last weeks of a quarter in which no Council sits would have been using the instrument you swore never to use. This is different. This is not making. This is standing."

She put the blank slip away.

"Someone going through the keeping in four years will read that minute and find your seal on it, and he will ask what you answered, and the answer will be on the face of it. The holding is yours. The ground is your own description of your own premises. A lodged man cannot be administered against a composition he is not keeping."

He went out into the court with seventeen days left in the quarter and a called sheet waiting for him in a room that stands at five, and the fear he felt was clean, because for the first time since the fifteenth of April somebody had told him what the fifth is for.
