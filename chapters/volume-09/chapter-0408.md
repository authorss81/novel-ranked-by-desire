# The Thing He Asks For At Home

He brought nothing.

That was the first thing the table noticed, because in five months at that address he had never come in with an empty pair of hands, and on the evening of the fourteenth of April he came in at about a quarter past eight with nothing on a plate and nothing in either pocket and stood at the end of the table with his coat still on.

Eight women looked at him.

"Sit down," said Agnete Lindqvist, from the doorway, where she had been standing for about four minutes.

He sat down.

"Agnete, I want to say something and it is going to take about nine minutes and I am going to do it in the wrong order on purpose, and if anybody wants to stop me they should do it now."

## What It Costs, Said First

"On the morning of the first of April the Council sat, because the Council had not sat since the twenty-ninth of December, and it restored one standing, and the room where nine people sit went from three to four." He put his hands flat on the table. "On the morning of the fifteenth of April, at a public post off a third court, at about ten minutes to nine, in my own hand, I am going to lodge my whole standing into a consignment."

Nobody moved. There is a clock over the door in that kitchen and it is the loudest object in Ostara at nine o'clock at night.

"A consignment is one movement. It cannot be partial, it cannot be topped up, it cannot be drawn against, and it cannot be given back. There is no paper in this city that puts it back into a person and there never has been one. That is the price and I am telling you the price first."

"And the second half of the price," said Runa Sato, not looking up.

"The second half is that a consignment is witnessed on the day it is lodged, at the address of the holding. And the address of the holding is the address you are all sitting in."

He let that sit for about four seconds and then he said the part he had come to say.

"The witness line is a document. Somebody else can produce it, in about four years, in a division you will never be told about, to a stranger who is not cruel and is not anybody in this story. It will carry every woman's name on it, under mine, and it cannot be struck. Not by you. Not by me. Not by anybody."

"How long," said Beatrix Halm.

"Four years."

Beatrix Halm wrote nothing down, which is what she does when the number is one she intends to hold in her head.

## The Ask

"And I am asking you for it," said Lin Hao. "Tonight, in this room, in front of all eight of you, and I have said the price first because I have learned what happens to people who find out afterwards from a form."

He did not soften it and he did not apologise for it and he did not look at one person more than another.

"I cannot get to nine on merit. There is nothing in me that scores and there has not been for five volumes. And I cannot get there on what I have, because none of it is scoreable. The only thing in this business that can be given away is a figure other people have already put under me, and every one of those names is now a document somebody can produce. That is the whole trade. There is no other door and there never was one."

He stopped.

That was the ask. It had taken him about a hundred and forty seconds and he had not been interrupted once.

## Eleven Minutes

Nobody helped.

That was not a refusal. It was the thing they had done on the twenty-sixth of November and again on the tenth of January, and it was done the same way both times, which was by nobody saying one word for about eleven minutes while he sat at the end of his own table.

A hand that helped him would have been a hand that had to be on it. That was the arithmetic and it had been true since the first of December and it did not get softer because he had finally said the number out loud in a room.

Two of the eight cried. The kitchen did not find out which two and was not going to find out, and that has been the rule at that table since the sixth of December, and there is no reason it should change because a man has finally asked.

## The True Thing, In The Least Noble Way Available

Isolde Marchetti spoke at about twenty to nine and she said the true thing in the least noble way available, and she was right, and she had thought about how to say it for about two days.

"Right," she said. "Can I say the unkind version."

"Say it."

"The unkind version is that you have bought this line once already, on purpose, in January, and you paid for it with eight women standing round a table for a minute each, and every one of them knew what they were signing for because you told them."

"You did it in a kitchen and you felt good. And every name that came off that sheet in February has since been wanted by somebody."

"That is not a moral. That is a fact with a rate on it."

"I know the rate. Runa priced it at four on the eighth."

"Then you know the other half of it as well, and nobody in this room is going to say it for you, so I will."

She put her hands flat on the table, which is a thing she does before she makes a room agree with something.

"You are not asking us because we are eight women in a house. You are asking because a line is a list of the people who were in a room on a date, and you need eight of them, and you need them because there is no other instrument."

"Yes."

"Then do not ask us as if it were a favour," said Isolde Marchetti. "Ask us as an instrument. We are better at that."

Nobody answered that either.

## Who Is Going To Read The Line

He waited, because he had come in with the idea that there would be something by about nine o'clock and he had been wrong about that twice now and he was prepared to be wrong a third time.

Sun Meiling had been at the end of the table with a cup she had not drunk from, and she had not said one word in ninety minutes, and she put it down now.

"One question," she said. "And you do not have to answer it tonight, and I am not going to let anybody else answer it either."

"Say it."

"Who is going to read the line."

The kitchen was very quiet.

"That is the only thing in this I care about and I have been sitting here for ninety minutes waiting for somebody else to say it."

She did not raise her voice and she did not need to.

"Not the four years. Not that it cannot be struck. Every woman at this table has had her name on a document that cannot be struck and we have all got through it."

"The question is who reads it, because a line is only a list until somebody uses it as a list, and then it is a thing that can be served on seven women at this address by an office that holds no register of addresses, so that all it can ever reach is the building and not the woman in it."

He did not have it.

That was the true answer and it took him about four seconds to establish, because he went through it in the room: the Field, counter-forensics, the Vestry, the guild, the Church, and a wing off a third court that has never once been on a street he has walked in four years, and that has had a shape to it since the first of January.

"Then you have asked us for a thing you cannot describe," said Sun Meiling.

"Yes."

"And you were going to do it anyway."

"I was going to do it anyway," said Lin Hao, "and you are the one person at this table who cannot be on it and cannot be protected by the roll you are not on, and I did not ask you to be in this room, and that has been in my head since the second of April and I have not got a better answer to it than the one I am giving you now."
