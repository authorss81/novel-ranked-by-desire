# Four In The Afternoon, To Seven Desks

He asked the question on the morning of the fourth of May, in the corridor, at about eleven, and it was the only question he had ever been entitled to ask that room and she had told him so herself.

"Where does the sheet go."

She had the book out because it was open to a week she had finished at about eleven the night before.

"Seven places," she said. "And I will give you all seven because you are a member and a member is entitled to know where this room's paper goes, and that is the only question in this city you are allowed to put to me."

"Go on."

"Field. Counter-forensics, on the second sub-level, which is not on a street you can look up. The Vestry. The guild. The Church of the Quiet Number, which keeps neither ranks nor a register, has never once asked anybody for a return, and takes about four hundred a quarter all the same." She turned the page back a quarter of an inch. "Veil-Research, on the second floor of a building off a court that has no name on it anywhere a stranger could look one up."

"And the seventh."

"The seventh is a wing off a third court and it is not a division and it is not in the Wing, and it has been on this stream since before I came here." She put her finger on the page. "The stream to the research division runs through it. That is the whole reason it is on the list. Nothing is ever addressed to it. Everything addressed to the sixth has been through it for twenty years."

"You have never named it."

"I have never been asked for it by anybody who could get it, and it is not on a plate and it is not in the index, and there is a number on a door and the number is on no register of the Wing that anybody has ever shown me."

## The Quarter To Four

He got to the third court at about twenty past three in the afternoon.

The wing is the ground floor of a building that is four storeys and mostly other people's, and the ground floor is a corridor with six doors and a glazed one at the end, and the glazed one is a counter with nothing on the wall behind it, and behind the counter was a woman who had been at that desk for about nine years.

It was a quarter to four and the corridor was empty and the light through the glazed partition came in at an angle off the yard.

He did not ask her anything. He stood at about four feet back from the counter, which is where a person stands who is not being served, and he waited.

She did not look up for about two minutes.

"You have not got a thing with you," she said.

"No."

"Then there is no reason for you to be standing there and I am not going to think of one, and that is not rudeness, it is a rule and it has been one since before you came up the stair." She put a pen down.

"The run comes at four. If you want to see it you can stand where you are standing, because in about forty minutes I am going to have to deal with whatever is on it and you are not going to be in my way any more than a wall is."

## What Came Up The Stair At Four

It came up at about two minutes to four on the ordinary stream, in a canvas bag, and there was one delivery in it.

He could not see the bag's contents from where he was standing. He could see the strap, and he could see that the strap was not the strap a return comes on, because a return comes in a flat wire basket with a clip and this was a canvas bag with a string and it had come up a stair.

The woman untied it and took out two things.

The first was a weekly return, flat, with nine ruled lines on it and a tenth with a box, and she put that on the left of the counter without looking at it, because a thing that arrives in the same bag every week is a thing you do not read.

The second was one sheet of plain paper with a line ruled across it, and there were seven names on it and a space in the middle of the space, and there was a heading at the top of it that carried an address.

She read it.

He watched her read it, which took her about four seconds, and then she turned it over, and there was nothing on the back of it, and then she turned it back and looked at the top of it for about two seconds more.

"That is a witnessed line," she said.

"Yes."

"That came off a house."

"Premises 34-6-118, on Ferrier Street. On the afternoon of the twentieth of April at four o'clock."

She put it down on the counter face down.

## Twenty Years Of Nothing With An Address On It

"You have got about nine thousand of those to put on a shelf."

"You have never had one."

"No." She said it the way she said everything, which was flatly and without any pleasure in it. "I have been on this desk nine years and before me there was a man on this desk for eleven and it has been a wing for about twenty and it has never had a thing with an address at the top of it."

"We keep no register of addresses. We do not hold addresses. We are not a division and we do not sit in the Wing and nothing can be served here."

"Then a sheet with an address at the head of it is a piece of paper."

"It is a piece of paper in a canvas bag in a corridor and it is the first one anybody has brought up that stair in twenty years." She put her hand flat on it, exactly the way a person keeps a page down in weather.

"And you are standing four feet back from my counter at about a minute to four in the afternoon with your hands behind you asking me nothing, which is a thing I can work with, and I have been working with it for nine years."

"What happens to it."

"It goes in the file of papers that does not come off this desk, in date order, with the date it arrived, and there is no index and there is no copy and there is no way anybody has ever come up this stair to ask me about one." She looked up for the first time.

"And there is no way out of it either, which is the part that is the same as every other room in this city: nobody can take it back off that desk and nobody has ever asked."

"Can that desk carry anything," he said.

## What He Actually Came To Ask

"No."

"No requisition, no order, nothing that a person could be made to answer."

"It is a wing." She turned the sheet over again and did not lift it. "It is on the stream because the stream goes through it. Everything addressed to the sixth goes up and past and through."

"If you want a thing carried by a division you want a division, and a division is the other six, and the sixth of those is on the second floor of a building off a court with no name on it, and I have never been in it and I do not know what is in it and it is not mine to know."

"I have never been in it," he said, "and I have been in six of them."

"Then you are in a better position than most people and I am going to say the last thing I am going to say and then I am going to move the bag." She put both hands flat on the counter.

"Twenty years this stream has come up that stair and gone past and gone down, and there has never once been anything in it about a house. And there has never once been a figure about a room in this city come up here, and there is one on that sheet with the other one this afternoon, and it says five."

"It says five."

"Five." She looked at him for about two seconds. "That is the first number about that room I have ever seen and I have been on the other side of it for twenty years and nobody has ever told me it stood at three."

He left at four because the stream empties at four and there is no reason to be in a corridor after it.

A requisition laid on the twentieth of February was seventy-four days old on the afternoon of the fourth of May, and a room of five can carry it, and had not carried it, and had not received it and had not decided it, and there was nobody in that wing who could carry anything at all because a wing is not a division and the wing is on the stream because the stream goes through it.

## What He Is Left Holding At Four O'Clock

The first thing that saves him and the third thing that endangers a woman at his own address have now been in three separate rooms, and the third one is a desk in a corridor on the ground floor of a building four storeys high that has never been named on a plate and has a number on a door that is on no register of the Wing.

The sheet had come off Ferrier Street at four on the twentieth and up that stair at four on the fourth and there is no copy and no receipt and no index and no way out of it.

Nobody could object to a line like that on the face of it.

That is the entire design of a line like that.
