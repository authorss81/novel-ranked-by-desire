# The One Who Was Never Told

The kitchen at Ferrier Street was empty at half past eight on the evening of the first of June except for Sun Meiling, who had come in late with a folder against her back and had put it on the chair beside her instead of on the hook, and he sat down opposite her because there was nowhere else in that house to do what he had come to do.

"I told you one thing on the twenty-ninth of May," he said. "I am here to tell you the other."

She did not touch the folder.

"You said there was no certificate for the quarter that begins on the first of July," said Sun Meiling. "You said a keeper is going to write my name from memory. That was the first of June two days away and now it is twenty-nine days away. Thirty taken from one leaves twenty-nine, with nothing after it in this month, and that is the quarter."

"It is one hundred and one days since the twentieth of February," he said. "Ten from twenty leaves ten, and ten with thirty and thirty and thirty and one makes one hundred and one. The requisition is still capable and still not carried."

"I know what it is administered against."

"Then hear the rest of it in the order it has to be said in."

## In Her Own Division

"You certify the composition of a house you sleep in," he said. "Four times a year. In your own division. In your own hand. Under your own name. At the head of it."

"I have known that since the nineteenth of April."

"You have been the officer of that holding since the thirtieth of November. A witness line naming you on a consignment of that holding would have made you an officer too, and you kept yourself off it on the eighteenth of April with a gap two fingers wide, and it did not save you, because the certificate had already done it five months earlier."

Sun Meiling's hands were flat on the table.

"Go on."

"The requisition is administered weekly against that composition. Since the first of April. It runs down the chain to the division that holds the fourth part, and a copy of its minute has been in the division of classification for one hundred and one days, and that division is yours."

"I have not seen it."

"You were not meant to see it. A minute is a production and a copy goes to every division holding a part of the thing produced. Yours holds the description. It went there on the twentieth of February."

## The Line In The Church Book

"On the first of June a line with this address on it went into a book in a building with eleven thousand people in it," he said. "A woman who sleeps under this roof put it there. She asked a board of eleven whether the place she prays in wishes to be counted as one of the places a room's paper goes to, and it said yes in about ninety seconds."

Sun Meiling closed her eyes for a moment.

"Agnete."

"She told this table on the nineteenth of May with her coat on. The book closes on the day before the first. The line went in this morning."

"Then there are three papers with this address on them," said Sun Meiling. "The certificate in my hand. The minute in my building. The line in her book."

"And a fourth. On the thirtieth of June a keeper fills the fourth block of a carry-forward with your name and no certificate behind it. Three blocks from documents and one from memory."

## What Cannot Be Sworn

She picked up the folder and put it on the table between them, but she did not open it.

"Say the part you have been keeping since April."

He said it.

"The account cannot be closed. Nothing closes one except money, and the figure it wants is seven, and the books that could take that seven were shut to a figure of seven on the morning of the sixteenth of November and have not been opened since. It will be carried on the first of July and administered for as long as it is carried and not one day less, and there is nothing printed against it anywhere that says how long it runs for."

Sun Meiling did not move.

"And you are the officer of it for as long as it stands. You cannot be sworn. A roll cannot protect you because a roll is a list and this is a chain. The nine who signed on the second of April are bound and you are not among them, and that is not a shelter. That is the reason the requisition can be taken against you."

The kitchen was quiet. Upstairs somebody moved a chair and put it down again.

"I was never told," said Sun Meiling.

"You were never told by anybody," he said. "Not by the division that sends the form. Not by the room that administers the requisition. Not by me. You worked out the first half yourself on the nineteenth of April and I told you the second half on the twenty-ninth of May and this is the third."

"And now I have been told."

"Now you have been told."

## What Telling Costs

She opened the folder then. Inside it was a printed sheet with a single box on it, signed and dated the twenty-ninth of May, with her own name under it.

"I certified against all of it," she said. "Two days ago. Knowing the first two parts and not the third."

"You certified the quarter you are in."

"I certified a house I sleep in with a requisition administered against it and a keeper about to write my name from memory and no way to be sworn." She shut the folder. "If I had known this on the twenty-ninth I would still have signed. Do not think you have given me a choice. You have given me the shape of the room I signed inside."

"I know."

"Then know one thing more." She stood up with the folder against her back again. "I cut a space into a line on the eighteenth of April to keep myself off a paper. It did not keep me off anything. I am the officer of this holding by a certificate I fill in four minutes, and I will go on filling it, because there is nobody else to fill it."

She went to the stair and stopped with one hand on the rail.

"You told me," she said. "Nobody else did. I am not thanking you for it and I am not forgiving you for the months before it. That is the whole of what this evening has done."

The door shut behind her. The telling could not be taken back, and the quarter that begins on the first of July still had no certificate, and the woman who would have to carry it had heard it in her own kitchen from the man who had kept it from her since November.
