# The Room Stands At Five

He went to the public post off a third court at about ten in the morning on the twenty-first of April because there is nothing else to do with a morning in that building, and he had walked up the iron stair at about half past nine and the light had been on on the second floor and the light had been on on the third floor, and nothing had happened, which was twenty days of nothing and was going to be a great many more of it.

The boy behind the counter read his plate off the stone and read back 8,131 and put the plate down.

"Still fifteen under."

"Still fifteen under."

The out-tray had gone down the chain at four the previous day with a sheet in it and seven names on the sheet and a space in the middle of the sheet, and there was no copy of anything and no receipt, and there was going to be a quarterly return in about nine days with a floor on it, and the floor was not going to be on it because a consignment has no floor and he had known that for three weeks.

He walked back down the stair and out of the building and did not go up it.

## The Book She May Show Him

The room at the end of a corridor off the second landing does not do anything at ten minutes to four on the afternoon of the twenty-first of April, and the corridor outside it is about forty feet long and has a bench at the end of it, and a member is allowed to stand in it.

He had been a member for six days. In six days he had learned three things and one of them was that a member may be shown the book and may not ask the room to do anything at all, and there is no instrument anywhere in this city for a member of that room to ask it for anything, and the clerk told him so on the second afternoon with the door open.

At about four o'clock she put her head out.

"You are standing there at the same time you stood there on the sixth of February," she said. "Do you want the book."

"Yes."

She brought it out and put it on the bench and did not hand it to him, which is what the room does.

## Nine Ruled Lines And A Tenth

It was a weekly return. Nine ruled lines, one under each seat, and beneath the nine of them a tenth, headed *composition of the holding*, with a box to be filled in.

A weekly return carries a standing. It does not carry names. A quarterly return carries names, on the last working day of a quarter, and it does not carry them on any other day, and this sheet was not a quarterly one.

The first line said seated. So did the second and the third.

The fourth said the standing restored by the Council on the morning of the first of April, and there was nothing else on it.

"Marguerite Vale," said Lin Hao.

"She is not in this book and she has never been in this book." The clerk did not look up. "Her name is in a quarterly return and the quarterly return went out on the twenty-ninth of March to four divisions on the ordinary stream, and it has been read aloud in this room once, and it cannot be struck by anybody including her, and this is a weekly sheet and it carries a standing and not a person."

"So a broker's name is in four divisions and the room at the end of a corridor does not know it is there."

"The room does not know it is there because the room does not read the quarterly," said the clerk. "That is the whole of what a quarterly is for. It is the one return that goes out, and it is the one return this room never sees again."

The fifth line was new.

It said five, and under the standing on that line there was the word.

## The Word Under The Fifth

"That is not a rank," she said. "I did not know that on the fifteenth and I have not found out since and I have looked at it in every book in this building."

She turned the sheet so he could see it and kept her hand on it.

"I wrote it at about two minutes to one on the fifteenth because the fifth line was new, and the sheet carries a ruled space under the fifth line for anything the fifth line cannot hold by itself. That space had been blank on every weekly return in this room for nineteen years. I wrote what a clerk writes when the space has to be filled and nobody has told her what goes in it."

"It is my hand and there are about four thousand other things in my hand in this building."

"What does it say."

She said the word. Then she said, "It is not a figure and it is not a rank and it is not one of the two hundred and sixty things in the index at the end of that book, and I have been carrying it about for six days as though it were a figure, and you are the only person in this building who might know and I have not been permitted to ask you because you are a member of this room."

"I do not know."

"That," said the clerk, "is the first honest thing anybody has said to me about that word since I wrote it."

She squared the return, and the standing of the room was at the bottom of it in her hand in about six words, exactly as it had been written under the nine lines for twenty-six weekly returns in a row.

*The room stands at five.*

## Half Past Four, Seven Desks

The return went out at about half past four on the ordinary stream, which is the ordinary time and has been the ordinary time since he has been in any building in this city.

It went to seven desks.

The first was Field. The second was counter-forensics on the second sub-level, where an officer initials the transmission above a part of hers, and the part has been in that division since the morning of the twenty-seventh of October.

The third was the Vestry. The fourth was the guild. The fifth was the Church of the Quiet Number, which has no ranks and no register and has never asked for a return in its life and receives four hundred a quarter anyway.

The sixth was Veil-Research, second floor of a building off that court, and a man on the third floor of the same building has been at his post for nine years and has not walked down her corridor since the fourth of February.

The seventh was a wing off a third court.

That is not a mistake, and it has never been a mistake. The stream to the research division runs through a wing, and that wing sits on the same court as a division that sits outside the Wing proper, keeps no register of addresses, has had the shape of something since the first of January, and has never been named on any paper he has been able to get hold of.

It is on the stream because it is on the stream. It has been on the stream for twenty years.

And this afternoon the return said five.

He stood in a corridor of about forty feet and worked out, standing there, that it was the first time in twenty years that a figure about that room had gone to that division, and that it had gone because a room stood up from three to four to five across six days and a woman wrote a word under the fifth line in a space that had been empty for nineteen years, and that a return goes where a return goes.

Nobody could object to that service on its face. That is exactly what makes it a service.

## What He Is Left Holding

The thing that saves him and the thing that endangers him are the same piece of paper leaving the same door at four in the afternoon in the same city, and both of those are true, and they have been true since about half past four this afternoon and he was standing in the corridor while it happened.

A requisition laid on the twentieth of February had not been carried, had not been received and had not been decided, and had been administered weekly from the first of April against the composition of premises 34-6-118. Sixty-one days. Two months and one day, laid on the twentieth of February, and it was two months and one day old at about half past four on the afternoon of the twenty-first of April. It was not three months, and nobody in that corridor was going to call it three months.

A requisition carried needs five, and the room stood at five, and the sheet of the twentieth of February could now be carried and had not been carried and would not be carried this afternoon because a room does not carry things on the day a member stands in its corridor.

He had sixty-nine days. That was the rest of April, and the whole of May, and the whole of June.

And eleven minutes away on a second floor there was a woman who had found out on the morning of the nineteenth, from a space two fingers wide in a line, what she had been the officer of since the thirtieth of November.

She had told him one sentence about a gap in a line on the evening of the eighteenth and had gone down a stair.

She was not on a roll and not on a witness line and could not be sworn, and as of the morning of the thirtieth of March she was the officer of a holding that five people in a room at the end of a corridor could serve anything on.

He had not told her any of it. He had gone up sixty-one steps on the sixth of December with a question about an address and been refused, and he had not asked her a second time since, and he was not going to.

There is a gap in a line with her reason written in it, in a book at the end of a corridor, and the reason is the best thing anybody in that kitchen has said since the thirtieth of March, and it is going to be read by a stranger in about four years, correctly.
