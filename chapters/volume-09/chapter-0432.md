# The Fourth Block Of A Sheet

The counting room is two floors under the Exchange and you go down behind the plate office, and on the morning of the twenty-seventh of May Solveig Rask had the chained real book open on the board with her writing hand tied into it by a cord, and Hanneke Ruis was standing at the rail holding a cup she had not touched.

"There is a thing I am doing on the thirtieth of June," said Hanneke Ruis, "and I have decided to tell you about it on the twenty-seventh, thirty-three days early, and you may put that in whatever file you have got and check the date on it."

"Go on."

"First. You are not going to get any more of me downstairs." She did not turn round. "I have said that to you in writing twice and you have honoured it and I am not going to take it back and I am not going to explain it. Solveig."

Solveig Rask turned a page and did not look up, and did not look at him, and did not stop writing, which is what she had been doing for about nine years and which is the entire of a career.

"She is not going to speak to you," said Hanneke Ruis, "and that is her rule of the floor and not a decision, and the rule has a condition in it and the condition in it was met on the thirteenth of March and I am the only person in this building who knows that she has not come down here to say so."

## Seven And Seven

"You said that in March."

"I said it in March at a rail with a cup in my hand and you gave me two figures and they were the same figure, and she said the words out loud in front of a witness for the record."

Hanneke Ruis squared the sheet.

"She said it because she is a keeper's clerk, and a person who comes down this stair does not get looked at, and she looked at you anyway and told a room full of me that she had put the book down and had a face on her."

"I remember."

"So there is one figure for one door now and has been for seventy-five days. The rule of this floor has been satisfied since the middle of March, and she has not come down here, and she is not going to today."

Hanneke Ruis turned round with the cup still in her hand.

"And I want to say the next part out loud before I say anything else, and I want you to know that I am saying it because of that and not because of you."

## What She Is Going To Enter

"You are going to enter a name on a sheet on the thirtieth of June."

"I am going to enter a name in the fourth block of the sheet that goes on the first of July," said Hanneke Ruis, "and there will not be a certificate behind it, and I will be writing it from memory, and there is no instrument in this city that lets a keeper do that."

He did not say anything for about four seconds.

"Say the rest of it."

"There is no certificate for the quarter that begins on the first of July."

She put the cup down on the rail, which she had not done in about nine years.

"A composition is only ever good for one quarter. A carry-forward gets drafted on the last working day of the month before the quarter opens, and you cannot put a sheet together at all without a certificate to work from, and what has been standing behind the composition of these premises since November is a printed certificate dated the thirtieth of that month."

"That lapsed on the thirtieth of March."

"It was done again on the morning of the thirtieth of March, on a printed form with a single box on it that came down to a division, and a woman filled the box in in about four minutes."

Hanneke Ruis did not turn round again.

"And there has not been one since. And there is going to have to be one before the first of July, and there is no office in this city that will call a woman up to a desk and ask her to certify a house."

"A certificate is not a demand, it is a routine, and a routine happens four times a year to a woman who has never once been asked whether she minded."

## Four Blocks And No Fifth

"Say it in the order it has to be said in," said Hanneke Ruis. "You have had that from somebody else this year and you are not going to get it from me in any other order."

She took the sheet out of the drawer and put it flat on the rail.

"A carry-forward is the size of a playing card with four ruled blocks on it and a heading at the top. Address. Quarter commencing the first."

"One."

"Figure carried. Seven. Being the composition of the address. That has not moved since the thirteenth of March and it will not move before the first of July."

"Two."

"Priced line. Seven. The sixteenth day of September. Initialled. Not signed. That has not moved since the fourteenth of March and it is never going to move."

"Three."

"Composition certified at. Premises 34-6-118. Certificate of the thirtieth of November."

"Four."

"And the fourth block is a name," said Hanneke Ruis, "and it is printed off the certificate, and it says Sun Meiling, and it has said that since the fourteenth of March, and on the first of July there will be a sheet with three blocks filled from documents and a fourth block filled from a woman in her own hand, and no certificate behind any of it."

## Why It Cannot Be Closed

"And that is the whole of the sheet," she said, "and now the second half, and I am going to say it once and you are not going to have me say it again."

"There is no fifth block."

"There is no fifth block," said Hanneke Ruis. "Four is what the form gives anybody. You can pull a figure off one of these later and you can pull an hour off one of these later, and a name you cannot pull off, ever, and that is the whole distance between a sheet of paper and a human being."

"It was said at this rail on the fourteenth of March by a woman with a pen on a chain and it has not been said since."

She put one finger on the sheet.

"The account cannot be closed. That is the sentence, and it is the only sentence. It cannot be closed because nothing in this city closes an account except money, and the figure it wants is seven."

"The books that were able to take that seven were shut to a figure of seven on the morning of the sixteenth of November and they have not been opened since."

She let that stand on its own for about a minute.

"And there is a second reason and it is four months old and I have not said it to anybody."

Hanneke Ruis took her hand off the paper.

"An account that is carried forward is administered for as long as it is carried and not one day less, and there is nothing on the face of the sheet that anybody in this city can read that says *this is the last quarter*. There is nothing printed against a thirty-year account anywhere in this city that says how long it runs for."

"And on the first of July this one will have no certificate in it. The block that would tell a stranger what is being carried forward is the block the certificate would have filled, and it is going to be filled by me out of my own memory."

"I am going to do it, it was not asked for, and there will be no way of saying afterwards that I did it because a man came down this stair."

## What It Costs Her

"You could leave the fourth block empty."

"No," said Hanneke Ruis. "That is the whole of it and I have known it since about half past seven this morning. If I leave the fourth block empty on the first of July then there is a carry-forward in this city with a name on it three times running and a hole on the fourth, and a hole is where paper is missing, and in four years a reader will take that hole for somebody losing interest."

She put the sheet back in the drawer and shut it.

"If I fill it from memory then the name of the woman who certifies the composition of premises 34-6-118 goes on a document that the composition of premises 34-6-118 has not certified. That is not a small thing and it is not a mistake, and it will be correct on the face of the paper and it will be false underneath it, and in about four years that is a sentence somebody will have to say out loud in a room."

"Say it yourself," said Solveig Rask.

Both of them stopped. Solveig Rask had not turned round, had not put the pen down, and had spoken without lifting her head from the chained book for the first time in a year and a half.

"Whose hair is it," she said. "Whose sheet. Whose floor. Whose rule." Each question took about four seconds and each one of them was a question of procedure and every one of them put the whole of the thing on her, and she did not turn round, and the pen stayed on its chain.

"Solveig—"

"I have said four words in twelve minutes and I have a book," said Solveig Rask, "and I am not going to take back the four words, and you can take them to the Council in four years if a Council is sitting, and I will not remember saying them and I will not deny them."

Hanneke Ruis stood at the rail for a while with the cup she still had not drunk out of.

"Her floor," she said. "Her rule. Not mine and not yours."

## The Third Of June

"There is a date," said Hanneke Ruis. "The first of July is a carry-forward and I have to have it drafted by the thirtieth of June, which is the final working day of the quarter, the day the Council sits, and the day a clinic in this city receives two returns about one matter."

"Yes."

"So there is a date, and it is the same date as everything else in this quarter, and there are two ways of getting to it and neither of them is a door."

She turned the cup over without drinking from it.

"A woman can be asked. Or a form can go down to her desk on its own in the ordinary way with a box on it and nothing else, which is what happened on the morning of the thirtieth of March, and which took four minutes, and which no one asked for, and everybody has been living with since."

"You are going to leave it to the ordinary way."

"I am going to leave it to the ordinary way because I am a keeper and I draft what I am given and I do not go up into divisions," said Hanneke Ruis.

"And because the last time a form came down to that desk it was on the morning of the thirtieth of March and a woman filled in a box and carried on with the rest of the morning. She has been certifying a house she sleeps in four times a year since the thirtieth of November without ever once having been asked whether she would rather not."

She put the cup down on the rail at last, still full.

"And on the thirtieth of June I am going to fill in the fourth block of a carry-forward sheet for the quarter that begins on the first of July out of my own memory, in my own hand, with a name on it that no certificate stands behind, and there is nobody to thank for it, and nobody is going to ask me about it."

She did not drink the tea and she did not look at him again.

"There is no thirty-first," said Hanneke Ruis. "People in this city say the end of the month and they mean the thirtieth, and there has not been a thirty-first since before I came down this stair."

"There are three days left in this month and thirty in the next one and every date anybody in this batch has is inside one or the other of them, and I have kept this floor for eleven years and I have never yet been out by a day."
