# Ten Lines On A Return

The return came back up the chain on the morning of the twenty-third of May, in a bundle with eleven other things in it, and the clerk of the room at the end of the corridor put it on the second table under the window because it was the only thing in the bundle that had been on that table nine days before.

"There is writing on the back of it," she said.

## Nine Ruled Lines And A Tenth With A Box

A weekly return carries a standing and carries no names. She turned it round so he could see it.

Nine ruled lines. One, two, three, four and five had something in them, and the first four were old enough that the ink had gone brown and the fifth was about six weeks old and still had the sheen on it.

Six, seven, eight and nine were empty. And under the nine of them there was a tenth line with a box at the end of it, and the box had something in it in her own hand that he had seen once on the fifteenth of April and once on the sixth of May and which this chapter is not going to print.

"It went out on the ordinary stream at four on the afternoon of the twenty-second, to seven desks," said the clerk. "It came back up this morning at about eleven, in a bundle, with eleven other things, and it came back because a division that has been sent a paper it has no business holding sends it back up the chain unanswered, and there is no instrument in this city by which a division can dispose of a return and dispose of it properly at the same time."

"Then it went to six desks and came back from one of them."

"It went to seven and it came back from one of them, and it came back on the top of the bundle and not at the bottom, which means whoever sent it up put it on the run when the run was already made. That is how a person sends a thing back without asking anybody's permission."

She put the return down.

"And there is writing on the back of it in pencil, and there is nothing on the front of it except figures. A return answers the question it was asked and does nothing else."

## The Back Of A Paper

"It is in her hand," said the clerk. "I have had a book on this table for eleven years and I know nine hands in this city that come through this corridor and that is one of them, and I am not going to say whose, because a member of this room cannot put a question to the clerk of this room about a name or a space, and I have told you that once and I am not going to have to tell you twice."

"You do not have to say it. I can see who wrote it and I am not going to ask you either."

"Good."

"She wrote on the back of it."

"She wrote on the back of it," said the clerk, "and that is the part I want to say something about, and I have been thinking about it since about half past eleven and I have not got it down yet and I am going to get it down now because in four years somebody is going to turn this over."

He turned it over.

*Ferrier line. Twenty-second. Change going out at the arcade and a hold at the Vestry and about nine hundred people stood still for four seconds and then went on. Twenty-two minutes, maybe. It is in my notebook too and it is in there first.*

## What A Signal Operator Is

"A tram stopped anywhere in this quarter puts a change through her the way a hand laid flat on the back of your neck would," said the clerk, "and forty thousand people move on that line on an ordinary afternoon, and she has been doing it for nine years, and she can feel the shape of a thing going wrong before anybody who has to be told is told."

"And there is nowhere to put it."

"There is nowhere to put it anywhere in this city and I have looked, and I am not the only one who has looked. Four offices have gone at that woman in writing over the years, asking for something she could put down on paper once a week, and she has never given any of them anything."

The clerk sat down on the bench at the end of the corridor, which she had not done.

"A weekly return is a paper that arrives at a division every week with nine ruled lines on it and nothing on six of them. Four offices have spent eleven years trying to invent a sheet for what she does, and here is one. It arrives by itself. Every week."

"It is sent by a room at the end of a corridor to seven desks, and six of the lines on the front of it are empty, and the back of it is not part of it."

"That is the whole of it."

"That is the entire thing about it, and she worked it out in about four seconds on the twenty-second of May, standing at a desk with a return in her hand." The clerk turned it over and put it back the right way up. "And the back of a return is the only part of a return that is not a return."

## The Ten Lines

"Nine lines and a tenth with a box," he said. "And the tenth one is the only line on that paper that anybody in this city has ever written anything into that was not a figure, and she wrote on the other side of it."

"The tenth line has had three separate entries made in it in nine years. Two of them were made in the last thirty-one days and neither of them was mine, and one of them is not in this book at all."

She said it perfectly level.

"And I could not tell you what any of the three of them means, because I wrote one and I have never been told what it means. There is a box under a space in a witness line about eleven hundred feet from this desk that a clerk filled on the sixth of May with the same word in the same hand and read it aloud in this building, and neither of those two words can be read by anybody, including the two women who wrote them."

He looked at the back of the return for a while.

"There is a time on it," he said.

"There is a time on it and the name of a line and a figure of people and a duration, and there is no signature, because a return carries no names. There is no line on the front of it that will take any of it, and the back of it is not part of it."

## Why It Cannot Be Asked

"Could you ask her."

"No."

"You could write to her division."

"A signal operator counts as a division whether she happens to be sitting on a tram or not, and the division has no officer that anybody can address, and the officer is on a tram from about six in the morning until about half past five."

The clerk stood up and took the return and held it.

"And the one thing this city has never been able to do with that woman is ask her anything. She is on the witness line of the eighteenth of April, and she is an officer of the holding, and she is at that kitchen table every evening."

"There are four people in this city entitled to put a question to her and you are one of them, and you are the only one of the four who would get an answer, and you are not going to use it. I have watched you decide that twice since the sixth of December and I would like it written down somewhere that I am right about it twice."

He did not argue with that.

"It is not a question about a name or a space and I would answer it," said the clerk, "and you would not ask it and you know why you would not ask it, and the reason is the fourth of December, and it is the best thing about you and it is going to cost her four years."

## What It Makes Her

"She has made herself an officer of it," said Lin Hao.

The clerk looked up.

"A witness line naming a woman on a consignment of that holding makes her an officer of that holding, and an officer is a person this room can serve. That has been said at that table since the eighteenth of April in six mouths."

He put his hand flat on the second table.

"And a division that is sent a paper and writes on it and sends it back up the chain has made itself an officer of that paper. It is a smaller thing. It is a pencil and four seconds and nine hundred people."

"And there is nothing in this city anybody can do with it, and in four years somebody will turn this return over and find that the only handwriting on it in nine years is a woman who cannot be asked anything, on the back, in pencil, about people standing still at a stop."

"Yes."

"And you are going to keep it."

"I am going to keep it in the bundle with the other eleven things. The bundle goes into the keeping on the thirtieth of May and the thirtieth of June and the thirtieth of September and the thirtieth of December, and nobody may correct anything in it and nobody may take anything out of it."

"In about four years there will be a return in this building with a figure of five on the front of it and a time on the back of it and no way on the face of the paper to say which is which."

She put the bundle down on the table under the window and squared it against the edge.

"That is the tenth line," she said. "Nine for the room and one that nobody in this building put there."

## A Quarter To Five At The North End Of A Wall

He went up to the wall at about a quarter to five that afternoon, because he had no other use for the evening and because he has stood at that end of it nine times this year.

Beatrix Halm was at the north end with her hands round a pencil and her back to the traffic and her notebook open on the ledge, and she could not hear him, and she did not turn round, and he stood about four feet away from her for about two minutes and said nothing at all.

Then she wrote something, and turned the notebook round, and pushed it along the ledge towards him with two fingers.

*Twenty-second. 4.58. Nothing.*

Six lines in nine years had anything under them. This was the seventh and there was nothing under it, which was the ordinary thing and was what the notebook was for, and there was a pencil mark in the margin where the pencil had been put down hard while she thought.

He put the notebook back the right way up for her.

At the end of the day there was a piece of paper in a bundle in a building off a second landing, in a book in the keeping, on which nine lines carried five figures, a tenth line carried a box with three separate entries in it, and the back of it carried one sentence in pencil in a hand that nobody had ever been permitted to ask about.

It was the first return in nine years that had anything on the back of it at all, and it had come back up a chain because a woman on a tram had put it there, and there was not one instrument in this city for putting a line back down.
