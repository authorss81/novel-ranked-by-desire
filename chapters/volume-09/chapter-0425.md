# The Minute That Carries Five Seals

The clerk of the room at the end of the corridor had the book open on the second table when he came up on the fifteenth of May, at about eleven in the morning, and it was open at the back, at the page where things go that are not about a person, and the line she entered on the ninth of May was the bottom one on it.

"There is one more kind of paper in this room," she said, before he had got all the way to the bench. "You have been in here six times since the first of April and you have not asked me about it, and I have been waiting since about the tenth of April, and I am not going to wait any longer, because the thing I am going to tell you stops being true in forty-six days and after that you cannot have it from anybody at all."

"Sit down," she said, when he did not. "And do not read it. I will read it."

## A Keeping Book And A Minute Are Not The Same Animal

"What I entered on the ninth of May is a line in a book," she said. "I wrote down what this room is holding and what it is not doing with it, and it went out on the stream at four that afternoon and it is a line and it is correct and it cannot be struck. That is the whole of it and you have it right, and it is not a carrying."

"And the other kind."

"The other kind is a minute. A minute is not a line in a book. A minute is a record of what a room did in a room, and it is kept in this building and it is also a production, because a production is a copy, and a copy goes to every division that holds a part of the thing produced." She turned the book round. "There are two documents in this room that are both about a sheet laid on the twentieth of February."

"One is the line I entered on the ninth of May, and it is in here, and it cannot be corrected. The other is the minute, and it is in here, and it has been copied to three divisions since the morning it was made, and one of those three is a division on a sub-level below this corridor, and I am not going to say whose desk it went to, and you are not going to ask me."

He did not ask her.

"You are not going to ask me," she said, "and that is the first sensible thing that has happened in this corridor since the first of April."

## What It Said On The Twentieth Of February

She had it on a sheet of her own, which was not a surprise; a clerk keeps her productions where she can reach them.

"I am going to read the whole of it and it will take about forty seconds and I am going to read it as it was written, including the parts that are against you, because in about four years somebody is going to read it in a folder and they will not have anybody in the room to explain it to them."

She read it.

*Eleventh requisition. Subject: the account of thirty years standing at premises 34-6-118. Holding: one sheet of plain paper in a citizen's own hand, witnessed at the address of the holding. Figure: seven, being the composition of the address, dated the sixteenth day of September, initialled and not signed. Ground: a description of premises 34-6-118 made over the head of the premises on the twenty-seventh day of October at about eleven in the morning, held in the division of classification, entered in this room on the second day of February.*

*Laid. Called. Served and heard. Not carried.*

*Nothing has been taken off any person and nothing will be taken off any person before the thirtieth of the month.*

*Sealed: three.*

She stopped there.

"Three," said Lin Hao.

"Three, because three of the nine could seal on that morning." She put the sheet down. "The fourth line had said *vacant* since the twelfth of February."

"The fifth was recused on her own signature from the fourth of December. The sixth had withdrawn her own seal on the twenty-third of December. The seventh had not been in this room since the twenty-third of December. There were seven people in the room on the sixth of February and three of the nine on the twentieth, and the three who sealed are the three who are on the ordinary stream, and none of them is you."

## The Sentence That Died On The Thirtieth Of March

"Read the fourth line again."

She read it again, and it came out word for word the same, all fourteen words of it, in the same voice.

"That was written on the morning of the twentieth of February by the chair, in those words, on purpose, because she wanted it read in the right order in about four years." He put his hand flat on the bench.

"The thirtieth was the thirtieth of March. On the first of April this room stood at four for the first time since the twelfth of February, and on the fifteenth of April it stood at five, and the fifth is me, and I did that myself with a pen in a queue at a public post off a third court. The requisition is eighty-five days old. It has been capable of being carried for thirty days and has not been carried on any of them."

"Yes."

"So that sentence in the minute has been true for about eleven weeks and false for about forty-six days, and it is the only sentence in that minute that was about me personally, and it is in a document that three divisions hold a copy of, and a clerk may only enter and cannot correct, and no person in this city can strike it."

"No," said the clerk. "And I want to be exact about what I am telling you, because it is the whole of what I know and it is not much."

"The sentence was true. It was true when it was written and it was true for as long as it could be true, and it stopped being true on the first of April because this room changed and not because anybody in it meant anything. A sentence in a minute does not expire. It is only untrue. And a minute is read by people who were not in the room."

## What A Minute Of A Carrying Will Look Like

"Then tell me what the next one looks like."

She looked at him for longer than she had at any point in twenty minutes.

"You are asking me what a minute of a carrying looks like," she said. "A minute of a carrying is a minute. It carries the seals of the five who sat, and it names the subject and the holding and the figure and the ground, and it records the finding in the words the finding was made in, and it is dated, and it is sealed five times."

"Five."

"Five, because five carried it, and there are five in this room, and one of them is a man who lodged a consignment at a public post in his own hand on the morning of the fifteenth of April at about ten minutes to nine and cannot get it back."

She closed the book and put her hand flat on it. "Your seal would go on it. That is not a figure of speech and I am not doing it for effect. You have never put a seal on anything in this room in your life and you would put one on the first of these, and a seal is not a signature you can say you were not in the room for. You would be in the room."

"And the copies."

"The minute goes out at four on the day it is made. It goes on the ordinary stream to seven desks, and it is also a production, so a copy goes to every division holding a part of the thing produced, and those two lists are not the same list and the second one is the larger."

She stood up and took the book under her arm, which is what she does when a conversation is about to contain something she cannot carry out of the room.

"There are seven of them and you have heard the names of six of them in this corridor about nine times since the fourth of May. The seventh is a wing off a third court, sitting outside the Wing, keeping no register of addresses, on this stream for twenty years, and never named on any paper you can get hold of."

"Six of those seven have a woman from this address at the desk."

"Six of those seven have a woman from this address sitting at the desk," said the clerk.

"You worked that out on the fourth of May and you went and looked at the seventh. The seventh is not a division, nothing can be served at it, and it is on the stream because the stream goes through it."

"Field is a woman who sleeps at that address. Counter-forensics is a woman who sleeps at that address. The guild is a woman who sleeps at that address. The Church is a woman who sleeps at that address. Veil-Research is a woman who sleeps at that address and is not on your roll. And the Ferrier line is a woman who sleeps at that address and is deaf."

"The seventh is the Vestry, and the woman who keeps the Vestry is on your roll and has never been at that table."

## What Was In The Copy That Went Down On The Twentieth

"The minute names the premises."

"The minute names the premises at the head of it, and the premises are at the head of it in the fourth part as well, and in the holding, and in the description, and a minute of a carrying of this sheet would name the address four times and would carry a seal of yours next to it."

She was standing very still. "A copy went to three divisions on the morning of the twentieth of February. I know the three because I write them out. Two of them are divisions that hold a part of this requisition. The third one holds the description that is the ground, and that division is not on a street you can look up, and I am not going to say anything else about the third one, and you are not going to ask."

He sat with it.

"The third one is her division," he said.

"I did not say whose division."

"You said the description is held in the division of classification. It is in the minute, in the fourth part, in the words I just heard read out in this corridor. That is not her saying anything. That is a form."

He stood up.

"That division drafted the description on the twenty-seventh of October and entered it on the second of February, and a copy of that minute has been sitting in its own building for one hundred and three days, and its head has never said one word to me about it."

"This morning that woman told me she has been carrying a sheet since the nineteenth of October that she is not permitted to send, and she is the head of that division, and a head of a division gets her own productions on her own ordinary run."

The clerk put the book down on the table and did not pick it up again.

## Forty-Six Days

"Forty-six days," he said.

"Thirty of June minus fifteen of May. Forty-six days including today, forty-five after it." She had the book under her arm again. "Here is what I can tell you about the last of them and I am telling you because I decided on the ninth of May that I would and I have not told anybody I decided it."

"Go on."

"My book is written up when this room sits. It is not kept up to date between times, a clerk may only enter and cannot correct, and everything I enter is entered once."

"When a sheet is carried this room writes the finding, the finding is sealed five times, and the minute goes out at four the same afternoon to seven desks and to three divisions."

"And it is not entered out of that book on any day either, because a consignment goes in on the day it is lodged and does not come out on any day. A standing may go. That is a different sentence and it is not one I am going to let you mix up."

"And on the thirtieth of June."

"On the thirtieth of June the Council sits, and it is the last working day of the quarter, and the room where nine people sit can be changed by anybody the Council can find, and not by you and not by me and not by the four people who have reasons for being where they are." She looked at the length of the corridor.

"And if this room carries that sheet on any day before then, there is a minute in this building with your seal on it and the address of your own front door at the head of it, and it will be copied to a division that cannot be served and to five divisions that can, and four of those five have a woman asleep at that address tonight, and the fifth has one who is not asleep there and has not been for nine years."

"What do you want me to do with that."

"Nothing," said the clerk. "I am a clerk and I may only enter. I have entered one line in this book in twenty years that was not put in front of me and it is on the page behind this one and it cannot be struck, and I did it because a minute in a division carries an address and a clerk cannot sit on an absence."

She went into the room and shut the door, and there were nine chairs under a window and eight of them had nothing in front of them, and the fifth of the five was empty in a way that had been empty for eleven days.

Outside, about eleven steps up, a glass front with a blind pulled four inches down had a division behind it that had made a description of a house at about eleven one morning in October, and had had its own minute in a drawer since the twentieth of February, and had said nothing about it to anybody, and had asked him for the only thing in the city she could ask him for and had been refused on the day before.
