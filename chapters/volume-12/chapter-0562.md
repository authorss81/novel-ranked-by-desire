# The Fourth Condition In A Demand

Thirteen days from the fourth of February to this morning, and this morning is one thousand four hundred and fifty-six days off the first of February of the first year. One thousand four hundred and forty was the first of February of year five, and sixteen days into that month is one thousand four hundred and fifty-six, and one thousand four hundred and fifty-six less one thousand four hundred and forty-three is thirteen.

Forty-three days from the thirtieth of March, which is the seventeenth sheet, and one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine less one thousand four hundred and fifty-six is forty-three. He did that one twice because the room he was standing in was the sort of room a man gets a figure wrong in.

## The Room Off The Court

He came in at about eleven in the morning through a door with a brass plate screwed to it that named the room and said nothing whatever the room did, and there were about nine people in it and a man in a clerk's coat behind a counter with a window cut into it.

He stood at the back.

The building is a court and the room is not the court. It is the room where people are told what a demand is before anybody is asked about anything, and it has a bench along one wall and a form on a clipboard and a man in a coat who has been doing it for long enough to have stopped being careful with his face.

Nobody looked at him. There are about thirty people a day through that door and a white plate in a coat with a name on it and no figure cut into it anywhere is the least remarkable thing in the room.

## What Is On The Counter

The form was one page, and it had been printed, and at the top of it under the room's name was a space for the address of the premises and a space for a date and then four lines.

They were not numbered. They were set in the same typeface as the rest of the form, in the same weight, and they were in the order he had written them in a kitchen off Ferrier Street in the second year.

*It changed what a person held.*

*It happened on a day the person can name and in a place the person can name.*

*Another person was there, or was given it, and that person can be named or described closely enough that a person reading this in four years would know which person.*

*And it is not on a page anywhere in this city.*

He had read them about nine hundred times. He had never once seen them in a form, in a typeface, under a room's name, with a space left beside each one for somebody to write a word in.

## The Man In The Clerk's Coat

The clerk did not explain the form. He put his finger on the first line and asked the woman in front of the counter the question, and he asked it in the same words every time, and about nine people heard it.

"Tell me what it changed for you."

The woman said it changed a roof. She said a man came in the night of a day in the ninth month of the second year and put two courses of slate on a cold roof and would not take anything and would not come back, and she can name the night because her boy was born on it, and she can name the street.

The clerk wrote one word. He asked about the second line and got the night and the street and wrote one word. He asked about the third and the woman stopped for about four seconds and then gave a description of a man that would let anybody reading this in four years know exactly which man, and he wrote one word.

Then he got to the fourth.

"Show me the paper it is on."

"There is no paper."

"Then put nothing in that box," said the clerk, "and I am going to write *nothing proved* at the bottom, and that is not a thing I have any choice about."

## What The Clerk Is Allowed

"You have four boxes and one of them is empty on every form in this room."

"I have four boxes," said the clerk. "A person comes in here and says something was done for them and was not paid, and I am not the man who decides whether it was. I am the man who decides whether anybody can look at it."

"If you can put a paper in front of a magistrate in about four years, then there is a thing to look at, and if you cannot, then there is not, and that is not me. I have been in this room nine years and that is the whole of what I do."

He put his pen down beside the form and did not cap it.

"You are going to ask me whether those four lines are mine."

"I am not going to ask you that."

"I will tell you anyway, because it is printed and I do not get to choose what I hand out." He turned the clipboard round and pushed it two inches towards the room. "A landlord brought me four hundred of these on the second of February and I have been using them since and they are the best form anybody has ever brought me, and I want to be exact about why."

He tapped the fourth line with two fingers.

"The first three are hard. A person who cannot name a day has nothing, and I write nothing proved, and about nine in every ten that come through here are in that box before they get to me. That is not a rule anybody made. That is just what people are."

"This one is different. This one is a gift." He said it without any weight at all. "This one says a person has done something that changed what they held, and can name the day and the place and the other person, and has it on no page at all. And I have to write *nothing proved* against that, in the box where the proof goes, in my own hand, and I do it about nine times a month."

## Nine In A Room

He stood at the back of that room from about eleven until about half past twelve and he counted, which he does not do, and which is the only instrument he has.

Nine people came to the counter. Nine asked.

Four could not name a day and did not get past the second line. One named a day and could not describe the other person closely enough and did not get past the third. One had a page, and a good one, dated and signed and countersigned, and the clerk took a copy of it and put a mark against the box and the woman put her hand flat on the counter and left it there.

And four got all the way to the fourth line, and all four of them had nothing.

One of the four was a woman who had been doing it in a side room off Cordwain for nine years for a man who is not alive. She could name the month and the street and the man's face, and there is no paper about any part of it anywhere in this city, because he did it with his hands and told nobody and died in the eleventh month of the third year.

The clerk wrote *nothing proved* and turned the clipboard round.

She read it. She read it twice.

"That is right," she said. "Write that I brought it."

"There is no box for that."

"Then make a box."

"I am not permitted to make a box," said the clerk. "I have a form and I fill the form and that is the whole of what a room off a court is."

## The Part He Does Not Say

He stood at the back of that room for eighty minutes and he said nothing at all, and there was one sentence in him the whole time and the sentence was true and he did not say it.

*You built a gate in one room for one counter and you got the hinges put in by a man who does not print type, and it is now the only gate in this city that unrecorded people can be put through, and the one thing it does is close.*

The Register of Unrecorded Merit holds four hundred and nineteen accounts with an hour against each and no figure on any of them. That is the entire instrument. He is the entire instrument. There is no second room in this city where a person can say a thing they cannot show.

And the sentence that kept that open was the fourth one. *It is not on a page anywhere in this city.* He had spent four years believing it was the sentence that let a person in.

It was the sentence that keeps them out. It was always going to be the sentence that keeps them out. It has been on a public page in this city since the sheet of the thirtieth of June of year two, and now it is at the top of a form in a room off a court, and it is doing exactly what it was written to do.

He worked that out standing at the back of a room with a bench along one wall at about twenty to twelve in the morning on the seventeenth of February.

He had worked it out on the eleventh of July of the fourth year. He had written it down at four in the morning on the top step of his own stair with a tally board across his knees. It had taken him a hundred and ninety days and it had taken a clerk nine seconds.

## The Plate

He stopped at the door on the way out and looked at the clipboard lying on the counter.

The four lines were not written in ink. Everything else on that form had been written in ink for nine years — the boxes, the headings, the room's name, the line where the date goes — and the four lines were not ink. They were part of the paper. They came up out of it in the same grey as the words above them and they had been on that plate before the first demand was ever printed.

The clerk had told him: four hundred, brought on the second of February, the best form anybody had ever brought him.

A plate is not a demand. A demand is four hundred sheets and a plate is one piece of metal, and the man who has the plate can print the four lines again on any other paper he is asked to print, at any size, on any day, for anybody, without anybody's name on it.

*He stood in a doorway and understood that the four conditions had left the room it was written in and that there was no longer any way of telling how far it had gone, and that the only man in this city who could tell him was a printer off Cordwain who had no idea he was holding anything.*

*Seventeenth of February of year five, about twenty past twelve in the morning, in the doorway of a room off a court. Thirteen days since the fourth and forty-three days to the seventeenth sheet.*

*Nine people at a counter and four of them got all the way to the fourth line and all four of them had nothing, and a man in a clerk's coat wrote nothing proved in the box where the proof goes, four times, in his own hand, and he has no choice about it and he was not being cruel.*

*I have known since the eleventh of July of the fourth year. I stood in a doorway on the seventeenth of February of the fifth year and a clerk showed me the same thing in ninety seconds.*

*And they are in type. They are on a plate. A plate is not a demand and a plate does not stop.*
