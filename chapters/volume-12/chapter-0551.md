# The Sheet With His Name On It

Three days before it goes out, at about eleven at night, in a room off a third court with two chairs in it and a table and a lamp on the branch that nobody switches on for him.

Fifty-nine days from the twenty-eighth of October to this evening: two days to the thirtieth of that October, and thirty of November, and twenty-seven days of December to today, and two and thirty and twenty-seven is fifty-nine.

Four years is three hundred and sixty days multiplied by four, which is one thousand four hundred and forty, and this evening is one thousand four hundred and six days off the first of February of the first year, and it is thirty-four days short of the first of February of year five, and thirty-four days back off that is three days of December after the twenty-seventh and the thirty of January and the first of February itself, and three and thirty and one is thirty-four.

The office has been open forty-six months and twenty-six days. Forty-six months is a thousand three hundred and eighty days, and a thousand four hundred and six less a thousand three hundred and eighty is twenty-six days, and there is no thirty-first in any month of it.

## The Two Columns

The book on the table has a strap across it and it does not have a date on the cover because a book that carries one date is a book that expects to be carried somewhere.

Two columns. The first is headed with the office's word and it has eight hundred and forty-one lines against it and it has had that figure since the thirtieth of June.

The second is headed *carried by*. It was ruled on the ninth of March of the third year, which is seven hundred and fifty-eight days off the first of February of the first year, and this evening is one thousand four hundred and six.

The difference is six hundred and forty-eight days. That is a year of three hundred and sixty, and nine months of thirty days each, which is two hundred and seventy, and eighteen days, and three hundred and sixty and two hundred and seventy and eighteen is six hundred and forty-eight.

It was empty on seven quarter-ends. The thirtieth of March, and the thirtieth of June, and the thirtieth of September of the third year, and the thirtieth of December, and the thirtieth of March and the thirtieth of June and the thirtieth of September of this year. Seven, and a quarter with eight hundred and forty-one lines in the column beside it that did nothing whatever to it.

And on the fifteenth of October, in his own hand, entered exactly as given by a clerk who may only enter, there is one line in it.

## What Is Going On The Page

He had the day's sheet in front of him and he was not permitted to improve it and neither was anybody else in this city.

*Register of Unrecorded Merit. Sixteenth quarter. Entered: eight hundred and forty-one.*

The figure has not moved for a quarter and the quarter is ninety days, and three months of thirty days each is ninety, and it has not moved now for two of those quarters and both of them are ninety and thirty and thirty and thirty is ninety.

Then the columns.

The first of them carries eight hundred and forty-one lines, and about four hundred and nineteen of those are the accounts of people who told nine strangers on a step what they had done, with an hour against every one of them and no figure on any of them anywhere. One of those four hundred and nineteen has his name at the head of it and about four hundred and eighteen of the rest are the words of other people.

The second of them carries one line, and it is his, and it is four lines long and a name, and it is at the head of one line and four hundred and eighteen other people's words are underneath it on the same page in the same hand on the same sheet.

About two hundred people a week in Cordwain read that page. He had known that number for a year and had never once been in the room they read it in.

## The Four Lines As They Will Go Out

A column headed *carried by* takes the name of the person who carries something, and it had been ruled for six hundred and forty-eight days without doing a single minute of work, and on the fifteenth of October he had put his own name in it against himself, in his own hand, entered exactly as given.

What goes out under that name, in four lines, is this.

*Entered into this Register on the first of October of the fourth year the unrecorded merit of about a million people, out of one account, and gave it back to those I found.*

*I decided who was found. I decided it in one room and there was no instrument in this city that could ask me why or stop me.*

*This office holds no money and has never held any and cannot pay this and will not be able to.*

*It is entered against me.*

Four lines and a name and a column, and three of the four conditions of his own definition are met against himself and the fourth is not, and the reason is that the thing the fourth describes is on a page in this city, and the page it is on is in a building off a fourth court and has been since the thirtieth of September of the third year.

He has read it about nine hundred times. It has not improved once and he is not permitted to improve it and neither is anybody else in this city.

## What Dread Is

Four years ago this man cleaned washrooms at a branch office because he had asked for the shift, and there was an office, and a drawer in it, and two chairs, and a page that went out at four.

For four years the sentence had been safe, and it had been safe for one reason only, which is that there was a handful of people in this city who had read it and about two hundred people a week who read everything else on the page and had never once stopped at that part of it, because that part of it is four lines with no figure against them and a city runs on figures.

In three days it goes out with a name at the head of it, and about two hundred people a week in Cordwain who have been reading that page for four years without stopping at it are going to stop at it.

That is the whole of what he is afraid of and it is not a large thing. It is that about two hundred strangers are about to find out what his good intentions weigh on a public scale, in a city where the scale is the only instrument there is.

## The One Question She Is Permitted

Wenna Raut came in at about a quarter to midnight because she comes in on the last night of a quarter's work and has come in on the last night of every quarter's work since the first of February of the first year.

She read the sheet from the top, straight down, at one speed, and she has read everything at one speed since the first of February of the first year. She squared it, put it down, and read it again the same way.

Then she asked him the question.

It is a clerk's question and there is exactly one of it in this office and she has never asked it of anything else that has come across that table in four years, and what she asked was whether the four lines in the second column were the four lines he had written.

He said yes.

"That is not me improving it," said Wenna Raut. "That is me being permitted one question about the one thing on the sheet that is in a hand a person may recognise. I have read it twice and the words were the words both times and I am entering it exactly as given."

She picked up the pen.

"There is no form in this city for asking a person to read a sheet differently," she said. "I have looked. There is a form for entering a deed and there is a form for striking an entry that was entered in error and there is a form for a person who has given something and wants it recorded as given, and not one of the three takes a sentence off a page."

## The Thing He Did Not Do

The drawer is under the book on the table and it has been open since the fourth of February of the first year and it has been opened about four times in four years and there has been nothing in it every one of those four times.

He did not open it tonight.

He stood in the corridor off the third court with his coat on for about nine minutes before he went down the stairs, and what he was doing for the nine minutes was arithmetic.

Everything he has done since the first of October of the first year goes out on one page at four o'clock in the afternoon of the thirtieth of December, in one column, under an address at the head of every line, and about two hundred people a week in Cordwain will read it before the end of the week.

He cannot hold the four hundred and eighteen back. He cannot strike his own line. He cannot ask for the sheet to be read any other way, because there is no form for asking, and because the only instrument in this building is a pen, and because the only person this office may ask is a man with a bucket who goes down a stair at half past five.

On the twenty-second of October a woman stood behind a stove in a kitchen off Ferrier Street and said that he was about the fourth thing this city had made permanent with a pen, and that she had kept the count since the second year and had never given it to anybody, and that there were three more.

He has not said thank you to her on that evening or on any of the four, and there is no form anywhere in this city for saying thank you to a person who keeps a count.

And there was one other thing he had wanted to ask in that room for about four years and had not asked, and the thing was whether the sentence on the sheet could be kept in, and the clerk answered it before he had to ask it, standing up out of the chair with the pen not in her hand.

"You are going to ask me if it has to go out," said Wenna Raut.

"Yes."

"It goes out at four on the last working day of the quarter because it has gone out at four on the last working day of every quarter since the first of February of the first year."

She put the pen down flat, which she does about twice a day.

"The reason it goes out is that a standing sheet is a thing a person may stand at a public desk and read, and that is the entire purpose of it and there has never been a second one. If a page can be read, it goes out. Everything anybody would like it to be instead is not on the form."

## The Stair

He went down the two flights of the third court at about ten past midnight and out into Cordwain with two hours of the night left in it and no work until eleven the following evening.

There is a woman on the second landing of that branch at about a quarter to eleven every night with a cloth and a bucket who has been there every night since the eleventh of October of the third year, and who stopped on that landing about a month ago and told him she had noticed something, and then went down the stair.

He had it in his mouth on the stair. It was not about the sheet and it was not about the wall. It was about a page, and what he wanted to ask was what a page is for, and she has never once in three years been asked anything by him.

He did not ask it.

That was the rest of the nine minutes. The other half of them was the number four hundred and eighteen, and the fact that none of those four hundred and eighteen is his, and that every one of them is going out on the same sheet on the same afternoon underneath his name.

## The Last Thing On It

The sheet goes out at four on the thirtieth and it will carry, under everything, in the one sentence a clerk is permitted to add, four words longer than that sentence is supposed to be.

*Where this Register has nothing, it has not been given anything, and it does not say that there was nothing.*

He did not write that. Nobody in this building wrote that. A woman typed it at a desk off a fourth court because it is the sentence the form takes and it has been on every sheet of this office since the first of February of the first year.

It is going to be the last thing on the page, under his name, under four hundred and eighteen other people's words, under eight hundred and forty-one, and about two hundred people a week are going to read it in Cordwain.

*Twenty-seventh of December of the fourth year, the room off a third court, about ten past midnight. One thousand four hundred and six days off the first of February and fifty-nine days after the twenty-eighth of October.*

*It goes out on the thirtieth at four and every line of mine goes out with it, and about four hundred and eighteen words said by other people go out underneath mine.*

*Forty-six months and twenty-six days since the first of February of the first year.*

*The column was ruled six hundred and forty-eight days ago and it has one line in it and the line is me.*

*And there is nothing in the drawer and I did not open it.*
