# A Copy With No Name On It

Thirteen days from the seventeenth of April to this morning, and this morning is one thousand five hundred and twenty-nine days off the first of February of the first year. The first of April was one thousand five hundred, and the thirtieth of April is the last day of that month, and a month is thirty days and has no thirty-first, and one thousand five hundred and twenty-nine less one thousand five hundred and sixteen is thirteen.

The board at the yard end has been up one hundred and twenty days. One thousand five hundred and twenty-nine less one thousand four hundred and nine is one hundred and twenty, and that is four months of thirty days, and the shutter went up on the evening of the thirtieth of December.

And the seventeenth sheet went out at four this morning thirty days ago, and one thousand five hundred and twenty-nine less one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine is thirty, and the figure on it is eight hundred and fifty-two and nobody has ever written down why it is eight hundred and fifty-two.

## Nine Boards

Aurel Stenner had eight hundred and something of his plate run out and about nine boards to fill and the date to do it on, and he came to the branch at about eleven in the morning on the last working day of April because he had been to two of the nine divisions and had been told a thing twice.

"They want them up," he said. "That is all. Nine divisions of the Bureau and nine boards in nine corridors and about thirty people a day walking past each one, and every division has asked me for about forty sheets since the ninth of March, and I have said yes nine times and I have not printed one."

"You printed eight hundred."

"I printed eight hundred for the district. Those were to be pinned up anywhere by anybody who wanted them up. A board in a division corridor is not anybody." He put the bundle down on the counter of a building with no sign on the door, which is a thing people do and which he did without asking.

"If a sheet goes up in a division corridor, it is in the corridor. And in about a month somebody is going to stand in that corridor and hand a piece of paper to the address on it, and then that division is where people hand pieces of paper to."

## What Adeyemi Would Not Allow

He said that last part to Yusuf Adeyemi and Adeyemi agreed with him inside four seconds and then went further than he had, and made it a rule nobody had asked him for.

"There will be no name at the top of them and no mark of this office on them," said the Registrar of Unrecorded Merit. "I will not have it. I am the Registrar of an office that has one line in a column and about four hundred and nineteen lines in another and no money, and the second I put in that column is the only sentence in this building with a name on it."

"It is already on eight hundred sheets in this district."

"It is on eight hundred sheets in this district with no name on them, because a compositor in this city struck his own name off his own plate in March and he was right and I have not found a way to be angry with him about it." Adeyemi had both hands behind his back, which is how he stands in that room.

"I am not going to be the reason a division office in this city becomes a place where people leave pieces of paper. There is no instrument in this building that can take one back out, and there is nothing anywhere in this city that would let me say to a person *this is not where you leave it*, and I have been looking for four years."

"You have no authority over a printer."

"I have no authority over a printer and I have never pretended to," said Adeyemi.

"What I have is nine divisions and a plate and a compositor who wants to be told. I am telling him. And the reason is not the office's name. It is that if a name is at the top of that sheet then every person who hands a paper in to that address in about four years is handing it to a man who wrote the rule that has just been used on them, and there is not one thing any of us can do about that afterwards."

## What Stenner Said

"So you want me to print them without his name on them."

"I want you to print what you are already printing, which is four lines and nothing else, on nine boards in nine corridors, where about thirty people a day walk past each one." Adeyemi turned round. "That is the whole of my instruction and I am giving it in a room with no sign on the door and it is not in writing and I would rather it were not."

The printer picked the bundle up.

"Nine boards," he said. "And no name. And a man who sets type for nineteen years has now been told by a Registrar and by a man with a bucket and by about nine hundred people in a yard, all in the same direction, in one month, and has not been told once by anybody who wanted a different thing."

"Go and print them."

"I am going to print them," said Aurel Stenner, "and I want it recorded that nobody made me."

## The Nine

They went up on the first of May, which is the first working day of the fourth month of the fifth year and not the thirtieth of April, because a division corridor is shut at four and the boards are put up by a man from the stores in the morning, and by eleven on the thirtieth there was not a morning left.

Nine divisions. Nine corridors. Nine sheets of the same four lines with a printer's mark in the corner and no name at the top of them and nothing above them and nothing under them.

About thirty people a day walk past each of those boards. There are nine of them and that is about two hundred and seventy a day, and about two hundred people a week read a standing sheet in Cordwain, and the two numbers are not the same people and have never been the same people and no instrument in this city has ever compared them.

## The Envelope

She came in at about half past four in the afternoon on the thirtieth of April and she was not a division and she was not a clerk and she was a woman who keeps a list and works for nobody.

She had an envelope inside her coat and she had put her hand flat on it twice on the way up the stairs, which Lin Hao knows because he was at the bottom of them.

"It came out of a boarding house in Lamp Street this morning," she said. "A woman there has been keeping a book for a year and a brother of hers has been keeping one for a fortnight and she has put the first one on the back of one of your compositor's sheets and given it to me at about eleven this morning and said *take it down there*."

"Down where."

"To a building with no sign on the door at the address that is at the head of a sheet that goes out at four." She put it on the table. "And I have walked it down here and I have not read it, because it is not mine and it was not given to me to be read by, and I have been carrying it in a coat for about five hours."

## What Is On It

Wenna Raut read it. It took her about four seconds and she read it and then she squared it, which is what she does with everything, and then she did not put it in the drawer.

She put it down in front of her and read it again at the same speed.

"That is not a deed," she said.

"It is not a deed," said Lin Hao.

"It is four columns and about a hundred and thirty-nine names in one hand and not one figure on any of them, and not one of them can produce a page, and not one of them has anything entered against them anywhere in this city, and there is not one of them that I am permitted to enter." She turned it round so that it faced the room.

"I cannot enter this. There is no form. I may only enter what I am given and I have been given a page and the page is not a thing that is on the form, and if I enter it I am improving what I am given, and if I do not enter it I am not entering what I am given, and both of those are the end of my office."

## The Drawer

The drawer is under the book on the table and it has been standing open since the fourth of February of the first year, and he has pulled it out about four times in four years and it has been empty on every one of those occasions.

He had not opened it since the twenty-seventh of December.

"There is one thing in this room that page can go in," he said, "and it is not a form and it does not make it an entry, and it is a drawer that has been open since the first year."

Wenna Raut looked at it for about nine seconds.

"That is the first time anybody has ever offered that drawer to anybody," she said. "And I have been at this counter for four years and I have known it was there the whole time and I have never once thought of it as a place to put a piece of paper, because it is not one."

"It is not one."

"Then I will put it in," said Wenna Raut, "and I am going to say it in this room first: I am putting it in a drawer and not in the book, and it is going to stay a page, and no instrument in this city is going to be able to take it out of the drawer and nothing in this building is going to be able to say it should."

She put it in and shut the drawer, which was already shut, so she shut it a second time.

## What It Is Now

About a hundred and thirty-nine names that have been chalked on a wall at the top of a hill for one hundred and twenty days, and about a hundred and thirty-nine names that have been in a book in a house in Lamp Street for a year, and about a hundred and eleven yeses in a book in the same house in a room at the back, are now one page in a drawer in a room off a third court.

There is no form in this city for entering them. There is no column in this office for them. The four conditions that were written to take in a street and a loaf of bread have just been put on nine boards in nine corridors where about two hundred and seventy people a day walk past, and there is no name at the top of any of them, and about nine in ten of the people who would put a name in that column cannot name the day.

And there is a page in a drawer.

"Tomorrow is the first of May," said Ruta Mielke.

"Yes."

"Then I will come down here tomorrow," she said, "and I will want to know whether you have looked at it, and you will want to tell me that you have not, and we will both be able to tell whether that is true, and I would like it done on the first of May because I have carried a piece of paper in a coat for about five hours and I am not going to be the only person in this city who has carried one today."

She went out at about a quarter to five.

*Thirtieth of April of year five, a room off a third court, about a quarter to five. Thirteen days since the seventeenth of April and thirty days since the seventeenth sheet and one hundred and twenty days since a woman with a piece of slate and a bit of chalk wrote four lines on a shutter in about nine minutes.*

*Nine divisions put up nine boards in nine corridors on the first of May at about two hundred and seventy people a day walking past them, with no name at the top of any of them, and not one instrument in this city can take a board down, and I could not have stopped one of them.*

*And a page with about a hundred and thirty-nine names on it went into a drawer that has been open and empty since the fourth of February of the first year, and it is the first thing that has ever gone into it, and a clerk said it out loud first and put it in and shut the drawer that was already shut.*

*And tomorrow is the first of May and she is going to come and ask me whether I have looked at it and we are both going to be able to tell.*
