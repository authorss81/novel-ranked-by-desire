# A Lane With Two Instruments In It

He went down to the lane off Cordwain at about a quarter to seven in the morning, because a lane with two instruments in it is a thing a man has to see before his shift, and because he works eleven at night until half past five and is awake at seven for the people who read at seven.

The lane runs about sixty feet between the wall end and the branch, and it has nine stools along it and a line and a slate, and a grain of chalk to a head for a figure about four inches high.

Emil Brack was at the wall end with the slate against his knee. Nine districts by the third year. The first morning of the lane is not printed here, because it is printed once at the opening of the volume's account and once at its close, and nowhere else.

## Two Instruments

At the wall end of the lane the office's hand is on the stone under the last tile, two words at the height of a name on a plate, in paint that goes over the top every month and has taken nothing with it every time. Beside it, in chalk, is a figure in Brack's hand, about four inches high, made up on purpose, with about nine others behind it he decided off nothing.

Two instruments stand in one street on one ordinary morning, one made of chalk and one made of the office's hand, and they disagree, and the man with the chalk is right about the disagreement.

Lin Hao stood at about nine feet and read both. The chalk figure changes every morning and says what it is. The painted words have not changed since the eighth of October of the first year and say what they are to nobody, because a city cannot read them. A woman at a stall beside the lane said the chalk one is the one she trusts, because it admits what it is, and she was right to say it.

He felt love for the city then, plain and entire, and it was love for the way it reads what it can read and walks under what it cannot. He wanted the two words where the city already reads every morning, and he had refused the two boxes that would have put them there, and both refusals were correct and both had cost him.

Isolde Marchetti came down the lane at about seven with a book under her arm and stopped beside him without looking up from it. She had said the one kind thing about the second column in January, and it had been about the column and not about him.

“The second column has two lines in it,” she said. “One is yours. One is a woman you never asked. The eighteenth sheet went out with her name against yours, and about four in every thousand get to the bottom. The repainting form has two boxes in it and both are still blank. That is where you are.”

“That is where I am,” he said.

## The Other End Of The Wall

She turned a page and told him, without looking up, what the branch corridor had done that week. About thirty people a day walk past the board at the end of it, and two of the thirty had asked her what the three lines are, and she had told them they are a rule with the fourth line cut off, because the fourth line is the only one that was ever about anybody but the man doing it.

“The division that prints the form will ask a third time,” she said. “They were refused a figure and refused a standing. A division refused twice stops asking and starts writing. Brack told you that at the wall end and he is right.”

Lin Hao nodded. He had worked eleven at night until half past five, four nights and three off, and the rota page carries four names and no band and no figure, and he was awake at seven in the morning for once because the lane has two instruments in it and he had to see them together.

“What will they write,” he said.

“The four lines,” Isolde said, and turned another page. “They are on nine boards with no name on them and on about eight hundred sheets with no name on them. Your definition was drafted in forty days from the ninth of March of the second year. Everybody who can read can read those four lines. That is what a division writes when it cannot have what it asked for.”

She went on down the lane toward the branch with her book under her arm, and he went up Cordwain toward the wall with his bucket in his hand, and the lane behind him had two instruments in it and neither was defeated.

The repainting form at the foot of the ranks wall carries two boxes, one of which holds a figure and one of which holds a standing, and they have both been blank since the eighth of October of the first year, and they are both still blank at the end of this morning. No hand in this city has filled either of them, and the figure box stays blank for the rest of the book.

*The eighth of August, one thousand six hundred and twenty-seven days. One thousand six hundred and twenty-seven less one thousand six hundred and nineteen, which is the thirtieth of July, is eight. One thousand six hundred and twenty-seven less one thousand five hundred and thirty, which is the first of May, is ninety-seven, which is thirty of May after the first, and thirty of June, and thirty of July, and seven of August.*

*A lane holds two instruments, one of chalk and one of paint, and the city can read one of them, and the two boxes at the foot of the repainting form are both still blank.*
