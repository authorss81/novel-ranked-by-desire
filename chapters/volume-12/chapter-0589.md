# Five Years Of A Second Of October

The two words went onto the wall on the eighth of October of the first year, which is two hundred and forty-seven days off the first of February of the first year, and one thousand six hundred and eighty-seven less two hundred and forty-seven is one thousand four hundred and forty, and three hundred and sixty multiplied by four is one thousand four hundred and forty, and this morning is the eighth of October, and the line has been up five years to the day.

There is nothing on the stone saying so. There is a date at the top of the repainting form and both boxes at the foot of it are blank, and they have been blank since the morning the line went up.

He had expected something and he did not get it, and he stood at about nine feet for about eleven minutes working out why.

## Relief

What he got was relief, and it was so strong and so immediate that he had to put his hand flat on the stone.

In five years the sentence on that wall has been painted over every month in the ordinary course of business and has taken nothing with it every time, and a man with a slate has chalked a made-up figure at each end of it and has said out loud every morning that he makes them up, and he is right about every fact he says.

A corridor board off a third court has three of four conditions on it with a drawing pin through the corner and the fourth line cut off with a blade, and about eight hundred sheets have gone out of a shop off Cordwain with no name at the top of them, and a yard off the Vestry has a rule on a shutter.

About a hundred and thirty-nine names went into a drawer that has been shut since the thirtieth of April.

And in the last week he himself has said the two words out loud under his own wall six mornings running and has put three wrong versions of them into a street.

That is what five years has been.

He had not made any of it, and he could not have stopped any of it, and the only part of it that was his own decision was the worst part and the shortest, and it took six mornings and it was over.

“You have had a face on you since you got to the wall,” said Brack.

“I did not know I had.”

“You came down here this morning to see whether anything happened and you were disappointed that it had not.”

“No,” said Lin Hao. “I was pleased. That is the part I did not know.”

Brack nodded at the slate and went back to his line, which was forming behind them, about nine people by half past six.

There is no notice on that wall and there is no form that asks for one. There was no ceremony on the first of the year, and there has not been one on any of the five anniversaries before this morning, because a ceremony is a thing a body arranges and there is no body in Ostara that has the second line on any of its papers, and it was put there by a boy with a paste brush who was not told whose premises he was reading.

## The Two Days

He stood at the wall and worked out what he had, because he had four hours before his shift and nothing to do with them, which is not a state he has been in since the first of January of the fourth year.

There are two days between the eighth and the tenth of October. A second is the smallest thing a clock cuts a day into and there is no clock in this city that will tell you what a second of October is worth; a second of a tenth of October is two days and nothing has ever turned on it.

The sitting was on the tenth of October of the year before the first. The line went up on the eighth of October of the first year, two days after the fourth anniversary of the morning the debt came due, and it has been two days ever since, and the debt came due once, on the tenth of October of the fourth year, to a woman with a sheet folded in four in her coat, and he got four words into the first of three things he had.

The fifth anniversary of the second line going up is two days before the fifth anniversary of the morning it came due, and not four days. One thousand six hundred and eighty-nine less one thousand six hundred and eighty-seven is two, and it is two by the month count as well, because ten less eight is two, and a chapter that says four has been written by somebody who did not subtract.

## What He Said To A Man With A Slate

He had not expected relief and he had not expected to be glad of it, and he found that what he actually felt was that he had been bracing for five years.

Every day since the eighth of October of the first year he had got up somewhere inside himself and checked the wall, and the check had cost him about four seconds and he had never once counted it, and it has been running in the background of a man cleaning three floors and a stairwell and a bin room for four nights in seven for five years.

Nothing came. It is the plainest fact of the morning and it is the only fact in it: nothing came, and it has stopped coming, and the reason it stopped is not virtue and not a decision. The fuse was lit once and it was lit by a woman nobody thanked and it came due once and it came due to a woman with a sheet in her coat, and a thing with no holder does not come round a second time.

Brack finished the figure and stepped back and wiped the chalk on his coat.

“I am going to say a thing to you and I want you to hear it as a fact and not as a kindness,” Lin Hao said. “There is nothing going to happen on the tenth.”

“No.”

“I am telling you now, two days early, on the fifth anniversary of the line, and I have never in my life told anybody what I was going to not do, because a thing said out loud in advance is a thing you cannot be talked out of in about four years, and that is the only reason I am saying it.”

“Why are you telling me?”

“Because you are right about everything you say and you profit nowhere a person loses,” said Lin Hao, “and in about three weeks a man in this city is going to offer me something I have had on a table since the twenty-seventh of April of the third year, and I would rather have said this first.”

He had worked that out on the stair between the tiled end and the lane end and it had taken him about eleven minutes and about nine of them had been spent on the word *offer*, because a man in a delivery coat does not offer anybody anything, and what is on the table since the twenty-seventh of April of the third year is not an offer. It is a standing.

Brack put the chalk down.

“I have written about nine figures a morning for three years,” he said, “and not one of them has been about a day something happened on. Put your two words on a wall on the eighth of October in five years and write that underneath them and it will be a lie before the paint is dry, and a city can read a lie on a wall about nine thousand times a day without ever finding out.”

*Five years exactly. One thousand six hundred and eighty-seven less two hundred and forty-seven is one thousand four hundred and forty, and three hundred and sixty multiplied by four is one thousand four hundred and forty.*

*Two days. One thousand six hundred and eighty-nine less one thousand six hundred and eighty-seven is two, and ten less eight is two.*

*He has told a man with a slate, two days early, in his own voice, that there is nothing going to happen on the tenth of October, and he has never in his life told anybody a thing he was going to not do, and the tenth of October is two days off.*
