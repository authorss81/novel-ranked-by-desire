# The Name Of The Man Who Makes Them Up

He came down to the lane on the third of December of the fifth year at a quarter to seven with frost on the rails off Cordwain and his breath showing, and the slate was out and the line was longer than usual because December mornings run late.

Ten days had gone since the twenty-third of November. One thousand seven hundred and forty-two less one thousand seven hundred and thirty-two is ten, and thirty less twenty-three with three after it is ten by the borrowing of thirty, and in the ten days the name at the foot of the standing sheet had become a thing people said to each other in streets.

He wanted to see what the name had done to the man. That want embarrassed him, and he carried it anyway, because embarrassment was cheaper than ignorance.

Brack wrote nine figures and took nine grains and answered three questions about the wait, and the men in the line looked at the slate and then at the wall behind it where the office’s hand stood in paint, and about two of nine looked back at Brack himself, which was two more than in October.

When the line cleared Brack wiped the slate and put the chalk down and faced him.

“You want to know whether I am glad,” Brack said.

“I want to know whether you are harmed,” Lin Hao said. “Every party with a real claim now stands on a standing sheet beside a man who has admitted in the same hand that he invents his, and Ostara has no instrument to tell them apart, and I am the one who gave that away.”

Brack nodded slowly.

“I am not harmed,” he said. “About forty people I asked after are still working. About eleven who asked me straight out have told others. About four who walked away in October came back in November and paid. A man who is written down as what he is cannot be harmed by being read.”

Lin Hao felt something tight in his chest loosen. He had braced for reproach or for gratitude, and Brack gave him neither, and the absence of both was a mercy he had not expected.

“You are right about every fact you say,” Lin Hao said. “You were right in July and in August and in October and you are right now. Beaten into the record is not defeated, and you know the difference, and so do I.”

“I know it,” Brack said. “I have written about nine figures a morning for three years and I will write about nine tomorrow. The sheet says I make them up and it says it in the hand of the office itself, once a quarter, with my name under it. That is the whole of what I asked for on the twenty-seventh of April of the third year, and I have it, and I will not ask for more.”

A boy at the back of the new line called out asking what the figure at the head was, and Brack turned and told him it was one of the invented ones, ringed in the book, off nothing, because the morning needed a head, and the boy paid his grain anyway and grinned.

Lin Hao watched that exchange and felt envy so plain it was almost joy. To say what a thing is, out loud, before anybody asks, and to be paid anyway.

“What will you do when the quarter turns,” he said.

“Write,” Brack said. “Nine a morning. About a quarter off nothing. Keep them in books with the date and the weather. Show them to anybody who asks. That is the whole of the trade and it has never been anything else.”

## The Ledger Minded

He went up to the rooms behind the lane after the line cleared, because Brack asked him to see the morning ruled in, and the eleven books stood on the table with a twelfth beside them, new, its spine blank.

Brack ruled nine figures into the twelfth with the date and the weather and what the line looked like, and ringed two of the nine, and wrote *off nothing, frost, line long* in the margin in plain words, and Lin Hao watched his hand move and felt the same cold admiration as in October.

“You ring them the same morning,” Lin Hao said.

“Before the chalk dust leaves my cuffs,” Brack said. “A man who waits until evening remembers himself kinder than he was.”

A landlord’s man came to the door asking for a paper with a figure on it for a court, and Brack told him a slate is not a paper a court will take and sent him to the rent board, and the man went, and Lin Hao saw the whole exchange and understood again why the opposition in this book is a rule that works and not a villain.

He walked the wall end to end before work, eleven minutes each way, and the December form was up with both boxes at the foot of it blank, one for a figure and one for a standing, blank since the eighth of October of the first year, and the sight of those two empty boxes steadied him the way cold water steadies a face.

*The third of December, one thousand seven hundred and forty-two days. Less one thousand seven hundred and thirty-two, the twenty-third of November, is ten. Thirty less twenty-three with three after it is ten by the borrowing of thirty.*

*Less the thirteenth of November, one thousand seven hundred and twenty-two, is twenty. Less the tenth of October, one thousand six hundred and eighty-nine, is fifty-three. The twentieth sheet goes out on the thirtieth of December, one thousand seven hundred and sixty-nine, and one thousand seven hundred and sixty-nine less one thousand seven hundred and forty-two is twenty-seven.*
