# He Decides Who It Is Not For

Ten days from the tenth of July to this morning. The tenth of July was one thousand five hundred and ninety-nine days off the first of February of the first year, and this morning is one thousand six hundred and nine, and one thousand six hundred and nine less one thousand five hundred and ninety-nine is ten.

The twentieth of July is twenty days into the seventh month of that year, and the seventh month has thirty days and no thirty-first, and the thirtieth of July is ten days off, because thirty less twenty is ten.

He spent the ten days the way he spends every ten days, working eleven at night until half past five at the branch off a third court, four nights and three off, cleaning three floors of washrooms and a stairwell and a bin room. The rota page in the corridor carries four names and no band and no figure: his, Nadia Ferrous's, Petra Ilyin's and Isolde Marchetti's.

## The Woman He Never Asked

On the third night Petra Ilyin came down the corridor at about two in the morning with a bundle of returns for the day room and stopped at the notice board where three of his four lines are still pinned with a drawing pin through the corner.

“You are going to put somebody in a box,” she said. It was not a question. Three of the nine sworn never live with him again and all three come in on the rota, and she is one of the three.

“I was asked whose name goes in it,” Lin Hao said. “I have carried the question for ten days.”

Petra Ilyin put the bundle down and looked at the three lines for about nine seconds. The fourth line is the one that is missing, cut off with a blade, because it says the thing it describes is not on a page anywhere in the city.

“The second column has two lines in it,” she said. “One is yours. One is a woman you never asked and never saw. The eighteenth sheet went out with her name against yours under the same heading, and about four in every thousand of the people who read one get as far as the bottom.”

“If you put her name in a box at the foot of a wall nine thousand people go under in a day, you will do to her in chalk what you did to her in ink, only worse, because chalk at the height of a name on a plate can be read by people who cannot read a sheet.”

He felt anger then, and it was anger at the shape of the choice and not at her. He wanted the standing box to hold something true, and there was nothing true that could be put in it without putting a person in it.

## Ten Days Of Carrying It

He had carried the question through ten days of nights, mopping three floors and a stairwell and a bin room, past the notice board with three lines pinned to it every night at ten and every morning at half past five. Nadia Ferrous had passed him twice in the corridor without a word, the way people pass on nights when there is nothing to say. Isolde Marchetti had read at the far end until four without looking up.

On the seventh night he had stood at the foot of the ranks wall at about two in the morning, off his route and on his own time, and had read the July form by lamplight. Both boxes blank. The paint tacky at the edges. About nine thousand people would go under it in the morning because the trams go under it, and not one of them would know what the boxes are.

He had thought about putting his own name in the standing box, and had put the thought away, because a man who entered a woman without asking her cannot fix it by entering himself beside her. He had thought about leaving the city to decide, and had put that away too, because a city that cannot read two words in four years does not decide, it walks under.

He went to the room off a third court in the morning after his shift, before the painters took the July form down, and he told Adeyemi and Wenna Raut the decision he had carried for ten days.

“The standing box is not for her,” he said. “It is not for Berta Skell. She did not write the page and she cannot read it, and she never gave me anything, and she was never asked. I entered her against myself in the second column without asking her, and that is on a sheet two hundred people a week read. I am not putting her where nine thousand people a day go under.”

Adeyemi nodded once. Wenna Raut squared the division's second sheet and put it face down on the table, the way she puts everything the office cannot use.

“What it closes,” Adeyemi said, “is the option of the second column being carried onto the wall by her name. What it costs is that the division will ask again, and the next time it asks it will ask for you.”

Lin Hao went down two flights into the third court and out into Cordwain with about four hours before his shift, and he understood that he had refused the one name the city would have accepted, and that the refusal would hold through every form this office puts up, the way the June refusal holds.

This is the decision, and it is his: the standing box is not for the woman he entered, and he has said so in a room with two chairs in it, and it cannot be unsaid.

*The twentieth of July, one thousand six hundred and nine days. Ten days from the tenth of July. He has decided who the standing box is not for, and it is not for her, and the July form comes down with both boxes still blank.*
