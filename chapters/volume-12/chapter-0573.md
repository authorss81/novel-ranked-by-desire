# The Form Wants A Number

Twenty days from the eleventh of May to this morning. The eleventh of May was one thousand five hundred and forty days off the first of February of the first year, and this morning is one thousand five hundred and sixty, and one thousand five hundred and sixty less one thousand five hundred and forty is twenty.

The first of June is the first day of the sixth month of that year, and the sixth month has thirty days and no thirty-first, and the eleventh of June is ten days off, because eleven less one is ten.

The sheet came to the room off a third court at about eleven in the morning, carried by a man from the division that prints the repainting form. It was a single sheet of the division's paper with the quarter at the top and one line under it in a clerk's hand, asking for the figure to go in the left box at the foot of the form before the quarter ends on the thirtieth of June.

## The Room Off A Third Court

Yusuf Adeyemi read the sheet twice at one speed and put it down on the table between the two chairs. Wenna Raut stood at the counter with her hands flat on it and said nothing, because a clerk may only enter what she is given and cannot improve it and cannot be asked to hurry, and a sheet asking for a figure is not a thing to be entered.

“The division prints the form,” Adeyemi said. “The division is right to ask. A box that has been blank for four years is a box somebody is going to ask about, and the quarter ends in twenty-nine days, because thirty less one is twenty-nine.”

Lin Hao stood with his coat on and his bucket by the door, because he had come in before his shift and there was nowhere else in the room to put a man who works nights. He is rank one throughout and the rota page at the branch carries four names and no band and no figure, and he works eleven at night until half past five, four nights and three off.

“The figure they want is the figure,” he said.

Adeyemi nodded. He is Registrar of Unrecorded Merit, a title he tells nobody the meaning of, and he had refused his own name at the top of nine boards on the thirtieth of April because if a name is on it then every person who hands a paper in to that address is handing it to the man who wrote the rule.

“The figure has one place in this city where it is printed,” Adeyemi said. “It is not on a public sheet. It is not on a private sheet. Nothing in this office's work prints it, and no floor under it is printed either.”

Wenna Raut squared the division's sheet and put it to one side, the way she squares everything before she reads it a second time. The drawer under the book was shut. It had been shut since the thirtieth of April, when she had put a page with about a hundred and thirty-nine names in it inside and shut it a second time.

There is no form in this city for striking a line a person entered against themselves in their own hand, and there is no form for putting a figure in a box either.

Lin Hao felt anger for the first time in about a month, and it was anger at himself and not at the division. He wanted the box to have something honest in it, because a blank box on a form a city reads every day is a question nobody has answered, and he was the man whose office owned the form.

He said the four conditions were drafted in forty days from the ninth of March of the second year and read out at the kitchen off Ferrier Street on the nineteenth of July of that year, and went out on the sheet of the thirtieth of June of that year.

The tally board at four in the morning on the thirtieth of March of the fourth year carried the request for a round and not the definition, and the thirtieth of September of the third year is when the second column went out, which is a different thing.

Adeyemi listened all the way through and did not write any of it down.

“The division asked in a room and not on a pavement,” Adeyemi said. “That is why it gets an answer in a room. If the answer were yes, I would have to find a hand to write it, and the only hand in this city that can put a sentence somewhere official is in this room, and it is not a hand that writes figures.”

Wenna Raut put her pen down flat, which she does about twice a day. The man from the division shifted his weight but did not sit, because there are two chairs in the room and neither is for a man who carries sheets.

“The reason is everything that happened before this morning,” Lin Hao said. “It is not going to be argued here.”

## What He Said About The Box

The man from the division waited by the door with his hands behind his back, the way a man waits when he has carried a sheet and is going to carry one back.

Lin Hao looked at the sheet on the table and at the book on the table and at the drawer under the book, and he said the one thing the room had been waiting for him to say since the eleventh of May.

“The figure is not going on this form.”

*The first of June, one thousand five hundred and sixty days. Twenty days from the eleventh of May. The form wants a number and the number is the figure, and the figure box stays blank.*
