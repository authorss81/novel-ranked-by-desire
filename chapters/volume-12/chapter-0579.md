# The Chalk Beside The Hand

Ten days from the twentieth of July to this morning. The twentieth of July was one thousand six hundred and nine days off the first of February of the first year, and this morning is one thousand six hundred and nineteen, and one thousand six hundred and nineteen less one thousand six hundred and nine is ten.

The thirtieth of July is the last day of the seventh month of that year, and the seventh month has thirty days and no thirty-first, and the first of August is the day after and not a day of it.

The August form went up at the foot of the ranks wall with both boxes still blank, and the painters had a new hand with them, a man in a delivery coat who chalked figures on a slate for a lane off Cordwain.

## A Grain A Head

Emil Brack was at the wall end of the lane at about a quarter to seven with a slate under his arm, writing figures in chalk about four inches high for a grain a head. Nine districts by the third year, nine stools in the lane, a line and a slate, and about nine figures he decided off nothing.

Lin Hao had not seen him since the winter, and he was right about every fact he said then and he was right about every fact he said now.

“You refused them twice,” Brack said, without looking up from the slate. “Once for the figure and once for the standing. The division told me, because a man with a slate in a lane hears everything a division says about a wall.”

“I refused the figure in June and the standing in July,” Lin Hao said. “The figure is not going on the form and the standing box is not for her.”

Brack finished a figure and stood back from the slate and looked at it the way a man looks at a thing he made up on purpose.

“The figure you would not put in is the figure,” he said. “It has one place in this city where it is printed and it is not on a public sheet. The standing you would not give them is the standing at the foot of your own wall, which no city has ever been able to read. Both refusals are correct, and both will cost you, because a division that is refused twice stops asking and starts writing.”

Lin Hao felt the particular calm a man feels when the only institution the return created tells him the truth without wanting anything for it. He wanted to ask Brack what the division would write, and he knew Brack would tell him without being asked, because Brack profits nowhere a person loses and is not cruel.

## What The Division Writes

“They will write the four lines,” Brack said. “They are on nine boards in nine corridors with no name on them, and on about eight hundred sheets with no name on them, and in four-inch chalk on a shutter with a true notice above them.”

“Your definition was drafted in forty days from the ninth of March of the second year and read out at the kitchen off Ferrier Street on the nineteenth of July, and it went out on the sheet of the thirtieth of June of that year. Everybody in this city who can read can read those four lines. Nobody in this city can read the two words at the foot of your wall.”

“A division that cannot have a figure and cannot have a standing will take the thing it can read.”

Brack picked up his slate and chalked a fresh figure beside the office's own hand at the foot of the wall, in chalk beside paint, about four inches high, at the height of a name on a plate. The chalk did not cover the paint and the paint did not cover the chalk.

About nine thousand people go under that wall in a day because it is an ordinary morning of an ordinary month and the trams go under it, and every one of them will see both.

“That is what a lane does,” Brack said. “It puts a figure where a city already looks. Your office puts a sentence where a city cannot read. Both are instruments and neither is defeated, and I am not defeated here either, because everything I have said in this lane is true.”

## The Line Forms

While they stood there the lane's line formed behind them, about nine people by half past six, each with a grain in hand. Brack took the first grain and wrote the first figure without looking down, the way a man writes who has written about nine invented figures for three years and knows exactly which ones they are.

A woman at the head of the line asked Brack whether the figure beside the paint was one of his, and Brack said it was, and said out loud that he makes them up, the way he has said since the twenty-seventh of April of the third year to anybody who asks. Nobody in the line moved away. Two of them laughed, the way people laugh when a man tells them the truth about a figure before they pay.

Lin Hao watched the chalk go up and the paint stay where it was, and he felt the calm go out of him and anger come back, not at Brack but at the wall. He wanted the two words legible, and he had refused the only two boxes that could have made them legible, and both refusals were correct.

He said so to Brack, standing at the wall end with the line behind them, because Brack is the one man in the city who can hear that sentence without using it.

“I know,” Brack said. “That is why I chalk beside it and not over it.”

Lin Hao went to his shift at eleven that night with chalk dust on his coat and paint on his hands, and he understood that the wall now carries two instruments side by side, and that the city can read one of them.

*The thirtieth of July, one thousand six hundred and nineteen days. Ten days from the twentieth of July. A man in a delivery coat has chalked a figure beside the office's hand at the foot of the wall, and he is right about every fact he says, and both boxes on the repainting form are still blank.*
