# The Cost Of Four Conditions

Five days from the sixteenth of January to this morning. This morning is one thousand four hundred and thirty days off the first of February of the first year.

A year runs from the first of February to the thirtieth of January and four years is three hundred and sixty days multiplied by four, which is one thousand four hundred and forty, and the first of February of year five is one thousand four hundred and forty. This morning is nine days short of it, and one thousand four hundred and forty less nine is one thousand four hundred and thirty, and the sixteenth of January is one thousand four hundred and twenty-five, and thirty less twenty-five is five.

He went down Cordwain at about ten in the morning to the rent board off it — a table, three chairs, a stove, and a window shut and painted years ago — and Corvin Ashby was sitting on one side of the table with a sheet of paper and a pen and did not get up.

"I want to show you something and then I want you to go," said Ashby. "You have about four minutes and I have got a woman coming at eleven and she is going to be told something she cannot read and that is not her fault and it is not mine either and it is going to happen in about a fortnight."

## The Demand

He turned the sheet round and pushed it across the table.

It was a demand. It is a thing a court in this city will take, and it is a thing a landlord can put on a step, and it is four lines long and a figure and a date and a room number.

The four lines were not his.

They were the four lines off the board at the yard end, in the yard's hand, in four-inch chalk, copied onto a sheet of paper that was going to be read by one woman who cannot read.

*It changed what a person held. It happened on a day the person can name and in a place the person can name. Another person was there, or was given it, and that person can be named or described closely enough. And it is not on a page anywhere in this city.*

"Somebody up that hill handed you that," said Lin Hao.

"A woman at a stall wrote them out for me on the ninth of January for nothing and would not take anything for it," said Corvin Ashby. "She said anybody could have them and I said I wanted all four and she said all four then."

## What He Is Doing With It

"A demand is the only piece of paper in this city that a landlord is allowed to put in front of a magistrate," said Ashby. "Not a lease. A lease is a contract between two people who have both signed it and a demand is a thing you put in front of a third person and make him answer. So a demand is where you put the things you cannot put anywhere else."

"And what I cannot put anywhere else is this. Nine years I have had a woman in number four who cannot read a lease she signed, or a demand, or a finding, and who adds up her money better than any clerk I have ever paid, and who does it on her fingers at the end of every month and has known she was short before I put a hand to her door."

"And every landlord in this district has been telling me for nine years that I have no case, because she has not refused to pay. She cannot read what she is being told she owes, and that is not the same as not paying, and there is no instrument in this city that can tell the difference."

He put his finger on the last of the four lines.

"So I took four lines off a board that eleven thousand people have read, and I put them in a demand, and in about four years somebody is going to stand up in a room and say: is there a page? And there is not. And that woman is going to be found to have never proved anything."

He sat back.

"That is not me being clever. That is me being the only kind of person anybody in this city has left to me, which is a man who can put a piece of paper in front of a third person."

## What He Could Have Done

There was not one instrument in this city that could stop a demand.

He had said that to himself about four hundred times in four years and he had never once said it to a person who was about to use it. He had no standing in a room off Cordwain, no counterparty anybody could be made to accept, no schedule and no fund and no hearing and no seal, and the office he is the whole instrument of has no money and has never claimed any and cannot be sued and cannot be compelled.

He could have written to the board. The board does not take letters.

He could have asked somebody at the Vestry to stand on a step and say four sentences. There is a yard of about eleven thousand people with a board on it and a woman in that yard who has been unable to stand in front of them since the first of October of the third year, and neither of those two facts is a thing he may use.

He could have taken the demand and torn it, and a torn demand in a landlord's hand is a second demand in a week and Corvin Ashby has let six rooms over Lamplit Row since the third year and every one of them has a different man behind it.

One hundred and ninety days.

That is one thousand four hundred and thirty less one thousand two hundred and forty, and the eleventh of July of the fourth year is one thousand two hundred and forty, and it is the morning a satchel went on a counter at a quarter to ten and about nine hundred pages came out of it. A hundred and ninety days is six months of thirty days each, which is a hundred and eighty, and ten days, and a hundred and eighty and ten is a hundred and ninety.

On that morning he worked out — standing on the top step of his own branch stair at four in the morning, with a bin-room tally board across his knees — that the fourth of his four lines had killed itself, because the thing it describes had gone onto a page. He wrote it down. He has known it every day since.

And he had not done one thing about it, and he had not told anybody, and he had spent a hundred and ninety days choosing not to.

## What It Costs

The fourth condition is not being enforced by anybody in this room.

Nobody is quoting it at him. Nobody is using it as a threat. It is sitting in the fourth line of a piece of paper on a landlord's table, in chalk four inches high on a shutter at the top of a hill, in a corridor off a third court with a drawing pin through the corner of it, and in a demand, and it will be in a room with a magistrate in about four years.

The cheapest enforcement there is: the absence of a form.

Nobody has to act. A definition sits on a page and is applied by whoever picks it up, and a definition that was written in one room for one counter by a man at rank one with a bucket is now the commonest sentence in this city about what counts as having been owed something, and every person the round found in ninety days at about nine hundred doors has lost it for ever.

There is no instrument anywhere in Ostara that can bring a person back inside a definition. That is not a figure about this city. It is a sentence about how sentences work.

## The Woman Of Number Four

She did not come, because she cannot come, because she cannot read what she would be told.

Her sister sat where she had sat in the third year and had brought the old lease, which is a second piece of paper, and the table could not weigh two pieces of paper, and a table that cannot weigh them takes either one.

"She will be told," said the sister.

"I will walk her home from my mother's one evening and I will tell her that she is the sort of tenant a room off Cordwain will not have again. And she will take it, because I have never once given her a false thing. What she cannot do is ask me where in it I got my evidence."

"And then she will go on counting on her fingers at the end of every month for four more years, and it will come out the same every month, and she will not know that it is the same."

She put the old lease down.

"You have been in this city for four years," she said. "Do you know a thing you can do about it."

"Yes," said Lin Hao. "I know exactly a thing I can do about it and I am not going to do it and I would like to say why out loud in a room with one person in it who cannot read, because there are three people in this city who would understand the reason and all three of them are not in this room."

He did not say it. It took him about nine seconds to decide and he decided in the nine seconds.

"A person who cannot produce a deed has not proved anything," he said. "That is the fourth of the four and it is the whole of them, and it is the rule this city has run on since the Bureau was started. And I wrote it. Forty days, in a kitchen off Ferrier Street, in the second year."

"And I am not going to be the one man in this city who gets to hold himself outside a rule he spent forty days writing because he found out that the rule is convenient."

Corvin Ashby looked at him for about four seconds.

"That," he said, "is the stupidest thing anybody has said in this room in about nine years and I am going to have to think about it for the rest of the day."

He picked up the demand and squared it.

"You are going," he said.

## The Thirtieth

He was in the arcade at about a quarter to five that afternoon when the woman who keeps the list came past with a bundle of slate under her arm, and she stopped, which she does not do.

"There is going to be a yard full on the thirtieth," she said. "Not the nine hundred. All of them — about eleven thousand, and they will all be there in daylight, which they have not been since the first of October of the third year. Somebody up there has told them there is going to be a man from the office at the yard end on the thirtieth and a woman is going to ask him whether those four lines are his."

"And you believe I will come."

"I believe you will not be able to not come," said the woman who keeps the list. "That is different and it is worse, and I have known you for four days."

*Twenty-first of January of year five, one thousand four hundred and thirty days. Five days from the sixteenth. The rent board off Cordwain, and the same table and the same three chairs and the same stove as three years ago.*

*A demand with four lines off a board chalked on it, and the fourth one says the thing is not on a page anywhere in this city, and the demand is a page, and I wrote it.*

*A hundred and ninety days since the morning I worked out that I had killed it myself, and I have spent every one of those days choosing not to do anything.*

*And I did not stop it, and the reason is not the office and it is not the definition, and it is that I am not going to be the only man in this city who is not held to a rule I wrote.*

*And on the thirtieth there is going to be the whole yard at the yard end and somebody is going to ask me whether those are my lines.*
