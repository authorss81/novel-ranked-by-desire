# A Landlord With A Condition In It

Four days from the thirtieth of January to this morning. The thirtieth was one thousand four hundred and thirty-nine days off the first of February of the first year and this morning is one thousand four hundred and forty-three, and one thousand four hundred and forty-three less one thousand four hundred and thirty-nine is four.

There is no thirty-first. A month is thirty days and the first of February is one thousand four hundred and forty, which is four years of three hundred and sixty, and the second is one thousand four hundred and forty-one, and the third is one thousand four hundred and forty-two, and this is the fourth.

Twenty-one days ago a yard of about eleven thousand people decided in about four minutes that a board was a rule and not a notice. One thousand four hundred and forty-three less one thousand four hundred and twenty-two is twenty-one.

Thirty-four days ago the board went up. One thousand four hundred and forty-three less one thousand four hundred and nine is thirty-four, and one thousand four hundred and nine was the thirtieth of December, which is the afternoon a sheet went out at four.

And fifty-six days from this morning to the thirtieth of March, which is one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine, and one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine less one thousand four hundred and forty-three is fifty-six, and the sheet that goes out on that morning is the seventeenth.

## The Table

At about ten in the morning he was in the room where the board sits, which has three chairs and a stove and a window that has been painted shut since before either of them came, and Corvin Ashby was at the table in his coat.

The demand was on the table with a second sheet on top of it, and the second sheet had been folded once and unfolded and folded again, which is what a piece of paper does when one person has carried it somewhere and thought about it on the way back.

"Sit down," said Ashby. "It has been thirteen days since you were in this room and something has happened, and I am going to tell you about it in about four sentences, because I have got a woman coming at eleven."

## Four Sentences

"I took the demand to a printer off Cordwain on the twenty-second of January because demands are printed things and mine is not a special one."

"He does handbills and he does the quarterlies for trades and he did four hundred of them for me, which is more than I wanted, because I asked him what he could do and he said four hundred was what the plate does and that the price is the same at four as it is at one."

"That is the first sentence and it is the important one and I have not been careful and I am not going to be now that I know it costs."

"And then three of them are not in this room."

## Three He Gave Away

"You gave them away."

"I gave one to a man who lets four rooms two streets off the arcade, and one to a woman who keeps a boarding house at the top of Cordwain and has about thirty people in it, and one to the man who shuts nine shutters off that arcade, who is a fool and has been for eleven years and who asked me on the second of January what a demand was for."

Ashby put his thumb on the folded sheet.

"That is the whole of it. Four hundred printed, and I have one, and the other three are three places in this city where I have no idea where they are, and I did not ask, because asking where a thing has gone is careful and I am not careful."

"You did not ask."

"I asked a man what a demand was for and I gave it to him," said Corvin Ashby. "A landlord in this city does not keep a good instrument to himself. That is not modesty. It is that the day I need it I do not know who has taken it, and by then it does not matter, because by then a demand is out."

## What A Copy Is

"A lease binds two people and no one else," said Ashby. "A demand is the only thing this trade can put in front of a third person and make him answer, and that is the whole of why a landlord owns one instead of a conversation."

"So you have put a good instrument in front of a third person three more times and you do not know where any of it is."

"I have put a printed rule in front of a third person four hundred times and I know where all four hundred of those are, because they are nailed to four hundred doors," said Ashby. "The three you are asking me about are the three that will be used."

He turned his head about nine degrees.

"You told about eleven thousand people in a yard at half past eleven on the thirtieth that those four lines are yours. I read that in a doorway on the second of February with six other people. I did not go up a hill to be told anything."

## The Sister Came

The woman of number four was not in the room and had not been in it since the first of January of the first year, and the reason is that she cannot read, and her sister sat where she sat.

She had the old lease and she had put it on the table and Corvin Ashby had not touched it, and the two pieces of paper were on a table that could not weigh them.

"She has been told," said the sister.

"You said that in the third year."

"I have said it twice now and I am saying it a third time and I am not going to say it differently." She turned the lease over so that the printed side was down, which is a thing a person does when they want a table to see a sheet and not a person.

"I told her in the evening I walked her home from my mother's that a room off Cordwain has said she is a woman who does not pay. She believed me. She has never once doubted a thing I have told her and she did not doubt that one."

"And what did she do."

"She did the sum on her fingers," said the sister. "At the end of it she did it again, because that is what she does at the end of every month, and it came out the same, and she put her hands in her lap."

## What He Says

He looked at the lease for about nine seconds and then he said the thing out loud, and he said it to Ashby and not to the sister, because she could not use it and he would not do that to her on purpose.

"A person who cannot produce a deed has not proved anything."

"That is not my sentence," said Corvin Ashby. "That is yours and you have said it to me in this room before."

"I know it is mine. It is the fourth of four and it is the whole of them and I wrote it in forty days in the second year." He put his hand flat on the table on the blank space beside the lease, which was not the hand-flat-on-a-table he does everywhere, and there was nothing to be done about it.

"And you have printed it four hundred times and given three copies to strangers, and the fourth of them is going to do exactly what I built it to do."

"You built it to keep things out of a room," said Corvin Ashby. "I have never heard a description of it that was less dignified than that and I am not going to pretend I have."

## The Price Of Not Being Careful

"There is one more thing and then you can go," said Ashby. "You want to know how long this runs for and nobody will tell you, so I will tell you, because I am the only man in this city who has got it in front of him and I am not careful."

"How long."

"A year here is three hundred and sixty days, four of them are three hundred and sixty multiplied by four, which comes to one thousand four hundred and forty, and that is what a finding does. It does not put a woman out of a room in a fortnight. It puts her name in a book, and the book goes to every landlord who uses this board, and in four years there is not one room on this street she can take."

"And you have told her that already."

"I have told her sister that, once, in the third year, and the sister has told her that she is a woman who does not pay, and the two of those are not the same and the difference is about four years." He squared the folded sheet.

"You have written a rule that a person who cannot show a page has not proved anything and you have put it in front of a third person and it is going to work, and it is going to work on her, and you came down here to tell me it would and I have watched you not tell me."

## The End Of It

The woman of number four's sister picked the old lease up off the table and put it inside her coat, and she did it without looking at it.

"There is nothing you can do," she said, to nobody in the room in particular.

"There is nothing I can do," said Lin Hao.

"That is the first true thing anybody from that office has said in this room," she said, "and I would rather it had been a lie."

Corvin Ashby got up at about a quarter to eleven and put the folded sheet in his coat, and he did not offer him the two rooms over the shop at the top of the row, which he has offered twice.

Then he said it from the doorway, with his hand on the frame, in the voice of a man thinking out loud about a shoe.

"You have been four days thinking about a demand," he said. "It has been in this city twelve days and it is on four hundred doors and I have had one conversation about it in a yard and about eleven hundred of them have had a conversation about it with each other."

He worked it out on his fingers, which he can do.

"Twelve days, because I gave him the copy on the twenty-second of January, and this is the fourth of February, and there is no thirty-first, so you cannot count it by months at all. You count it by the sheet. Eight days to the thirtieth of January, and then one more, because the first of February is the day after the thirtieth and not a day of it, and then three more days of this month, and eight and one and three is twelve."

"You are counting in front of me on purpose."

"I am counting out loud because you are standing there doing sums and it is about the only entertainment this room has had in nine years," said Corvin Ashby.

"The point is not the figure. The point is that you are behind and you have been behind since the sheet went out, and I am going to have four hundred of these in this district by the middle of the month, and every single one of them is a conversation I do not have to have with a man in a coat."

*Fourth of February of year five, the rent board off Cordwain, ten in the morning. Four days after the yard, and twenty-one days after a yard decided a board was a rule, and thirty-four days after the board went up.*

*And the demand is printed, four hundred of them, because a man asked a printer what he could do and the printer said four hundred was what the plate does and the price is the same at four as it is at one.*

*Three copies of a good instrument are in three places in this city and I cannot find one of them and I am not going to be able to.*

*And in four years there is not one room on that street a woman who cannot read can take, and the fourth of my four lines is what will do it, and I came down there to tell a landlord he was right and he let me.*

*Fifty-six days to the seventeenth sheet. One thousand four hundred and ninety-nine less one thousand four hundred and forty-three.*
