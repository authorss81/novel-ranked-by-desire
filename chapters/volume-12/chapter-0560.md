# He Says Yes Out Loud

Nine days from the twenty-first of January to this morning. This morning is one thousand four hundred and thirty-nine days off the first of February of the first year, and the twenty-first was one thousand four hundred and thirty, and nine off thirty is thirty-nine and a thousand is a thousand four hundred and thirty-nine.

Thirty days since the sheet went out. The thirtieth of December was one thousand four hundred and nine, and one thousand four hundred and thirty-nine less one thousand four hundred and nine is thirty, and thirty days is the whole of January, and a month is thirty days and has no thirty-first.

Twenty-eight days since a woman two floors under a sales floor shut a page and asked him a question she had carried for eleven years and then did not get an answer to.

## The Yard End

There were about eleven thousand people in that yard in daylight, which they had not been since the first of October of the third year.

The Vestry door was shut and there was a man standing beside it who had been standing beside it for four years, and he was not the chaplain and he was not going to be asked anything, and nobody in that yard looked at the door at all. She was not there and he was not going to be asked whether she was and he did not ask.

The shutter was still up at the yard end with the four lines in four-inch chalk and a true notice at the top of them naming the quarter and the thirtieth of December and the Bureau, and about nine hundred of the eleven thousand could read all of it and the rest could not read any of it, and both of those groups had understood the same thing.

He had a plate in his coat with his name on it and no figure cut into it anywhere and no bucket, because he was not on a shift. He came up Cordwain at about eleven in the morning on foot with his hands in his pockets and stood about nine feet off the board with his back to the wall, and he did not get any further than that.

Thirty days is the whole of the month the sheet has been up, and the figure at the head of it has not moved once in thirty days. A quarter in this office is three months of thirty days, which is ninety, and thirty is not ninety and there are two of them still to come before anybody can say the number has held.

## The Question

She came out of the crowd at about twenty past eleven and she was not the chaplain and she had never seen him before and she did not say so.

She had a slate under her arm the size of a shutter board and about nine words on it in the yard's own hand, and she came to about six feet and stopped.

"You are the man they sent for."

"They told you somebody was coming."

"They told us the man from the office at the yard end." She looked at the plate and then at his face. "Are the four lines on that board the ones you wrote."

And there it was, the thing he had been carrying the whole length of Cordwain, and it was a courtesy, and it was the trap underneath the courtesy, and it had been built that way on purpose by somebody who understood what a courtesy does.

Because there were about eleven thousand ways to answer it.

He could have said the office did not write them. That is true and it would have taken about four seconds and about four hundred of them would have believed it and the four conditions would have gone on that wall exactly as they were, and the yard would have had a rule with no author on it, which is the one kind of rule that can never be argued with in any room, because there is nobody in it to answer.

He could have said he had read them. Also true.

He could have said nothing and let somebody else answer and there are about eleven thousand people in this yard who would have answered for him inside a minute, and some of them would have said what he wanted said.

"Yes," said Lin Hao.

Out loud. In his own voice. About nine feet off a shutter, to a woman he had never met, with about eleven thousand people in a yard in daylight.

"I wrote them," he said. "In forty days in the second year, and I read them out at my own kitchen on the nineteenth of July. They went out on a public page on the thirtieth of June of the second year, and the one that went out on the thirtieth of December had my name at the head of the second column. I am the man whose name is on that page."

He stopped there.

He had the rest of it ready and it was the true rest. It was the day the yard decided those four lines were a rule and the fourth of them stopped being any use to anybody at all, and he did not say it.

A man who explains the limits of his own sentence to a crowd is making a speech, and he was not going to make a speech on a morning when he had already said the one word that cannot be taken back.

The woman with the slate wrote something on it with a bit of chalk. She did not write *yes*. She wrote *he said so*, and then underneath it, *in front of us*, and then she turned round and held it up, and about eleven thousand people read two words and a line at about twenty-five past eleven in the morning.

Nobody said anything at all. Not one person in that yard agreed with him and not one person said anything about what he had done, and about four thousand of them went back to the middle of the yard to wait for something else.

## What She Asked Next

It was Ruta Mielke that came out from under the arcade at about half past eleven and she did not come to him.

She came to about six feet off, the way the other one had, and she stood with her back to a post and she had her hands in the front of her coat.

"That is the right answer," she said. "I want that said before anything else. You could have said the office wrote it and nobody in this yard would have been able to argue with it and about nine hundred of us would have gone home this morning."

"I wrote it."

"You said you wrote it." She turned her head about nine degrees. "Now I am going to ask you the only question I have, and I have had it since about the eleventh of January, and I am asking it in front of these people on purpose."

"On the sixteenth of January I stood on those steps and I said that about nine in ten of what a person walks into a room with cannot name the day. Nine in ten. Every time anybody has ever measured it. And about ninety people have put something at the foot of that board since then and about nine of them can do all four of the things on it."

"What are you going to do about the other eighty-one."

He had that ready too. He had four things ready and three of them were true and one of them was a sentence.

He looked at about eleven thousand people in a yard at about half past eleven in the morning on the thirtieth of January of the fifth year.

There is not one instrument in this city that can put a person back inside a definition. A magistrate's room in about four years will be the only place on earth where his four lines have ever been applied by anybody with the power to make them stick.

And what he says next will be printed on about four hundred sheets in this city by the middle of next month whether it is true or not.

"Nothing," said Lin Hao.

She waited for the rest of it, because a person says nothing in this city either because they have nothing or because they have something, and she wanted to know which.

"Nothing," he said again.

"I have no instrument, I have no form, I have no money and I have no second person. I wrote a rule in one room in about forty days that was always going to leave out about nine in ten of what people walk in with, and I knew that when I wrote it. I have known it since the nineteenth of July of the second year, and I am not going to stand here and tell eleven thousand people that I did not know."

Nobody in that yard said that they had not known either. Nobody said anything about his sentence being narrow, or about the four conditions, or about the first of October. It was half past eleven on the last day of January and about eleven thousand people were standing in a yard in daylight waiting for something to happen and nothing happened.

"You could have said you were sorry," said Ruta Mielke.

"I could have," said Lin Hao, "and I have been sorry about about nine things for four years and being sorry is the only instrument this city has left for a man in my position and it has never once moved a line on a page."

"You are not a position," she said. "You are a man with a bucket and you are the only reason that sentence is worth saying."

And she turned round and went back under the arcade and did not say whether that had been a compliment and was not going to say it, and he did not ask.

Not one person in that yard said a good word to him on the way down the hill, and he had not been waiting for one, and wanting one was not among the things in him.

## About A Hundred Names

He came off the wall at about twenty to twelve.

The board was still up. The four lines in four-inch chalk, and the true notice above them, and underneath the fourth line somebody had begun a column.

Not the two words. A column — a ruled line down the left of the board and then names, one under another, in the yard's own chalk, going down and down past the bottom of the shutter and on to the wall beside it.

About a hundred of them by the time he got to the bottom of the hill.

Every one of them had brought something to the foot of that shutter on a day they could not name, from a person they could not describe, on a street they had moved out of.

Not one of them will be taken by any of the four conditions. Not one of them will ever be taken by any of the four conditions.

The fourth one is not on the board at all, because somebody cut it off with a blade and put a drawing pin through the corner of it in a corridor off a third court. The first three of them will stand in a room in about four years with a magistrate in it, and not one of these hundred names will be in that room.

He came off that hill at about five to twelve on the thirtieth of January of the fifth year and he did not look back once on the way down.

The office is a counter off a third court with a drawer under it that has stood open and empty since the fourth of February of the first year. There is nothing in that drawer. There is not going to be anything in it.

*Thirtieth of January of year five. Nine days from the twenty-first. Thirty days since the sheet went out and twenty-eight days since a woman asked me a question in a counting room that I could not answer and am not going to be able to answer.*

*I said yes out loud in front of about eleven thousand people and it cannot be unsaid and it was not a speech and nobody agreed with me and nobody disagreed.*

*And she asked me what I was going to do about the eighty-one and the answer is nothing, and it was the true answer, and it is the only sentence in four years that I have said out loud that I cannot improve afterwards.*

*And the board is still up and there is a column under the four lines with about a hundred names in it, and not one of them will be taken by any of the four things I wrote, and a thing said out loud in a yard at half past eleven in the morning is a thing that happened in this city for ever.*
