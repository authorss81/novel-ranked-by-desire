# Two Boxes At The Foot Of It

Ten days from the first of May to this morning. The first of May was one thousand five hundred and thirty days off the first of February of the first year, and this morning is one thousand five hundred and forty, and one thousand five hundred and forty less one thousand five hundred and thirty is ten.

The eleventh of May is ten days into the fifth month of that year, and the fifth month has thirty days and no thirty-first, and the first of June is twenty days off, because thirty less ten is twenty.

He went to the division that had asked, because a question put to a man who puts up a form is a question put to the office whose form it is, and the office is a room off a third court with two chairs and a clerk who may only enter what she is given.

## A Room With Returns In It

The division keeps its returns in a room with a counter in it and a board at the end of the corridor where the nine sheets went up on the first of May. About thirty people a day walk past that board. None of them had stopped that morning.

The returns clerk was a woman he had never met, with a pen behind her ear and a bundle of weekly returns squared off on the counter the way Wenna Raut squares a sheet before she reads it. She did not ask him his name. The white plate in his coat said it.

“We asked the painters,” she said. “They said you said the left one is a figure and the right one is a standing. We want it on a sheet that we can keep, because we print this form every month and nobody has ever told us what the two at the bottom are.”

Lin Hao stood at about nine feet from the board at the end of her corridor and read his own four lines there. They were the same four lines in all nine divisions, in a clean even hand with nothing at the top and nothing under it and no mark of his office anywhere on them. He had read one of them at thirty feet on the first of May and it had taken about eleven seconds.

“The form goes up with every repainting,” he said. “The wall is repainted every month in the ordinary course of business and it has taken nothing with it every time. The form goes up beside the last tile and comes down when the paint is dry.”

“We know when it goes up,” the clerk said. “We print it. What we do not know is what the two boxes are.”

He told her, because she had asked in a room and not on a pavement, and because a question in a room is a thing a man can answer without it going into a book.

The left box holds the figure of the thing at the address. The right box holds the standing of it. Both have been blank since the eighth of October of the first year.

On that morning a clerk was handed a sheet and a form and no instruction, and she typed two words into the standing box because the box was there and the words were there, and a boy painted what was in the box. The two words have been on the stone ever since at the height of a name on a plate, in the office's own hand, under the last tile.

The clerk wrote it down on the back of a returns sheet in her own hand, in about nine seconds, and read it back to him to check it.

“And there is no sheet anywhere that says this,” she said.

“There is no sheet anywhere that says it,” Lin Hao said. “There is the wall, and there is the form, and there is the fact that both boxes have been blank for four years.”

He felt the particular dislike a man feels when his own sentence is being written down by somebody who is right to write it. He wanted her to have the words right, because if the division that prints the form gets the boxes wrong then every wall in the city gets them wrong with it.

## The Nine Sheets

She took him down the corridor to the board at the end of it, because she wanted him to see where his four lines sit in a division corridor. The sheet was up at the height of a name on a plate, pinned at one corner with a drawing pin, in a clean even hand with nothing at the top and nothing under it.

About thirty people a day walk past that board, and about two hundred and seventy a day walk past the nine of them. He had worked out in about four seconds on the first of May why each of the nine is right, and not one of the nine is about him, and he had not asked any of the nine for anything.

“Do your nine stop at it,” he said.

“About nine of the thirty stop,” the clerk said. “The rest go past to the counter. The nine who stop read all four lines. About half of them can read all four. The fourth one is the one they read twice.”

Lin Hao nodded. The fourth line says the thing it describes is not on a page anywhere in the city, and a man who cannot show a page has not proved anything. He had spent a hundred and ninety days choosing not to do anything about that, from the eleventh of July of the fourth year to the twenty-first of January, and the days since had not changed it.

“What will you do if the figure box stays blank,” the clerk said.

He did not answer that either, because the answer belongs to the first of June and not to the eleventh of May. He said the four conditions were drafted in forty days from the ninth of March of the second year and read out at the kitchen off Ferrier Street on the nineteenth of July, and that they went out on the sheet of the thirtieth of June of that year, so that she would have the provenance right on the back of her returns sheet.

The clerk wrote that down too, in the same hand, and read it back, and he said it was right.

## What She Wanted Next

She squared the returns bundle and put her pen down and looked at the board at the end of the corridor and then at him.

“If the left one holds a figure,” she said, “then this quarter's form ought to have one in it. The quarter ends on the thirtieth of June. That is fifty days off, because twenty days of May and thirty of June is fifty. We have never once put anything in it.”

Lin Hao did not say yes and did not say no. He said the eighteenth sheet goes out at four on the thirtieth of June with his name on it and one other name against his under the same heading, and that about two hundred people a week read one and about four in every thousand of them get as far as the bottom, and that the figure at the top of that sheet has stood at eight hundred and fifty-two since the thirtieth of March.

The clerk nodded as if he had answered her, which he had not. She said the division would send a sheet to the room off a third court before the quarter ends, asking for the figure in writing, because a division does not put a thing in a box on a man's word in a corridor.

He walked back down Cordwain in daylight with the four lines behind him in nine hands and the two boxes in front of him still blank, and he understood that a repainting that comes round every month had become a thing with a date on it, and the date was the thirtieth of June.

*The eleventh of May, one thousand five hundred and forty days. Ten days from the first of May. The division that prints the repainting form has asked what the two boxes at the foot of it are, and he has told them: the left one holds a figure and the right one holds a standing, both blank since the eighth of October of the first year. The division will send a sheet asking for the figure in writing before the thirtieth of June.*
