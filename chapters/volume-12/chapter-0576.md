# A Figure That Did Not Move

A bundle was squared off on the table by midday and the eighteenth sheet was the top of it, and it went out at four that afternoon.

The thirteenth sheet heads itself the thirteenth and the fifteenth quarter is the fifteenth and the sixteenth went out on the thirtieth of December and the seventeenth on the thirtieth of March, and this is the eighteenth, and a quarter is three months of thirty days and a year is three hundred and sixty, and there is no thirty-first in any month of this city.

Wenna Raut had squared eleven sheets, every one of them written out on the back of a printed sheet and sent to the address at the head of a public page. Three of them were the three her division had sent on the twenty-first, entered exactly as given. Eight were other people's words, with an hour against each and no figure on any of them.

## The Eighteenth Sheet

Yusuf Adeyemi stood at the end of the table and read the figure at the top without touching the sheet.

“Eight hundred and fifty-two,” he said. “It went to eight hundred and fifty-two on the thirtieth of March, for the first time in four quarters, and it has not moved since.”

Lin Hao stood by the door with his coat on. He had worked eleven at night until half past five, four nights and three off, and the rota page at the branch carries four names and no band and no figure, and he had come in daylight because the quarter ends at four and there is no second time for it.

“The three move it by three and the eight move it by nothing,” Wenna Raut said. “A clerk may only enter what she is given. There is no column on the sheet, in the building, or in this city that says which meaning of the word at the top a person is supposed to read. That is why nobody in this office can say why it moved in March and why nobody can say why it does not move now.”

She read the second column before she read the figure, the way she has read it every quarter since the ninth of March of the third year. It was ruled on that date and it was empty on seven quarter-ends and it has had one line in it since the fifteenth of October of the fourth year, and the line is his. Now it has two.

The second line is hers. Berta Skell, carried by Lin Hao, rank one, in four lines written exactly in the shape of the four lines he entered against himself. She did not write the page and she cannot read it. A woman she has never met wrote the hundred and thirty-nine names in her own hand in one night in Lamp Street. She never gave him anything and she was never asked, and she is going to find out from a stranger at a desk in Cordwain.

He felt grief when he saw the two lines together under the same heading, and the grief was not for himself. He wanted the sheet to go out without correcting anything, because getting the sheet out without correcting anything was the whole of his immediate goal, and he had learned in December that if a page can be read, it goes out.

## The Hour Before Four

At about three the room filled with the particular quiet a room gets before a quarter ends. Wenna Raut read the sheet a third time, because she reads everything twice and the third time was for the two lines in the second column, which she had never read three times before.

“The thirtieth of December had one line,” she said. “The thirtieth of March had one line. This one has two. The figure went to eight hundred and fifty-two in March and it does not move now. A quarter is three months of thirty days, and three months of thirty days is ninety, and ninety days have gone past since the seventeenth.”

Adeyemi put his hands behind his back and looked at the drawer under the book, which has had one page in it since the thirtieth of April with about a hundred and thirty-nine names on it, and which is not an entry.

“The drawer stays shut,” he said. “The sheet goes out. The form at the foot of the wall stays blank. Those are three different things and all three are true.”

Lin Hao stood with his bucket by the door and his coat on, and he felt the deadline the way a man on nights feels a four o'clock he cannot move. He wanted to be at the branch by eleven, and he wanted the sheet out before that, and both wants were about to be answered in order.

## At Four

At four the sheet went out. About two hundred people a week in Cordwain read one, and about four in every thousand of them get as far as the bottom, and the bottom is where the second column is.

The office's own sentence is at the foot of the sheet and he did not write it. The figure at the top stands at eight hundred and fifty-two and it did not move. That is the quarter.

He stood at the foot of the ranks wall at about ten to five while about nine thousand people went under it, because it is an ordinary evening of an ordinary month and the trams go under it, and the repainting form was up beside the last tile with both boxes still blank.

This is what the first of May cost, and it went out at four with everything else on the sheet: his name against hers on the same page under the same heading, in a column with no rule in it saying a man may not do that, in a city that reads every morning.

*The thirtieth of June, one thousand five hundred and eighty-nine days. One thousand five hundred and eighty-nine less one thousand five hundred and eighty, which is the twenty-first, is nine, and thirty less twenty-one is nine.*

*Less one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine, the thirtieth of March, is ninety, and thirty and thirty and thirty is ninety. Less one thousand four hundred and forty-three, the fourth of February, is one hundred and forty-six. Less one thousand four hundred and nine, the thirtieth of December, is one hundred and eighty, which is half of a year.*

*Less one thousand four hundred and seventy-eight, the ninth of March, is one hundred and eleven. Less one thousand four hundred and thirty-nine, the thirtieth of January, is one hundred and fifty, which is five months of thirty days.*

*The eighteenth sheet has gone out at four with eight hundred and fifty-two at the top, which did not move, and two lines in the second column, one of which is his and one of which is a woman he never asked.*
