# The Seventeenth Sheet

Eleven days from the nineteenth of March to this afternoon, and this afternoon is one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine days off the first of February of the first year.

One thousand four hundred and forty was the first of February of year five, and the first of March was one thousand four hundred and seventy because the thirtieth of February is the last day of a month that has thirty days in it and not the last day of the year, and the thirtieth of March is twenty-nine days after that and is one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine.

The thirtieth of December was one thousand four hundred and nine, and one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine less one thousand four hundred and nine is ninety, and a quarter in this office is three months of thirty days, and thirty and thirty and thirty is ninety.

And it is sixty days from the thirtieth of January, which is one thousand four hundred and thirty-nine, and sixty days is two months of thirty days each and there are no thirty-firsts in either of them.

## The Room At Four

Wenna Raut came in at about a quarter to four with the day's sheet and read it from the top, straight down, at one speed, which is the whole of her and has been for four years.

She read it twice. She squared it. She put it down and read it again the same way.

Then she turned it round and pushed it two inches towards him across the table and did not say anything at all.

## What Is At The Top Of It

[Register of Unrecorded Merit. Seventeenth quarter. Entered: eight hundred and fifty-two.]

Eight hundred and fifty-two.

It had stood at eight hundred and forty-one since the thirtieth of June of the fourth year, and it had been eight hundred and forty-one on the thirtieth of September and eight hundred and forty-one on the thirtieth of December, and a quarter is ninety days, so it had stood still through three of them, which is two hundred and seventy days.

It had never stood still for four.

## What Is In The Second Column

The second column is headed *carried by*. It was ruled on the ninth of March of the third year and had been empty on seven quarter-ends and has had one line in it since the fifteenth of October of the fourth year, and the line is his, and it is four lines long and a name at the bottom of it in his own hand.

It had one line in it this afternoon.

That is the first thing he looked at and it is the thing he has looked at every quarter for about a year, and it had not gained anything while the figure at the top of the sheet had gained eleven.

## The Bundle

"There were eleven of them," said Wenna Raut. "They came on the twenty-eighth in a bundle with the returns off a desk off a fourth court, and they were addressed to the sheet, which is an address, and about two hundred people a week in this city have that address on a piece of paper they have read."

She put her hand flat on the bundle.

"They are not deeds. I am permitted to say that I have looked at them and that they are not in the form for a deed and there is no clerk in this city who could enter one of them as a deed, and I am permitted to say nothing else."

"Then what are they."

"They are eleven accounts." She turned one round without giving it away. "Each one is in a different hand. Each one says a day and a place and another person, and each one says what was given and what was held afterwards, and each one was written out by the person who did the thing, and every one of them was written on the back of a sheet of the four conditions with a printer's mark in the corner."

"Who brought them."

"Nobody brought them. That is the point and I have been going round it for two days." She took her hand off the bundle. "There is no division in this city that went out for them. No division took a return from them. Nobody in this building asked a single person a single question, and there is no instrument in this building that could have, and the only person this office may ask is you, and you have not asked anybody anything this month and neither have I."

## The First Of The Eleven

He read the first one. He was permitted to. A clerk may only enter what she is given and what she was given is eleven sheets, and there was no instruction on any of them and there had never been one, and there is no form in this city for a thing nobody thought of.

The first one said that in the fourth month of the third year a man carried eleven sacks of coal up the outside stairs of a building in Lamp Street because the stair had been out since the second month and the man who lived at the top could not get down, and the man would not take anything and would not come back, and he could name the night because it was the night the woman at the top had her second baby.

And there is no page in this city about any part of it. The man is not on a roll. The coal is not on a return. The eleven sacks are not a figure anybody has ever had.

He wrote it in the first column exactly as given, and it took him about four seconds and he did not improve it, because there was nothing in it to improve and there was nobody to improve it for.

"It is not a deed," he said.

"It is not a deed," said Wenna Raut. "And the figure at the top of that sheet has been a figure about deeds for four years and about two hundred people a week read it that way and they are wrong about that and they have been wrong about it since the first of February of the first year."

## Why It Moved

"Nobody in this city can tell me why it moved," he said.

"No."

"It moved by eleven and I have been in this room for two days working out how eleven got into a figure and there is nothing in this building that puts a thing in a figure. A clerk enters what she is given. You were given eleven sheets and you entered eleven sheets. There is no step in between that anybody can see."

"There is a step in between," said Wenna Raut. "It is the word at the top of the sheet. It says *Entered*, and it is the office's word for what goes in the first column."

"It has meant one thing since the first of February of the first year and a different thing since a woman read four lines out at a kitchen off Ferrier Street on the nineteenth of July of year two, and there is not one line on that sheet, or in this building, or in this city, that says which of the two a person is supposed to read."

## What Adeyemi Did Not Do

Yusuf Adeyemi came in at about twenty to four, read the sheet on the way past without stopping at it, and left himself standing at the head of the table with his hands behind him.

"That is entered," he said.

"Yes."

"I have no view." He put his hand on the back of the second chair, which is where he puts it.

"A figure has moved on a public page for the first time in four quarters and there is no instrument in this room that could have moved it and no instrument in this room that could have stopped it, and I am a Registrar of a thing nobody has ever explained to me and I am not going to begin at four o'clock on the last working day of a quarter."

"What do you think it is."

"I think it is eleven people out of about a hundred and forty who could describe precisely what they did and had nobody to tell," said the Registrar of Unrecorded Merit.

"And I think that is not the same as a deed, and I am not going to write the other thing down, because if I write *entered* at the top of that column every quarter and I cannot say what entered means then in about four years a clerk is going to put something in that column because the word is there and not because it belongs there."

"Nobody can say why it moved."

"Nobody can say why it moved because there is no column on that sheet for why," said Adeyemi. "You built the condition. You did not build a column."

## At Four

It went out at four.

About two hundred people a week in Cordwain read a standing sheet somewhere and about four in every thousand of them stop at the bottom of it. About two hundred of them on the day it was new last quarter, and fewer than that this quarter, and it is not a measurement and it is not a figure anybody keeps.

Eight hundred and fifty-two at the top of it, and a figure that has moved for the first time since the thirtieth of June of the fourth year, and about four hundred and nineteen lines under it, one of which has his name at the head of it, and about four hundred and eighteen of which are other people's words with an hour against each and no figure on any of them.

And one line in the second column, which is his, which was there last quarter and is there this quarter.

And under the whole of it, in a clerk's hand, typed at a desk off a fourth court, the one sentence on the page that says what the page is.

*Where this Register has nothing, it has not been given anything, and it does not say that there was nothing.*

He did not write that. He has never once been in the room it is typed in.

## The Eleven

He stood at the foot of the ranks wall for about eleven minutes at about ten to five while about nine thousand people went under it, because it is an ordinary afternoon at the end of an ordinary month and the trams go under it.

A woman carried eleven sacks of coal up the outside stairs of a building in Lamp Street in the fourth month of the third year, on the night a woman at the top of that building had her second baby, and she is alive, and she is forty-one or she is thirty-eight, and she does not know it is on a page.

She will find out the way everybody finds out, which is that a person she has never met stands at a desk in Cordwain and reads the line.

*Ninety days from the sixteenth sheet to the seventeenth. Thirty and thirty and thirty is ninety, and the thirtieth of December is one thousand four hundred and nine and this afternoon is one thousand four hundred and ninety-nine.*

*Eight hundred and fifty-two. It stood at eight hundred and forty-one for three quarters and a quarter is ninety days and three of them is two hundred and seventy days and it never stood still for four.*

*Eleven of them wrote on the back of a printer's sheet and sent it to an address that has been at the head of every line of a public page since the thirtieth of December, and no division went for them and nobody asked anybody anything.*

*And the second column has one line in it and it has one line in it, and I looked at that before I looked at the figure.*

*A clerk in this room said out loud that the word at the top of the sheet has meant two things since the nineteenth of July of year two and there is no line anywhere that says which one a person is supposed to read, and that is the whole of the answer and it took her four days to get to it and it is right.*
