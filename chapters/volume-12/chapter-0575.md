# A Sheet Off A Division

Ten days from the eleventh of June to this morning. The eleventh of June was one thousand five hundred and seventy days off the first of February of the first year, and this morning is one thousand five hundred and eighty, and one thousand five hundred and eighty less one thousand five hundred and seventy is ten.

The twenty-first of June is twenty-one days into the sixth month of that year, and the sixth month has thirty days and no thirty-first, and the thirtieth of June is nine days off, because thirty less twenty-one is nine.

The sheet came off her division the morning after, carried to the room off a third court by one of her nine. It was a single sheet of division paper with nine lines on it in three hands, each line a return in the order it arrived, with the day and the place and the other person named, and none of them improved.

## Nine Lines

Wenna Raut read the sheet twice at one speed and squared it and put it on the table. A clerk may only enter what she is given and cannot improve it and cannot be asked to hurry, and she said so before anybody could ask her.

“Three of these can be entered,” she said. “Six of them cannot, because six of them have no day a person can name, or no other person a person reading this in four years would know. That is not a fault in them and it is not a fault in the four lines. It is the shape of what a person is when the thing they did happened while they were the only person in the room.”

Yusuf Adeyemi stood at the end of the table and did not sit down. He had stood that way at the end of a table in March when nine boards went up with no name on them, and he had been right then and he was right now.

“The division that printed the repainting form is the division that kept the district book without ever losing a street,” he said. “Her nine wrote these in the order they came out. That is why three of them can go in a column and six of them cannot.”

Lin Hao stood by the door with his coat on, because he had come from the branch in daylight and there was a shift waiting for him at eleven that night. He felt shame and relief at once, and the relief was that somebody had brought a sheet to a door instead of a division going to one.

## The One Who Carried It

The one who had carried it was the youngest of her nine, a woman who writes small the way Beatrix Halm writes. She waited by the door with her hands behind her back, the way people wait in that room when they have carried something and are going to carry something back.

“She told me to tell you the order,” the woman said. “The first three came on the same evening from the same street, one after the other, and the six after them came over four days. Nobody went back to the first three to make them read better beside the six. That is the rule.”

Lin Hao nodded, because the rule was the room's rule and it was named by nobody, and because he had heard it on the eleventh of June in the order it arrived and had not gone back over it either.

“Tell her the three are entered,” Wenna Raut said, without looking up. “Tell her exactly as given means exactly as given, and that the six are face down on the table and not in the book, and that no instrument in this building can take one out of the other.”

The woman nodded and went out down the stair into the third court, and Lin Hao went out behind her as far as the court arch, because he had a question that was not an act of the office and could be asked in an arch.

“Did she say anything else,” he said.

“She said the quarter ends at four on the thirtieth,” the woman said. “She said you know what that costs.”

He watched her go down Cordwain toward the chandler's with nothing in her hands, and he felt the shame again, and under it the relief that her nine still wrote things down in the order they came out, even when the order proved his definition could not hold them.

“What does she want done with the six,” he said, back in the room.

“She wants nothing done with them,” Adeyemi said. “She sent them so that you would see what your four lines leave out when they are applied by people who apply them correctly. About nine in ten of what a person walks in with cannot name the day, and it is the same nine in ten every time anybody has ever measured it.”

Wenna Raut entered the three that could be entered, exactly as given, in about nine seconds each, and she did not look up while she did it. The other six she put to one side, face down, the way she puts everything the office cannot use.

“The quarter ends in nine days,” she said. “The eighteenth sheet goes out at four on the thirtieth. The figure at the top has stood at eight hundred and fifty-two since the thirtieth of March. These three will move it by three, and there is no column anywhere that says why, because the word at the top of the sheet has meant two things since the nineteenth of July of the second year.”

Lin Hao wanted to thank her and did not, because there is nothing in that room to thank a person with and because he had learned in January that being readable is not the same as being agreed with.

He went out into Cordwain with the six lines face down behind him and the three entered ahead of him, and he understood that a division that prints a form can also print a sheet that proves the form cannot hold what the division finds.

*The twenty-first of June, one thousand five hundred and eighty days. Ten days from the eleventh of June and nine days before the thirtieth. A sheet off her division has come in with nine lines in the order they arrived, three of which can be entered and six of which cannot, and the quarter ends at four on the thirtieth.*
