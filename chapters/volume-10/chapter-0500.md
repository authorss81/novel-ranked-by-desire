# The Number That Is Not Zero

The rota for the twenty-eighth of January had him on from eleven at night until half past five, and he had it in his coat, and he got to the branch at about ten to eleven and the building was cold in the way that a building is cold at eleven at night when the heating is whatever the heating has been all day.

Four floors. Three floors of washrooms and a stairwell and a bin room and a cistern on the second that somebody had written about in a ledger on the first of January and that had stopped running by the sixth.

There was a woman on the second floor at about twenty past eleven with a cloth and a bucket who had been there every night since the eleventh of October and who had never once asked him a question, and there was a woman at the counter from one until seven who was the night man and who had the page and the page was the page.

Rank one on a white plate in his coat with no figure on it.

## What He Is

He is not a poor man.

There is a plate in his coat with his name on it and a white ground and no figure cut into it anywhere, and there is a page on a desk in this building with four nights on it and a name on the line, and the two of those together are the whole of what he is now.

The first of them means he will eat. Not today and not for a while yet, and then for the rest of his life, at a figure somebody else's office decides, out of a guarantee made in one movement at the front of nine people in a queue.

The second of them means he will be where he is put.

He has had eleven weeks of both and has spent most of them on a step in the cold working out whether the first one is a comfort, and the answer he gets every morning is the same answer and it does not improve and it does not get worse, and a man who gave a whole standing away at a counter has not got anybody to hand him a different one.

He chose it. That is the sentence and he has said it out loud to about four people and it holds.

## Two Streets East

He went east instead of west at about twenty to five with a bucket in his hand, because he has done that eleven weeks running and has not looked at the reason for it once.

The quarter-end card went up on the stone at half past four the afternoon before, and the ordinary repainting of the eighth of January came and went and took nothing with it, which is what an ordinary repainting does.

The two words and the dash and the two words were there in the office's own hand, in letters the height of a name on a plate, and they had been through a wash of rain and one winter and they were still there and nobody had taken them down, and this city has no schedule anywhere for taking them down.

And on the stone below them was a white tile with a name on it in chalk and nothing at all under it.

He stood under it for about two minutes. He has done this about sixty times since the first of January and about four hundred people have gone past him doing it, and there is nothing to be done at a wall after the first twenty times except turn up.

## The Standing Sheet Of The Quarter

A month in this city is thirty days and the thirtieth is the last day of it, and the last day of December is the day the standing sheet of the quarter went out, and it does not matter in the slightest what anybody in this building is doing on it.

The standing sheet of that quarter went out at four in the afternoon on the thirtieth of December with an address at the head of every line, and any person in this city may go to a public desk and read it on a working afternoon.

He read it on the twenty-eighth of January at about twenty past three in the afternoon, in a corridor, on a bench, with about nine people behind him who were not reading it.

The line for the address carried the disposition the same way it had on the fourth of October.

*the reservoir, held by no person, claimed by the Church of the Quiet Number, answered by the subject. Disposition: given away in the open, the first of October, holder none.*

And under the disposition, in the hand of a clerk who may only enter and cannot improve what she is given and cannot be asked to hurry, there was one figure, and she had been given it, and she had entered it.

## The Figure

Two hundred and eleven thousand, eight hundred and forty grains.

He read it three times.

That is not zero.

It is about one in seven. One grain in seven of everything that was in that account on the first of October at about ten minutes to four in the afternoon did not go anywhere, and it did not go anywhere because the four days ended, and the four days ended because the thing that emptied it was a man standing at a wall who had four days in him and then did not have a fifth.

That is the sentence. There is not one instrument in this city that could have taken a fifth day out of him, and there is not one instrument in this city that can go and find the two hundred and eleven thousand now, because there has never been a list, and there could not be one now, and that was the argument at two o'clock on the afternoon of the first of October and it was correct then and it is correct now.

He read the figure a fourth time.

It is not going to be zero. Nothing will take it and nothing will close it, because the books that would take it met at seven on the morning of the sixteenth of November and did not move, and they open once in the year and not twice, and they will not meet again until the fifteenth of November, which is about six hundred and forty-five days off.

And on the thirtieth of March a woman two floors under a sales floor will write six words under her own name in a chained book, as she has four times a year for thirty years, and the six of them will say that it is still going and is not shut and has nobody's name on it, and that will be true, and nothing else in this city is going to say anything at all about it.

## What It Is Not

It is not a debt. No creditor, no debtor, and not one person in this city has been told about it.

It is not a fund. It cannot be drawn against, it cannot be allocated, it cannot be closed, and it does not stand against anything, and in about four years the chain will come off the book it is written in and the plate will be scraped and a new one cut from whatever was on the old one, and after that this figure will exist in cut metal in a hall in a case, and a woman who kept the room will be retired or dead.

It is not a floor. He has got no floor under him and has not had one since the first of October, and this figure is not one and is not going to become one, and it is the first figure in this city in four years that has stood against nothing at all and has stayed.

It belongs to nobody. That is the entire sentence about it and it has been the entire sentence about it since the second of July, and there are nine thousand people who believe one half of it and four hundred who believe the other half. About a million people in this city stood still in a street on four particular days, were handed something, and cannot produce a receipt for it or anything else either.

## Not Zero

He put the sheet back where it was and stood in the corridor for about a minute and a half.

Four hundred turned lines off a district round, three pages, a time beside every one, no figure on any of it, not one of them his.

A white plate in his coat with no figure on it.

A tile on a wall two streets away with a name in chalk and nothing under it.

Three floors of washrooms and a bin room and a cistern and a woman with a bucket who has never asked him anything, and a woman at a counter until seven, and a woman who has been auditing this building since the first of October and has not once said a word about why.

And a number.

He walked back down the hill at a quarter to five with a page in his hand and put his key in the door and started his shift.

The number was not zero, and nothing in this city can make it be anything else, and it is going to be there on the thirtieth of March in a woman's handwriting under her own name. He is going to read it in about two months from a bench in a corridor off a third court.
