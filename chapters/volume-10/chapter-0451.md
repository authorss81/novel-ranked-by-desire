# The Answer And The Chair

The list was made in the room at the end of the corridor on the morning of the thirtieth of June in the way a list is made, which is that it is made on the day and not one person is asked whether they are on it, and it was read out at about ten past two in the afternoon, in order, with four names and then his.

"It is one hundred and thirty days since the twentieth of February," he said, to the back of the second chair on the left, which was four inches lower than the other eight and had been made for a man who was not a member and had never been repaired. "Ten from twenty leaves ten, ten with thirty and thirty and thirty and thirty makes one hundred and thirty."

"Sit down," said the clerk. "You are the fifth. It has been the fifth since the fifteenth of April."

## What The Room Is At Two O'Clock

There was a real book on the table with a chain lying across it in a long curve, and a woman standing at the rail who could not sit down because a keeper is not a member of that room and a member's chair is a member's chair, and she had been told so twice by the clerk and had been extremely polite about it.

There was a man in the fourth seat who had come at nine on the twenty-sixth and taken his coat off over the back of his chair, which is what a man who has not sat in a chair for five months does.

There was a chair at the end of the table that was not one of the nine, and a woman in it with her back to the window and her own division's paper on her knees, and there had been a woman in it on the nineteenth of June who had come down that corridor on her own feet with a docket under her arm.

He did not see her come in and he was not going to find out.

The room read him. He was the fifth and the fifth was read last, and when his standing was read he stood up with his hand on the back of the low chair and said one thing, which was the thing he had said in a corridor eleven days ago to a bench nobody had ever offered him.

## Two Sentences

"The holding is mine," he said. "The ground is my own description of my own premises, made over it on the morning of the twenty-seventh of October at about eleven, by a clerk called Imrie, on four words I said in a kitchen on that same morning. And a man who has lodged his whole standing is not keeping the composition of those premises, so there is nothing in this sheet to administer me against."

He said it in about twenty seconds and he got it out the way the clerk had made him get it out in a corridor, short and plain, with no nouns in it that a stranger could read in four years without hearing his own voice in the middle.

Four people at that table had just heard the four words said out loud in a building with a clerk in it, and one of them was a man in the fourth seat who had asked on the twenty-sixth what the fourth part was and had been told, and had gone back up a stair and said in a stairwell that he would rather be a nobody than a rank.

The clerk wrote it down. A clerk may only enter and cannot correct, and she entered the whole of it, and it went into a book.

Nobody thanked him. It was not a favour.

## The Seals

The tray came off the shelf at about twenty past two and there were five seals in it and four of them went down one at a time over a printed finding, and the fifth was a piece of brass that had been on his own tray since ten past nine on the morning of the fifteenth of April.

He put it down beside the minute because he had been told to put it down beside the minute, and a member does not refuse his own seal when the room sits, and after that he would carry it out of the building at four and into every room in this city for the rest of his life.

The sheet was carried. Not called. Carried.

That is the whole difference and no one in that room needed to explain it to anybody: called is a thing you do to a person in a corridor and carried is a thing the room does to the world, and the seals go down, and the finding is sealed five times, and at four o'clock it goes out on the ordinary run to seven desks.

## What The Finding Says

The clerk read back what she had entered, in order, the date first and the hour with it, and he stood at his table while she read it.

"The twentieth of February. Three seals at the head of it, laid, called, served and heard, and not carried. The premises named four times. The ground described in the fourth part, being a description made against the head of those premises at about eleven on the morning of the twenty-seventh of October by a clerk of the division of classification. The subject named under the fifth line. The composition of those premises stated at seven with the hour it was read."

"And the finding."

"The finding is that the holding is the subject's and the ground is the subject's, and that the subject is not a person keeping the composition of those premises, and that the premises are."

She put the pen down.

"That last one is the whole of what you have done in four years," said the clerk, "and it is in the finding, and it is sealed five times, and you said it out loud in a room and asked for it."

## What It Does To Eight People

He had bought the clause on the twenty-first of June with eleven words of a second sentence, and a woman who prices things had told him in that corridor what it cost, which was that from the thirtieth of June he was not a man in a building but a doorway, and that a doorway cannot be moved and cannot be closed and cannot be got out of the way.

He had understood that.

What he had not understood until about twenty-five past two on the afternoon of the thirtieth of June, standing at his own table with a piece of brass still warm from his hand, was that the thing he had bought was a guarantee about *him*, and not a wall.

A carried requisition runs down the chain to the division that holds its fourth part, and its fourth part is the premises, and it has been administered weekly against the composition of those premises since the first of April, and the composition of those premises is seven.

Eight people slept under that roof, and a composition is administered against the persons keeping the address and not against the man who lodged his standing in a queue.

He could not be administered against. He had made sure of that on the fifteenth of April in ninety seconds with a pen.

The other eight could be.

## The Fourth Block

At about half past two a keeper filled in the fourth block of a quarter's sheet at the end of that table, out of her own memory, with nothing behind it, and a second woman stood beside her and put her name to the fact that it had been done.

"Seven," said the keeper, and her name on it.

It was the thirtieth of June and there was no certificate for the quarter that begins tomorrow, because a form cannot be drafted without one and no one had drafted one, and in about four years the chain would come off that book and the plate would be scraped and a new one cut with whatever was on the old one and no more.

Not one person at that table could strike the figure, correct the figure, or ask her where she had got it.

## Under The Minute

After the last seal and before it went down, the clerk took a slip out of a drawer and entered under the minute thirty-eight words that had been in that drawer for nine days, and she read them out because she read everything she entered.

"Where a minute names premises, no person keeping an address so named may be produced by that minute otherwise than in the presence of the subject, and the subject may not be absent from the making of it."

Then she looked up.

"That is the clause of the twenty-first of June and it is now under a minute of the carrying and it is not in the body, and there are no parties in this room, so it does not bind anybody in it. It does not stop the run. It does not unname the premises and it does not put them out of the fourth block."

"I know what it does."

"You know what it costs," said the clerk. "It costs you a corridor, every morning, for as long as that address is on anything any room of mine does."

At four o'clock the minute went out.

## The Woman At The End Of The Table

He had been in that room for two hours and ten minutes as the fifth and he had not once looked at the chair that is not one of the nine, on purpose, and he had learned why in a corridor in June in about nine sentences from a woman who cannot be asked anything.

At about four the room emptied in the order it always empties in. The man in the fourth seat went out first, because a man who took his coat off the chair in June takes his coat off it again. The keeper and the woman with the cloth went down the corridor with the book on its chain between them. The four seals went into the tray.

The woman in the low chair at the end of the table did not get up when the door was shut.

He got as far as the door, and he stopped, because there was something in him that had been in him since the nineteenth of June and had been building all afternoon, and it was two words long.

"Do not," said the clerk, without turning round.

She was writing up her book. She writes it up after the seals and before anybody leaves the building, and she had not written one line of it yet, and she had said one word in a corridor in June and she said the same one now and did not look up.

He went out and shut the door.

He stood in a corridor of forty feet with a piece of brass in his coat and he did not know whether the woman at the end of that table was the officer of the composition of his own front door, and there was no instrument in this city that would ever tell him, and he had been told on the nineteenth of June that there was no way on earth to keep her out of that corridor while he did what he had come to do.

He had not done anything about it.

He could not have. It was half past two in the afternoon and the seals had been down for two hours and the sheet was already in a bundle.

## Four O'Clock

Seven buildings.

He walked down that corridor with the brass in his pocket and worked the whole of it out on the stair, which is the only way out of that building and also the way up to the second and third floors.

The minute goes to seven desks at four in the ordinary way and there is no way to put it back in a drawer once it has gone to the first one. Six of those seven have a woman from his address at them and he had never met a single one of them. The third desk is a glazed counter in a wing off a third court that nothing is ever addressed to and everything addressed to the sixth goes past.

And in four years there will be a stranger with a folder, and he will read a clause that says not one person keeping that address can be produced without the subject present, and he will put it down as a man who bought himself a protection.

Four years is a long time and it is not a wall.

Three weeks is a long time.

Six of the women it is about were asleep under that roof at that moment, and one of them was two floors down this stair with her own rooms and her own plate, and one of them was on a cold store, and one of them was in a smaller set of rooms at a guild, and none of them had been told anything by him and none of them was going to be told anything by him in the next twenty days.

The court outside was the colour it is in this city in the last week of a quarter.

He put his hand in his coat and the brass was there, and it was going to be in his pocket in a corridor, and at a counter, and on a tram, and in a kitchen with eight women in it at eleven o'clock that night, for the rest of his life.
