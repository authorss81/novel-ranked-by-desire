# A Desk Twenty Years In Service

The Ninth Terrace intake hall opens at seven and Dagmar Oyelowo had been at the same desk since about a quarter past six every morning for twenty years, and on the third of October she got there at about ten to six and did the whole of her twenty minutes in silence, which is what she does.

He came in at about eleven in the morning.

She did not stand up. She has never stood up for anybody at that desk, and there are four ranks in this city that would not get her out of her chair, and he is the first of them she has met.

"Sit down if you are going to stand there," she said. "The chair is a chair. There is no difference between you this morning and anybody else and I want that said before we begin."

"What is different about this morning, then."

"Nothing at that desk," said Dagmar Oyelowo. "There is a queue outside that has been outside since about a quarter to nine and there will be about three hundred in it by the middle of the afternoon and I have not got one thing in this hall that helps any of them, and I am going to be here at this desk until it shuts at four, and that is the entire difference and it is in the corridor and not in this room."

## The Same Sentence

She had the day's sheet already ruled off at the left-hand side, in her own hand, the way she rules it every morning, and she turned it round about a quarter of an inch so that he could see that it was blank, and she did not offer it to him.

"You told me in the summer," he said, "that there is nothing in this building that would let you remember it for you if you lost it."

"That is still true and it has not got less true in three months, and it was not true because of the reservoir and it is not true because of anything that happened on the first."

She put her hands flat on the desk. "I have no way of carrying a thing that is not in a book, and I am not going to get one, and I have never wanted one, and the two of us are not going to have a conversation about that today, because we had it once and it did not need having twice."

"You told me your brother was alive."

"I told you what was in a return with ten quarters on it and no name on it," said Dagmar Oyelowo. "I did not tell you anything else and I am not going to today, and what has happened to that file since the first is a thing that happened in a room at the end of a corridor and it does not touch this desk and it does not touch me."

"You knew before anybody."

"I knew it on the eighteenth of March and I have not spoken about it to one living person until a man with a chest that is spent sat down in that chair, and he is not going to get a second use out of me and I am not going to give him one."

## The Figure He Came For

"Then tell me what the counter has."

"I have told you what there is and what there is not," said Dagmar Oyelowo. "I have got a day's sheet and it is ruled and it is blank, and there have been eleven people at this counter since eight o'clock and ten of them were ordinary business and one of them was not, and I am not going to put any part of any of it in front of you."

"Not the one."

"Especially not the one."

He looked at her.

"You are not going to ask me a second time."

"No," he said. "I am not, and I want to be exact about why, because four months ago I would have. I would have asked you a second time this morning and a third time three days from now and I would have called it duty."

"What are you going to do instead."

"Nothing," said Lin Hao. "I am going to sit in that chair for about four minutes and then I am going to go out and stand in that corridor for four minutes, and I am not going to ask you and you are not going to tell me."

"In about four years one of us will remember this morning and neither of us will be able to do anything with it, and that is the arrangement. And I have just made it out loud in a public hall at eleven o'clock in the morning, and I would like you to notice that I made it myself and that you did not ask me for it."

Dagmar Oyelowo looked at him for about four seconds.

"There," she said. "That is the first thing you have said to me since the fourteenth of December that was not about you."

## Twenty Years Of The Same Job

She turned the day's sheet back and squared it to the edge of the desk with one finger.

"I have run this desk for twenty years and I have scored once. Not once in four years, not once this month, not once since the day I took it. It is not a matter of virtue and it is not modesty and I am not going to be kind about it, because being kind about it would be a lie of exactly the kind this desk exists to stop people telling."

"Then what is it."

"It is that the Board does not read what comes off it," said Dagmar Oyelowo. "A score is a figure against a standing, a standing is a figure against a composition, and a composition is a certificate made four times a year out of a woman's own memory."

"Everything I have ever done at this desk has gone into a chain and out of a chain and about four years later into a case in a hall where a woman with a plate is taking the numbers off tiles. In four years there will be a new plate with whatever was on the old one and no more, and I have watched it happen twice."

"And today."

"Today there are about three hundred people outside that have been outside since a quarter to nine, and every one of them has come to a public counter in this city, and a public counter is the only place a person in Ostara is allowed to say that something happened to them."

She put her thumb on the blank of the sheet. "And I am going to hear all of it. That is my job and it is the whole of what I am, and by four o'clock this afternoon I will have heard whatever they came to say."

"And it is going to go into me and not out of me, because not one box in this building will take it and not one box in this city will take it, and by the first of January it will be in a woman's head and nothing else."

"And you will keep it."

"I will keep it the way I keep everything at this desk," said Dagmar Oyelowo, "which is that I will have it and there will be nobody who knows I have it, and I have not got a way of putting it anywhere and I am not going to build one at a public counter in twenty years of service, and that is the answer to the question you did not ask me in the corridor at the end of June and are not going to ask me today."

## What She Was Going To Say And Did Not

There was a queue at the counter for about nine minutes of it, in the ordinary way, and about eleven people came to the desk and nine of them had papers and two of them did not, and she took the two without papers exactly as she took the nine with them, which is to say she looked at them and told them what they needed to hear and they went away.

At about half past two he said, "Is it going to stop?"

"I do not know," said Dagmar Oyelowo. "And I am not going to guess in a hall, and I am not going to guess at all, because a guess from this desk in this month gets repeated by about eleven o'clock tomorrow and I have watched a rumour come off this desk and go around a district in nine years."

"That is the first time anybody has told me they do not know and meant it."

"It is the only thing anybody at a counter has ever been able to give anybody," said Dagmar Oyelowo. "The truth, with a date on it, from a person who has to be here tomorrow and the day after."

## Four O'Clock

The hall shut at four and the corridor emptied out into the street in about four minutes and the last of the three hundred went off up the hill towards Ferrier Street at a walk, at about four, with nothing on them.

He had been in the corridor for four minutes of it, as he had said he would.

"Sixth of October," he said, when she had the sheet squared and the cloth out.

"The sixth is an ordinary day," said Dagmar Oyelowo, "and I will be here at about a quarter past six and I will have it open at seven and there will be nobody in front of it, and I will be at this desk anyway."

"What happens on the sixth."

"Then it will be a counter again," said Dagmar Oyelowo, and she put the cloth across the desk and did not look up, and that was the last thing she said to him, and she has not given a number to anybody since and she was not going to give one to him on his way out of a public hall on the third of a month with three hundred people in her corridor and nothing in her day's sheet.
