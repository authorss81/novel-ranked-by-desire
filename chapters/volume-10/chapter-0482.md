# A Council That Cannot Sit Again

The chair of the Concil of the Nine had him up the outside stair at about eleven in the morning on the sixth of September for the fourth time since the third of July, and she had not asked him to come and he had not offered to.

"One hundred and ninety-six," he said, in the doorway, and then stopped, because he had said it four times in eleven days and the number was not going to improve by being said.

"Say it or do not say it," said Anouk Ferreira.

"One hundred and ninety-six. One hundred and sixty at the end of July, one hundred and ninety at the end of August, and one hundred and ninety with six is one hundred and ninety-six."

"It is two hundred and twenty-one on the first of October and you have known that since the fourteenth of August." She did not look up. "Sit down. I have got ninety minutes and I have used about four of them on paper work."

## The Two Things On The Table

There was one sheet on the table and it was the standing sheet of the twenty-sixth of July with the quarter's first item gone off the top of it, and there were two documents pinned to a board against the wall, and both of them had been read by about four hundred people in a room anybody might attend.

"Those two," said Anouk Ferreira. "Say to me what they are."

"The Church's claim and your room's answer."

"That is the shortest thing anybody in this city has said about them in four months and it is wrong, and it is wrong in the way that will cost somebody something in about four years."

She put a finger on the first of them. "That is a claim. It was entered on the twenty-ninth of July by a chaplain who has been in this city for twenty years and it is three hundred and ninety words and every one of them can be supported by eleven hundred people she has personally asked for nothing from in twenty years."

"And the other one."

"The other one is a hearing record," said Ferreira. "Four lines and an hour, kept in a building off a third court, producible for four years, and it carries no seal and it can never be ordered and it can never be classified and it can never be read at a valuation and it can never be struck out, because it is not a finding and it never was one and no one in that room has the power to make it one."

She let that sit for about two seconds.

"And the answer to the claim is a minute of a sitting on the thirtieth of July, and a minute is a different animal from a claim, and the room spent ninety minutes on the thirtieth of July answering the first and did not touch the second one at all, and that is correct and I will not have it called a failure in this building."

## What Is Not On Either Of Them

"There is a thing on neither of those documents," he said.

"There are four things on neither of those documents and I am going to give you all four because you are going to need three of them on the first of October and the fourth one is for me."

"There is a fifth."

"There is a fifth and it is the fourteenth of August." Ferreira turned her chair about a quarter of a turn. "Three women put a hand into a held thing on purpose in a kitchen off Ferrier Street, each of them knowing what it would cost, and there were nine other people in the room. Is that on either of them."

"No."

"Is it on the standing sheet going out at four this afternoon."

"No."

"Correct, and not by any cleverness of mine. There is no box in this city for the decision of nine people who are not sitting in a room with the power to hold it. That is what happened on the twenty-sixth and your auditor spent a day finding it out and came back with nothing, and she was right to come back with nothing, and every word she said to that kitchen was true."

## What The Room Can Actually Do

"And what can the room do about the disposition," he said.

"Nothing at all before the first of October, and I want that in your head in the ordinary way, because it is the only thing keeping me calm."

"Why."

"Because a disposition is a finding, and a finding is made at a quarter-end, and the last quarter-end was the thirtieth of June and the next one is the first of October, and every week in between is a week that cannot carry anything."

She put her finger on the standing sheet. "And it cannot be carried at the hearing either, and I have watched four people try that in four months and it is the single most expensive misunderstanding in this city, and it is mine as much as anybody's."

She turned the sheet round and pushed it at him.

"Look at what it says."

"It says the reservoir, held by no person, claimed by the Church of the Quiet Number, answered by the subject."

"Read the last four words again."

"The answer was given by the subject."

"The answer was given by the subject," said Anouk Ferreira, "and I did that, and I want it entered in this conversation that I did that and that I would do it again, and I would do it again because the alternative was a heading with his name on it, and a heading with a name on it is a document that a man can be requisitioned against for the rest of his life."

He looked at the sheet for a while.

"That is a different question, and you have asked it at me four times and I have let you."

"It is not the question and I am not going to answer the question," said Ferreira. "I answered the fact of it in July and he conceded the fact of it in four hundred people on the thirtieth and both of those are in the record."

"And what belongs to whom is a question that this room is not going to decide in a hurry and neither is anybody else in Ostara, and I have been in a chair long enough to know that a question you decide in a hurry is a question you have lost."

## The Thing That Has Been Built

"What can it do on the first of October, then."

"It can hold it."

She said it without any weight at all.

"That is the whole of the room's power over a thing nobody owns, and it has been the whole of it for thirty years, and there is a form for it, and the form was drafted in the first week of July by a clerk in this building who was told to draft a form for the custody of a held thing, and she drafted one, and it has been sitting in a drawer ever since."

"What is on it."

"Three parts. A room — quiet, at the Terrace, that a thing may be kept in, with a door and a keeper and not one person in it after dark. An account, held, at a figure, not drawable on by anybody and not closable by anybody while it is held. And a form, on which a person may enter, once, one thing they did that no instrument witnessed."

She let him look at her.

"A quiet room, a held account and a form," said Anouk Ferreira. "That is the entire machinery of this city and it has worked for thirty years, and I did not build it and neither did he and neither did you, and on the first of October it is the only instrument in Ostara that can touch that account, and it is not mine, and it belongs to the room, and the room does not know it has got it."

"Yusuf wrote it."

"Yusuf Adeyemi drafted the wording of the procedure and Yusuf Adeyemi has been in this city for nineteen years and drafted it out of four hundred precedents and would have drafted it out of the air."

She shrugged. "You may attribute it to the procedure or you may attribute it to the weather. I am not going to help you with it, and I am not going to ask you a second question about it when you get here on the first of October, because a chair does not ask twice."

## Ninety-One Days Of It

"Ninety-one days," he said. "Thirty of July, thirty of August, thirty of September and one is ninety-one, and a week is seven, and ninety-one is thirteen of them, and it has been thirteen weeks since the thirtieth of June."

"Thirteen weeks of four people drafting a document," said Ferreira, "and not one line of it requires anybody in Ostara to accept it."

"That is a strange thing to have built."

"That is the most valuable thing this city owns and it has cost nothing and it has taken an afternoon, and if you want to know why it has taken an afternoon then look at the wall of that clerk on the first of July and count the days after the thirtieth of June at which anybody in this building can be made to say yes to anything."

She stood up, which meant about forty minutes of the ninety had gone.

"So there is your instrument," said Anouk Ferreira. "A quiet room where nothing can be done to anybody. A held account that cannot be drained and cannot be closed. And a form on which one unrecorded thing may be entered, once, by the person who did it, with their own name on it, and that name will never be read by an auditor and will never be entered anywhere else, because there is nowhere else for it to go."

"That is not justice."

"It is the only justice this city has ever built," said Ferreira, "and it has been used about nine hundred times in thirty years and it is not enough and it is the reason Ostara is not a graveyard."

"I am not going to argue that it is sufficient. I am going to argue on the first of October that a man standing at a wall in the open is not sufficient either, and I am going to lose, and I would like it noted that I did not know that until today."

## Eight Names At The Door

He was in the corridor at about twenty past twelve with the standing sheet going out at four, and Beatrix Halm was at the end of the stair with a sheet of paper in her hand and her notebook under her arm and no coat on.

"I said it to you in a kitchen and I said I would not say it again," she said.

"You said you would say it in a room anybody may attend."

"This is the corridor to a room that cannot sit again until the first of October, and it is the room your clause puts you outside of, and I have thought about that for eleven days and I have not found a better corridor."

She gave him the sheet.

A sheet with eight names and a date on it and no addressee: the twenty-sixth of August, in that kitchen, at about nine at night. Eight to one, and the one against had said why out loud, and the eight had not.

There was no resolution in it, because a resolution of a household cannot be put in a box, and there was no address, because the woman who had written it did not know whether it was addressed to the house or to a man who could not be found.

"Which of you is going to read this into a room," he said.

"Not me and not you," said Beatrix Halm. "Ines has been three days finding out where a thing like this can go and she came back with a corridor and a standing sheet, and a standing sheet goes out at four with an address at the head of every line, and it cannot be a finding and it cannot be a resolution, and it is the thinnest thing in this city."

She looked at him.

"And it is a thing that exists on the first of October, in a building, that somebody cannot produce and silence. And I do not know whether that is worth anything at all and I have decided that it is better than the four hundredth person in this city who was on the ground on the eleventh of July and not one person stood next to."

She went down the stair.

And at four o'clock in the afternoon of the sixth of September a clerk in that building went out with a standing sheet.

It carried an address at the head of every line, and the answer to the Church's claim, and four lines on a shelf, and the heading with no name on it, and underneath the heading, in a hand that was not the clerk's and was not anybody's in the building, eight names and one date and the words: *and the one against has said why out loud.*

Nobody had to accept it.

There were twenty-five days.
