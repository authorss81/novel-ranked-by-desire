# The Kitchen At Ferrier Street

There were nine of them at that table on the afternoon of the sixth of August and there had not been nine of them at that table since the twenty-ninth of December, which is a thing that kitchen has not seen in nine years, and the light was coming in flat off the wall opposite because it was the hour when nobody in that kitchen does anything useful.

Six days. That was what they had, between the night he asked the question and the afternoon they argued about it, and six days is a long time for nine people who all know the same thing and none of whom is the person who has to decide.

"One hundred and sixty-six days," said Lin Hao. "Ten from twenty leaves ten, ten with thirty and thirty and thirty and thirty is one hundred and thirty at the end of June, and July is thirty, so one hundred and sixty at the end of July, and one hundred and sixty with six is one hundred and sixty-six."

"Say it in years," said Isolde Marchetti.

"I cannot. It is a number of days and it is going to stay a number of days and I am not going to make it into years in this kitchen because you find them more moving."

## Ines Duarte Reads The Instruments

"Somebody should say what is actually enforceable," said Ines Duarte, "because everybody in this room has been arguing for six days about what they feel like saying about it and I have read every instrument this household has touched since the twenty-seventh of April and I can tell you what four of them can do."

She put her forearms on the table.

"One. The requisition cannot be stopped by any one of us. It is administered weekly against the composition, and the composition is a thing about the house and not about a person, and none of the nine of us can say no to it on the house's behalf. One. Two."

"It cannot be answered in this room either, because it is not ours to answer, and if we all nine wrote a letter and put our names at the bottom of it, it would go down a chain to a division that has no door and the division would enter it and file it."

"Three," she said. "The account is not ours. I want that said before we go any further because I have watched five people in this room start an argument by assuming it is. It has never been his. He said so in four hundred people on the thirtieth of July and a clerk wrote a heading about it and his name is not on the page. It is not the Church's either, whatever eleven hundred of them came to a hearing about."

"It is a thing that thirty years of strangers have been putting into."

"Four," said Ines Duarte. "Nothing in this city will produce a copy of a household's draw for a household. So if you have drawn, you have drawn and not one person in this city has ever been told, including whoever drew it, and that is not a scandal, it is the ordinary condition of this account and it is why he cannot get round it."

## Nadia Has Been Arguing About The Number For Years

"The number is seven," said Nadia Ferrous.

"The number is seven," Ines Duarte agreed.

"The number is seven since the thirteenth of March at about ten to nine in the morning and there are eight of us under this roof and he is the ninth." Nadia had a cloth in her hands again.

"It has been seven for about five months and before that it was eight and there was a reason and the reason was that somebody offered to give the address up at a counter on the fifteenth of November and it went into a queue behind about forty other things and never came out."

"Nadia—"

"I am not accusing anybody. I offered to hand the address in and I would do it again." She put the cloth down.

"What I am saying is that for five months a weekly piece of paper has been coming to a door this city's own record says is kept by seven people, and the city cannot tell the difference, and if it ever wanted to it would be obliged to write down the name of one of us to make the difference, and every one of the nine of us knows which name it would pick."

"Isolde."

"It would pick me," said Isolde Marchetti. "I sing in Bellhouse four nights a week and it is the only post in the nine of us that the city can serve a paper at without a clerk standing in a corridor. If the city ever wants to make its seven, it will do it by finding me at four in the afternoon and asking whether I still sleep here, and I would answer it honestly, and it would then be eight again."

There was no argument with her, which was how the room usually worked.

## Runa Sato Has Not Filed Anything Since The Third Of May

"Runa."

"I am going to say it because if I do not then somebody else will say it and they will say it wrong," said Runa Sato.

"I have not filed a return since the third of May. Twenty-seven days from the third of that month to the end of it, and thirty of June, and thirty of July, and six of this month, and that is ninety-three days, and a week is seven days, and ninety-three is thirteen weeks and two days."

"I am a rank-six field operative in a division that takes returns at four in the afternoon, and I have sent nothing into it since the third of May, and there is a space on a board in Cordwain with my division on it and nothing under it. The board does not carry a name. It carries my division, and my division has no door."

"You have been at your table every day."

"I have been at my table every day. I have been here, in this kitchen, taking the weight of eight other people off the floor of a building and putting it in my own back, and I have been doing it for four years and I have never filed one word of it anywhere." She looked at the table. "I was sent to watch him. That is all I ever was, in that division."

"Somebody put a woman on a man and I have been lying in my own files since the thirty-sixth chapter of my own career and I have lied quietly and well and I did not know that was going to be the thing that mattered most about me."

"You could have told somebody."

"I could have told him in the first year," said Runa Sato, "and I have thought about it every week since and there is no version of that in which it is not a woman in a kitchen saying you have been carrying the city's unmeasured good in your own spine for four years and never once said. So I did not. Say the rest of it."

"Petra has been drawing for eleven years and did not know," said Nadia Ferrous. "You have been drawing for four and knew. Say the difference."

"There is no difference and I am not going to invent one for a kitchen," said Runa Sato, "and if you want to be angry at somebody in this room tonight then be angry at me, because I am the one who has had the most chances."

## The Ugly Part

"Isolde," said Beatrix Halm.

"I am not going to sing it," said Isolde Marchetti.

"You are going to sing it," said Beatrix Halm, "because it is in the room and everybody in it is thinking it and one of us has the standing to put it where it can be looked at, and if you do not do it then it gets said badly by somebody worse than you."

"I will do it badly."

"Four bars is not doing it badly, it is doing it exactly."

And Isolde Marchetti sang four bars in the middle of a kitchen at four in the afternoon, which is not a performance and is not a room being made to agree with a man, and which took about eleven seconds.

It was about a man who had lived in a house with eight women for four years and had never once told any of them where the money came from.

It was not true. He had told them the answer to a requisition on the ninth of July and told them about the chair on the third and had said nine words in a corridor on the seventeenth of June about the price of a word, and he had sat at that table for four years and been fed by every woman in it.

The room agreed. That is what the gift does and it does not stop at the end of the four bars. About six of the nine agreed with it inside about two seconds and one of them agreed so hard that her hands went flat on the wood, and Beatrix Halm looked at her and did not say one word.

## And Then The Woman Who Cannot Be Compelled Answered

"I am going to say what is wrong with that," said Agnete Lindqvist, "and I would like it noted that I have been outside a room for eleven years until the twenty-seventh of July and I have not got better at this, and I have not got worse either."

She put her hands in her lap.

"It is not true, and it is not unfair, and both of those are the same thing in a room like this one. He has never once made one of us carry anything. In four years I cannot find one occasion. I came to this city with a rank-four clerkship and no name in any list and I slept in that building and nobody ever made me prove it."

"Agnete—"

"And on the twenty-first of June he wrote thirty-eight words into a minute that put him in a corridor outside the door of every matter about this address, for ever, and he did it in nine minutes because he wanted six of you not to have to be fetched through a man." She looked round the table. "It has cost him. It has not cost us one hour."

"I was in the yard behind that building on the thirtieth of July telling him what I thought of him and I have been wrong about him since about half past twelve and I have not told anybody because it is mine."

The kitchen went quiet in the way it went quiet.

"Eleven hundred of my people were in a room about this on the twenty-eighth of July," said Agnete Lindqvist. "Eleven hundred out of eleven thousand. And there were about nine of us in this kitchen arguing about a door for six days, and not one of us has ever been in a room where nine thousand people were standing under a wall being discussed by somebody with a brass."

"Is that a complaint," said Nadia Ferrous.

"That is a measurement," said Agnete Lindqvist. "I gave them two hours and fifty minutes of the truth and about nine hundred people went out believing me over a man with a cough, and I would do it again on the morning it is asked of me, and I have not slept properly since the twenty-seventh and I am not going to pretend that cost me nothing."

## He Tried It Once And Was Put Down

"I have a standing of nine," said Lin Hao. "And a clause, and a brass, and a clerk who may enter and cannot correct, and I have not used one of them in this room and I am not going to."

"Good," said Runa Sato.

"I am telling you they are there so that you know the shape of what I am not using," he said. "Because it is going to look, when it happens, like a man standing in that kitchen with nothing, and I would rather nine of you knew the difference now."

"You have been standing in this kitchen with nothing since the first of July," said Runa Sato, "and we have all known it, and you telling us now is not a gift, it is a receipt."

Marguerite Vale laughed, once, which almost nobody in that room had ever made, and not one of the eight enjoyed it.

## Who Is On Whose Side

The argument did not end. It changed shape about half past five and became the thing it had been trying to become for six days, which was not about him at all.

Marguerite Vale had not spoken for two hours.

"Eleven years," she said. "Eleven years somebody promised you something and you have kept it and you do not remember who promised it and you cannot get out of it."

"Yes."

"And you were fed for eleven years out of a thing that was never earned and never recorded and has been filling for thirty years out of strangers' doorways, and you did not know, and you have been getting warmer since you came to this house." Marguerite did not raise her voice and she was not being cold; that was the terrible part.

"Somebody has been taking money out of a pot that thirty thousand people have put something into, in order to keep you warm, and has been doing it in her own hand, for eleven years, and has never once asked whether she was allowed."

"Marguerite."

"And you have come into this kitchen and told us that a rule stops you," said Marguerite Vale. "You have come in here and made it about a rule. I am not interested in the rule this evening."

"Petra has been made comfortable for eleven years out of other people's good and she cannot remember being promised and cannot get out of it and she is going to spend the rest of her life not knowing who to be angry at, and I would like somebody in this room to say one single sentence about that before we go back to arithmetic."

Beatrix Halm said, "She said one on the night of the second."

"On the night of the second she said it to him," said Marguerite Vale. "I would like to hear what the rest of us say."

## He Asked It Properly

He waited until six, and then he asked it, and he asked it in the plainest words he could find, which took him about eleven seconds to assemble.

"On the thirtieth of September a keeper writes this house out of her own head again, in her own hand, with her name at the foot of it and no certificate behind it, and that is the sixth time. And if any of you has put a hand in that pot, then I cannot empty it while you keep this address. I cannot empty it alone, ever, and I have been alone at it for four years."

"Is that the rule."

"That is the rule."

"Then that is a hell of a thing to say to a table of nine people at six o'clock in the evening," said Nadia Ferrous.

"I know what it is."

"I did," said Nadia Ferrous.

"And I am not going to," said Runa Sato.

She did not explain, and not one of them asked her twice.

"Not because I have not," she said, after a moment, in a different voice. "And not because it would take forty minutes and it would be about me. I am not going to because I have stood in this kitchen for thirteen weeks as a woman who has not filed a return, and if I say one more thing tonight that is mine then Runa Sato is a woman in this house with two of them."

She looked at the end of the table where Marguerite Vale was sitting with her hands round a cup she had not drunk from.

"Marguerite," said Lin Hao.

Nine people looked at her.

Marguerite Vale looked back at all of them, and she did not say a word, and she did not say no either, and she picked her cup up and put it down again without having drunk from it.

"She is not going to say it here," said Lin Hao, "and I have known that since the night of the second, and I am not going to make her, because a draw is a thing that declares itself and I have had it explained to me in a cold room this week by a woman who has been keeping books for twenty-nine years and she used those words."

"Which words," said Beatrix Halm.

"That a thing gets in because somebody insists, in the ordinary way, more than once."

## The Number On The Piece Of Paper

He wrote it on the back of a delivery docket at about twenty to seven and put it in his coat, and it was not a balance and it was not a count of anything and there was no column for it anywhere in this city.

Three hands. Two of them said so out loud. One of them did not.

Petra Ilyin read it upside down from the other side of the table, which she can do, and did not say anything about it, and put it back on the wood face down.

"Tomorrow," she said. "Not tonight. Tonight somebody has to wash those plates and it is not going to be me."
