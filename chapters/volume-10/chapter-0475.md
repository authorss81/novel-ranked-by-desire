# The Price Of Eleven Years

He asked for the arithmetic on the morning of the eleventh of August and got it by eleven o'clock, and the reason he got it is that nine women had been doing it in six kitchens and four rooms and one cold counting house since the sixth of August and had run out of things to do with their hands.

"One hundred and seventy-one days," said Lin Hao. "Ten from twenty leaves ten, ten with thirty and thirty and thirty and thirty is one hundred and thirty at the end of June, and July is thirty, so one hundred and sixty at the end of July, and one hundred and sixty with eleven is one hundred and seventy-one. And I am not going to ask anybody to say anything about that number."

"I am going to ask for a different one and I have never asked anybody in this room for one before."

"Say what you want," said Ines Duarte.

"What a draw is worth. In this city. In law. In a body. I have had two answers and both of them were refusals and one of them was in a cold room on the second of August and one of them was from my own mouth in June, and I want a woman with a pen to do it properly."

Ines Duarte put a clean sheet on the table. She had brought it herself, which she did about four times a year, and it was not mentioned.

## Why She Would Not Add It Up

"I am going to set it out," she said, "and I am going to leave the total off, and I am going to tell you why before you decide I have been clever, because you have all decided that about me four times and you were wrong twice."

"The sheet is clean," said Lin Hao.

"The sheet is clean and it is going to stay clean at the foot," said Ines Duarte. "Eleven years is a figure."

"Eleven years of draws against an account with no instrument is a figure I can add up in about four minutes and you would all sit here and believe it, and a figure that a room has believed and that has been written on a sheet in a house is a ledger line."

"And a ledger line against this address is a figure that somebody certifies, and there is no certificate for the quarter that began on the first of July and there was none for the one before that and there will be none on the thirtieth of September."

"Hanneke writes it from her own head."

"Hanneke writes the composition from her own head once a quarter and puts her own name under it and has a woman witness it," said Ines Duarte. "She is not going to do that for a pot of unrecorded good in a kitchen on Ferrier Street and if I asked her to she would tell me what it is worth, which is nothing, and she would be right, and then we would all be wrong about it for the rest of our lives."

She wrote the date at the top of the sheet and did not write anything under it.

"I will give you the parts. You may hold them all at once. You may not have them added up."

## The Parts

"Part one, which is Ines's, because I am the auditor and I am the only one at this table who can read a holding." She turned the sheet round. "A draw against an account with no holder is worth nothing on paper. It is not a transaction. It is not a service."

"It is not a composition and it does not alter one, and it cannot be called, cannot be reconciled, and cannot be answered, and no column in this city will ever take it and there never has been one. If a woman in this house drew against that pot every working day for eleven years, then on paper for eleven years this house would be exactly what it is on paper now."

"Which is."

"Seven, out of a band, under a floor, with eight people asleep under the roof and no certificate behind any of it," said Ines Duarte. "That is what it is worth. Nothing. And the reason that is a monstrous sentence and not a merciful one is that the paper is the only thing in this city that is not a liar."

"Part two, which is Beatrix's, because she counts and she does not know how to stop."

## What Beatrix Felt And Cannot Put At A Minute

"I felt it on the night of the second," said Beatrix Halm. "All nine of us, at that table, and it came up through the floor. It was the same shape as a line on a tram that has four hundred people on it and not one of them knows. I have got nine years of that and I could tell you the minute of every one of them."

"And the night of the second."

"I cannot tell you the minute of that night," said Beatrix Halm, "and I have been trying since the third of August and I am not going to invent one, and this is the same rule I gave you on a roof and it has not changed and I am not going to improve a report after it has gone out."

"So it did not happen."

"It happened," said Beatrix Halm. "Nine women at a table and the whole room changed and I was the only one in it who could tell you that it had. I know what it is and I do not know when it is and I am telling you that in front of nine people and I am not going to be pressed on it afterwards by anybody at this table, including him."

"That is fair," said Lin Hao.

"It is more than fair, it is the only thing I have got."

## Part Three, Which Is Runa's, And Which Has No Figure In It

"Part three," said Runa Sato, "is mine and there is no figure in it and I am not going to supply one."

"Runa—"

"You have known since the sixth of August that I am not going to do this and I have known for four years why, and I am not going to explain it twice." She put her forearms on the table. "What I carried for four years was not a figure."

"It was a division in Cordwain with a space on a board under my name and nothing in it, and a rank-six field operative who had stopped filing and had told herself she had stopped filing because she was tired."

"And what did you actually carry out of that pot."

"About four pounds and a spine," said Runa Sato, "and the four pounds went in a way I am not describing and the spine is mine and will be mine when I am forty and I am twenty-nine and I have stopped discussing it, which is in my file and is my own handwriting and I wrote it there on purpose so that it could never be described as somebody else's doing."

"Isolde."

"I am not going to be priced either and I will tell you why, and it is because I sing it." Isolde Marchetti had both hands flat on the wood. "When I sing four bars in a room the room agrees. That is the gift and it is the price and they are the same thing."

"If I say a number about this, then nine people in a kitchen agree with that number in about two seconds and none of them will ever be able to say how they came to hold it, and every one of them will hold it for the rest of their life."

"That is the opposite of a number."

"That is exactly what it is," said Isolde Marchetti, "and I have been the opposite of a number for a long time and I am not going to start tonight."

## Who Has Been Carrying It

And then Beatrix Halm said, "Somebody say it. All of us. In order. Because you are all doing it one at a time and he is going to leave this table having been told nine things that are not the same size, and one of them is going to be twice the size of the other eight and I would like to know which one before I am forty."

It took nineteen minutes and it went round the table and not one of them interrupted another.

Marguerite Vale carried it in her hand, for eleven years, out of a pot that thirty years of strangers had been filling, and did not know whose hand it was that filled.

Runa Sato carried it in her back, for four years, and filed nothing.

Nadia Ferrous carried it in a shaft between two floors, about forty times, and told it nine hundred times so that not one person in this city would ever have to believe her.

Ines Duarte carried it in her head, out of every instrument this house has touched since the twenty-seventh of April, and had not told one person in this room a single figure she had read.

Beatrix Halm carried it as a thing she could feel from a moving tram and could not put at a minute.

Tessa Okonkwo carried it as four hundred words she had not said since the fifth of July.

Agnete Lindqvist carried it as eleven hundred people out of eleven thousand, and had been outside a room for three hours in August because of it, and would do it again on the morning it was asked.

Isolde Marchetti carried it as the fact that if she said a number then it would be true in nine people's mouths and it would not be hers.

Lin Hao carried it alone, for four years, and that was the whole of his pride and it had cost him a household.

## Petra Ilyin Said The Thing That Had Not Been Known

And Petra Ilyin, who had been at the stove end of that table for nineteen minutes without speaking, said:

"You have all got it wrong."

"Not one of those is wrong."

"Every one of those is wrong," said Petra Ilyin, "and all of you have been assuming the same wrong thing about eleven years, and I have let you, because not one of you has ever asked me and because the person who could have asked me was the person it was about."

She put her hands flat on the wood.

"A sworn promise binds my body," she said. "Not my word. My body. It does not bind me to take anything. It binds me to give."

The kitchen went very quiet.

"Eleven years," said Petra Ilyin. "Eleven years and I have been under a promise and I have been fed and warmed and housed and put first in about four hundred small ways by a table that has never once been told, and none of that is the promise. None of it. I did not ask for any of it and I have not been able to refuse any of it and I did not know that those were two different facts until about four days ago."

"Petra—"

"I have not been able to say no to anybody in eleven years," said Petra Ilyin. "Not to a man in a bay, not to a woman in a room, not to a guild, not to a plate of anything at this table."

"I said no to an attestation at a doorway in April and that took me about eleven seconds to do not say no to and it is the hardest thing my body has ever been asked to do and it is the only thing anybody has ever asked me to do."

She looked at Nadia Ferrous.

"You have been going at me for four years about the eleventh of May."

"You have been going at me about that day since the eleventh of May," said Nadia Ferrous, "and you have never once told me why, and I have had four years of it."

"I could not tell you why," said Petra Ilyin, "because I did not know there was a why. I had a thing in me and no reason and no maker and no instrument, and if anybody had asked me why I would have told them that I did not know and they would have thought I was lying."

## One At A Time

"Petra," said Lin Hao. "Can it come off."

"No."

"Can anything go on top of it."

"No," said Petra Ilyin. "That is the thing I have been saying in my own mouth for eleven years and not one person has ever asked me to say it in front of other people. A binding instrument is one at a time."

"Mine has not been discharged and the only thing in this city that can discharge it is the man who holds it, and I have spent eleven years failing to find him, and I am telling you the whole of it because it is the only thing I have and you may as well have it in one piece."

"He swore," said Beatrix Halm. "A promise gets sworn."

"It gets sworn and it gets discharged and it gets recorded and one of those three things happened and I have been sitting in a house for eleven years with a promise in my body and not one word of paper anywhere."

"And on the fourteenth of August," said Lin Hao, "there are going to be three of you who have put a hand in that pot, and all three of you will have taken cold minutes yourselves, and every one of you will be able to say exactly what it was worth, and Petra — you will not be able to say no to any of them."

"Then they will not ask me," said Petra Ilyin.

## What She Said At The End

The kitchen went back to being a kitchen at about one o'clock, and somebody washed the plates, and at about twenty past one Petra Ilyin stood up to dry them and put the cloth down without drying anything and said:

"Right. One more thing, and then I finish that plate, and I am not going to say it again in this house."

Nine people stopped.

"I cannot remember who promised me," said Petra Ilyin. "I have gone at it for eleven years and I am telling you the true shape of it and not the sad shape of it."

"I do not remember his face and I do not remember his voice and I do not remember whether he was in a room or a doorway, and it is not that the memory has been taken out of me, because nobody took anything, I have simply never once had it in the first place."

"Petra."

"And I have decided something this morning and I am going to tell you what it is before any of you can guess at it," she said. "Eleven years of something I cannot remember agreeing to, and a body that cannot refuse, and a table that has never made me ask for anything, and a pot that has been paying for me out of strangers' doorways since before I came to this city."

She picked the cloth up again.

"If there is a thing in that pot that can be given back to a person, then I would like to know what it is and I would like to be allowed to ask," said Petra Ilyin. "That is the whole of what I have wanted out of the last four days and I have not got the vocabulary for it and that is why it took me a plate."
