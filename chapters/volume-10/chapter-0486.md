# The Account Opened In The Square

The room at the end of a corridor off a second landing sat at about two o'clock in the afternoon on the first of October, and it had nine thousand people under the wall outside it on an ordinary afternoon, and about four hundred of them had come on purpose, and about forty were standing still.

He was in the corridor for the first hour and ten minutes of it because of a clause of thirty-eight words he had entered himself.

"Two hundred and twenty-one," said Yusuf Adeyemi, at the door. "One hundred and sixty at the end of July, one hundred and ninety at the end of August, thirty of September, and the first. That is the last time anybody in this building will say a number to you for a while."

"That is the last one."

## Two O'Clock

There were five at the table and a chair that was not one of the nine at the end of it. Alma Quayle was in it in her coat with her division's own paperwork on her knees, and she had been there since about twenty to two, and she had not spoken in a sitting in that building since the twelfth of December.

The clerk read the quarterly. The requisition of the twentieth of February, one hundred and thirty days old at the end of June and two hundred and twenty-one days old at that table, carried under five seals on the thirtieth of June and administered weekly against the persons keeping the composition of premises thirty-four, dash, six, dash, one-one-eight, and the seventh delivery had gone out on the twenty-sixth of September to nine persons.

Then she read the claim of the Church of the Quiet Number, entered on the twenty-ninth of July, three hundred and ninety words, and the hearing record of four lines and an hour kept in a building off a third court, and the answer given by a man on the thirtieth of July, and the standing sheet, which carried the address at the head of every line.

And then the item of business, which was the fifth.

"Disposition," said the clerk.

Anouk Ferreira got up to make it, which she does not do, and which she had decided about in July.

## The Argument, In Full

"A man handed this city's unrecorded merit to a stranger and then vanished," said Anouk Ferreira. "That is what happened, three years ago, and there is no argument in this room about the fact of it, and I have never once pretended otherwise and neither have you."

"The pot did not fill itself. It was given, once, deliberately, by a person with a whole life's work in his hands, to a stranger who had not asked for it. And it has been filling for thirty years since, out of doorways, by people who did the same thing on a smaller scale and did not know it."

She put one hand on the table.

"I have run this city's numbers for twenty years. I have held it still through two collapses and a shortage and a classification error that put four hundred people in the wrong building."

"I want to say something about that before I say anything else, and I want it said by me and not by him. I have held it still because there are nine people in it who are countable. Nine. Not nine hundred and not nine thousand."

"Nine, and every one of them answerable, and every one of them replaceable by a vote, and every one of them removable."

"That is the whole of what I have. If the nine stop being countable then this city has no floor under it and everything that happened in the spring of a year four years ago happens again, and this time in about nine streets at once."

She looked along the table and not at him.

"Here is the case. A man is standing at a wall in the open in the afternoon of the first with his own name on a thing, and he is going to give away the unrecorded merit of a city. That is what he has said he will do and I have not tried to stop him and I have no instrument that could."

"Now. Every person who has ever been helped in a doorway by a stranger has just been handed something back, and there are about five of them for every one in his pot, and not one of them knows it, and not one of them was ever written down, and five people helped for nothing in a doorway is the single most ordinary act in Ostara and it is the foundation of everything in it."

"And that man is going to decide, on the first of October, in about nine seconds, on behalf of all of them, who was owed what, and in what order, and by what measure, and he will do it in the open so that it looks clean."

"And not one person in a square of nine thousand will be able to tell you afterward whether they were given something or had something taken off them, because not one instrument in this city can answer that question and none ever has."

She was quiet for about two seconds.

"And that is not the worst part. The worst part is that the alternative to a man doing that is not nothing. The alternative is us. Nine people in a room, deciding who was owed what, on behalf of a million people who are not in the room and have not been asked."

"You do not need a pot and a wall for that. You need a ledger and a desk and four hours, and you have all three, and I have had them for twenty years, and I want the man at the end of this table to understand that when I say *we would decide* I am not describing an abstraction. I am describing the next week."

## What She Is Right About

"That is the argument," she said, "and I am going to leave it in the room and I am not going to summarise it, because a summary is how a correct argument gets made to sound like an unfair one, and I have watched that happen in nine hundred rooms."

"So let me put the three things I know to be true about it and let the five of you take them or leave them."

"One. He cannot say who it belongs to. Neither can I, and neither can the Church, and the Church knows it and has said so in three hundred and ninety words and then attached a better answer to it."

"Two. The form of a thing given away in the open is not a gift. A gift has a giver. What he has on the first is a distribution, and a distribution is administered, and the moment it is administered there is a desk and a column and a keeper, and I have spent twenty years watching that column arrive and I have never once been able to stop it."

"Three, and this is the one I would like him to hold on to, because it is the only one of the three he has never argued with."

"On the second of October there will be a person in this city who asks an auditor a question about that address, and there will be no figure on the address to answer it with, and she will be refused. Not in four years. On the second."

"And there will be no instrument, no sheet, no appeal and no remedy, and that is what the price of a wall in the open actually is, and it is not rank and it is not money and it is not a cough."

She sat down.

"And I am right. I would like that entered in the minute in those words by whoever is responsible for entering things, and I have said I am right before a room of five so that nobody in this city can tell you afterwards that a woman in a chair argued against it."

## What The Room Has Got

"That is the case," said the chair. "Yusuf."

"There is one disposition the room can make on a quarterly," said Yusuf Adeyemi, "and I am going to state it because I drafted the wording of it in July out of four hundred precedents and I am not going to pretend to be surprised that it has turned up."

"The majority can constitute a custody. Three parts, and they are the whole of it, and they are what any person in this city would call the proper way to do such a thing."

"A room. Quiet, at the Terrace, with a keeper and a door, and nothing done in it and not one person in it after dark. An account, held, at a figure, not drawable on by anybody and not closable by anybody while it is held, so that what is in it is still there in November and the month after and the year after that, and does not go out into a city in four days and cannot be taken back by whoever is standing nearest to it."

"And a form. One sheet. On which a person may enter, once, one thing they did that no instrument witnessed, in their own hand, with their own name on it. That name is never read by an auditor and is never entered anywhere else, and that is not a defect in the form. That is the entire service the form does."

He put the paper face down.

"That is the whole of the machinery of this city. It has been used about nine hundred times in thirty years and it has never once been cruel and it has never once been enough, and it is not mine and it is not the chair's and it is not any one of us's, and it is the only instrument in Ostara that can touch that account on a quarterly, and it was in a drawer in this building from the first week of July until this morning."

"It requires a holder," said Anouk Ferreira. "A custody is a holder. Somebody has to be the person it is held by, in a hand, on a sheet, or it is not a custody, it is a piece of paper about a custody."

"Yes."

"And there is one person in this city who could be that person and he is not going to take it and I have known that since the third of July and I have not asked him about it once."

"I am not taking it," said Lin Hao.

"Then that is the end of the item," said Yusuf Adeyemi, "and I have stated it because it is the only disposition on the table and a room that has had a thing in front of it for two hours has to have put the thing it has got on the record."

## What He Said

"No," said Lin Hao.

"You have not heard it refused yet."

"I have had it in a drawer since the first of July and I have known what was in it since the sixth of September," he said. "I am going to say the four reasons and then I am going to stop."

"Two hundred and ten of them, and I am going to use four."

"One. A quiet room, a held account and a form is a decision that nine people make in a building on behalf of a million people who are not in the building. That is the whole objection and I am not dressing it up."

"That is not an objection," said Ferreira. "That is the description. What is the objection."

"Two. It is better than doing it in the open. It is enormously better. It is the best answer this city has and you are right that it is not cruel and I have not one word against it, and if I had to choose between your machinery and a wall I would choose your machinery, and I am not going to choose it, and you are going to have to stand there and be told why by a man who thinks you are right."

"Then why."

"Because I do not want to be the person who decides," said Lin Hao. "Not in a room, not at a wall, not in the open. I have spent four years being the person who decides and every single thing that has gone wrong in my life has gone wrong because I was the person who decided, and I am not going to put a million people behind my shoulder on the afternoon of the first and call it honesty."

"That is vanity."

"That is the opposite of vanity and I know the difference better than anybody in this city," he said. "Vanity is wanting to be watched. I do not want to be watched. I want nine women to be doing it and for me to be the reason."

## Will You Be Bound By It

"Chair," said Yusuf Adeyemi.

"The chair will ask one thing," said Anouk Ferreira. "Are you bound by it."

"Yes."

"You understand that the room cannot compel a person in a square."

"The room cannot compel anybody anywhere," said Ferreira, "and it has never been able to and it is going to be dead in about four years, and it will not matter, because if you say you are bound by it then in about nine days this city will know that you are a man who says what he will do and does it, and that is the only currency the Chair has ever actually held."

"Yes," said Lin Hao. "I am bound by it."

"Then I move that the room does not adjourn," said Anouk Ferreira, "until the quarterly is sealed, and I will tell you why I have moved it, and it is not a trap."

She looked round the table. "I have been wrong in this room before and I have never once found out which decision was the wrong one, because everything adjourns at ten past three and the minute goes out at four with the standing sheet, and by the time anybody has read it the thing has already happened somewhere in the city without us."

"The clerk may only enter and cannot be asked to hurry," said the clerk.

"Then we will wait on the clerk," said Ferreira, "which is the first time anybody in this building has ever said that to me and I am not going to waste it."

## Ten Minutes To Four

There is a public counter in the ground floor of that building and it opens at eight and closes at four and there was a queue of about nine people in it, and he went down the stairs past all of them and stood at the back of it.

"You have been in this queue before," said the woman at the desk.

"Ninety seconds," said Lin Hao. "No instrument, no witness, no holder, no receipt and no copy. And it is the whole of it and it is not reversible and nothing in this city can top it up."

"That is what a consignment is, sir."

He wrote it in his own hand at the front of nine people in a queue, and it took ninety seconds, and he did not fill it in wrong.

The figure it took was the figure of a standing of nine, and a standing of nine is the whole of what he had, and when it went into a keeping book at about ten minutes to four on the afternoon of the first of October nothing else of him was anywhere in the city.

What he was afterwards was the first band. The first band has no floor. There is a subsistence that is guaranteed and an assigned work that is not his to choose the hours of, and he had put himself into the first band at a public counter in his own hand in a queue, and no instrument in this city can put him anywhere else.

## The Amendment

They went up at about ten past four and the quarterly was on the table with the seals out and the clerk had her book open, and the room had waited on the clerk.

"There is one item of business and it is under my hand and it is not a finding," said Lin Hao. "A member may speak and the clerk enters. I am going to speak and you are going to enter and you are not going to improve it and you are not going to be asked to hurry."

"Yes," said the clerk.

He took the sheet off Yusuf Adeyemi and read it out, once, in the flat voice of a man reading an address.

"That the standing sheet of this quarter, at the line carrying premises thirty-four, dash, six, dash, one-one-eight, be amended at the foot of the page and in the same session, and without appeal and without a second session until the first of January."

"To remove from the record of this city the file opened on the ninth of March, bearing the number it bears and the name of the man it names, Lin Wei, together with the transfer receipt, the index and the four lines; the same being removed permanently, and being not restorable by any standing, any seal or any instrument hereafter."

"And the same having been closed at about ten minutes to four this afternoon by the exhaustion of the only standing in Ostara that stood upon it."

The clerk wrote it down. She wrote it in full and she did not look up and she did not improve it, and when she had finished she read it back, because that is done, and she read the name of his brother once in a room of five people and a chair.

Not one of them said anything for about eleven seconds.

"That is a nail in a document," said Yusuf Adeyemi, quietly, to the room.

"It is a nail in a document," said the chair.

And Anouk Ferreira took the pen off the table and signed under it, in her own hand, with her own name, and put the pen down, and did not look at the man who had read it.

"You will want to know why I signed it," she said, when the room had sat down again.

"I will not ask you in this room."

"Then I will tell you in this room," said Ferreira, "and you may take it into the corridor or leave it."

"I signed it because it is correct, and because you said it out loud, and because if I had refused to sign it then in about four years a stranger with a folder would come to a desk and open that file on the grounds that it had been closed by a mistake. And they would be able to do that, because nothing in this city would stand in their way."

"A signature prevents that. That is what a signature is for. It is not for the man who signs it and it is not for the man it is about."

She looked up. "And I would like it observed, in the minute, that I argued that case at two o'clock this afternoon and I am signing at ten past four, and that I have not withdrawn one word of it."

"And that a room which loses an argument and then makes the losing permanent is doing the whole of what it exists for."

## The Wall

He walked from the end of a second corridor to the north end of the Reading and it is about a mile and he took fifty-one minutes, and he was at the wall at about eleven minutes past five and there were about nine thousand people under it.

About four hundred of them were standing in one place because a chaplain of twenty years had told them to and because they had come on purpose. About forty of them had stopped. The rest were going to the trams.

She was there at about nine feet from the stone with her hands in her sleeves and her own four hundred behind her and she did not move for the whole of it and she did not put a hand on anything, and that was the entire argument she has made since the twenty-ninth of July and she made it by standing still.

He got his hand out and could not put it anywhere.

That is the thing about it, and he said it out loud to four hundred people, and it is the only sentence he said that day that anybody has ever written down.

"I cannot empty it," said Lin Hao. "On the fourteenth of August three women in a kitchen drew against it and under the law of a standing household I may never empty it again, and there is no instrument in this city that can undo that, and I told nine women that at a table and I have told no one else until now."

The square did not make a sound.

"So I am not going to do it," he said, "and not one person in this square is going to watch a man be brave in the open on an ordinary afternoon, because I cannot, and I have been carrying that since the eleventh of August and I have not once put it in a sentence that goes anywhere."

He put his hands in his coat.

"That is what the whole thing is," he said. "It is not a thing I can do. It is a thing nine women can do, and every one of them is standing in this square and can be named, and if you want to know who decided then you can walk over there and ask them and they will tell you the date and the hour and how many of them there were."

## The Nine Of Them

Beatrix Halm got there at about eleven minutes past five because a tram had been held up at Ferrier Street by nine women getting off it, and she had her notebook under her arm and she did not open it.

Marguerite Vale was already there at the rail. Petra Ilyin came up the hill from the bottom on foot because it was faster. Nadia Ferrous was not there and then she was there, and not one person saw her arrive, and she has never once in her life been able to go up and that has never once stopped her being in a room.

Runa Sato. Ines Duarte. Isolde Marchetti, who had come down off the Bellhouse with her coat over her guild dress and her hair wet.

Eight women.

The ninth was standing nine feet off with her hands in her sleeves and four hundred of her own behind her and she did not move and she did not put a hand on it, and she said, to Beatrix Halm, in a voice about eleven inches long:

"I will not, and I will not, and do not look at me."

"That is what the eight decided on the twenty-sixth of August," said Beatrix Halm, "and you said why out loud, and there is a sheet in a corridor with it on, and I am going to read it out now in front of four hundred people because a clerk took it at four o'clock yesterday and could not put it anywhere and this is the only place left."

She read it out.

Nine names and one date and the words: *and the one against has said why out loud.*

Then eight women put their hands on the stone of the ranks wall under a painted card at about eleven minutes past five in the afternoon on the first of October, and the man at the north end of it stood four feet away with his hands in his coat, and nine thousand people under a wall on an ordinary afternoon saw nothing happen at all.

Then something happened everywhere.

## What Went Out

It did not go out to a list. There has never been a list, there could not be one now, and not one person alive could build one, and that is why thirty years of strangers could put a thing into a pot and walk away feeling fine.

It went out into the whole of the city at once, to anybody standing still in a street at about eleven minutes past five on the first of October, whether or not they had ever put a grain into it and whether or not they knew anybody who had.

A cold minute, one at a time, for about four seconds, in about a million places, over four days.

Roughly one million four hundred thousand grains went out of it.

And the chest went with it. He felt it go about a second and a half after the wall, standing four feet away with his hands in his coat, and it was not cold, it was the lifting of a weight off a shelf that has been in a room too long, and it went further than anything that has ever come out of him, and after about nine seconds he put his hand on the rail of the wall and stayed there.

Every permission he has ever bought in this city went in the same hour and went in the same way. The brass was in a coat and the coat was on a chair and the standing was in a keeping book and the figure it stood against was eight, written out of a woman's own memory at half past four yesterday afternoon with nothing behind it.

He was the first band. He had no floor and no band floor to be out of, and not one person in that square knew what had just happened to him, and no instrument in Ostara could have told them.

And the requisition of the twentieth of February was two hundred and twenty-one days old, and it was administered weekly against the persons keeping the composition of premises thirty-four, dash, six, dash, one-one-eight.

And on the second of October there would be a division in this city that would send a clerk up two floors to ask a woman at that address a question, and she would be refused. The clause of thirty-eight words would be the reason, and it would be the best thing he ever did and it would not be worth anything.

## Nine Feet Of Stone

Agnete Lindqvist did not move for about four seconds and then she turned round to her own four hundred and said four words in the voice of a woman holding a room, and four hundred people in a square of nine thousand said them back, and it made a noise, and it was the only organised sound in the Reading on the first of October.

That it cannot be given back. Because there is no list of what it is made of.

She came back and stood at the nine feet.

"You were right in July," she said.

"I know."

"You were right in July and you were right on the twenty-ninth, and I said so to four hundred people at the end of August, and I am going to keep saying it for as long as there is a Vestry in this city," said Agnete Lindqvist.

"And you did it anyway, and you did it with eight women and not one of mine, and I stood here and let you, and I want you to hear one sentence from me and then I am going to go and stand over there again for the rest of the afternoon."

"Say it."

"That is the first time in twenty years that I have watched the right thing happen and been on the wrong side of it," said Agnete Lindqvist, "and I did not find out until about eleven minutes past five this afternoon, and I would have liked to have found out before."

## Afterwards

He was at the wall until about half past six because his chest was going and he did not trust it, and Tessa Okonkwo had a table of her own at the north end by the Vestry gate from about ten in the morning with two borrowed trestles and a blanket over the top of it, and about two hundred people had been brought to it, and she had a thirtieth of what was going past them and not more, and she did not look at him once.

At about half past six he walked back up the hill and eight women were in a kitchen off Ferrier Street and one of them was at the stove and one of them was washing up and one of them was sitting on the floor with her back against it because her hands were not working.

Nobody said anything about it.

He put his coat on the back of a chair, because there is a chair in that kitchen that has never had his coat on it and has not had one since the fourteenth of December, and at about seven o'clock somebody came to the door of that house on Ferrier Street and asked whether there was anything here to sign.

"There is not," said Lin Hao.

The woman stood in the doorway for about eleven seconds and then she said thank you and went away down the hill, and he shut the door and stood in a passage with a cough in his chest that is going to be there until he is dead and a first band of standing that is going to have assigned work in it and not one figure anywhere in Ostara that could have told a stranger what he had done and who had done it.

And in a building off a third court, a hearing record of four lines and an hour sat on a shelf where it had been sitting since the twenty-eighth of July. It carried no seal, it could not be ordered, it could not be classified, and it could not be read at a valuation.

And on the second of October there would be a great many people in this city who felt something for about four seconds and could not point at it, and not one office in Ostara could enter the sentence.

And it was the thirtieth of July, and it was a fact, and it was going on a wall in the morning of the eighth of October, and almost nobody in Ostara would know what it was for.
