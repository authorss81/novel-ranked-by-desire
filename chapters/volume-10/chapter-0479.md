# Petra Under A Promise She Cannot Name

She asked him to come out to the yard at about nine on the evening of the twenty-third of August, and she asked him in the ordinary way at the stove door, and he said yes before she had finished the sentence, and then stood there for a second and worked out what he had just done.

"One hundred and eighty-three days," he said. "Ten from twenty leaves ten, ten with thirty and thirty and thirty and thirty is one hundred and thirty at the end of June, and July is thirty, so one hundred and sixty at the end of July, and one hundred and sixty with twenty-three is one hundred and eighty-three."

"Lin Hao."

"I said it anyway. I have broken my own rule four times this month and I have just made it five and I am going to stop."

## What He Worked Out On The Way Down The Stairs

There were two stairs between that door and the yard and he worked out on the first of them that he had not asked her a question in eleven years.

Not that she refused. That she could not. A sworn promise binds her body to give, and being asked to come out to a yard is a small thing, and a small thing is a thing, and not one door in her can be shut against a request.

He had spent four years being a man who could not make anybody do anything, and he had spent nine years resenting it, and on the second stair he worked out that he has been living in a house with a woman who could not refuse him for eleven years and neither of them had ever once mentioned it.

## The Yard And A Stool

She had a stool out and a blanket over her knees and a cup of something she was not drinking, and she was in guild cloth with her sleeves pushed up.

"Sit down," said Petra Ilyin. "You are standing up because you have not worked out whether this is an interview and it is not. It is the opposite of an interview and I have never done the opposite of one before."

"I have got four questions."

"I know. I have had them since the eleventh." She turned the cup round. "Ask the first one and do not dress it up."

"Eleven years," said Lin Hao. "What is actually still on you."

"Giving." She said it as if she were correcting him on a figure. "Not receiving. You wrote it in your own notes wrong and everybody in this house has been writing it wrong for six days and I have let you, because the wrong version is nicer and I have not had the energy for the right one until the eleventh."

"That is the whole of it."

"Wrong," said Petra Ilyin, "and here is what not one person has thought to ask, including you, including a woman who keeps books for a living and a woman who has read every instrument in this house since April. If the promise is to give, then the promise has a limit in it. It does not say give forever."

"It says give, and a body that has given for eleven years does not have the same amount left in it that it had, and on the afternoon of the fourteenth in a kitchen I found out where the line is."

"What happened."

"I gave eleven years of somebody else's cost back to the pot," said Petra Ilyin, "and it went through the promise, and the promise held, and afterwards I could not put my hand on a hot pan."

## What A Binding Instrument Is

"I am going to tell you the rest of it in the order it comes," she said, "and I am telling you the order because you asked me once, in a doorway, to name the price before I charged it, and you were right then and you are right now, and I have remembered that you were right for about nine years."

"Go on."

"A promise binds my body to give. That is what my gift is and nothing else about it that anybody would call magic, and I said that to you in a doorway and you wrote it down." She looked at the yard wall. "A binding instrument is one at a time. Mine is on. It has been on for eleven years and it has never been discharged and the only thing in this city that can discharge it is the man who holds it."

"You do not know who he is."

"I do not know who he is," said Petra Ilyin, "and I have told you that before and I am telling you it again because I want it entered in this conversation in a room where you are the only other person and no keeper is standing in it to put anything in a book."

## What She Was About To Want

"And I am going to want a second one," she said.

He did not say anything.

"You may say no."

"I am not going to say no to that," said Lin Hao. "Tell me the rest of it."

"A binding instrument is one at a time, so I cannot have a second one until the first is lifted, and the first cannot be lifted by anybody except him, and I cannot find him, and you have four years of finding things in this city and you will tell me whether that is a thing anybody can do."

"No," he said. "It is not."

"No," said Petra Ilyin. "I have had two of the eight women in this house read every instrument this household has touched since the twenty-seventh of April and not one of them found a name and one of them read a file that does not exist. There is not one thing in this city with his face on it."

"I have been going at it since about the third year and I am not going to stop going at it and I have stopped expecting it, and those are two different states and I can live in both of them."

"So a second instrument is not available to you."

"A second instrument is not available to me," said Petra Ilyin, "and I am going to want one anyway, and I have been not wanting it for about three days and I have got to about as long as I can hold that."

## Why She Wants One That Cannot Exist

She was quiet for about eleven seconds.

"You do not need a reason," she said. "You are about to give me one and I am telling you now that you are about to give me one and that it will not be a reason, it will be a way of feeling better, and I have had a week of those from you and I would rather not have another."

"Then I will keep it to myself."

"Keep it to yourself and listen to this," said Petra Ilyin. "I do not want to be released. That is not what a second promise would be for and I want it said in this yard tonight before anybody starts being kind at me."

She put both hands on her knees.

"I want something to hold the first one down with. Eleven years of a promise to give, and a body that has nothing left to give with, and the afternoon of the fourteenth."

"If I stand in a kitchen in October and somebody hands me a plate, my hands are going to do what they do, and whatever they do is going to be the promise and not me, and there is no second instrument in this world that makes that different because a second instrument is still a promise to give, and the only thing that would make it different is a promise that says *stop*, and I cannot swear one of those at myself."

Not one of them said anything for about twenty seconds.

"That is the whole of it," said Petra Ilyin. "I have wanted to say it since the sixth of August and I have got it out in a yard at nine o'clock at night because you are the only person in this city who is allowed to be told and I have not had the terms for a conversation like this since a bay off Cordwain in the spring."

## He Offered And Was Refused

"I could swear one," said Lin Hao. "I have never sworn anything in my life and I could stand up in that kitchen tomorrow morning and put a promise on you and it would hold your body exactly the way his holds it, because it would be the same instrument and there is only one of them."

"I know you could," said Petra Ilyin.

"Then say why not."

"Because you are not him," said Petra Ilyin, "and a second promise from a man who is not the one who holds the first one is not a second instrument. It is a present."

"And I have spent eleven years in a house where I have been given things and I cannot take a present and call it a chain, because the second I call it a chain it is a chain with a man on the end of it, and then in about four years you will be the thing I am standing at the end of instead of the thing I am standing at the end of now."

"That is not what I was offering."

"I know exactly what you were offering," said Petra Ilyin, "and it was the best thing anybody in this city has said to me in four years, and it is still a present and I am not going to have it and I would like you to be a man about it and not write me a list of reasons why I should."

He was quiet for about four seconds.

"Right," he said.

"Right," said Petra Ilyin, and drank about a mouthful of the thing she had not been drinking.

## The Paper

At about half past nine she got up off the stool and went in and came out again with a sheet of the kind that comes off a docket roll at the Exchange, and she had asked Runa Sato for it a week earlier and Runa Sato had given it to her without one word, which Runa Sato has never done about anything.

"I am going to write it down," said Petra Ilyin. "That is all. I want the words on a piece of paper in this house."

"What words."

"Eleven years. A promise to give. No instrument. No maker I can find. A body with nothing left in it." She put the sheet on the stone of the yard table and put the pen down beside it.

"And it is going into my own hand with my name at the foot of it, and it will not bind me of anything, because a written promise is not a sworn one and everybody in this city knows the difference and that is the whole of what I want."

"Who is it addressed to."

"That is the part I have not got," said Petra Ilyin. "And I have thought about that for three days and there are two answers and I do not like either of them."

"Which are."

"One is that it is addressed to him and I do not know where he is, and if he ever comes back to this city and somebody gives it to him then it is a piece of paper he will put in a drawer, and it will be worth exactly nothing, and it will be the only thing in thirty years that anybody who put anything into that pot ever got written down about."

"And the other."

"The other is that it is addressed to the house," said Petra Ilyin.

"And it is not addressed to him, and it is not addressed to you, and it is not addressed to the nine of us, because in about five weeks a keeper is going to write this address out of her own head in her own hand with her own name at the foot of a block and no certificate stands behind any of the six of those."

"And if I write a piece of paper before the thirtieth of September then in five weeks there is a woman in a cold room who has a sheet with a line in it and a name in a block and nothing behind either of them."

She looked at him.

"That is the two answers and I do not like either and I am going to write it anyway, and I am writing it at about half past nine on the twenty-third of August in a yard off Ferrier Street because if I do not do it tonight I will not do it, and I have known that about myself since about three days ago."

She picked the pen up.

"Read it afterwards," said Petra Ilyin. "Not while. And do not correct anything, because nothing in it is a thing a person of this house is in a position to correct."
