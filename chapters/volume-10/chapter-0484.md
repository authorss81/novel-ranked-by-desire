# The Amendment On The Same Page

Yusuf Adeyemi had a room off a third court with no window worth the name and he had asked him to come at about ten in the morning on the twenty-fourth of September, and he had asked him to come because he was the man who held the procedural chair and it was his instrument and it was the only defence there was.

"One hundred and ninety of August and twenty-four," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "Two hundred and fourteen. One hundred and sixty at the end of July, one hundred and ninety at the end of August, and one hundred and ninety with twenty-four is two hundred and fourteen, and you have said that number to me before and I have never once known why you say it."

"It is the only arithmetic in my head that anybody else also has."

"Then say it on the first of October," said Adeyemi, "because on the first it will be two hundred and twenty-one and there are nine people in a room who will have done the same sum, and you will not be the only person in Ostara who has an opinion about a number."

## What A Page Can Say

He had a quarterly form on the desk with nothing in it and a pen beside it and he had not written on it.

"I am going to show you the thing that nobody in this city knows, and I am showing you four days early and on purpose, because in about nine days five other people at that table will arrive at it by themselves, and I would rather you had it from me than from a clerk."

"Say it."

"A quarterly is one page," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "One page, one heading, one date, one finding, one seal. Not two pages. A page cannot have a continuation because a continuation is a document and a document is a thing that can be produced on its own in about four years by a stranger with a folder."

"And an amendment."

"An amendment goes on the same page," he said. "Under the finding. Over the seal's room, in the margin, in the same hand of the clerk, at the making or at the sealing. And it is put by a member, and the member speaks and the clerk enters what the member said and cannot improve it and cannot be asked to hurry."

"What can it remove."

"That is the exact question and there is an exact answer," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "It can remove a thing that is in the record, permanently. Not strike a line. Not note a disagreement. Remove. A standing. A classification. A finding. A file."

"And it may not be undone."

"It may not be undone, there is no appeal, and a quarter has no second session, because the next sitting that can carry anything is the first of January." He put his thumb on the form. "That is why four people in this city have put their whole life into a room that sits once a quarter, and it is also why not one thing in this city is done in the middle of a week."

## The Thing He Had Not Known

Lin Hao had been sitting still for about eleven seconds.

"An amendment may not be written without naming the thing."

"No," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "An amendment that removes everything is not an amendment. An amendment that removes a thing names the thing exactly, in full, as the record carries it. You cannot remove *a file*. You remove a file by naming the file, and the file is an index and a name, and the name is on the Board's own index and any person may read that index at a public desk on any working morning of the week."

"I know what my brother's file is."

"Then you have no difficulty," said Adeyemi, "and I have been holding that sheet for four days waiting to see whether you would say it and you have said it in nine words, and I want you to notice that I did not ask you for it and that I would not have taken it from you if you had not offered it, because I have run a hearing room for eleven years and I know exactly what it is worth to be the man who asks."

"You did not ask."

"I did not ask and I am not going to," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "I am going to tell you the whole of the order instead, because you will have it wrong and you will have it wrong in the way that men have it wrong, which is that the amendment is the thing that closes the file."

## The Order, Which Is Fixed

"The order is fixed," said Yusuf Adeyemi, "and it is the whole of what is left for me to say to you this month."

"Go on."

"One. The thing is opened. The first of October, in the open, wherever you are going to do it, and not one word of it in a corridor."

"Two. The standing is spent. The same morning, at a counter, in your own hand, in a queue, with a pen, ninety seconds, no instrument and no witness and no holder and no receipt and no copy, exactly as you did it on the fifteenth of April at ten minutes to nine, because that is how a consignment is lodged and nothing else in this city does it that way."

"Three."

"Three is nothing. That is the point of two and it is the thing you have not thought about in four years, and I am going to say it slowly."

He leaned forward. "A standing is not a rank. A standing is a figure, and a figure stands against something, and the only thing yours has ever stood against is the composition of an address."

"And when you have lodged the whole of it in a public counter at ten minutes to four on the afternoon of the first, then not one figure anywhere in Ostara stands against anything."

"So it is gone."

"So it is spent, and spent is not gone, and spent is a word with a meaning, and the meaning is that no instrument in this city can ever be made to stand against it again," said Yusuf Adeyemi.

"And on the morning of the second of October an auditor may ask a question of that address, and there will be nothing on the address to answer it with, and she will be refused. There is nothing in this building that any person can do about it, ever, and there is no sheet for it."

## Four

"And four," said Lin Hao. "Four is the name."

"The name is not part of it. The name is the whole of it," said Yusuf Adeyemi.

"Then it is not a fourth thing. It is the whole of the other three."

"You have about nine seconds," said Yusuf Adeyemi, "to work out that I am not going to argue with you about it, and after that I am going to tell you when the file shuts."

Yusuf Adeyemi sat back. "Listen. A file does not close because of an amendment. A file closes at about ten minutes to four on the afternoon of the first, on its own, with not one person writing anything, because not one standing left in this city is left to stand on it."

"That happens whether you are in a room or in a corridor or in a square, and it happens in about nine minutes, and no clerk sees it happen."

"And the amendment."

"And the amendment is how you write it down so that in about four years a stranger cannot come to a desk with a folder and open it on the grounds that there was a mistake," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "The amendment does not do a thing. The amendment is a nail in a document so that a stranger cannot take it out again. It is the difference between a door that has fallen open and a door that has been taken off its hinges and screwed back."

He put the pen down.

"You can refuse the nail. You can open that thing on the first of October and say nothing about the file and walk away, and the file will still be shut, and about four years from now a stranger will find the thing shut and will not be able to tell whether it was shut by a man who gave it up or by an error in a division."

"And if I put the nail in."

"Then you say a brother's name out loud, in a room, into a record, and a clerk enters it and cannot improve it, and a page has no continuation, and a continuation is a document, and a document can be produced on its own, and you may never in your life speak that name in that room again." Yusuf Adeyemi looked at him. "That is the entire cost and I have priced it and it is not the worst thing you are going to do this week."

## The Yard Off Cordwain

He was in the yard at about half past two in the afternoon because Agnete Lindqvist had sent nine words to Ferrier Street at about eleven and they had been, in her hand, in the ordinary way, and one of them was *no*.

"No."

"She wants an answer."

"She wants a hundred and twenty people in a square on the first of October and she is not going to have a hundred and twenty people in a square on the first of October," said Agnete Lindqvist. "She is going to have four hundred and she is going to have them because I have told her, and not one of them is going to carry anything."

"You have told them."

"On the twenty-second I stood at the end of the yard off Cordwain for about eleven minutes and I told four hundred people of my own church what the Church's position is on the first of October."

"I told them where to stand and I told them not to bring the banner, and I told them that if a man in that square asks them what they want they are to say the same four words they said to me. The four words are that it cannot be given back, because there is no list of what it is made of."

"And you."

"I will be there," said Agnete Lindqvist, "and I will not have a hand on it, and there will be about nine feet of stone between that thing and me for four days, and I want you to understand that I have thought about that for nine days and I have arrived at it and I have not arrived at it happily."

"You were right on the twenty-ninth of July."

"I was right on the twenty-ninth of July," she said, "and I have not stopped being right, and I voted against eight women in a kitchen on the twenty-sixth of August and I have not stopped being right about that either, and I am going to be standing off from it with four hundred people behind me who are right as well. Do not come and tell me on the first that I was on your side. I will not be on your side."

## The One Person Who Would See It

They walked back up the hill and he said the last of it at the corner where the lane comes out.

"Two hundred and twenty-one days on the first of October," he said. "And then a standing at a counter, and then a name in a room."

"Yes."

"And there is one person in that room who is going to watch the order of those three things and know exactly what it is."

Yusuf Adeyemi stopped walking.

"Alma Quayle," he said. "She is recused, she will be at that table in the chair that is not one of the nine, and she will be there because she has been in that chair for every day since the fourth of December and because nobody has ever thought to move her, and she will watch a man spend a standing and then read a name and she will not be able to say one word about any of it."

"She will know."

"She will know in about four seconds and she will not be able to say one word about it, and she will keep both hands in her lap in front of nine people, and that has been her position since the twelfth of December and I have never once known what it costs her."

They stood in the lane.

"Then I am going to say the name at about ten past four," said Lin Hao, "after the standing is spent and not before, and a clerk will enter it, and there will be no second page."

"Yes," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "Say it after. If you say it before, a stranger in four years reads a man who gave away a brother and then lost his standing, and that is a man we run hearings on."

"And if I say it after."

"Then a stranger in four years reads a man who lost his standing and then gave away a brother, and there is not one office in this city that can do anything with that, because nothing in this city makes a brother answerable and nothing ever has."

He started walking again. "Go and be somewhere else until the first, Lin Hao. You are the only one of the five who can be got out of it by standing somewhere else."

"And I have watched you stand in a corridor for two hours ten minutes on the morning of the twenty-eighth because of a clause you wrote yourself, and I have never admired anything more in this building and I have never thought it was a good idea once."
