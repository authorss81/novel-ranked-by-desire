# The Man Who Filed It Instead

There were about three hundred people in the hearing room of the Correction Office at ten in the morning on the twenty-sixth of July and the room holds nine hundred, and the rail was along the back wall with forty people standing at it, and a woman was in a chair by the door with a day's book on a stand taking the hour and the name of anybody who wanted to speak.

The room on the first day was an ordinary room.

That was the thing to understand about the twenty-sixth, and about the twenty-seventh, and about the twenty-eighth and the twenty-ninth, and it was Agnete Lindqvist who had told him on the twenty-fourth and not in a room: on the first day there was nothing holding anything, and everything said in that room could be used against the person who said it for the rest of their life.

The twenty-sixth was the first day.

## A Man With Nine Lines On A Sheet

He came in at about ten past ten and he was thin, and he had been at that building for longer than the room had been standing, and he had a coat over his arm instead of on his back, which is what a man does when he has decided he is going to take it off at some point, and he had a sheet of paper in his other hand with nine lines on it that he had written out himself in his own hand.

He came down the middle of the floor and put it on the table in front of the chair and squared it with two fingers.

Lin Hao was in the fourth row of the public seating with a brass in his coat, and he had spent four years finding out what a person's hands do about a second before their face, and he had about eleven seconds.

Perrin Sowe squared the paper a second time.

"I am not here to defend anybody," he said, to the chair, and to the room, and to the rail. "I want that entered before I start. It is the whole of what I came for and if it is not the first thing in the record then I have not said anything worth four days of anybody's time."

## Eleven Seconds

There were eleven seconds and there were about three hundred people in the room and he had everything he needed.

He had a standing of nine and a piece of brass in his coat. He had a clause of thirty-eight words in a book in another building, and a clerk of that room who might only enter and could not correct.

He had the standing and the clause and the brass and about nine seconds in which he could have entered, in about nine seconds, a statement that the man at the table was a witness in the Council's own matter and not a subject of the Correction Office, and which would have taken the sheet off that table and put it in a drawer in a different building for a year.

He had the phrase. Eight of them in nine years and not one of them spent, and the whole of what it was for was *to make a room need something*, and he had sworn on the ninth of May at ten past five that he would never make a room need something, and there was a room at the end of a corridor that had needed nothing from him for four years and would have gone on not needing anything from him for ever.

He had about eleven seconds and he had, for the first time since the fourteenth of March, an instrument in his hand that would have cost him nothing to anybody except himself and would have taken a sentence off that table in a single movement.

He did not move.

It was not a decision he made at the eleventh second. It was a decision he made about two seconds before the eleventh second and did not know he was making, and it went out of him the way a breath goes out of a man, and afterwards he spent a long time working out where it had gone.

## What He Said, In Order

"There were two of us in Veil-Research in the year the register was built and I am going to use the year, because the year is the only thing about this that anybody in this room can check."

Nobody made a noise.

"Lin Wei was right. I want the record to have that first, because everything after it is going to sound like an excuse and it is not an excuse, it is what I did after I knew he was right."

The room was extremely quiet and somebody at the back put a hand on the rail.

"I knew. I have known for three years. He came to me in a room with no window in it on the third floor of a building off a third court and he told me what the account was and what it was for and he told me it was not his and had never been his,"

"and he told me that the only reason it was at his address was that he was a man nobody audits, and he was right about all four of those things and he was right about them before I had said one word."

"Then I filed it."

## The Four Sentences

The officer of the hearing read his nine lines back before he went on and he did not smooth anything, and Lin Hao recognised the shape of it because he had watched a clerk do it four hundred times and once had watched a woman do it with a return in her own memory.

"There is a second sentence," said the officer. "Read the second sentence."

Perrin Sowe read it.

"I knew that he was right and I filed it instead of giving it, because giving it would have meant it went out of my hands into a room, and a room has a clerk in it, and once it is in a room it is a matter that the Bureau can look at, and a man in the third sub-level with a good name is worth more to this city than a file about an account that nobody can prove exists."

"That is the whole of it and it is not a reason and I have never pretended it was one."

"And I wrote the file."

"The file I filed on the ninth of a year three years ago is the reason Lin Wei was removed. It went in under my hand and above my name and it was accepted in about eleven minutes, and he was taken out of a room he had worked in for nine years because of a document that was true in every word and that I should not have written."

He put his hand flat on the paper.

"That is four sentences and I have got nine lines written out and I am going to read the other five so that somebody has read them, and then I am going to sit down and I am not going to say anything else for four days."

## After

He read the other five. They were about what the file was, where it went, which desk it went to, and the eleven minutes, and they were so exactly like a minute made by a clerk in a corridor that about forty people in that room understood at the same moment that this man had written a thing down in the shape of a document rather than in the shape of a speech, on purpose, so that it could be entered.

The officer entered it. Four sentences and nine lines and the date first and the hour with it.

Then the chair asked whether the subject wished to respond to anything that had been said, and looked down the room at the fourth row, and about three hundred people turned round in a chair that was built for nine hundred.

## The Twelve Words He Had Ready

He had twelve words ready and he had had them ready since the fifth of July in a records room with a shut door, and they were the truest sentence anybody had ever said to him and a woman had told him once that she would say it once and not again this year or after it.

If he had said them, then a man who had just put a true sentence about himself into a public record for the first time in his working life would have had about four seconds in which something had been answered.

A room of three hundred people would have gone home with two things in their heads instead of one, and every one of those three hundred people would have been able to say for the rest of their lives that the thing about the account was answered on the twenty-sixth of July.

He looked at the man at the table.

Perrin Sowe was not looking at him. He was looking at the rail, at forty people standing at it, at the room he had just emptied of every other possible subject, and he had a coat over his arm and he was not going to be able to pick it up for about an hour because he would have to stand in the corridor while the officer wrote out a copy for the file and for the witness.

## What The Record Is, And Who It Goes To

The record is the Correction Office's and it is not the Council's, and it is made on the afternoon of the day and it goes up on the standing sheet at the head of every line, and the standing sheet has nine lines on it and the fifth of them is a man in the fourth row of the public seating.

It goes out at four in the afternoon. It goes to a room at the end of a corridor off a second landing that stands at five and has not sat since the first of April and will not sit again until the first of October, and there is nothing that room can do with it before the first of October, and there is nothing anybody can do to it after the first of October either.

It is producible against every person named in it for four years, and it cannot be struck, and a clerk may only enter it.

Perrin Sowe's posting is not a matter for this room. What is in the record is that he knew, and that he filed instead of giving, and that the file is the reason a man was removed, and every division in this city that has ever taken a paper off him will have that in the morning, and there are nine of those and the Auditorate is not one of them and does not need to be.

## What He Did Not Do

He did not interrupt. He did not stand up. He did not use the standing of nine or the brass or the clause of thirty-eight words.

He did not spend anything on himself, and the phrase had been unspent for four years and was unspent at the end of the twenty-sixth of July, and that was the first time in nine years that a man had had an instrument in his hand, in a room, with a true question in front of him, and had worked out in eleven seconds what it would cost him not to use it, and had let it stay in his pocket.

And he did not forgive him, and there was nobody in that room to forgive him and he was not going to pretend to himself in a kitchen at two in the morning that he had.

## The Four Cents And What It Closed

At about half past eleven Perrin Sowe picked up his coat off the table and put it on and came down the middle of the floor and did not go out of the door at the front.

He stopped in the corridor outside the hearing room with his coat done up and a copy of the record under his arm, and Lin Hao was on a bench against the wall because he had gone out four minutes earlier and had not gone far.

"I have been going to ask you one question since the sixth of December," said Perrin Sowe. "It is not that one and I am not going to ask it here."

"Then why are you standing here."

"Because I have been in this city for thirty-one years," said Perrin Sowe, "and there is exactly one thing in it that I have never once been able to do, and it is stand in a room and hear what somebody says about me and not go and make it better, and I have wanted somebody to see me do it and there is not one person in this building who is going to."

He looked at him for about two seconds.

"You could have," said Perrin Sowe.

Then he said, "Perrin," which was the last time in four years that either of them had used a first name, and he went down the stairs.

## What Closed

He sat on a bench in a corridor at about ten to twelve in the morning for about twenty minutes and worked out one thing, and it was the last thing he worked out that day and it was the last thing he was ever going to be able to buy.

One hundred and fifty-six days. Ten from twenty leaves ten, ten with thirty and thirty and thirty and thirty is one hundred and thirty, and one hundred and thirty with twenty-six is one hundred and fifty-six, and not one person in the whole of that building had any use for the figure.

For four years he had been a man who could get out of anything. A consignment that could not be topped up, a clause at a pencil's cost, a standing bought in a queue with a pen, eight attestations and a gap, and behind all of it a brass and a clerk who would enter anything he brought her and correct nothing. Every ruin he had ever walked past in this city had had a price attached to it that he had been able to find.

A man had just gone up four hundred in a room because he had said a true sentence, and he had had eleven seconds, and the instrument had been in his pocket, and he had let it stay.

The option of buying his way out of somebody else's ruin was gone. It had gone on the twenty-sixth of July at about ten past ten in the morning in front of three hundred people, and it was not going to come back in October, and he knew exactly which afternoon in October he was going to want it.
