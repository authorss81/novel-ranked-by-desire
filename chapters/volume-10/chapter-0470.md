# The Argument Against Good People

It was not a fifth day of the hearing. The hearing was four days and it closed on the twenty-ninth and there were two documents on the standing sheet and the record of a separate sitting, entered at ten to four on the previous afternoon by a woman in a chair who had said it was not in anybody's gift to move.

There were about four hundred people in that room at ten in the morning on the thirtieth of July.

She offered to hold it, and he said no, and the reason he gave was in front of four hundred people and it was the best reason anybody had given in that building in four days.

"You cannot do that," he said. "Not for this. If you hold it then what I say is free, and a free statement binds nobody, and I have come here to be held to something."

"You are giving up a thing that is worth about three hours of my life and that I had already decided to spend."

"I know what it is worth. I have known since the twenty-fourth. If you hold that room then every word I say this morning can be set aside afterwards by anybody who does not like it, and the person I am arguing with has been telling the truth for four days and is not going to be able to set aside one line of it. I would rather be beaten in an ordinary room."

## One: The Concession He Came In With

"I am going to start with the thing you came here for," he said, "and then you are going to be angry with me about the rest of it for about eleven minutes, and there is not one thing in the next eleven minutes that I can help you with."

The room had four hundred people in it and there was no compulsion in it at all.

"It is not mine."

He said it at the volume a man uses at the front of a room and not at the volume he had been using at the foot of a wall for nine days, and it went to the back of that room because a room with four hundred people in it carries sound further than a man thinks it does.

"It has never been mine. I have never said that it was mine, and she asked me on the twenty-ninth of July in front of eleven hundred people whether it was, and I said no, and I am saying it again this morning because it is true and because she has built four sentences on it and they are the only four sentences in her case that I am not going to spend the morning taking apart."

Somebody at the back said, "Then who does it belong to."

"That is the part where she is better than me and I am going to come to it in about four minutes," said Lin Hao, "and I am not going to answer it yet."

## Two: The Part He Agreed With

"Before I say anything else, the two things she has said that are true and that I am not going to pretend are arguable."

"The first is that it cannot be returned. I have said in this room and in a kitchen and to four people at a table that I do not know how many there are and I have never known."

"There is a book in my coat with a thousand faces in it and those are the faces of people who did a thing in a doorway that got into a round and got written on a line somewhere and turned."

"And the city has about a million people in it who did a thing in a doorway and got nothing on no line at all, and those two numbers have never been reconciled in four years and they are not going to be reconciled in four days, and I have never tried, and that is mine."

"The second is that she is right that the man at the end of it is not a holder. I have spent four years discovering that a thing with a holder is a thing with an owner and that I am the only man in this city that this city does not audit, and that is a job description and not a sentence anybody put in writing about me, and it is the whole reason there is an address on it."

"She has said the same thing in a different order and she got there first and she got there first on purpose."

## Three: What Distribution Is

"Now the part I came for, and I am going to be slow at it and I am going to be boring at it, because it is not an exciting argument. It is an arithmetic one and I have got two thousand people's worth of arithmetic in my coat and I would like to give it to the room before lunch."

"A distribution is a decision," said Lin Hao. "Somebody has to decide the order, and deciding an order means having an aim, and having an aim about people you have never met is easy for a room and it is hard for one person and it is worse for one person who is thirty and has been wrong about his own account for four years."

"So I stood at the foot of a wall for nine days. That is not an argument either, and I am going to tell you the figure because I have got it and nobody else has, and then you can decide whether it is evidence."

"About two thousand people came. On the eleventh there were about nine hundred and by the fifteenth there was a queue at half past six in the morning and about two hundred at the wall in the afternoon, and I stood at that wall from the eleventh to the nineteenth, which is nineteen taken from eleven leaving eight, and eight days from the first of them to the last of them is nine days at a wall."

"I did six and a half hours on the seventeenth and about a hundred and ninety hands on the nineteenth and nothing after that."

"About forty of the two thousand noticed anything. Forty. And every single one of those forty was already standing in that square, because it is the second largest crossing anybody has ever measured in this city and about nine thousand people walk under that wall a day, and every person who needed it was somewhere else with a cold hand and had thought they were cold."

He waited for the room to get to it and it took about four seconds.

"I cannot tell you the name of one of the forty. If the order had been mine, and it was mine,"

"that is exactly what I would not have been able to do about it. And if the order is on the sheet of a body of nine people who have never met me and think I am a man who took it, then you will get about the same forty, out of about a million, and the rest of that will be about a theory held by some people on an ordinary morning."

## Four: The Thing He Was Not Allowed To Finish

"There is a reason I cannot give you for why I know that as well as I know it," he said, and he got four words into it and stopped.

He started again about ninety seconds later.

"There is a reason I cannot put in this room for why I know that the way I know it."

He stopped again.

And a third time, about halfway through, with four hundred people in a room that was not holding anything at all:

"There is a thing I know about this that I am not going to be able to say to you this morning, and I am not going to pretend that is modesty."

"It is that I know it from a place I cannot stand in front of, and if I said it I would be lending this room a sentence that is not mine, and a man arguing against a woman in a room in the morning with a sentence somebody else put in his mouth is not making an argument. He is collecting one."

"That is all. Do not ask me for it today or on any other day and you will not get it and I am telling you now so that it is not a thing you think you are owed."

## The Only Honest Sentence He Had

"There is one more and it is the only thing I have got in my pocket this morning and it is not much, and I am going to say it and then I am finished."

"I can take the cold. I have taken it for nine days at a wall and about four thousand people's worth went out through a chest that had nothing left in it and something in me is spent and it is not going to get better, and there is a cough in it now that is mine for the rest of my life."

"And a woman who counts for a living at a counter in a hall with no door on it went grey on one side of her head on the thirteenth of July in a room I put my hand into, and she is the eleventh name on a turned line and I put it there myself."

"That is not an argument," said Lin Hao. "That is one man's report on himself and it is in a room with four hundred people in it and I would not be able to use it if I was trying to win and I am not."

## What The Room Did

The room did not carry it. He could tell inside four minutes, from the sound of it, the way he can tell inside four minutes how long a queue is going to take.

About a third of that room agreed with him and about a third agreed with her and about a third went out into the yard at about eleven o'clock and did not come back in, and that last third was not hostility.

It was a woman in the nineteenth row with her sleeves turned back who put her hand up and said, in a voice everybody heard, that she had been under a floor for eleven years and had a daughter who was under one too, and that if a decision was to be made about a thing that belonged to people like her then she would like to be in the room where it got made, and she was not going to go and stand in a yard about it.

Agnete Lindqvist sat down and did not say one word.

She had been on her feet for four days. She did not answer him, she did not correct him, she did not concede a clause of it and she did not praise a word of it, and she looked at him across four hundred people for about two seconds and then she looked at her own hands.

## The Heading Of The Record

The officer made the entry at about half past twelve on the thirtieth of July and read it back the way a clerk reads back, and what he read back was the heading first, because a record with a heading that is wrong about itself is a record that a stranger in four years will read as a lie.

He read it twice.

"Correction Office. Public sitting, the thirtieth of July. The reservoir. Held by no person. Claimed by the Church of the Quiet Number, claimed on the twenty-ninth. Answered by the subject on the thirtieth."

There was not one word of it about a man.

The number of the finding of the thirtieth of June was in the body and the premises were not in the heading and his name was nowhere on the page, and the only mention of him in the whole of the heading was the word *subject*, which in this city is not a name and is not an address and is not a person anybody can be produced by.

He had spent four years making sure that there was not one paper in this city with the word on it. There was one. It was made by a clerk who might only enter and could not correct, and she had entered the matter as one that belonged to nobody and a claim and an answer, and his own concession of the morning was in the body of it under the heading, four hundred people and a wall.

## Outside, In The Yard

She came out into the yard at about ten to one and stood in the sun and let him catch up and did not begin by saying anything about what he had said.

"I held the room on the second day for two hours and fifty minutes and I have had no sleep," said Agnete Lindqvist, "and there is one thing I want said here and then I am going to go and sit down somewhere with a wall behind me, and you are not to answer it."

"Go on."

"I did not want the concession," she said. "I want to be very clear about that, because a person in your position would expect me to have got what I came for and I have not. She asked me on the fourth day whether I had got what I came for and I said I had got it and I did not tell her what I meant and I am telling you what I meant."

"What did you mean."

"I meant that in nine weeks there will be nine seals in a room and a claim on the table and no answer on the table at all,"

"because a man who says yes to a classification in July and says on the thirtieth that it was never his has spent the only card he had and there is nothing left for him to play except an account that he has admitted he does not own." She turned round and looked at the yard.

"I did not want that. I wanted the answer on the table to be one that the room had gone into."

She looked at him for about two seconds.

"And it was not, and that is the whole of what happened this morning, and I am not going to pretend to you that you were right, because you were not, and I have been doing this for twenty years and a thing that is not right is not right whether a man argues it well or badly."

## One Hundred And Sixty

He walked back up the street with a piece of brass in his coat and nothing else in his hands.

One hundred and sixty days. Ten from twenty leaves ten, ten with thirty and thirty and thirty and thirty is one hundred and thirty, and one hundred and thirty with thirty is one hundred and sixty. And on the first of October, thirty taken from thirty leaves nothing, and a month of thirty more, and the first, it is one hundred and ninety-two.

There were two documents from four days sitting on a standing sheet with nine lines on it going out to a room at the end of a corridor, and the second of them had his name nowhere in the heading and the word *subject* where a name goes, and it would be on that sheet for four years and would be producible against every person named in it, and there was not one office in this city that had ever been asked to destroy a record.

He had gone into that room at ten in the morning with nothing that he had not walked in with, and had come out of it at half past twelve with a sentence he had said out loud that he was never going to be able to take back, given away for nothing, to a woman who had told him on the twenty-fourth in a room about twelve feet by nine that she was not going to be thanked for it.
