# Three Of Them Draw

He told them the price at eleven in the morning on the fourteenth of August, standing at the end of the kitchen table with his coat on, and he told it to nine women and it took him about ninety seconds and it is the only ninety seconds of the last four years he has ever wished he could have taken back.

"One hundred and seventy-four days," he said, first, because he had started saying them and could not stop.

"Ten from twenty leaves ten, ten with thirty and thirty and thirty and thirty is one hundred and thirty at the end of June, and July is thirty, so one hundred and sixty at the end of July, and one hundred and sixty with fourteen is one hundred and seventy-four."

"And where does it get to," said Runa Sato.

"It gets to two hundred and twenty-one on the first of October," said Lin Hao. "Thirty taken from fourteen leaves sixteen, and there are sixteen more days of this month and thirty of September and then the first, and one hundred and seventy-four with forty-seven is two hundred and twenty-one, and on that morning there are nine seals behind a door at the end of a corridor."

"A draw has no instrument, no column and no form anywhere in this city," he said. "The only hand that has ever touched one is the hand of the person who drew, on the thing itself, in a room, with other people in the room. I can open it. I am the only person alive who can open it and it is open now, and it is on this table, and I am not going to put a hand on anybody in this room."

"Lin Hao—"

"You will each do it yourself or it will not count," he said, "and I am not going to be the reason anybody is warm for one and a half seconds tonight. That is not modesty, it is the rule, and a keeper in a cold room told me the rule on the second of August and she told me the method as well and I am going to use both."

He put his hand flat on the closed book on the table.

"And here is the price, and I am giving it to you before anybody touches it, and every one of you is going to hear it."

"The law of a standing household is that a person may not draw against an account that the head of that household is answerable for, while any person keeping that address has drawn against it."

"And the moment one of you puts a hand on that pot, I cannot empty it," said Lin Hao. "Not on the first of October, not in October, not ever. It does not matter what I do after that. It does not matter what I pay for, or what I give up, or what I am willing to spend a chest over."

"Three women in this room have said out loud that they have done it and one of them has said it and not said it and I am not going to be able to empty that thing while this address stands."

He looked at them.

"That is what it costs. It costs him the only thing he has been doing for four years." He did not point at himself. "I am telling you that at eleven in the morning because if I tell you at four o'clock in the afternoon after one of you has already done it then I have arranged this."

## What Nine Women Did With That

Nobody moved for about eleven seconds, which in that kitchen is a long time.

Then Agnete Lindqvist said, "Ask us properly, then, and ask us one at a time, and let us say no."

"I am not asking."

"You have just told us what it costs you and told us not to do it and told us you would not stop us," said Agnete Lindqvist. "That is not asking, that is a speech with a hand on a table. If you are going to be in this room then be in it properly, because a room in which nothing can be compelled is the only kind there is and you have spent four years being careful about it and you can stop."

He put his hand off the book.

"All right," he said. "I am asking. And I am not going to help and I am not going to stay in this room while it happens, and I am going to be on the landing."

"Lin Hao—"

"You three said yes before the ninth of July," he said. "I have not moved an inch since the second of August and I am not going to start today."

He went out and shut the landing door behind him and stood on it.

## The Call, In Order, Three Times

They did it in the kitchen at half past eleven in the morning on the fourteenth of August, three women, in the form the guild has, and nobody wrote any of it down.

Marguerite Vale went first, because she was the broker and had said so.

"What moved," she said, "is eleven years of a line I have been carrying out of a pot I did not know the size of. When: the sixth of May of a year eleven years back, at about four in the afternoon, and every quarter since, in the guild's own week, because I did it on the guild's week so that it would reconcile."

"At what price."

"My name at the foot of a disposition I am not going to sign, and the knowledge that I have been taking out of other people's doorways for a decade to keep one woman warm," said Marguerite Vale, "and I am not going to know today whether that was theft or housekeeping, and I have decided I would rather not know than not do it."

She put her hand on it.

The cold went up through her wrist and stopped at the elbow and she did not make a sound, and she stood there with her eyes open for about four seconds, and then she sat down on the edge of the table in a dark dress with the sleeves pushed back and put both hands in her lap.

"It is not cold," she said, to the wall. "It is not cold. That is what it is. I have been reading about it for eleven years and it is not cold."

## The Second One Was Eleven Years Long

Petra Ilyin went second, and she went second on purpose.

"I am going to take eleven years," she said, "and I want everybody in this kitchen to hear that I have said eleven and not have heard it come out as one number, because it is not one number, it is about four hundred separate occasions over a decade and a half and I am going to feel every one of them and I would like the room to be in for it."

"Petra—"

"You are not going to talk me out of this and you are not going to make it easier for me," said Petra Ilyin.

"I have been unable to refuse anybody in eleven years and I found out on the eleventh why, and there is a pot in this city full of things strangers did in doorways, and I have decided that I want to be given something back and I want to know what that is worth and I am not going to find out from a sheet of paper."

She put her hand on it.

It was a long time.

Nadia Ferrous held her by the shoulders from behind because Petra Ilyin went down onto the kitchen floor about a minute and a half in, and did not let go, and did not say anything, and there was a point about two minutes in where not one person in that room could tell whether the woman on the floor was taking something or giving something and it did not matter and nobody in that room will ever be able to say afterwards which of the two it was.

Petra Ilyin came back up on her own at about the four-minute mark and sat on the floor with her back against the stove leg and her hands open in front of her.

"That is what eleven years is," she said. "I have had eleven years and I did not know and I would have taken it again this morning if I had known."

She said it in the ordinary voice of a woman stating a day and an hour, and four of the women at that table cried, and one of them was the singer and she did it in nine seconds and could not stop it and did not try.

## The Third One Had Been Doing It For Two Years

"I have been in that shaft since I was sixteen," said Nadia Ferrous. "And about forty times I have been cold in it and I have told this kitchen nine hundred times that the cold was the building, and it was me, and tonight I am going to tell the truth in a kitchen where the whole of it is about to happen in front of me."

She did not put her hand on it straight away.

"Can I say the thing first," said Nadia Ferrous. "I know exactly what is coming and so do you and I want it in the room before it starts. The three of us are not going to get warm. We are going to be cold, and the cold is not going to last a second and a half, and it is going to be the coldest thing any of us has ever felt and then it will be over."

"Yes," said Lin Hao, from behind the landing door.

"And afterwards every one of us is going to be exactly the same as we are now and be able to prove it," said Nadia Ferrous, "and there will be no instrument anywhere in this city that says any of it happened. And I have decided to do it anyway and I would like it minuted that I said so in advance and was cheerful about it and that was a choice."

She put her hand on it.

Nadia Ferrous did not go down. She said afterwards, and she said it to be difficult, that she could not go down, because she could not, and that the whole of the cold in that kitchen went through her and out through the floor of a lift shaft that she has been sitting in since she was a girl, and that it went *down*, which is the only direction she has ever been able to go.

## What It Cost The Three Of Them

Marguerite Vale was sick twice and had it in a bowl she was holding on her own knees in a kitchen with nine people in it, and refused to leave the room, and would not let anybody carry the bowl out of it for her.

Petra Ilyin sat on the floor for half an hour and could not get up off it without a hand on the table, and when she did get up she went straight to the sink and put both hands under the water and left them there.

And Nadia Ferrous sat in the one chair in that kitchen that has never had anybody's feet off the floor in it, which is the chair she is carried in from the landing when she comes up the stairs, and she said the whole thing was extremely interesting.

"There," she said, at about twenty past twelve. "That is the whole of it and it took four minutes and none of you are different."

## What Had Changed And Was Not Going Back

At about ten to one the landing door opened and he came back in and looked at the three of them and did not ask, and Beatrix Halm told him afterwards that she had felt the whole of the room come up about twenty seconds before the door moved and had put her hand flat on the wood.

He came in and stood at the end of the table with the book still on it and closed.

"That is it, then," said Lin Hao. "That is the whole of what I have been doing for four years and I did not do any of it."

"You did the hard part," said Ines Duarte, from the far side of the table, in the voice of an auditor entering a line. "You opened it and you told them what it cost and you went out on the landing. There is no instrument for that either."

"No," he said. "There is no instrument for that."

And then he said the thing that made it permanent, and he said it standing up, in front of nine women, because he had learned in June that a thing said sitting down in a room gets taken back later.

"The three of you have drawn against it. All three of you, on purpose, today, in a room. Under the law of this household I cannot empty that account while any person keeping this address has drawn against it, and none of the three of you is going to be asked to withdraw, and if one of you does I will not accept it."

"We are not going to withdraw," said Marguerite Vale.

"No," he said. "I know. And I am not going to try to talk you out of it tonight and I am not going to spend anything on myself about it tonight, and I want it understood by every person at that table that I did not arrange this and I cannot reverse it and there is no version of the next four weeks in which I am the one who is doing anything."

He looked at the three of them.

"You took the cold yourselves," said Lin Hao. "In the middle of the afternoon, in a kitchen, where I could hear all of it from behind a door, and none of you asked me to."
