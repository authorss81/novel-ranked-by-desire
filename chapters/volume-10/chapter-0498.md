# A Night Shift And A White Tile

The plate came in the post on the ninth of December in an envelope with a keeper's name on it and no other paper in it, and it is white, and it is the first white plate the Bureau of Merit has issued in the memory of anybody who keeps the issue books.

There is no figure on it. Every other plate in this city carries a band and a floor and a number, and this one carries a band that is the first band, which has nothing under it, so the office cut a plate with a white ground and put his name on it in a small level hand and gave it to him in the post.

He keeps it in his coat. It has been in his coat for twenty-three days and he has taken it out about forty times and put it back about forty times.

## The Assignment

The branch office off a third court had four floors and a counter that shut at four and a night man at the counter from eleven until seven, and on the morning of the eleventh of October Yusuf Adeyemi had given him a rota and a floor.

"First band gets assigned work," he had said. "That is what a band is. It is not a punishment and it is not a comment."

"Which floor."

"The washrooms," said Yusuf Adeyemi. "Three floors of them, and the stairwell, and the bin room, and you will do them from eleven at night until about half past five, and there are two other people on that floor and neither of them is yours."

"I asked for it."

"You did not," said Yusuf Adeyemi.

"I asked for the shift," said Lin Hao. "I did not ask for a floor and I got the floor, and I have read the sheet."

"And a night man at a counter is a counter at four in the morning with nobody at it. I have been at that counter at four in the morning for four years of my life and I would like to go back to it once a week. Once a week is not what the assignment says. Once a week is what I said."

"You did not say it to me."

"I said it to the rota man on the ninth of December in front of a woman who runs an intake desk twenty streets away," said Lin Hao, "and he wrote it down, and he is a keeper and cannot be asked to hurry."

## What A Night Shift Is

The hours are not his.

That is the whole of it and he said it out loud to the rota man on the ninth of December and the rota man wrote it down, and it is the second line on the sheet that week that came from a man who used to give out his own hours.

Eleven at night until half past five, four days on and three off, and the rota is kept by a man of about nineteen years' service who is not a clerk and cannot be asked to hurry and has never been asked. He does not care what a person's rank is. He has the page and the page is the page.

"There is a first name and a surname and a band and a number of nights," said the rota man. "That is the whole of what I have got for anybody and I have got it for a rank nine the same as for a first band, and I could not tell you the rank off my own page if you asked me."

"Do not ask him," said Lin Hao. "He is right and it is the correct arrangement."

## Three Months Of An Auditor

Ines Duarte had been auditing that branch since the first of October and on the morning of the first of January it was three months, and neither of them had said a word to the other one about it.

She came in at about one in the morning on the first day of the new year with a bundle under her arm and a slate and stopped at the counter that shuts at four because it was twenty past twelve and it had shut.

"You are the night man," she said.

"I am at the washrooms until half past five and I come through here for the water."

"Then I have thirty minutes and you have eight of them," said Ines Duarte.

She put the bundle down on the counter and did not open it.

"I have been auditing this branch for three months and I have sat at that counter for about two hours a night for eleven weeks and neither of us has ever once mentioned it, and I would like it on the record that I noticed and that I chose not to, and I am not going to say anything else about it tonight."

"That is the whole of the conversation."

"That is the whole of the conversation," said Ines Duarte, "and I have had it, and I am not going to have it again."

Then she said something else, and it was about something else.

"The bin room on that stair is being done at about two and not at about eleven," she said. "Whoever is on the rota for that floor is doing it at two, and whoever is on the rota for that floor did not decide to do it at two."

"I do not do the bins at two."

"No. You do the washrooms and the stairwell and you have said so out loud and I am not asking you anything," said Ines Duarte, "and I am telling you that the bins at two are going to be audited in about four years by somebody who will have a sheet with a time on it, and you will not be on it, and neither will I."

## The Correction

"On the twenty-ninth of December I filed a correction."

He did not look up.

"Against what."

"Against a finding of eleven weeks ago that had a thing in it that should not have been in it," said Ines Duarte. "The finding is not going to be re-opened and it is not going to be struck and I have not asked for either, and I filed it, and it is filed."

"Is it pressed."

"No."

"Why not."

"Because it does not need pressing," said Ines Duarte. "It is filed and it will be read in about four years by a stranger with a folder, and it will do in about four seconds what I have spent eleven years being unable to do in a room, and that is the whole of what it is for and I have got what I wanted out of it."

She picked the bundle back up.

"That is the whole of what I am going to say to you about that, and it is four sentences, and it took eleven weeks to write and one minute to say, and neither of us has said a word about what is in it, and you are not going to ask me and I am not going to tell you."

"No."

"Good," said Ines Duarte. "There is a cistern on the second floor that has been running since the middle of December and I am going to write it down and it is going to be the last thing I do tonight, and you are going to carry it up two flights and you are going to hate me by about the second one."

She did write it down, in her own hand, on the audit sheet, and she did not put her name at the head of it.

## Four Hundred Lines That Are Not His

Beatrix Halm came in at about twenty past one on a shift of her own that ran to four, and she had a sheet in her hand and she put it face up on the counter.

"Four hundred," she said.

"Four hundred what."

"Turned lines off the district round, since the second of October, to about midnight tonight." She squared it.

"One line for each of them and a time beside each of them, and the time is the time I was standing in the room. I write times and not figures because I told you in the summer that I would and because a round writes a line when there is something on it."

He looked at it. It went down the page in a single column of small level handwriting for three pages.

"You have not got a number on it."

"I have never had a number on it," said Beatrix Halm. "You know that. What I have is four hundred and a page and a time on each one and I could not tell you what any of it was worth, and I have not tried, and I have been asked eleven times since the second of October what the figure off that sheet is."

"And?"

"And the answer is that a figure does not exist, and I have said it eleven times, and about four people have decided that means I am keeping it from somebody."

She shut the sheet. "It is four hundred people and a time."

He put his hand flat on the counter on the sheet.

"Not one line of that is me."

"Not one line of that is anybody who is spending," said Beatrix Halm. "Four hundred people got cold for about four seconds in a street in three months and stood there for it."

"You are at rank one in a washroom on a night shift going down three floors with a bin room, and not one thing is going out of you, and that is why you are not on it, and that is not a judgement and do not make it into one."

"That is the third time somebody has explained this to me and the second time it has made it worse."

"Then it is the third," said Beatrix Halm, "and I am going to put the sheet in the post to a woman who holds records in her head and does not sleep, because she asked me for it in November and I am giving it to her tonight, and you will be the last page of it in about four years and by then it will be a number and neither of us will know what it was."

## The Three Who Came To The Shift

Nadia Ferrous came in at about three o'clock on her own rota with a cloth bag and a key she had not been issued and had taken off a board in a shed, and she said nothing to anybody and went down to the fourth floor.

Petra Ilyin came in at about ten past three and stood in the corridor for about nine seconds before she picked up anything, and then she did the whole of the second-floor landing and did it fast, and she has been able to do a landing in nine minutes since she was a girl.

Isolde Marchetti came in at about half past three, forty minutes before she had to be anywhere, and stood at the counter.

"I have four hundred and eleven nights and I have taken two of them," she said, "and the man who keeps the rota wrote my name down in front of me and did not ask me a single question about why, and that is the correct arrangement and I want it observed that I said so."

"You do not have to work here."

"No," said Isolde Marchetti. "But I asked, and a man wrote it down, and there is a fourth line on that page that nobody has put a name on in nineteen years, and I would like to be a woman who wrote her own name on a rota in front of the man who keeps it."

They worked. That is the whole of it. Nine hundred people went past that counter on the morning of the second of January and about nine thousand went under a wall two streets away, and four women and a rota man and an auditor did three floors of a building between eleven at night and half past five.

## Two Streets Away

He went out at about twenty to five for the water and went the wrong way and went two streets east instead of west.

The wall was there in the dark. The card of the thirtieth of December had gone up at half past four the afternoon before and was on the stone in a boy's paste, and the column down the wall was a column, and the floor across the top of it was a figure he had read a hundred times.

And at the foot of the stone, under the last tile, where the two words and a dash and two words had been painted in the office's own hand in October, there was a tile.

It was white. Not one band, floor or figure on it, and nothing at all under it, and the only white tile on that wall, and a name on it in chalk, in a hand he had watched read that wall alone every morning for nine years.

He stood under it for about a minute and a half.

He knew the hand. He did not ask her about it when he went back in, and there has never been a moment since when he could have, and that is not because she is difficult and it is because of a kitchen table on the seventeenth of October and a sentence spoken out loud in a room, and he is not going to unsay it for a name in chalk.

## Not Poor

He is not a poor man.

Rank one means a subsistence that is guaranteed, which means he will never be hungry again for as long as there is a Bureau to guarantee it, and that is a real thing that has happened to him and it happened on the first of October at ten minutes to four in the afternoon in a queue of nine people with a pen.

Rank one also means assigned work, which means he will never choose his own hours again, and he asked for the shift, and the shift is eleven at night until half past five, and the hours are the rota's and not his.

He did the arithmetic in the first week of November, on a step, with a woman from the Auditorate who had come about a cistern:

"You are the only person I know who has given away everything and ended up with a wage," she said.

"Yes."

"Do you want it back."

"No," said Lin Hao. "I did all of it in a queue with a pen in ninety seconds and I would do it again this morning, and the two halves of it are the same decision and I have not spent one second since the first of October wishing for the other half."

He had a sink full of water and four floors and a plate in his coat and a sheet of four hundred times and three women on a rota he did not choose.

At about ten past five on the morning of the second of January he went up his own street past a white tile with nothing under it, and did not look at it twice, because he had stood under it for a minute and a half and that was the whole of what there was for a man who had been four streets away from a wall for four years.
