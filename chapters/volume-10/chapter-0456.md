# Yes Said Before Five Seals

The room was called at about ten past two in the afternoon on the ninth of July, and the list was made in that building out of the room at the end of the corridor on the morning of the day, and there were five standings on it, and not one of the five items on it could carry anything.

"You know what I have done," said the chair.

"I know what you have done."

"Then we will not spend the sitting on it."

Anouk Ferreira had a single sheet on the table in front of her and nothing else, and a room that had not sat since the first of April and would not sit again until the first of October, and she had called it on the ninth of July, in the middle of a quarter, for no purpose that could be written down.

"A requisition is carried at a quarter-end. The thirtieth of June was the last one and you used it. The next one is the first of October, and there is no instrument between this table and that date."

"So today carries nothing."

"Today carries nothing at all," said Ferreira. "Which is why I called it. A chair can be given in a room that cannot carry a requisition, and a chair is not an instrument, and I am not asking the room for a finding."

## The Five

The fourth seat had a man in it who had come at nine on the twenty-sixth of June and taken his coat off the chair, and he had been in the room twice since, and there was a woman from a division at the end on the left who had not spoken in a sitting in this building since the twelfth of December, and there was a man opposite her who had a district of his own and had voted to restore a standing on the first of April.

There were five. There had been three through twenty-six weekly returns in a row, and four from the first of April, and five since the fifteenth, and one of the five was standing up at the low chair on the left with his hand on the back of it.

"Read them."

The clerk read five names in order and read his last, and when a standing is read a member may stand and say one thing about it and may not say a second thing, and he stood up.

## The Thing He Said Out Loud

"I accept the offer made in this building on the third of July by the chair," he said. "A standing at the Terrace and a chair when one turns and a name that outlives me and the Ninth Seal for the good of the city, on the condition the chair named, which is that the reservoir be classified once and cleanly by Alma Quayle."

There were five people in that room and four of them were seals.

"I will have Alma Quayle's recusal lifted so that she may make it, and I will say the sentence that lifts it and nobody else can say it, and it will be said out loud in this room and entered and it cannot be taken back."

The room was extremely quiet and the lamp outside the corridor went on making its noise on the wall.

"You have four minutes," said Ferreira.

That was the whole of what he had said. It was in the minute and it was in a book and there was nothing in it about the account being emptied, and nothing in it about the eleventh of July, and nothing in it about a man who had learned on the seventh that a division with no door kept a list of people who came in and did not go out.

Nobody was allowed to say a second thing about their standing, and nobody took it.

## What The Room Believed

A woman at the end on the left wrote something on the corner of a slip, which was the ordinary business of a sitting, and the man opposite her had his hands flat on the table and did not write.

"You understand what you have just said," said the man from the fourth seat. "You have said it in front of four seals and there is a clerk in this room and the whole of it goes out at four."

"Yes."

"Not to me," said Bertram Sallow. "I have had a standing for three months and I have been in this room four times and I am the only one at this table who has come in knowing what a room does, because I sat in it for nine years before I lost it."

"Say the thing."

"A man says yes to a chair in a room that cannot carry a requisition, and the chair says yes back, and both of them know that the instrument comes on the first of October, and in between those two dates anything at all can happen and neither of them has to be here for it."

He put his hands flat. "I would still have said yes. I want that on whatever you write down. I have wanted a chair since the sixth of December and I would have said yes to a worse sentence than that."

## Ferreira Lets It Run

"You are aware," said Anouk Ferreira, "that you have just undertaken to do a thing that cannot be administered until the first of October, and that four days of the eight that remain to me are going to be spent on a plate at the head of the Reading."

"I am aware of it."

"I want to be exact about what I have heard, because I am the chair of this room and there are five of us and one of us is you." She turned the sheet round on the table. "You have said you will have a recusal lifted and made a classification. You have not said when, and you have not said that you have accepted the standing, and a member who has accepted a standing on this sheet reads differently from a member who has only accepted a classification."

"That is deliberate."

"That is deliberate," said Ferreira, "and it is perfectly within the rules, and I am not going to ask you about it in this room, because I asked you for a chair on the third of July and not for a promise and I have got the chair."

She looked down the table at the fifth seal.

## The Clause Costs Him A Corridor

"Then the minute of this sitting names those premises," said the clerk, from behind the second table, "because your standing is in the standing sheet and the standing sheet carries an address at the head of every line, and there are five lines, and one of them is yours."

"I know."

"There is a clause under the minute of the thirtieth of June and it is in a bundle and it was entered by me and it says that a person keeping that address may not be produced by a minute otherwise than in the presence of the subject, and that the subject may not be absent from the making of it."

She put her pen down.

"So you are going to stand in the corridor while I make it, with the door open, because that clause is in the book in front of me and it is not mine to decide whether it applies, and a clerk may only enter and cannot correct and if I entered this minute in that room with you at the table then in four years a stranger with a folder could read that minute and say the clause was not honoured, and he would be right."

The room looked at him.

He went out into the corridor and stood against the wall opposite the door with his hands in the pockets of a coat with a piece of brass in it, and the door was open, and four seals and a clerk worked eleven feet away on the other side of a threshold.

## Eight Feet Of Corridor

He could hear all of it. He could hear the clerk read back the minute in order, the date first and the hour with it, and he could hear the chair's hand on the wood, and he could hear a man in the fourth seat ask whether the minute of a sitting that cannot carry a requisition is a different animal from a minute of the carrying.

"It is a different animal," said the clerk, from where he could not see her, "and the difference is four months, and in four months one of you will have to be in this room again and the other four will be here because they are seals."

They were sealing at about ten to four. Five of them, one at a time, and the last one was his.

He had been in that room for about an hour and fifty minutes and thirty of it had been spent in a corridor at the end of a corridor off a second landing, and he could not have told anybody which thirty.

## The Chair That Does Not Count

At about ten past four the room emptied and the seals went into the tray and the bundle was made up and the clerk began to write her book before anybody left the building, and he came back in off the corridor to take his coat off the back of a chair.

Alma Quayle had not got up.

She had been at the end of that table since the fourth of December with a bundle of her own division's paper on her knees and she had sealed nothing and entered nothing and taken no part in one word of that sitting, and she had been in the chair that is not one of the nine for every day of it, and she had not moved her head when a man said yes to four seals.

On the nineteenth of June she had put two fingers flat on the oak at about twenty to three and that had been the entire conversation, and he had been told since that a woman in that chair puts a hand on the table when she wants a man to know she came.

She did not do it.

Both her hands stayed in her lap, and she looked at the table, and then she looked at him for about four seconds, and then she got up and went out with her bundle and did not speak in the corridor and has not spoken in it since.

## Two Floors Down

He got out into the court at about ten past four with the brass in his coat and eleven minutes of a corridor still in his ears, and he stood on a step in the last of the light and worked out the whole of what he had done in about ninety seconds, because he had got two days.

Two days to the eleventh of July, and after that a man would stand at the foot of a ranks wall with a black tile at the top of it and take strangers by the hand all day, and after that there would be nothing in the account to classify, and a classification is a holder and a holder is a room with a sheet on a desk in it and there would be nothing to put on the sheet.

Every person in that room believed him. His household believed him, because they believed everything he said in a kitchen and had believed it since the fourteenth of April, and six of them were asleep under a roof he had named four times in a sealed finding on the thirtieth of June, and the seventh was on Lantern Row with her own plate, and the eighth had rooms of her own in a guild, and the ninth had rooms over a cold store.

The ninth had read his return to him on the fifth of July with the door shut and had said the true thing once, and he had not asked her whether she believed him and he was not going to ask her, because she had told him on the fifth of July that he was not going to get it twice.

One person in that room had watched a man say yes to four seals with her hands in her lap and had put nothing on the oak, and she had been recused since the twelfth of December, and a recusal is lifted by the subject and by nobody else, and there is no sheet for lifting one, and he was the only man alive who could say the eleven words that would make her a seal again.

He was going to have to say them.

He had two days in which to work out how a man says a thing he means in a room and goes on doing the other thing, and there was not one instrument in this city that would let him have both, and there was not one person in the room who was entitled to tell him which one he had done.

The light went off the court at about half past four and the standing sheet on the second floor had five lines on it and one of them had a word under it that he had not seen a clerk put there.
