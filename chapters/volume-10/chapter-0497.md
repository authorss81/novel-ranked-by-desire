# Three Who Never Lived Here Again

The thirtieth of November is the last day of a month in this city and it is a hundred and fifty days after the thirtieth of June, and the requisition of the twentieth of February laid in the spring was one hundred and thirty days old at that same thirtieth of June.

Thirty of July, thirty of August, thirty of September, thirty of October, and the thirtieth of November. That is two hundred and eighty days, and he was carrying it in his head the way he had carried a date in his head for four years, and he did not have anybody in the city to say it to.

## The Books On The Sixteenth

"They met at seven on the morning of the sixteenth of November," said Yusuf Adeyemi, "and they have not moved since, and they open once in the year and not twice."

"It is the fourteenth day."

"It is the fourteenth day, and in about three hundred and forty-nine days it will be the fifteenth of November again and they will meet at seven, and they will not move, and that is the whole of the engagement you have with them for another year."

Adeyemi said it on the step, because he had come up the hill on foot in the rain and was not going into a kitchen for it.

"You have never asked me whether they will move."

"I have never asked you," said Lin Hao. "If I ask you, you will tell me what you believe, and I will carry it for a year, and on the fifteenth of November next year I will either be right about it or I will have been wrong about a thing I said out loud to a man standing on a step."

"That is the first sensible thing I have heard you say since the third of July."

"I have said four or five sensible things since the third of July," said Lin Hao. "You were in a corridor for most of them."

## The Roll, And What It Holds Now

Ines Duarte had read the roll of the household on the twenty-fourth of November, at a public desk, in the ordinary way, because it is on a public index and any person may read it on any working morning.

"The roll binds that house by the highest rank in any of its members," she said. "That was nine on the second of April. It has been one since the first of October. It is one now and it will stay one, because there is no instrument in this city that puts a rank back into a man after he has lodged the whole of it at a counter in his own hand."

"So the protection is gone."

"The protection is gone and it went on the first of October at ten minutes to four, in the same nine minutes as everything else," said Ines Duarte. "I have read the roll twice this week to see whether it did what I thought it would do and it did, and there is no second page and no continuation and no instrument that can put a clause under it before the thirtieth of December."

"And nine women."

"Nine women," said Ines Duarte. "Which is not what the roll is for, and which is why I am telling you about it at half past five on a wet step instead of leaving it alone."

## Where The Three Of Them Are

Petra Ilyin was in the room over the laundry off Cordwain and had been for thirteen weeks, and had put a second table in the front room in the middle of November because there is a woman in there now who teaches at two evenings and has to sit somewhere that is not a kitchen.

Nadia Ferrous was at the bottom of a shaft in a building off a third court on four nights out of seven and had been since the eleventh of October, and had asked for the fifth night in the middle of November and had been refused by a man who keeps a rota and who said no without any particular feeling about it, and had asked again four days later and been given it, and had told nobody either request.

Isolde Marchetti had had a bed in the passage at Ferrier Street from the second of October to the eleventh, and had had two rooms above the Bellhouse stage since the eleventh, and had four hundred and eleven nights of work in front of her between the twelfth of November and the end of the year, and on the thirtieth of November she did not sing.

Three women out of a house of nine, and none of the three had said why, and about two hundred and fifty days had gone by.

## The Other Six

Beatrix Halm has been under that wall at half past four every morning since the eighth of October and about forty people have come and asked her what the last line says, and she has read it out to every one of them and told them the truth, which is that it is two words and a dash and two words and she cannot read it either. About nine have come back a second time.

Ines Duarte has audited that branch since the first of October and will be there until the end of January and has not asked him one question about an account, and has entered one line about the branch in a ledger of her own with her own name deliberately not at the head of it.

Tessa Okonkwo went onto a night rota at the Vestry on the middle of November and has taken a thirtieth off about nine people in three weeks and has told nobody which nine.

Agnete Lindqvist has written to her board of nine for the first time in twenty years, asking for one thing, and has been refused nothing, and has not said to anybody in this city what the thing was.

Marguerite Vale is at the Vant with the real books chained on her table and has taken four pieces of work since the second of October and refused nine.

Runa Sato has not filed a return in a hundred and fifteen days. Twenty-five days left in August after the sixth, thirty of September, thirty of October, thirty of November, and a week is seven days, so that is sixteen weeks and three days, and her division has no door and she has not explained that to anybody either.

## The Rota, And The Fourth Line

The man who keeps the rota at that branch office is a keeper of about nineteen years and he is not a clerk and cannot be asked to hurry and has never been asked.

"There are three names on your December page," he said. "There are always three names on it. They are on it because they wrote their names on it in front of me and I wrote them down."

"There is a fourth line."

"There is a fourth line on every page I have ever kept," said the keeper. "It is for anybody who wants nights and has not asked. That is what it is for. I have never put a name on one in nineteen years and I am not going to start in December."

He squared the pad.

"You have not asked me for one either, and I want it on the record that you did not, because you were here and you were the only person here who could have."

## The Three Things He Has Not Bought

There are three things in this city he could put on a table, and he has put none of them on a table since the ninth of May.

The first is a phrase. Anything spent on himself once, to answer a true question in public. It has been available in eight uses across nine years and it has been carried in his coat and it has not been spent, and on the twenty-sixth of August he had nine women in front of him, a decision on a table, and a true question, and he did not.

The second is an instrument he swore on the ninth of May at ten past five never to use, and it has never been used, and he has not needed it since October and will not have it.

The third is not a thing anybody else in that house could do.

That is the whole of it. He lodged his whole standing at a public counter at ten minutes to four on the first of October in his own hand in a queue of nine people with a pen, and every permission he ever bought went into a keeping book, and what is left of nine years of buying is a man who is allowed to ask a woman a question.

There is not one other person in that house who could ask her. Every one of the other eight would have to get leave.

## Eleven O'Clock At Night

He was in the kitchen off Ferrier Street at about eleven at night on the thirtieth of November and two of the three were at the table, and he had it in his mouth for about four seconds.

"Petra."

He did not ask it.

He put his hand flat on the table instead, on the other side of it from hers, and left it there, and said it out loud to the room.

"I am not going to ask you why," he said. "Any of you. Not tonight and not in January and not at the end of any of it, and I am saying it once and then it is not going to be said in this house again, and I would like it put somewhere that I said it and not that I was asked."

Nadia Ferrous did not look up from the cloth she was folding.

"I know you are not," she said. "I have known since the seventeenth of October and there has been a fourth line on a piece of paper on that table ever since and you have walked past it about ninety times."

Then neither of them said anything else about it, and there has not been a word about it in that house since, and the question is still on the table, and it is going to be on the table when they are all four of them old.

## What Is Left Of A Household

"There is one more thing and then I will go down to the branch."

"Go on."

"So the roll binds that house by the highest rank in it, and the highest rank in it is one, and one has nothing under it," he said.

"That house has no floor under it this year and will not have one next year, and every protection nine women had came out of a figure I spent in a queue in about ninety seconds."

"So that is the cost."

"That is the cost and I paid it in public and I would do it again," he said. "And three of them have been sleeping somewhere else since the first of October and two of them are in this kitchen and one of them is in a building off a third court, and not one of the nine of them has left, and there is no instrument in this city that can tell you a household is a household, and it is one."

"Isolde is singing on the ninth of December at the Bellhouse and I have a ticket and I am not giving it to anybody in this room."

She put the cloth down.

"She has four hundred and eleven nights before the end of the year," said Nadia Ferrous, "and you have one shift on the first of January, and I am on it, and the two of them are on it, and a man who keeps a rota wrote all three of our names on a page in his own hand because we asked him to, in front of him, and the fourth line is going to be empty in December the way it has been empty in December for nineteen years."
