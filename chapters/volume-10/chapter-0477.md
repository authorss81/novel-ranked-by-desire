# Nadia Takes The Worst Of It

The car stands between the Vestry floor and the clinic floor and has stood there since about half past seven in the morning, and the doors were shut on it when he came down the stair at about eleven on the seventeenth of August.

"One hundred and seventy-seven days," he said, getting in. "Ten from twenty leaves ten, ten with thirty and thirty and thirty and thirty is one hundred and thirty at the end of June, and July is thirty, so one hundred and sixty at the end of July, and one hundred and sixty with seventeen is one hundred and seventy-seven."

Nadia Ferrous was sitting on the floor of her own car with her back against one corner and her feet in the other.

"That is a stupid thing to come down a stair and say to me," she said.

"You have been taking more than the other two."

"Yes."

"For three days."

"For three days," said Nadia Ferrous, "and I would like you to sit down, because you are going to want to make a speech and a car is the worst place in this city to make one and you will trip."

## What The Depletion Does

"The cold minute is the part that goes into you," said Nadia Ferrous. "That is the part everybody thinks about. It is about a second and a half and afterwards you think you were cold and then it is over, and that is the whole experience and I have been telling it since I was a girl and I have never once been wrong about it."

"And the other part."

"The other part is what is left over," said Nadia Ferrous. "There is always what is left over and it does not stay where it lands. It goes down. It goes down a floor in about four minutes and it goes down a shaft in about nine and it ends up in the one person in this city who is standing at the bottom of a hole she cannot climb out of."

She said it without any weight at all.

"I have been sitting in this car since half past seven letting all of it come up through the floor," she said.

"Petra's and Marguerite's and mine, in that order, with mine last because I would not have it any other way, and it comes up out of the ground of this building and I am the one it finds, and then it goes on down into the rock, and I have no way of knowing what the bottom of that is and I have had three days and about eleven hours to think about it."

"You should have said."

"I did say. I said at about eleven o'clock this morning to a woman at the top of the stair and she did not understand one word of it and went to work."

## He Came Down To Stop Her

"You have to stop."

"There is nothing here to stop."

"There is you in a car at the bottom of a shaft taking the cost of three women out of a building for three days, and you have not eaten since the fourteenth."

"I had a bun at about nine and I will have a bun at about two," said Nadia Ferrous. "Lin Hao. Listen."

"You have a standing of nine and a piece of brass and a clause of thirty-eight words in a book and you could take a paper off a division in about nine seconds, and you have been trying to find a thing to spend on this for three days, and there is not one, and there is not going to be one, and I want you to hear me say it now so that you can stop looking."

"What is the rule."

"There is not a rule at all," said Nadia Ferrous. "That is what makes it unanswerable. Nothing in this city will put a form, an instrument or a column to it, nobody certifies it, and not one office in this city can be walked into by a woman who wants to say that a man made her do it."

"If there were I would have gone to it and I would have made them stop him in about nine seconds and I would not have let you up the stairs today."

## The Thing He Had Closed Off Himself

"Then say the reason properly, because I am going to come at this every way I can think of."

"I will not have you pay it back," said Nadia Ferrous.

The car was very quiet. Somewhere below the Vestry floor a lift that was not this one went somewhere and stopped.

"There is nothing in the world you could give me that would make it even, and there are three reasons, and I have known all three since the fourteenth and have not said one of them out loud in this building, and you are the only person alive entitled to hear them, and I am giving them to you in this order and you are not to interrupt."

She held up one finger.

"One. If you take it back off me it goes into your chest, and there is something in your chest that is spent, permanently, since the fifteenth of July, and you said so in four hundred people on the thirtieth and it is on a record on a shelf in a building off a third court where anybody can go and put their name in a day's book and ask to read it. I have read it. I went and read it."

"That is the first time anybody in this house has read it."

"Two. A person thinks they were cold. That is what it has always been and it leaves no mark and no figure and nothing on anybody's skin, and in about four years there will be a woman in a hall who felt cold in a car on the fourteenth of August."

"And that is how this thing has worked for thirty years and it is the only reason it has worked."

She put the finger down.

"If you take it back, then on the twenty-eighth of July a room in a building will still have a record of me being cold in a car, and that will be the only place any of it exists, and it will be a lie with a date on it."

"And three."

Nadia Ferrous did not put up the third finger. She put both hands flat on the floor of the car.

"Three is that I want it," she said. "That is the whole thing and there cannot be a fourth reason, and I have been sitting in this car for three days working out how to say it in a way that would not sound like a woman being brave at a man's expense."

## What She Wanted And Was Not Going To Have

He got down onto the floor of the car with her, which took him a while, and the car is about five foot by four and not a great deal of it.

"You are not refusing me."

"No."

"You told me on the ninth of May that you wanted me and you have been saying it out loud in this house ever since, and not one of those evenings was about me."

"I have and I do, and it has not gone anywhere because of a shaft," said Nadia Ferrous. "What I am refusing is not you. It is about six seconds in which you get to feel better and I get to be the reason."

"You have got a cough in you and you came down a stair to tell me a number of days, and if I let you have me right now then for about a minute you would be a man who has given something to the woman who took the worst of it, and we would both know exactly what it was."

"That is not a bad thing to want."

"It is a terrible thing to want," said Nadia Ferrous, "and I want it, and I have wanted it for four years, and I am not going to take it out of you on an ordinary afternoon."

She put her hand flat on his chest, over the place, and left it there.

"The twenty-sixth," she said. "The kitchen, after the washing up, and I will come up the stairs that day because that is what I do, and I will tell you then and you can do what you like with it and so can I. Not today. Today you sit on that floor and you let it be a thing that three women did and that I am not going to be talked out of."

## What Was Left Of It

He stayed on the floor of the car with her for about an hour and a half and he did not say anything that moved it.

At about half past twelve she said, "That is enough of you in my car, go and do whatever it is you are doing today," and he got up and his knees went, and she got hold of the rail with her left hand and pulled him the last of the way up with one arm and made nothing of it and did not let go of the rail for about four seconds longer than she needed to.

"One thing," she said, before the doors.

"Say it."

"I have been lighter since the afternoon of the fourteenth," said Nadia Ferrous. "That is what it is like. I am lighter and I cannot put it anywhere, because everything that came down has gone on down into the rock under this building, and the only thing I have is that I am not carrying it."

She looked up at him.

"That is the price and I knew what it was on the fourteenth and I chose it in front of nine people, and I am not going to be told about it by anybody in that kitchen including you, and if you try I will put the car somewhere you cannot follow me to."

The doors shut. She did not move the car.

He went up the stairs at Ferrier Street at about one o'clock and the plate on the wall on the landing had his name at the top of it in his own hand and the date the twelfth of May, and he looked at it for a moment and then went in and washed the plates and did not tell anybody where he had been.
