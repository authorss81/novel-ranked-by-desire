## 1. THE BLOCKING FINDINGS, AND WHAT WAS DONE TO EACH

### 1. The calendar correction was arithmetically wrong, and it had been copied into three files.

`outline/volume-07.md` §5, `state/continuity.md`'s Volume 7 block and `state/open-threads.md` flag 1 all
stated: *a month is thirty days and advances the weekday by two, so August 1 a Tuesday makes September 1 a
Thursday and puts September's Thursdays on the second, ninth, sixteenth, twenty-third and thirtieth.*

The premise is right. **The conclusion is not.** Verified by command:

```
Aug 1 a Tuesday (idx 1) -> Sep 1 = Thu -> Sept Thursdays: [1, 8, 15, 22, 29]
printed Sept list 7,14,21,28 needs Sep 1 = Fri
```

A September whose Thursdays are the 7th, 14th, 21st and 28th has its first on a **Friday**, which wants
August 1 to be a Wednesday. *(The review that raised this said the printed list corresponds to a September
whose first is a Wednesday. That half of the report is also wrong, and the corrected derivation is the one
now on the page in all three files.)*

**The operational ruling is untouched and survives whole: the page governs, both printed lists stand, and
Volume 7 names no day of the week at all.** Only the stated derivation was wrong. It mattered because §5 is
a section this file declares *may not be contradicted by a later batch* — a writer inherits the reasoning as
well as the ruling, and would have inherited an arithmetic error dressed as canon.

### 2. The Chapter 302 card contradicted a closed chapter.

The card's major turn was *"the name of the eighth person is the woman who asked not to be written down."*
The eighth person is Wren Kallo. Chapter 298, line 83, has him say it in his own hand: **"I did it because
Ines Duarte asked not to be written down on the ninth of July."** Either the card meant Wren made that
request — which 298 assigns to Ines — or the eighth name becomes Ines, which breaks 301 and 302's own
premises.

**The turn now names Wren Kallo**, which is what the page already knows, and keeps the strongest half of the
original: the keeper reads both figures off one leaf and says his sworn answer is not wrong, it is **one day
old**, and an answer that has been overtaken does not correct itself. *(The original said nine days old. He
swore the figure on the **first of August** and again on the **first of October**, and Chapter 302 is the
second of October. One day is both correct and much worse, so it is now one day.)*

**A guard was added to the card's required continuity facts**: the request not to be written down was
Ines Duarte's, it may not be attributed to Wren Kallo or to anybody else in that building, and Ines Duarte
is not at those premises and is not on the leaf. The card is where a writer would have gone looking.

### 3. `volume-07.md` §2 overclaimed Ines Duarte's invisibility.

The old §2 said a woman *"kept an address on a composition return of the twelfth of June … and nobody will
ever be able to find her in any instrument in this city."* **Chapter 283 line 99 and Chapter 298 line 83 both
put that address into four divisions in her own hand, on purpose, and close it.**

What is actually true is narrower, and it is what the blank seventh line buys: **the line can never be
attributed to her; the address is findable.** §2 now says exactly that, in bold, with the two chapters named
so that a writer can check it. §1's summary of his defences was corrected the same way — it read *a woman
unnameable in any instrument*, and now reads *a blank seventh line that cannot be attached to the woman it
was cut for*.

**The closed Volume 6 block that carries the original overclaim has been left exactly as written.** A note
in the head block of `state/continuity.md` points at it, names item 7, and states the narrower true thing,
so a reader who arrives at the closed block is corrected on the way in rather than by an edit that would
have quietly changed a closed volume's record.

### 4. "The thirty-fifth of September" does not exist.

Card 303's ending hook used it as the date he swore the other figure. Months are thirty days and he swore
twice, on the **first of August** and the **first of October**. The hook now carries both dates and the
nine seconds, which is the detail the card was reaching for and which is true.

### 5. Batch 0002's header miscounted its own range.

*"Nine chapters, nine days"* for 311–320, which is ten chapters. The chapter count is now ten. The day count
of nine was right and stands.

### 6. A quarterly card came off the board mid-quarter.

Card 328, on the **sixteenth** of November, had *"the card comes off the board and the number goes up."* The
quarterly card is a fixed object that comes down at six on the last working day of a quarter (289, 298,
349). The wall posting belongs at 328 and the card does not. The card now says the figure on the ranks wall
changes and the card does not move, with the reason.

**One related line went with it.** Batch 0003's climax line closed *"the man nobody could read is now a
figure on a wall every Thursday."* Two faults in eleven words: `nobody` is one of the four constructions
this volume rations, and naming a Thursday in a card is how a weekday gets into prose in a volume that
forbids them. It now reads *the man no division could read is now a figure on a public wall every week.*

**339 is now *The Wall Says It Again*.** It names the chapter, it uses no rationed construction, and it is
the last thing the square does: the word the square has read since the thirtieth of July, read out again at
a worse figure.

### 8. The refusal at 310 was described as two different instruments.

`volume-07.md` §6 movement 1 said *a fresh production is the only instrument that can restate a
composition.* Chapter 286 established that **a production answers a call**, and no call is outstanding here.
Cards 309 and 310 correctly said a **head of premises corrects a composition by sworn answer**. The cards
were right and §6 was wrong; §6 now says so, and the live prompt said the wrong thing too and now says the
same thing the cards say.

### 9. The band arithmetic at the volume's last image was never resolved, and §7 claimed it was.

He reaches seven at 328. Every band he built goes flat for eleven days in late November. The floor rises to
8,145 on the thirtieth of December. **No card said whether he prints at or under the floor**, and §7 listed
the question among the things the volume answers.

**The plan now answers it: he prints AT the floor — 8,145 on the nose.** The spine of it is on the page in
§6 movement 5: six under at 301; three women attest things he did and the wall figure goes up at 328; the
eleven days take every band he built flat in public and put him at the lowest he has ever been on a wall;
**and what brings him back to the line is the page he signs at 348 with his own name at the top of it,
attested by the bench and carried into circulation by the minute in four hands at 347.** He is restored to
rank by the one thing the last seven volumes taught him to refuse, and he stops one short.

**The consequence is the volume's closing image and it was not in any card: a man on the floor is not out of
band, so for the first time since the thirtieth of July the ranks wall carries no word about him at all.**
He has spent seven volumes being a sentence on a wall and the last thing that happens to him is that it goes
quiet.

This is stated as the plan's answer in §6, echoed in §7 where the question used to sit unanswered, and
written into card 349 and Batch 0005's required power changes. **All four say a batch may find a better
figure and rewrite them.**
