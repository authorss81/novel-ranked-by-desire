## 1. THE THREE BLOCKERS

### 1. The requisition sheet was described the wrong way round, in five files, and it is load-bearing.

Ch 346 reads the four parts in the flat voice of a clerk reading a column: *The subject: the account carried
at premises 34-6-118* · *The holding: thirty years* · *The figure: seven* · *The ground: that the line cannot
be paid and cannot be produced*. Then: *"Then the clerk read the fifth line, which was not a part and was
only a line"* — *Held by* — *Nothing*. Ch 343 says the same from the other side: *"The tick is against the
held by line, and it is the only tick on the whole of the four parts."*

Five files said the opposite, and one of them — the Chapter 342 summary's loose phrasing, *"Four parts, three
of them full, and a box headed Held by"* — had been hardened into a false claim of fact inside a block
labelled *written fact*.

| File | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `outline/volume-08.md` §2 | the **fourth part** is a box headed *Held by* | four parts, the fourth is the ground; *Held by* is a ruled box on a fifth line that is not a part |
| `outline/batches/volume-08-batch-0001.md` card 355 | the fourth **is** a box headed *Held by* | as above, with 343 and 346 cited |
| `workspace/volume-08/batch-0001/PROMPT.md` | **Its fourth part** is a box headed *Held by* | as above, plus *do not write it as the fourth part* |
| `state/current.md` §2 and the Ch 342 record | **Its fourth part** is a box headed *Held by* | as above |
| `state/chapter-summaries.md` rows 8 and 342 | the requisition's **fourth part** is a box | as above |
| `outline/volume-07.md` §6 close | *four parts and three of them were full and **the fourth is a box** headed held by* | as above — **a sixth file, not in the review's five** |

The sixth was found by grepping the phrase rather than reading to the list, and it is the closed volume's own
outline, which is the file a writer is most likely to consult first when the mechanism is in question. It is
corrected the same way and nothing else in it is touched.

**This was not cosmetic.** `outline/volume-08.md` §6 and `state/open-threads.md` PC-3 both say the fourth
part is the **ground**, and a ground that is a man's own assertion carries no seal while a ground older than
his holding does — which is the whole of how a man at band six opens a file at 372. PC-3 is the volume's only
load-bearing invention. A writer who read §2 before §6 got the exact opposite of the mechanism.

### 2. Three different dates for when the fifth sheet's copies leave, one of them after the chapter that needs it.

Card 357 (14 Jan) forecast four days · card 359 (19 Jan) put service at desks on **the fifteenth** · card 358
(16 Jan) said the copy had been served at Sun Meiling's desk on **the nineteenth**, which is three days in
its own future.

**Decision, and the day spine does not move: 358 keeps the sixteenth of January and the desk service is the
fifteenth.** `outline/volume-08.md` §6 already had it right — *"Service on the people named happened on the
fifteenth, at the desk it was served at"* — so the card was the outlier and the scene keeps its slot. Two
consequences were fixed with it:

- **357's forecast is now five days, not four**, and it names the channel: the ordinary stream at four in the
  afternoon on the nineteenth, which is what a woman on Ferrier Street can actually see coming, because the
  four sheets went out at four on the twenty-ninth of December the same way. Fourteen to the nineteenth is
  five days.
- **The service spine is now stated once, in §6 card 357**, with a line forbidding any chapter from putting a
  fifteenth-service in the future of a sixteenth chapter. This is the guard that stops the next batch
  re-deriving it.

### 3. *"It went out on the twentieth of December four sheets."*

The four sheets were laid and left at four on the **twenty-ninth** — Ch 348: *"The copies left the building
on the ordinary stream at four o'clock that afternoon, bound for eight addresses"* — received up the sixty-one
at about twenty past five, and `state/continuity.md` row 348 reads *eight copies out at four*. The list of
eight went out on the thirtieth of **October**, not December. **Fixed to the twenty-ninth**, with 348 cited
and the Ferrier Street destination spelled out.

**One correction to the review itself, recorded because the plan inherits reasoning as well as rulings:** the
review attributed the twentieth of December to *the list of eight going out (337/338/344)*. On the page that
list went out on the **thirtieth of October** — 337 and 338 and 344 all say so in the same sentence. The
review's main finding stands and is verified independently; only its explanatory aside was off. The fix in
the card names what is actually true rather than repeating the error.

## 2. THE SEVEN SHOULD-FIXES

**4. Card 360's closing beat restarted an expired countdown.** 358 ended with *he has about four days*, and
358 is the sixteenth, so the four days ran out on the twentieth — eight days before 360 closed on *and Sun
Meiling has four days.* **No second deadline was invented and no chapter day moved.** 358's clock is now
fixed and closed on the page (sixteenth to the twentieth, and no chapter in the batch is dated the
twentieth, so it runs out between two chapters and is not a scene). 360's beat now carries the *consequence*
of the clock having run out: ten days of her saying nothing, which is a change rather than a repeated
figure.

**5. Card 351's *seventeen days*.** That is Chapter 302's figure, at the fourth of October, and Hanneke Ruis
took the keys on the seventeenth of September (302: *seventeen since the keys changed on the seventeenth of
September*). At the first of January she has been in that room **a hundred and four days** — seventeen of
September to the seventeenth of December is ninety, and seventeen of December to the first of January is
fourteen — **and the subtraction is printed in the card** so it cannot drift again. The live-thread list in
§7 carried the same stale figure and was corrected with it.

**6. Card 354's *nine weeks*.** The twenty-eighth of October to the eighth of January is seventy days. **It
is ten weeks**, and the card now prints the subtraction (sixty to the twenty-eighth of December, seventy to
the eighth of January).

**7. *The sixth week running*, claimed twice, three weeks apart — and the silence does not start where the
plan assumed.** It starts on the thirtieth of December, when the card came down and the wall said no word at
all (349). **The silence clock is now counted once, in §5a, and every anchor is stated: five days to 353,
which is the first week; twenty-six days to 360, which is the fourth week; ninety days to 400, which is the
thirteenth week.** 353 is corrected to the first week, 360 to the fourth, and the two *six weeks* claims at
400 and in the Movement 5 close to thirteen. The file now forbids two chapters calling the same week of that
silence. **There is no thirty-first in that month**, which is why 30 December to 5 January is five days and
not six.

**8. *"Under it by the middle of January" contradicted the volume built on it.** If he goes under in January
the two words come back in January — but 357, 359 and 360 all report a silent wall through the twenty-sixth,
and §6 says 400 is the chapter in which the wall talks.

**The date moved, not the chapters.** What holds him on the floor of 8,145 is now stated, and it is the
page's own arithmetic: **a plate is a figure for a week and it moves when somebody else writes a deed down,
and eight witness hands signed under a page on the twenty-ninth of December and again on the fourteenth of
January.** The fall therefore belongs to the last week of March, which is exactly where the volume's last ten
days-one-a-day chapters already were. `state/open-threads.md` **PC-9** carried the same mid-January landing
and was corrected in the same pass, including the thirteenth-week figure. **The volume still ends with the
wall talking about him at 400 and nothing about that ending has moved.**

**9. `outline/volume-08.md` inverted a cited source.** The plan read Dagmar Oyelowo as *being wanted by a man
at the bottom of the Board*. `bible/characters.md` says: **she is wanted and says so and it is tender and it
is about being wanted at the bottom of the Board by a man who is at the top of it.**

**The bible is right and the outline was wrong, so the outline was corrected and the bible was not touched.**
Band five on the plate and band six on paper is not the bottom of anything; a woman who has never scored
once is. And *at the top of it* is the phrase this book already runs on — Ch 348, *I am the man at the top of
this page.* **No canon amendment is recorded because no canon changed.**

**10. Card 353 invited a re-derivation the page had already closed.** Ch 349 is explicit: *"Eight people had
signed a witness line under a page on the twenty-ninth. **That was the whole of the arithmetic.**"* The card
framed the unpublished aggregate split as *the arithmetic behind* the figure, which reads as a replacement
cause. **The split explains why nothing on the plate can be traced back to him. It did not put him on the
floor**, and the card, the §6 card, the world-reveals list and `state/current.md` §4 item 4 now all say so,
with 349 quoted and *a writer may not re-derive the rise* printed on the face of it.
