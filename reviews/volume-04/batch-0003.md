## What the gate output does not tell you

**It did not see the seven interval errors in Gate A**, six of which are the most expensive kind this
repository produces — a correct word attached to a wrong number, invisible to every mechanical gate in the
file because none of them uses a banned word:

- Ch 171 said **nine days** in six places where 21 − 14 is **seven**
- Ch 171 said the box had been empty for **eleven days** where 21 − 7 is **a fortnight**
- Ch 171 dated the seventh of March for an event that belongs to the fifteenth
- Ch 172 gave a man **nine weeks** of waiting against a date in March that no page fixes, and had his
  count fall from four thousand to two nine hundred, which is one band and does not lose the school place
  he tells the reader is at stake
- Ch 175 said Ines Duarte was **nine years** into a finding dated the twenty-ninth of March, and said
  **four days** where 24 → 27 is **three**
- Ch 176 said **six days** where 21 → 26 is **five**
- Ch 177 said **nine days** of thinking about the box, and **four days** where 24 → 27 is **three**
- Ch 178 said **nine days** where 24 → 28 is **four**
- Ch 179 said **the first of this month** where Chapter 161's date is the **eleventh**

**It also did not see four things a reviewer should attack first**, and they are set out in full at the
foot of the Batch 0003 close record in `state/open-threads.md`:

1. Whether Chapter 171 is too much machinery too early, given that Chapter 165 already gave this
   household the volume's thesis in a kitchen with more heat in it.
2. Whether Chapter 175 is a decision or a ratification — he worked the answer out on the twenty-first and
   told Ines Duarte on the morning of the twenty-fifth, so what the chapter stages is a man saying aloud
   what he decided alone two days earlier.
3. Whether Chapter 177's first-person close repeats Chapter 174's. Both close with a man alone on a street
   saying a thing to himself about how fast he did it. **They are different in construction and I think the
   defence holds, but a reader who reads them back to back may not.**
4. Whether Chapter 179's scene is the right one at this point. He bought findability at a counter nine
   days earlier; she is being asked to give up the only thing that could find her. **Different trades —
   hers is written and takes three months — but the same coin.**

**It also returned zero on the two gates that did the most work in the last two batches and were
worthless today.** The weekday census found fourteen in a first pass, none of which would have been caught
by any check I had: the twenty-third of April is a Monday, the twenty-fifth a Wednesday and the twenty-eighth
a Saturday, and the prompt says so nowhere. That gate is the one with no automated counterpart in this
repository and it earned its place again.

**And it returned clean on the prompt's own two errors, which are recorded rather than reproduced:** the
Batch 0003 prompt places a one-day countdown at Chapter 177, which is the deadline itself and cannot
print a countdown to a date it has reached — the figure is printed at Chapter 176 instead, with its
subtraction; and the prompt describes the return as filed *a fortnight and ten days before this volume
opened*, where the day map makes it ten days, which Chapter 174 prints with the working.
