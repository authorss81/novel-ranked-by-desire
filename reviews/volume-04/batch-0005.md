## What the gate output does not tell you — read this part

**1. The sentence duplication gate returned ZERO on a scene that reproduced a scene forty chapters behind
it, word for word, across forty twelve-word runs.** Chapter 197's first draft carried Chapter 189's
physical sequence out of Beatrix Halm's signal cabin and into Marguerite Vale's Exchange aisle: the same
handhold, the same timed announcement, the same two-handed instruction, the same finish with her mouth and
then her hand again, the same unhurried second half. Chapters 169, 179 and 189 were all opened and read
before 197 was drafted. **The sentence gate is structurally incapable of seeing it, because the passages
had been transposed rather than copied and a sentence is a different sentence once it has moved.** It was
caught only by a 12-gram scan run deliberately after the sentence gate. **This is the finding of the
batch, it is now adjudicated item 66, and it is the second time in this repository that the sentence gate
has returned clean on a real duplication.**

**2. The prompt's own calendar was wrong and the mechanical gates cannot see a calendar.** The Batch 0005
prompt listed the twelfth of May as a Thursday with the Board repainting on it, and also listed the
seventeenth as a Thursday with the Board repainting on it, and both cannot be true. Chapter 181's printed
card makes May's Thursdays the 3rd, 10th, 17th and 24th. **The card governs; only the seventeenth repaints;
the twelfth was written with no weekday on it.** Registered in `state/open-threads.md` rather than
smoothed. **This is the ninth batch in a row in which a weekday could have been named on a date that does
not carry it and was caught only by reading.**

**3. Six real interval errors survived into the first draft and were found by hand, not by any gate.**
They are listed in the Gate A table in `state/open-threads.md`: *four months* for *eight days* at Ch 192,
*eleven hours* for *the best part of a day* at Ch 193, *nine days* for *five* at Ch 194, *forty-nine gone*
where forty-nine left belonged at Ch 195, *nine of them* for *seven* at Ch 199, and *the third caller* for
the second at Ch 191. **A count is not a reading and an inference is not an anchor, and the anchor for the
caller count was Ch 190's Nunn, who has to be read on the page before a chapter can count her.**

**4. Five banned weekday names and one over-budget `nobody` were in the first draft and cut.** The ration
on `nobody` is one per batch and Batch 0004 came in at zero; the first pass carried more and the single
shipped instance is Ch 193's *Nobody stopped the woman*, which is the loss beat and is load-bearing.

**5. `nobody` / `no one` / `no-one` is at exactly one, which is the ration and not under it.** A reader
should not be told this file found that acceptable. It is acceptable because the one instance is the
chapter's central loss; a seventh instance anywhere in the batch would not have been.

**6. The paragraph pass was done by hand, with a copy taken first.** Per adjudicated item 65, Batch 0004's
rewriting script deleted nine sentences and every gate returned zero. A snapshot of all ten chapters was
taken before any edit and the sentence sets were diffed after; **no prose was lost.** The only words that
disappeared are the scene words cut from Ch 197 when 189's mechanics were removed, which is the point of
the removal.

**7. The 8-gram scan returns 783 shared grams between this batch and the earlier chapters, and almost all
of them are the volume's own recursive motifs** — *do every figure out loud*, *six women to a guild
broker's standing*, *answers in his own name for every member of it*, the roll's physical description, the
holding consent formula. **These are callbacks carrying the volume's weight and the 8-word *sentence* gate
returned zero, which is the meaningful check.** The one exception was the Ch 197/189 scene sequence, and
that was real and is fixed.

## Out-of-batch repairs, and why they were made anyway

**Ch 185, two sentences.** It called the fifth of May *the second Thursday on the card by the time
clock*; **Ch 181 prints that card and the fifth is not on it — the tenth is the second Thursday**, and
both Ch 191 and Ch 197 build on the card. It also said the pale rectangle had been bare for *the fifth
week* when Ch 190 prints *fourteen days* on the tenth and the rectangle went bare on the twenty-sixth of
April, which makes the fifth nine days.

**Ch 160, one sentence.** *The only thing in Ostara that nobody asked for.* **`nobody <verb>` is retired
outright and the prompt's gate is specified at zero across the whole of Volume 4**, and this instance is
in a volume this batch closes.

**All three are one-clause changes. None alters a plot point, a date that anything else depends on, or a
character's standing. They were made because they are factual contradictions with canon printed in other
chapters, and a closed batch is not a reason to leave a live contradiction in the book.**

## The duplication scan, cross-chapter, and why it had to be re-run

**Sentence-level 8-word duplication was run over all 260 chapters, not only this volume.** Sixty-four
sentences repeat across chapter boundaries in the book and every one of them is a recursive motif that
the book runs on — *she put her hands flat on the table*, *he said it without any weight at all*, the
plate's own figures. **Four repetitions touch Chapters 191–210 and two survive deliberately: the printed
indemnity clause, which must recur verbatim because it is printed on the instrument in three chapters,
and *he put his hand flat on the rail*, which is the same Exchange clerk making the same gesture in
Ch 196 and Ch 201.** The two that did not survive are in the table: **Ch 196's *I am a guild broker of
nineteen years' standing*, which ran whole in Ch 195 as well, one chapter apart.**

## What this file still does not tell you

**It does not tell you whether the ten chapters are good.** It certifies counts, arithmetic and
cross-references, all of which are re-derivable, and it certifies nothing about a sentence's quality.
**The most useful thing in this second pass is not in it: eleven arithmetic defects survived a full
gate suite that had already been run once by the same process that wrote the pages.** A reviewer who
wants to find something should not start with the gates.
