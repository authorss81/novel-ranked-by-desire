## Gate A found eight real errors, and two of them are invisible to every gate in this repository

**The mechanical gates returned clean on a batch that contained eight wrong intervals.** They are listed
in full in `state/open-threads.md`; the six that mattered:

1. **A clock from the morning inside an afternoon scene.** Chapter 170: Agnete Lindqvist says *it is
   four minutes to six and you have a queue* at about one o'clock in the afternoon. Corrected.
2. **A "yesterday" that was two days.** Chapter 170, Beatrix Halm on Ossian Kerr, who came to the gate
   on the eighteenth, described from the twentieth as yesterday. Corrected to the day before yesterday.
3. **"Forty minutes" in two places that is twenty.** Chapter 162: she leaves the room at twenty past one,
   he is brought in at twenty to two, he comes out at two. Corrected.
4. **The hearing dated to the wrong month.** Chapter 162: *the twenty-ninth of the month before last*.
   The hall was the twenty-ninth of last month. Corrected.
5. **Two intervals asserted across a month boundary whose length is not fixed.** Chapter 167: the
   eight of February to the twelfth of March printed as *five weeks*, which is only true of a thirty-day
   February, and the twelfth of March to the chapter's own day printed as *four months*, which is thirty-
   six days. Both were rewritten so that they are true under either month length — **and D4's M−1 row
   is still flagged and still not to be used, so a month length was never assumed anywhere in this batch.**
6. **A document given an impossible age.** Chapter 169 said a description had been in a file since the
   fourteenth of March and that a woman had been reading it aloud in that kitchen for four months. The
   description went in on the twenty-ninth of March, which from the nineteenth is three weeks, and the
   kitchen has been talking for four months. Both halves were cut and rewritten.

## What a reviewer should attack first, in this author's own judgement

1. **Whether Chapter 165 gives away the volume's argument too cheaply.** *The only thing that defends a
   person against an audit is being the same woman all the way back* is stated in one scene, by the most
   reliable mouth in the household, and the chapter then points at the visitors' book as the only
   document in the city that proves it. **Chapter 195 is Ines's and is named *Nothing Said At All*, and
   it has to land without the argument having been made this plainly once already.**
2. **Whether Chapter 168 is a nuisance or an escalation.** The case for the escalation is in the close
   record: it is the first thing in four months the household cannot answer, refuse, price or record. The
   case against is that a private citizen with six questions in pencil is a very civilised device, and a
   reader nine chapters deep in forms may put it down as a change of register. **The defence is that he is
   right, that he says so, that he warns them about the next one, and that the household's reaction to
   him is dread rather than relief.**
3. **Whether Chapter 169's scene earns eleven years.** Four terms is a great deal of front matter for a
   scene, and the chapter's strength is its terms. The defence is that she is a former clerk and a woman
   who cannot let a room take a testimony, and that the terms are how she is professionally able to want
   this. **The staging is new against all ten entries in the grid, the rib is never mentioned, nothing is
   spent on anybody, his name is used once and as a word, and her last act is the door left open.**
4. **Whether Chapter 162's solution to its own problem — lying — is survivable.** He keeps Runa Sato's
   promise by telling her a falsehood and then tells her he has told her a falsehood, and she says
   *right*. **If that reads as a victory rather than as the first thing the promise cost, the chapter's
   whole second half falls.**

## One departure from the outline, registered rather than silently adopted

`outline/volume-04.md` §4 has Sun Meiling ask Agnete Lindqvist's question *in a washroom on Ferrier
Street*. **The chapter has her ask it at a door in a corridor in a building off the Vestry market, at
eleven at night on the seventeenth, alone.** The reason is in the close record: a washroom implies she
left the house in daylight and to a stranger, and the whole of Chapter 163 is that she has spent the
batch refusing to be the cheapest door in the city. **The washroom's function is preserved in the staging
of the scene itself.** This is flagged in `state/continuity.md` and `state/open-threads.md` as well as
here, because a plan and a page that disagree is adjudicated item 50 waiting to happen.

`outline/volume-04.md` §12 puts M+4's Thursdays on the 5th, 12th, 19th and 26th, so **the twentieth of

worst defects have been produced.

## Repair pass — the prose review against the page, and what it changed

**Run against the committed batch, findings in `logs/batch-0002.review.log`. Six findings were carried
into the manuscript and one was examined and not actioned. No chapter was restarted, no date was moved,
no thread was opened or closed, and the planned plot is unchanged.** Every gate figure in this file and
in `state/open-threads.md` was re-derived by command after the repairs, not before them.

1. **THE SCENE WAS WRITTEN AS IMPLICATION AND IS NOW THE SCENE.** `outline/volume-04.md` §10 schedules
   five scenes and 169 is one of them, and `AGENTS.md` is unambiguous that implication is not the
   target. The encounter had run to about seven hundred words of withheld explicitness. **It is now
   written out — the cardigan over the chair, the skirt with the staple in the zip, the shirt thrown
   nine inches, the cold hands, the runner with its worn path and her knees on either side of it, the
   hand laid flat and kept there, her weight given all at once.** **Every staging commitment survives:
   the corridor, the four doors, the window that does not open, the rib never mentioned by anybody,
   nothing spent on anybody, no impaired state, her professional register, his name used once at the
   end as a word inside a sentence, and the door left standing open behind her.** Chapter 169 moves
   from 3,618 words to **4,719**, which is over the 2,600–3,900 scene band and inside the Chapter 55
   and Chapter 8 precedent; the exception is named in `state/open-threads.md`.
2. **A CONTRADICTION IN THE ROSTER WAS REPAIRED AT BOTH ENDS.** Chapter 170 had Runa Sato saying *I
   was struck off a roster*, against Chapter 162's rule that a member entered as refusing is still on
   this roster and still paid and still in the division. **The duty clerk at Chapter 163 now says
   plainly that the fourth column took her off nothing and that he ruled the line off the wall himself
   the previous afternoon for a strength count the office had owed since February — and Chapter 170's
   speech is rewritten to say the same thing she would say, which is that there are two pieces of paper
   on her and one of them was hers.** This is better than the defect: the thing that removed her from a
   wall is a man doing a count he was late doing, which is the volume's own argument about arithmetic,
   and the refusal still costs her the premises.
3. **A DUPLICATED PARAGRAPH WAS FOUND BY A GATE THAT DID NOT LOOK FOR THEM.** Chapter 161's
   eighty-word loading-yard paragraph appeared twice, six lines apart, with four words differing. **The
   second instance is now two new paragraphs: he counts the bodies coming out of that gate in the way
   he counts washrooms, and understands standing there that this is the whole of what a division clerk
   will ask anybody for.** It is better than the copy, and it sets up what Chapter 162 asks him for.
4. **ONE ANTI-PATTERN WAS SPENT TWICE IN ONE CHAPTER'S CLOSING BEATS.** Chapter 169 carried *never once
   been thanked* and *went home without waiting to be thanked* three paragraphs apart. **The second is
   rewritten**, and the first is kept, because the chapter's last line now has to land on the woman who
   asked the question rather than on the word.
5. **A SINGLE-ASTERISK ITALIC WAS CUT AT 161:7.** The gate that reported zero emphasis markup had
   checked bold and underscore and not italics. **That gate's line is corrected above.**
6. **THE THURSDAY COUNT AND THE HOT-PATH BYTES ARE CORRECTED ABOVE.** The first draft of this record
   claimed the word appears once in the batch; it appears five times, in three chapters. The hot path
