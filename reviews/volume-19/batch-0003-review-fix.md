## 1 · HIGH — sixteen date glosses that contradict the subtraction in the same line

The finding is right and it is the most serious thing in the batch. By Method A (`outline/volume-19.md` §5a) the predecessor's day sits in the **same month** as this chapter's day in eight of the ten pairs, and all eight pairs called it *of last month*. Each appeared twice — the opening line and the closing spine paragraph — for sixteen wrong glosses, and the chapter then contradicted itself inside one line: 921 asserted that the third of one month to the tenth of the next is an interval of seven.

**The sixteen are repaired and the repair is one word in sixteen places, and the two words that replace them are the ones this book's model volume already uses.**

```
SAME-MONTH PAIR  ->  "the <N> of this one",  with plain subtraction in the same line
CROSSING PAIR    ->  "the <N> of last month" (or "of the month before this one"), with the
                     crossing named as itself, which is what 925 and 929 already do
```

Volume 18, which is the model, prints *the second of this one* for a same-month pair and *the twenty-fifth of the month before this one* for a crossing. Volume 19's own immediately preceding chapter does the same: **920 is itself a crossing, 26 May to 3 June, and it prints *the twenty-sixth of last month*.** So the crossed form is house, and only the eight same-month glosses were wrong.

**THE TWO CROSSINGS, 925 AND 929, WERE CHECKED AGAIN AND LEFT EXACTLY AS THEY WERE.** They were right, the review said so, and they are right. Normalising them to *the month before this one* was tried, measured, and dropped: it added **10 shared twelve-grams, 13 shared eleven-grams and 24 shared eight-grams** against `chapter-0856` and `chapter-0907`, every one of them apparatus, and bought nothing a reader would notice. The batch's twenty-two edits were all reverted to the reviewer's own approved form before the numbers above were taken.

**THE ONE `of last month` THAT STANDS IN 921 IS NOT A GLOSS.** It is Brack, at line 97, saying he has been trying since **the ninth of last month** — the ninth of May, thirty-two days back, about a card that went up on the thirtieth of March. It is speech, it is accurate, it is not tied to any spine figure, and changing it to *the ninth of this one* would make him have been trying for one day about a man who has been standing at his third stool once. It is left and its leaving is recorded so a later pass does not "correct" it.

**A GATE IS NOW PUBLISHED AND IT PASSES.** Every one of the ten pairs was re-derived, the required gloss was computed from Method A rather than read off the page, and the printed gloss was tested against it in both the opening line and the closing paragraph: **ten of ten agree, and every crossing is named as a crossing on the page.**
