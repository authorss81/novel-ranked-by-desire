## What was wrong, and what it is now

**1 · HIGH — 908 printed the banned figure three times, and the prompt told the writer to.** `PROMPT.md` §3 puts *any eighth hand in the stone* under **NOT PRINTED, IN ANY FORM, ANYWHERE IN 901–950**. The same file's §5 said *908 is the hill: no eighth hand*, and CARD 908 said *seven hands, no eighth*. **A writer reading §5 printed what §3 forbids, and §3 is the allowances clause and governs.** Three prints went, and a fourth the review did not list: the closing clause's *seven hands and not one more*, which asserts the absence by counting to it and fails the principle the review stated — that the absence stays unasserted.

Every fact underneath them is kept whole. The last inch and a half at that end is dressed flat and has been dressed flat for years, so anybody coming to add to it would have to take a chisel to a face with no history on it, which is a different act from adding to a wall about four hundred people a week read past. He looks *past where that cut stopped, the way a man looks along a wall for the next thing on it*. The stone has *seven hands and nothing cut past the end of the last one*. He tells the clerk *seven hands and nothing cut past the last of them*. The closing clause names the seventh hand stopping in the middle with the cutting clean behind it, which is what 895 says and what this stone has said since.

**A second sentence went, and the review was right about it for a reason the review did not give.** *And nothing whatever is said about them here either* was a note about the outline's own prohibition sitting inside the narration. It is now a fact about a man: *he has never once found out who put them there and is not going to start this afternoon.* The two words are still not printed and still nothing whatever is asserted about them.

**The three places that taught the fault are corrected too, or it comes back at 936.** `PROMPT.md` §5's hill sentence, CARD 908's revealed-information line, and the §7 movement-three row that would have handed the same instruction to whoever writes 936. The prompt now says in terms that §3 governs and the absence is never named or counted to.

**2 · MEDIUM — 907's grievance rested on a closure the batch never showed.** The woman said her neighbour knocked on the twenty-eighth and found nobody; he explained it by the office being shut at half past five. That contradicts a knock in the morning and the card's own quarter to ten, and it invents a shutdown on a day nothing else in 901–910 accounts for. **The review dated the gap day as 28 January; the chapter says the twenty-eighth of the month before this one, which from the second of March is 28 February, day 3,987. The finding stands either way — it is a gap day inside the batch's own span — and the review's day number is recorded here so the next verifier does not inherit it.**

**The grievance now runs on the copy, which is what the chapter and CARD 907 were always about.** The neighbour goes up **at a quarter to eight in the morning because that is what the paper says**, finds nobody on the landing, goes again the next morning at a quarter to eight, and finds the same. The bad paper reads a quarter to eight instead of a quarter to ten, so **the wrong copy and the wrong knock are now one mistake and the chapter's central image closes on itself**. His answer names an ordinary working morning with that door open at a quarter to ten like any other, and a quarter to eight as two hours before we open — and it is still the truth, and it still fails to help her while she is standing there, which is the beat the chapter needs and the beat the review did not have to point at.

**Nothing was invented to replace what was cut.** No day in 901–910 now says that office was shut, and no closure, holiday, absence or errand was written in to keep the removed sentence's shape. 908's account of the same morning still holds untouched, because the delivery man and the woman selling bread were on the page before this pass and still are.
