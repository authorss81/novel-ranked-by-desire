## The eleven, as published

1. **The word-count row was false and it was hiding a ceiling breach.** The batch is 21,858 words, not the 21,309 the block below published, and 913 was sixty-four words over the ceiling when that figure was written.
2. **917's central count did not add up, and a chapter whose spine is a number cannot have a number that does not add up.** Four, then eight, then nine, with a man in the doorway saying eight.
3. **The half-past-five carry was used four times in ten chapters and is now four different sentences.**
4. **918 read *About nine in nine of them are strangers and about nine in nine of them are asking where something is*, which breaks itself twice in one line.** Nine in nine is every one, and the prepended `about` turned an exact count into an inexact one.
5. **The batch paid twice for one `thanked` effect.**
6. **The hedge had become the prose's default connective: 133 `about N` in 21,800 words, one every 170 words, and twelve `about four` in 919 alone. It is now 44.** Sixty were cut across all ten files, and the cuts were not random: a hedge is kept where a person is uncertain and dropped where a person is certain.
7. **The signature constructions ran to seventy-six and are now fifty-seven: `the whole of` 25→9, `the only` 24→21, `the first` 27→27.**
8. **One principle was restated six times.** 914, 916 and 913 stay, because each is a different speaker making a different argument with different consequences.
9. **916's date clause did not parse.**
10. **916 slipped a referent.**
11. **Three chapters closed on something flat.** 912 ended on a null result, 919 on a self-observation, and 920's last line before the footers was a calendar note.
