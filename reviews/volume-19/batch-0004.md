## 1 · WHAT THE BATCH OWES ITS WRITER

**The refusals travel, the comers ask for a list so they need not knock wrong, and the office learns what a door cannot give.** In ten chapters the movement answers a question 930 opened: if a refusal can only be given in person, what is the instrument that lets an office reach a person who cannot come? The answer, arrived at four different ways and conceded in the same room, is that every such instrument is a record of who comes, and that the record is the thing this office has spent nine years not making.

**THE SPINE OF THE BATCH IS THE CONVERSION OF THE NO-ROLL RULE INTO A STANDING REFUSAL TO COUNT.** At 931 the office discovers the rule has a cost in a person. At 932 it breaks its own first practice to avoid the cost. At 933 a woman shows it the one place an answer is safe and cannot be used. At 934 it declines the one tool that would help and learns the refusal was structural. At 935 the people start asking for the roll themselves. At 936 it learns a stranger has written the door down at a kerb. At 937 it puts its own figure in public beside a man who has been keeping a truer one. At 938 it refuses to be the man who says the address aloud and lets a stranger do it four feet away. At 939 it watches a correction make the error worse. At 940 it closes the count.

## 2 · THE TEN CHAPTERS

**931 — The Room That Cannot Wait.** A woman at about twenty past four with a question about a sister who will not come, and a row that stands with the space under the hour empty for ever. He offers to send somebody to that house. The woman refuses it and the clerk stops him, and the reason is that a promise about a person written down is a thing that can be produced against her in her own front room. He says the remedy out loud in the passage with about nine people going past, and the clerk will not enter it. **Cost: he has told a corridor that this office will come to a house, and nothing in that room will ever be able to tell him whether she told her sister.**

**932 — The Night Asking.** Two in the morning on a landing, on one of his three nights off. The door opens for the first time in nine years at an hour the card does not carry. The real question is whether this office keeps anything about who comes up the stair, and he says no, in the dark, to a stranger, and hears himself give the shape of it. **The question cannot be entered in the words used; the asking is entered; the space under it is where the rest of it is. THE DOOR'S SILENCE AT NIGHT WAS A PROTECTION AND IT IS GONE.**

**933 — The Stopped Platform. THE SCENE.** A platform where the service stopped calling in the fourth month and about nine people stand every morning; a brick waiting shelter at the end of it; forty minutes. **THE AXIS IS THE PASSAGE AND NOBODY NAMES IT.** None of 833's staging returns. She has been reading those hours aloud at a market end eleven times and has written nothing down. He keeps the practice she asked for and says its cost out loud: neither of them can ever prove it happened.

**934 — The Sheet She Leaves Alone.** A woman who cannot write her question and speaks perfectly well; the blank sheet on that table not put in front of her for two reasons given aloud; the row standing empty; and **the first entry in nine years that is about the man who owns the room.** She says she would have taken it and got it down badly and that it would have been the only paper in her kitchen with her own handwriting on it.

**935 — The Row She Leaves Alone.** The carriers report a stair that is lighter and heavier. **About nine people a fortnight are asking the clerk to write down that they came, with no question attached, because a visit is the only proof left now that a refusal on paper is gone.** She refuses, and sees it first: a row about a class of person is a row about people. He stops asking her for a count and says why out loud.

**936 — The Stone Holds Seven Hands.** The corner. Seven hands and nothing cut past the last of them, the absence never named or counted to. **A sheet of paper on the rail of that board that he cannot read and will not take a lamp to read.** He does not take it, does not send anybody, does not cut, and goes back up four flights and tells the clerk, which is not what he did in the spring.

**937 — The Forty-Seventh Sheet. QUARTER-END.** The head in a lane, in another man's small chalk, beside the sooner figure, **the first time any part of that office's standing sheet has ever been in a lane.** `4,199 − 1,499 = 2,700 = 30 × 90`, thirty-one sheets and thirty quarters. He refuses to let a second sentence go under the two figures, because a second figure under a first one is a second figure.

**938 — The Seventh Of October.** The month's form on the board, the two squares empty, the boy not on the page. A man cannot make it out and asks him to read it. **He refuses in about four seconds and stands twelve feet off while a stranger reads it out loud four times to about nine people.** No form ordinal is printed. Upstairs, the clerk hands him what she has been carrying since the eighth.

**939 — Corrected Wrong Twice.** A frame with three inks on it, corrected at a counter, wrong at the other end. Refused in four seconds. **She is early and not careless: she was sent by the paper and stood in a street for an hour and three quarters.** Then a man comes up with four hands on it and the four hands do not agree.

**940 — The Comers Without A List. THE IRREVERSIBLE DECISION.** A division return wants one figure and the figure exists in the heads of two people. **No list of comers, ever — not a roll, not an index, not a count, not a number in any form, for any reason, and not for the reason that a number is better than a name.** Said out loud with the door open and both carriers in that passage. What it closes: keeping hours by keeping people, and the last instrument the office had for telling itself whether its own door was working. What it costs, named before it is spent: about nine people a fortnight knocking wrong for the rest of the year. The drawer is sixty months shut.
