He writes four sheets of plain paper at his own kitchen table, in his own hand, with his name at the top of the first one and at the foot of the last one, and lays them into the keeping of a room where nine people sit, and eight of them sign under him. **The option closed: he can never be the address again in a document.**

**And the second one is at 345, and it is a refusal with a price.** She offers him the small way — bring it in, let it be classified once, properly, and it will be administered instead of taken. He refuses it with an instrument and not an argument. **Both doors close in the same afternoon: he will never ask to be classified, and she will never make the offer again.**

## 2. WHAT IT CHANGED

- **The account of thirty years is CALLED.** The word went into the chained real book at ten to nine on the first of December, by the broker holding the book on a pen on a chain, with the rule under it and the date against it. **It is not paid and not produced and it cannot be produced, and it will be called again at every valuation on the first of the month for as long as he lives.**
- **A requisition was tabled, sealed by five of nine, and voided at the ninth minute** because a requisition is laid against the person who holds an account and there is no person on the sheet. **The machine worked. This is the proof of it. It did not close.**
- **The ground the room had is the best ground in this city and it has been available since the second of October**, in a fresh leaf by the fourth, in four divisions by the sixteenth, in fourteen by December, and nobody wrote the word requisition for six weeks and three days.
- **The requisition began at Marguerite Vale's own guild table** at seven on the morning of the sixteenth of November, nine people reading her real books, because he had put them there on purpose. **The exposure that saved her handed a room the best reason anybody had this year, and she says so out loud in a kitchen and will not be thanked for it.**
- **A minute in four hands went out on the ordinary stream at four on the twenty-sixth** and put his name in the middle of a record made by nine people he cannot argue with. It is the first document in this book with his name in it that he did not write and cannot take back.
- **The quarterly card printed 8,145 as the floor and put him at 8,145 on the nose**, and the plate had read 8,131 without moving for the whole of December. **Eight people carried him fourteen points in one day to the line of the floor and not one grain past it.**
- **The ranks wall says no word about him at all for the first time since the thirtieth of July**, and Beatrix Halm's notebook has a line in it about him with nothing in it for the first time in nine years.
- **A man who could not be requisitioned because there was nobody in the box is now a man who has written his own name in it.** Volume 7 arrives at *requisitioned* and stops one chapter short of *received*.
- **Wren Kallo is going to speak to somebody she has not chosen, and the volume ends on her walking out of a door at twenty to two in the morning.**
- **Nothing was taken off anybody in ten chapters. No requisition was received, no grain was spent, no draw was made, nothing was routed, no folio opened, no settle clock started, and the classification is described and not completed.**

## 3. THE CALENDAR, EVERY INTERVAL RE-SUBTRACTED

**A month is thirty days and has no thirty-first.** Every interval in this batch is derived by subtracting day numbers in the world's own calendar and **not** by counting a real month. **The first pass of this batch got eleven of them wrong that way and corrected every one** — see §5.

The 12-gram scan ran **first**, before anything else, against all 350 chapter files, and again after every repair round. It found **forty-seven classes** touching 341–350 on the first run and **twenty-nine more** that the repair rounds created while correcting their own date arithmetic. **Fourteen were fixed by hand in the prose and recorded:**

- Solveig Rask's *without any weight at all, which was how she said the things that cost her*, lifted verbatim from **Volumes 2 and 4** into 341

The paragraph-level gates found and closed: **three-word duplicate paragraphs** (*She looked at him.* in 343, colliding with 338; *She squared the slate.* in 344 and 345; *She said it to the table and not to him.* in 343 twice and once in 348), **one intra-paragraph sentence repeat**, **one repeated eight-word sentence opening** (*Called on the first of December at ten to nine*, 346 and 348), **two over-ninety-word paragraphs that reappeared** after date repairs and were split by hand, and **two unclosed quotations** caught by the whole-file balance gate after hand splits.

The banned-construction scan found one `He felt`, two `afterwards`, two announced ages, three weekday names and one *Saturday's notice*, all removed or rewritten and recorded in the live gate block. **Sixteen over-ninety-word paragraphs existed in the first draft and every one was split BY HAND at a speech beat or a sentence beat; no splitter ran at any floor at any point.**

**THE ONE REMAINING 12-GRAM MATCH IS FRAMED AND DELIBERATE:** *I want it to be a page with a name at the top of it* is said in the hearing at 344 and repeated by him at 345, and the chapter names what it is returning to in the sentence between them. A gate record is a claim; the four re-runs after the last prose edit are what is being claimed.

## 5. THE ONE THING THE REPAIR PASS FOUND THAT COST A CHAPTER

**350 shipped as a structural clone of 340.** The first 12-gram run found **445 whitespace windows and 502 variant windows** shared between the two scenes — the terms, the collar over the one chair, the shift, the ink at the cuticles, the collarbone, the three orgasions, the roll onto their sides, the sixty counted against his skin. **The whole of the scene in 350 was rewritten from nothing.**

The rewrite kept the five terms and the shape of a scene chapter and changed everything underneath them: **the terms are on a sheet she wrote and capped, in a clerk's hand, and he reads them rather than hears them; she undresses first and in order and says it takes eleven seconds; she tells him to get a button wrong on purpose; she makes him describe her body twice and says she will tell him when to stop; and the physical sequence is oral first, then she takes him herself, then three orgasions, and she holds him still with a fist and says *now*.** The shared figure is now **one window**, and that window is the framed refrain in 344/345 above.

**This is the third time in this volume that a gate found something a word count could not see**, and the second time the answer was to throw a chapter out and write it again.

## 6. WHAT THE GATES DO NOT SEE

- **That the requisition is not the volume's villain.** There is no villain in 346. There are nine countable people, one of whom walked out of the room on the twenty-third and has not been back, one chair that has been empty since the first of November, and a chair who offered the small way and was refused and will not offer it again. **A majority was spent for nothing and the four people who put a seal on it remember it for the rest of their lives, and that is the entire cost.**
- **That the failure is not a rescue.** She says so to his face at ten past eleven on the twenty-third: *nothing is what happens on the first of January either.* A called line nobody answers is not a safe line, and the only reason he is walking about the city on the first of December is that a woman with a blotter said nine words at a desk under a window.
- **That the volume ends on the wall going quiet.** He has spent seven volumes being a sentence on a wall, and the last thing that happens to him is that the sentence stops, and it stops because eight people he met a fortnight ago signed a piece of paper.
- **That Isolde Marchetti is not softened and not redeemed.** She gets four sentences and they are about the fact that a reason was given back to her for the first time in two months and she is not going to make it larger than it is. **Nothing in this batch touches what she did on the fifteenth of October.**
- **That the scene is Wren Kallo's and not his.** He is a man who has been the object of five scenes in a book and the object of none of them on the last night of this volume. She directs every beat of it, she chose the room, she wrote the terms on a sheet, and the thing that happens afterwards happens to her.
- **That a woman with a professional standing is more frightening than a woman with a gift.** At 343 Marguerite Vale is the most dangerous person at that table and she has no instrument in this book at all.

## 7. THE LESSON, AND WHAT THE NEXT PHASE OWNS

**Lesson: a batch that ends a volume inherits the whole apparatus of the volume's climax, and the apparatus is where the duplication lives.** Three scenes in six batches have now been written against the same template — terms, a body described in words, three orgasions, a minute of stillness, tea. **The template is useful and it is the single biggest duplication risk in this manuscript, and the only thing that has caught it four times in three volumes is the 12-gram scan run first.**

**What the next phase owns.** `PHASE_SYSTEM.md`: a writing phase never plans a volume. **There is no `outline/volume-08.md` and no `outline/batches/volume-08-batch-0001.md` and no `workspace/volume-08/` directory, and none may be created by this phase.** The next phase is a volume-planning phase and the dispatcher seeds it. `outline/ending.md` is untouched: the reservoir is returned in full at 486, Lin Wei's file opens at 372, he ends at rank one, and **the great offer is Chapter 486's and is not Wren Kallo's and not Anouk Ferreira's.**

**What Volume 8 inherits, in one line each:** a man who **can be requisitioned and has not been**, standing at the floor of his band, with the most legible figure in Ostara and the quietest wall in the city · a called account that will be called again on the first of January at ten to nine and cannot be paid or produced · a minute in four hands that says a requisition was laid against him and voided · a page in the keeping of a room with eight witness hands under it and a gap in the line · four sheets of file 4,112 in a drawer on Lantern Row and the whole of that file now in an auditor's hands who is not a party to it · a false attestation of the twenty-seventh of November in a Bureau division's standing papers which he may not ask about · **and a woman who has decided to speak to somebody she has not chosen.**
