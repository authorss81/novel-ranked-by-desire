**THE TWO IRREVERSIBLE DECISIONS ARE BOTH HIS AND NEITHER IS REVERSIBLE.**

**The first is the eleven days.** He knows the band will fall, he knows an account under its floor is callable, and he knows the books that could call it are in hands he did not appoint because nine days earlier he put them there on purpose in front of nine people. He does it anyway, and the stranger lives, and she does not die and will not walk again, and nobody in this book who stays dies.

**The second is 336 and it is worse.** He breaks the standing undertaking of the ninth of July — one hundred and thirty-eight days old and never once tested — by carrying a leather wallet up two flights of a laundry stair and asking the one woman in this city he swore never to hand a paper to, **in her own rooms**. **The option closed: the term does not exist again.**

## 4. WHAT THE GATES DO NOT SEE

- **A plate falling is not a punishment and the chapter that says so had to be written, not asserted.** 334 exists because the band going down is only a mechanic until a woman who attested to it reads it and says she would do it again and that it got her nothing.
- **A cost paid by refusing to act is invisible on every instrument this project owns.** There is no gate that reads *what he did not do*. The eleven days pass every mechanical check in this file and are the most consequential eleven days in the volume.
- **A framed refrain is allowed to repeat and an unframed one is not.** The eleven dates are said twice because a card requires it; they are framed, and the second telling counts backwards.
- **Two new named characters are on the page and neither has an age:** **Wanda Rej** (bins, eleven years, a heart going since the spring, four pencil marks, a broken leg, a promise) and **Herta Vogel** (laundry, one of the three attestations, would do it again). **Ages are fixed for neither in this file or any other and none may be given on the page.**

## 6. LESSON

**The 12-gram gate caught this batch's worst duplication defect and it caught it only because it was run first and run again after every prose edit.** Forty-nine duplicate groups on the first run, and then twenty-nine more that this batch created *while fixing something else* — the same interval, said the same way, in four consecutive chapters, put there by a date correction. A gate run once, at the end, from memory, by the pass that wrote the prose, would have published the second figure as clean. **Run it first, run it again, and run it against the files rather than against what you meant to write.**

**The second lesson is worse and it is the one this batch actually learned late. See §8: seven intervals were derived against Gregorian month lengths into a world with no thirty-first, and the fix was recorded here as a success. A repair pass that writes its own justification into the record can certify its own error. Nothing that repairs an arithmetic claim may publish the arithmetic claim without a second derivation that does not use the same method.**

## 9. THE SECOND LESSON

**Every gate in §3 passed on a batch whose intervals were derived from the wrong calendar, and the gate that would have caught it — one that subtracts day numbers in the world's own month length — was not written.** The 12-gram gate found a lifted sentence across chapters 280 and 336. It could not find a number that was wrong by exactly one in seven places, because no gate compares a stated interval to a derived one. **The lesson is not "check the arithmetic" and it is not "be careful"; it is that a repair pass must not be allowed to certify its own corrections.** This batch's first pass found six wrong intervals, corrected all six, and wrote down in its own record that they were correct — and the seventh was still wrong, because the corrector's reasoning was the thing under suspicion. The corrections were wrong in the same direction as the errors. **Run any repair that changes a number through a second derivation by a different method, and if the only check available is the reasoning that produced the number, there is no check at all.**
