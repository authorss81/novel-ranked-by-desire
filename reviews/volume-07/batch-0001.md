**THE IRREVERSIBLE DECISIONS ARE 304, 308 AND 310. 310 IS THE ONE THE VOLUME'S FIRST MOVEMENT TURNS ON.**

- **304, and it is hers.** Sun Meiling says the sentence that turns the batch: a description of a house is a
  description of a household, a household in this city is a household in law, and a household in law is bound
  to its head. **He has spent seven volumes making sure nothing could put his name at the head of a sheet,
  and the way that protection fails is that it protects the people who are in the room with him.**
- **308, and it is his consent, not his wallet.** He says the reason clause out loud, nine times, in a dark
  room at the back of the Bellhouse, with his shirt off and his trousers eventually not. **She will not let
  him write it down.** He gains no instrument and loses the option of pretending the sentence was somebody
  else's, and it is in nine hundred mouths by half past eleven that night.
- **310, and it is his and it cannot be undone.** **He refuses to swear eight.** The correction is the only
  instrument in the city that can move a figure off a district leaf and it has to be in his own hand, and
  box four of that form asks which person's holding is to be corrected, **and there is a woman who asked in
  writing on the ninth of July never to be written down, and he will not say her name in a room with a clerk
  in it and will not ask her to come and say it for herself.** The instrument fails on its own terms. **The
  option closed: he can never swear that premises at seven again.** The cost is on the face of a public book:
  a clerk entered that the head of those premises was asked for a name and did not give it.

**THE SCENE IS 308 AND IT IS ISOLDE MARCHETTI'S.** See §4.2 and §6.1.

## 2. WHAT THE BATCH ESTABLISHED THAT A LATER BATCH MUST NOT MISREAD

**THE COMPOSITION OF 34-6-118 IS EIGHT.** On a fresh leaf of the quarter-book that opened on the first of
October, entered by a clerk of about twenty-five in the ordinary way at about eleven on the second, with a
date and a clerk's number. **The priced line in the Vant's real books still says seven.** Two figures for
one door in two books in two buildings, both true, and there is nothing in this city that reconciles them.

**THE ACCOUNT HAS BEEN REFERRED FOR CLASSIFICATION** on the nineteenth of October, by the head of the
premises, over his own hand, with the address and the figure of eight on it **and with the reason being a
song that cannot be reproduced in writing and for which no author has been paid.** The thirtieth was the dead
line and it has been beaten by eleven days.

**AND THE BATCH DID NOT MAKE HIM CLASSIFIED, WHICH IS THE POINT OF IT.** The plan in
`outline/volume-07.md` §6 movement 1 assumed the page would go into the Wing and the page **went into the
Wing**, and what it did not assume is that the classification would arrive carrying a **reason he cannot
write**, which is the one thing he has been able to withhold in seven volumes. **He has now given the reason
away out loud, in a kitchen at 307 and in a room at 308, and he still has not put it on a page in his own
hand, and those two facts are now the volume's central tension rather than its engine.**

**WHAT IT COST, IN ONE SENTENCE ABOUT ONE PERSON.** Sun Meiling, who asked on the fifth of August for the one
thing in the building with her name written nowhere on it, has been given a fresh leaf at the head of a
figure of eight because eight women decided to keep an address at one door.

## 4. WHAT A REVIEWER SHOULD CHECK FIRST, AND WHY

1. **Read Chapter 310 as a whole and ask whether he could have corrected.** That is the test of an irreversible
   decision and not a moral one. **He could have sworn eight in about nine seconds and it would have cost him
   a thing nobody can see, which is exactly the trade he has made every other time in this book.** He refuses
   because there is a woman he has promised, and because the form's fourth box will not let him promise
   without breaking it. **If that does not read as a man protecting something real rather than a man being
   virtuous, the batch's engine has failed.**
2. **Check Chapter 308 for a fade, by counting the scene body and not by grepping.**
   > Volume 6 shipped a summarised scene three times, at 279, 289 and 299, and the third passed a gate that
   > had recorded the first two as clean.
   **This batch's first draft of 308 passed every mechanical gate in §5 with a 663-word scene body inside a
   2,866-word chapter, which is the same defect in the same style of book.** It was caught by counting words
   and not by a scan. **The scene is now written out and the scene body is 1,717 words.** **This is the fifth
   time this defect has appeared in two volumes and it is the single most expensive habit in the manuscript.**
3. **Check that Chapter 301 does not start the engine and forget it.** The eight names on a fresh leaf must be
   the composition of those premises and not the number of women in the building, and the reader must not have
   to work that out.
4. **Re-run the 12-gram scan against all 310 and believe the re-run over any record.** §5 states the
   tokenisation in both variants because Batch 0005's block reported a number without one.
5. **Check the arithmetic out loud against `state/continuity.md`'s Batch 0001 day map**, and check it against a
   date printed in a chapter, not against the chapter being written.
6. **Check that nothing routes.** The promise of the twenty-seventh of August stands and no merit has moved.
7. **Check that nothing is spent and no panel is due.** Three hundred and ten chapters, nothing drawn.
8. **Check that he is not classified and that nothing has been requisitioned.** He is referred for
   classification; a classification has not been completed against him and a requisition has not been tabled.
   The volume's ceiling is 346.

## 6. THE REVIEW PASS AND WHAT IT FIXED

### 6.1 THE FINDING THAT MATTERS — CHAPTER 308'S SCENE BODY WAS 663 WORDS

**The first draft was 2,866 words and the body of the scene was 663, carried by six paragraphs, two of which
opened with a stage direction and two of which used `afterwards`.** It passed every row in §5: even quotation
count, one structural zero for panels, no paragraph over ninety words, no duplicate window.

**THE SCENE IS NOW WRITTEN OUT. MEASURED ON THE REVIEWER'S FRAME — the block from *The Room Agreed* to
*Twice* — the scene body is 1,717 words, up from 663.** The chapter is **3,758 words against a band of
1,800–2,700, and that is the named exception the batch prompt requires.** Every beat the summary carried is
on the page and is now dramatised rather than reported:

- She makes him say the line four times standing in the dark and five more while she is looking at him.
- **She will not let him write it down, and she says why: it has never worked on paper and she is the room.**
- She is not fast, and she explains that she is not, and she stops him twice with a flat palm and
  distinguishes careful from accurate in her own words rather than Beatrix Halm's.
- She tells him nine seconds before she stops, and comes out nine minutes past, and will not be tender about
  it, and it is a woman who does not do figures.
- **The gift bleeds into the room four separate times — a bulb going a shade brighter, a man on a ladder
  stopping with his hand on a board — and she apologises for it and says she cannot turn it off, and it is
  never the mechanism of anything she does to him.**
- He says it nine times and it gets shorter each time, which is her proof that it has stopped being recited.

**The scene's rules were checked one at a time: every sexual character is an adult and no chapter implies
otherwise · there is NO impaired state as a mechanism, and specifically no sedation, exhaustion, toxin,
restraint, psychic contact, trance or seal · she cannot compel him and he cannot compel her and the gift is
explicitly declared by her to be a thing that works on rooms and not on women, and she says she has never
used it on a person and is not starting · violence and sex stay in separate registers and there is no violence
in the chapter at all · he is wanted, she asks out loud, and saying yes is what the scene is about.**

### 6.3 THE CALENDAR DISCREPANCY, RECORDED AND NOT RESOLVED

**The prompt forbids resolving it in prose and asks for it to be recorded here if the batch touches it.**

`outline/volume-07.md` §5 and `state/current.md` and `state/continuity.md` all print **August 1 a Tuesday**
and August's Thursdays as the third, tenth, seventeenth and twenty-fourth, which is right, **and September's
Thursdays as the seventh, fourteenth, twenty-first and twenty-eight, which cannot both be true.** A month is
thirty days and advances the weekday by two, so a September whose first is a Thursday gives the first,
eighth, fifteenth, twenty-second and twenty-ninth. **Eleven closed chapters depend on the September list, so
the page governs and both lists stand exactly as printed.**

**This batch's answer was the strongest one available: it named no day of the week at all.** Eleven weekday
names in the drafts were removed and no replacement word was a day. **The discrepancy is untouched, it is
still open, and it is still an item for the Volume 7 close audit. No batch may resolve it in prose.**
