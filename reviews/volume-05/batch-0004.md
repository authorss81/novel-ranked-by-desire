the morrow). A fourth count — 235's district-bag nothing becoming five — is an in-scene count starting

**2. ONE WEEKDAY ERROR WAS DRAFTED AND REMOVED, AND THE RULE HELD THROUGH THE REPAIR.** 236 carried two
Thursday mentions in the first pass. Both were removed. The repair pass added Friday and Saturday while
making the weekend legible and **removed them again in favour of the Office's sitting days**, so the
rule survives: the batch names no weekday but Thursday.

**3. THE SCHEDULE/CERTIFICATE MECHANISM IS A STRUCTURAL CLAIM A REVIEWER SHOULD STILL TEST.** Both bars
stand and both are bypassed by exactness (wrong paper, an hour late). Whether that reads as an
institution acting or as the writer sparing the plot is the question neither a drafting pass nor a
repair pass can answer about itself. So should a reader test whether 238's receipt is a third
irreversible act or a second donning of the same lie. **On repair, 238 was given the one thing that
makes it a third: the form offered a line for a dissent, printed under his signature, and he left it
blank in front of the whole household. That is the chapter's irreversibility now, and it is a blank
rather than a lie, which is the same shape as the blank a district officer left on the third of this
month.**

**4. A REPAIR PASS CAN FIX A RATIO AND NOT A HABIT.** The thirty uses of "no person" and the seven uses
of "He understood it perfectly" were both symptoms of one habit: reaching for a construction that
states an absence instead of writing what is on the page. The ratio is now clean and a Batch 0005
writer should not read that as permission. The batch's actual strength is its physical specificity —
swept square edges, painted seams, a clock closing at half past three — and that is what the absence
constructions kept standing in for.
