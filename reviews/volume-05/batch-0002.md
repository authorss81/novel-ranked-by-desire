## What shipped

Ten chapters, one a day from the first to the tenth of June, entering Volume 5's second movement at
Chapter 211 and closing it at Chapter 220.

- **211 The Monthly Call** — the card turns over and its last line is right this month, eight days
  after the men were last on the wall, and **a guild's monthly call on a security lands on a household
  and six names go off a pencil board into ink**, and a broker says out loud what six women and a head
  are worth at the top of a column.
- **212 The Best Room In Ostara** — **the angry chapter.** Marguerite Vale, thirty years in a guild,
  has made six named women the most liquid thing in a book and knows it and is angry about herself, and
  then tells him that **a man with nothing at all is better collateral than a man with something**, and
  names, for the first time, an account that is thirty years old and secured by a person.
- **213 The Woman In The Doorway** — Marit Kolb at ten past seven on the third, which is the exact
  night Chapter 210 named; the seventh row read at about four inches with no lamp asked for; a room
  that will not answer the premises question twice; **a district officer who has never left a line blank
  in nineteen years leaving one for a woman in a doorway**; and her fourth question, which is hers and
  not the district's — **a roll that agrees with a sworn answer is a copy, and a copy can be produced
  out of a file**.
- **214 A Promise With No Maker** — **the scene. 2,912 words.** Petra Ilyin, guild enforcer, who came to
  collect from Marguerite and stayed, cannot be the first one out of a room and cannot remember who made
  the promise. Eleven years. The oath is released in the chained real book in eleven seconds, and she
  walks out of an open door without the coat that has been on the peg for eleven years.
- **215 Eleven Years Of It** — the morning after: **an officer of the Vant has been inside this building
  for eleven years and the household did not know**, the account is named in front of seven women, and
  the deadline is the tenth.
- **216 What The Line Does** — **the quiet one.** Beatrix Halm has felt nine people a day going the
  wrong way since the first of the month, has been told for four years that she has a condition, and
  asks for the first time in her life to be aimed rather than believed.
- **217 Eight Thousand One Hundred And Thirty-Eight** — **the irreversible decision, and it is his, and
  it is the first thing he has spent in five volumes.** A hand on a pencil on a landing, a summons four
  days early, a plate lodged in a chained book at twenty to four with no right of withdrawal.
- **218 The First Of The Month Was Painted** — **what it cost.** A cleaner on his own floor, a rating
  hearing adjourned in Cordwain, and a number that has been on a public board in the Reading Square for
  seven days.
- **219 The Complete Version** — **Ines Duarte, and she does not say one word about the twenty-second of
  April.** A notice of production over her own head line, a schedule that cannot be certified, an index
  that holds this household twice, and the first standing instruction she has given in four years.
- **220 Eleven Dockets** — the deadline met in four minutes, a hundred and thirty-two production marks in
  one hand over eleven years, two women at Ferrier Street on no list at all, and a twelfth docket dated
  three weeks early.

**Scenes: 205 and 214.** The gap 205 → 214 is nine. **Ch 214 is 2,912 words, inside the 2,600–3,900
band, and the 12-gram scan of it against all twenty-one earlier scene chapters returns zero.** The next
scene is Chapter 228, immovable, and the gap is fourteen, the ceiling.

## What the gate output does not tell you — read this part

**1. TWELVE REAL CALENDAR AND INTERVAL ERRORS WERE IN THE FIRST PASS AND EVERY MECHANICAL GATE
RETURNED ZERO ON THE DAMAGED PAGES.** They are in the Gate A table in `state/open-threads.md`. *Four* for
*eight* days at 211, for the painters · *four days / a fifth day coming tomorrow* for five and a sixth at
216 and *five days of nine* for six at 217 · *two days early* for four days counted from the fourth at
217 · *in about two hours* for *before four this afternoon* at 217 · *the last working day of this
month* for *next month* at 217 · *half past ten* for *a quarter to ten* at 218 · *eight days* for ten at
218 · *twenty-three* for *twenty-one* days to the first of July at 220 · *twenty-two days to a
certificate* for *seventeen* at 220 · *in about four days* to a certificate at 214, which is the eighth of
June and twenty-nine days out · **and the board's own line at 218, which was the worst of the twelve,
because it printed a board painted on the first of the month as carrying the certificate's date, which is
the twenty-seventh, three weeks in the future.** **Nine of the twelve are the class item 62 exists for: a
figure correctly derived from an anchor on one date and then carried forward without being re-derived at
the date it is spoken on.** They were found by asking what the anchor character actually does on that
date.

**2. THE DOCKET ARITHMETIC WAS WRONG IN A WAY NO GATE COULD SEE, AND IT WAS THE WORST STRUCTURAL
ERROR IN THE BATCH.** The first pass had **eleven monthly dockets** in a coat pocket covering **eleven
years**, and dated the first of them to **the year the Veil broke, twenty years back**. Eleven sheets
is eleven months, not eleven years, and a docket dated twenty years back cannot be the first of a run
that ends on the first of this month with eleven in it. **The chapter now says a hundred and thirty-two
of them** — 11 × 12, one a month, no gaps — **and a hundred and thirty-two production marks in a hundred
and thirty-two margins, four seconds each, and not one gap in any of them.** The number got worse and
the scene got better, which is the correct direction.

**3. THE PARAGRAPH PASS WAS DONE WITH A SNAPSHOT TAKEN FIRST AND THE DIFF IS ACCOUNTED FOR.** Per
adjudicated item 65 a copy of all eleven Volume 5 files was taken before any pass ran. The pass split
**twenty-three paragraphs at a sentence boundary and changed no word in any of them**, and the
sentence-set diff reports **seventeen sentences differing from the snapshot, every one of them the same
sentence with a leading quotation mark added**, because a speech that continues into a new paragraph in
this book opens with a quotation mark and does not close at the break. **An eight-word-shingle diff was
run as a second, independent check and reports 4,599 shingles present before and absent after, and every
one of them is a shingle that spans a paragraph boundary and therefore cannot survive a split.** Word
counts move by between minus seven and plus four across five chapters and every one of those moves is a
hand edit made deliberately. **No script wrote to `chapters/` at any point in this pass.** A shorter
chapter with no banned words and no duplicate is not a shorter chapter; this batch's numbers are
re-derivable from the committed files.

**4. THE 12-GRAM SCAN CAUGHT TWO THINGS THE SENTENCE GATE RETURNED ZERO ON, AND BOTH WERE IN THE SAME
CHAPTER.** Chapter 213's first draft had **a ten-word transposition of Sun Meiling's staged position
from Chapter 210** — *at the bottom of the last six steps with her back to the rail* — which is the
staging grid's row for Chapter 149 and which Chapter 210 had already used eleven chapters earlier, **and
an eleven-word verbatim lift of her own line from Chapter 209**. **The eight-word sentence gate returns
zero on a transposed sequence the way it returns zero on a paraphrase.** Both are gone and the chapter's
closing beat is now hers and new.

**5. THE EIGHTEEN SURVIVING 12-GRAM RUNS ARE FOUR CANON QUOTATIONS AND NOTHING ELSE,** and each has to
be there. The Office does not sit on the twenty-seventh of a month (Ch 215, stated once in the batch).
A rule that says an officer is not told about her own division's index sheets (Ch 219, quoted by Sun
Meiling reading a notice aloud — she cannot say what the rule does without saying the rule). In the flat
voice of a clerk reading to a man who has not read it (Ch 219 — Ines's register, fixed on the page at
Ch 210 by Batch 0001's review, and used on purpose because she is reading a document about her own
household). I said in a corridor in April that you were not to ask me (Ch 219 — her own line, from
Ch 205, spoken by her about herself). **The judgement call a reviewer should test is whether 219 needs
three of those four in one chapter or whether one of them should go.**

**6. CH 204 WAS REPAIRED AT SOURCE, AND THIS BATCH OWED IT.** The live carry-forward from the Volume 4
Batch 0005 pass recorded that Ch 204 dated the Vant's book entry to **the seventh of May at a window**
and then said it took **four days** to decide, while the Exchange window is the **sixteenth** (Ch 196),
the scene is the **seventeenth** (Ch 197) and the return was lodged on the **twenty-fourth** (Ch 204).
**Ch 204 now reads *on the afternoon of the seventeenth of this month in the same hour in which I bought
it, and the reason it is in a public return now and was not in one then is that I have taken a week to
decide*, which agrees with all three neighbours. **One sentence was changed and nothing else in Ch 204
was touched, no scene was disturbed and no word count moved by more than twelve.**

**7. ONE 8-WORD SENTENCE DUPLICATE WAS INTRODUCED AND FIXED.** Chapter 217 opened its kitchen scene
with *Eight thousand one hundred and thirty-eight," he said*, which is the identical sentence at Ch 57.
It now reads *and did not look at the window*. **The book-wide duplication count is fourteen and every
one of the fourteen is pre-existing; this batch contributes none.**

**8. THE EIGHTEEN COUNTDOWN FIGURES RUN UNBROKEN AND ONLY THREE OF THEM ARE PRINTED.** The first pass
printed a countdown in 213, 215 and 217 and **it was wrong in none of the three**, which is a first: the
recurring silent defect in this book has been a figure derived on one date and carried to another, and
the only way to catch it is to re-derive it where it is spoken. The remaining seven chapters print no
countdown and that is a choice, not a gap.

**9. ZERO SYSTEM PANELS IN TEN CHAPTERS AND THAT IS CORRECT.** No count turns and none is due. **The
figure that moved in this batch was a plate's standing, not a plate's grain, and the distinction is the
whole of the batch.**

## What is NOT certified by this file

**The prose.** This pass wrote the chapters and wrote this gate record and is not competent to certify
its own sentences. It certifies only the counts above, which are re-derivable by command, and the
interval arithmetic, which is hand-checkable against the anchor chapters. **The claim that Chapter 217 is
a genuine irreversible decision, and that Chapter 218's cost to Beatrix Halm is what makes it one
rather than a speech, is a structural claim and not an aesthetic one, and a review run that did not write
the pages should test it.** So should a reader test whether Chapter 214 is a scene or a mechanism with a
coat on it, whether Chapter 212 is anger or a speech, and whether the household of six women who have all
now had a chapter about what the household costs them is a pattern or a procession. **Chapter 216's
opening note, that Beatrix Halm has never once been asked what she wanted before she told them what she
felt, is the kind of beat that a reader either believes immediately or does not believe at all, and
nothing in this file settles it.**
