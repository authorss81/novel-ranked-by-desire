# Volume 5, Batch 0001 — Gate Record (Chapters 201–210)

**This is the drafting pass's own gate output and its gate output's limits. It is NOT a review of the
prose.** A review is a separate run that did not write the page. Seven earlier reviews in this repository
were run and never committed and Volume 3 closed with one batch of five reviewed on the page; **do not
count this file as a review and do not let a later phase cite it as one.** The full trace, the Gate A
interval table with its eight errors and the Gate B date screen are in `state/open-threads.md` in this
batch's close record, which is the only live gate block in that file.

## What shipped

Ten chapters, one a day from the twenty-first to the thirtieth of May, opening Volume 5 at Chapter 201.

- **201 A Paper With His Name On It** — the batch's spine and the volume's anchor. Chapter 181's card
  read in the basement, June derived out loud from it, and the first piece of post in four years that
  carries his name instead of the address. The Declaration of Membership in a Standing Household, one
  side, his name already typed into it, and a deadline that is the last working day of a month with no
  thirty-first in it.
- **202 The Fifth Line** — the card's last line is six days out and this batch is why it has never been
  read; and **Marit Kolb**, district, nineteen years, a card, on the bottom step at ten past seven.
- **203 The Fourth Question** — the caller again with four questions, and the room will not answer the
  third one, and the only person who can answer it correctly is the woman it is about.
- **204 The Quarterly Return** — **Marguerite Vale's cost.** The bill is read by somebody else at last:
  the Vant's quarterly return, a public filing, his name typed in her own dictation and the holder
  entered as whoever shall be holding the book at the annual sitting.
- **205 The Sheet She Would Not Complete** — **the scene.** Ines Duarte, Lantern Row, 3,553 words, the
  household's whole record read aloud in date order, her own line with nothing under it, and an ask.
- **206 Off The Strength** — Runa Sato's division, a document that was produced and came back as a copy,
  and the load order of the household worked out loud.
- **207 Three Grains** — Tessa Okonkwo's arithmetic, and what a certificate is actually for, and what it
  costs the clinic that has been unfunded on its own invisibility for nine years.
- **208 A List Of Six** — **the decision.** He says the figure out loud in his coat and it is six, and
  she tells him he may not do it for her, and he does it.
- **209 The Seventh Row** — Sun Meiling rules her own name into it in pencil, a day early, in her own
  hand, and takes it back off him.
- **210 Six Names And No Spaces** — filed at two forty-one on the last working day of the month, and a
  slip with a household's number on it, and four words in a third column in an office two floors above a
  washroom.

**The irreversible decision is Ch 208 and it is his, and it is about who is on the file.** Three
irreversible acts that are a woman's each are Ch 203 (Ines, in her own hand, in four seconds, on a card
she was handed), Ch 204 (Marguerite, in a public return, in her own dictation) and Ch 209 (Sun Meiling,
in pencil, in the dark, on her own row).

## What the gate output does not tell you — read this part

**1. Chapter 205's scene was written twice and the first draft was a different scene.** The first pass
staged the whole of Lantern Row as a conversation, and it read the file aloud and then talked, and the
chapter was implication for four hundred words with a fade. It is now the scene, written out, adults
awake and unrestrained and unmedicated, consent taken in plain statements before anything started, no
draw spent on anybody, violence and sex in separate registers, and the eleven seconds are counted aloud
afterwards. **What it changes is on the page and is not a mood: she asks to be entered on a sworn list
on his word rather than on an instrument, and she tears the member form in half so that no one is ever
going to open a drawer and find her name on a piece of paper with nothing else in it.**

**2. THE MAY CARD'S LAST LINE IS WRONG AND CHAPTERS 201 AND 202 FIX IT ON THE PAGE.** The card printed at
Ch 181 ends AND THE SEVENTH OF NEXT MONTH. Under Ch 181's own fourth entry and Ch 181's own thirty-day
month, the next Thursday after the twenty-fourth is the first of next month, and the line is six days
out. **Ch 181 derives the month's length from the fourth entry and never asserts that the fifth is
correct, and Ch 190 quotes the last line only to establish that there is no thirty-first, so this is a
reconciliation and not a contradiction.** It is on the page in his mouth, twice, and it is what makes
the June derivation possible at all.

**3. Chapter 191's printed premise is not supported by the derived calendar and this batch does not
repeat it.** Ch 191 says the twenty-seventh of June is not a working day, so the schedule goes up on the
twenty-sixth. **Under the derivation the twenty-seventh is a Tuesday.** The date does not move and no
chapter here explains the twenty-sixth with a working day. Registered in `state/open-threads.md` with the
instruction that no later chapter may use it.

**4. EIGHT REAL CALENDAR AND INTERVAL ERRORS WERE IN THE FIRST PASS AND EVERY MECHANICAL GATE RETURNED
ZERO ON THE DAMAGED PAGES.** They are listed in the Gate A table. *Twenty-nine gone* for *twenty-eight*
at 202 · *twelve days* for *twenty* at 202 · *the fourth caller* for *the third*, twice, at 202 and 203 ·
*thirty-two and twenty-eight* for *thirty-one and twenty-nine* at 208 · *three days* for *two* at 208 ·
*two days* for *a week* at 208 · *thirty-three and twenty-seven* for *thirty-two and twenty-eight* at
209 · *thirty-four and twenty-six* for *thirty-three and twenty-seven* at 210. **And two of the eight are
the same class of error and are worse than the numbers: a repaint on the twenty-second and a repaint on
the twenty-ninth, and both of those dates are Tuesdays.** **There is exactly one Thursday in this batch,
the twenty-fourth, and the first draft put men on a ladder twice on days the card forbids.** They were
found by asking the anchor-character question — what does the anchor character actually do on that date —
and not by any count. **This is the tenth batch in a row in which the mechanical gates were clean and the
hand trace found real errors, and the two structural errors are the first of that kind this repository
has had to record.**

**5. THE 12-GRAM SCAN RETURNS A HUNDRED RUNS AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS A DEFECT, AND THAT IS AN ASSERTION
THAT HAS TO BE READ, NOT BELIEVED.** They fall into five clusters, each adjudicated in
`state/open-threads.md`: the card and its derivation (the anchor, which is Chapter 201's whole job); the
ritual of doing every figure out loud (five uses in ten chapters, which is a lot and is deliberate — the
promise is the book's clock); quoted document text, including the printed indemnity, Runa Sato's
correction and the pencil sentence from the tenth of April; **one character's own repeated line** (Nadia
Ferrous's *waiting since the fourteenth of March for a document with my name and my decision in the same
place*, said at 185, 195, 200 and here); and the painters. **The scene test is the one that matters and it
returns zero: Chapter 205 against all twenty earlier scene chapters, twelve-word runs, no hits, and the
staging grid's ground, hand, name-exchange and aftermath are all new — a flat on Lantern Row, the record
on her own floor in date order, no lamp on it, his name used not once, and her last act a copy of the
file in a book with nothing on its cover.**

**6. One real sentence duplicate was found and fixed.** Chapter 201 opened by reusing Chapter 181's *Then
he went up at seven and did the second floor, and the third, and the fourth, and the fifth, and the
sixth.* The six floors are load-bearing and had to survive; the enumeration did not.

**7. `nobody` is at exactly one, which is the ration and not under it.** The single instance is Chapter
205's *nobody's book*, and it is the sentence the volume is built on: for four years the one thing she
had that cannot be taken off her is that she is in nobody's book. **The first pass carried seven and every
one of the other six was rewritten into a positive form.**

**8. THE PARAGRAPH PASS WAS DONE BY HAND, WITH A COPY TAKEN FIRST, AND THE DIFF IS ACCOUNTED FOR.** Per
adjudicated item 65, a snapshot of all ten chapters was taken before anything ran over them and the
sentence sets were diffed afterwards. **The diff reports thirty-nine sentences changed. Every one was

any point in this pass.** Nothing was deleted. **A shorter chapter with no banned words and no duplicate

## What is NOT certified by this file

**The prose.** This pass wrote the chapters and wrote this gate record and is not competent to certify
its own sentences. It certifies only the counts above, which are re-derivable by command, and the
interval arithmetic, which is hand-checkable against the anchor chapters. **The claim that Chapter 208
lands as the batch's irreversible decision and that Chapter 209 takes it back off him in a way that costs
her more is a structural claim, not an aesthetic one, and a review run that did not write the pages
should test it.** So should a reader test whether the seventh row being filled in pencil is a scene or a
device, and whether Chapter 204 is a cost or a speech.
