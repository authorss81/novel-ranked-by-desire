# Reviewer note — Volume 1, Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10)

**Second pass.** The first pass returned nineteen hard-rule and canon violations. All of them are
fixed. This note records what changed and what is still deliberately on the edge, so the next reader
does not have to re-derive it.

## What was fixed, and where

**The three hard gates.**
- **Ch 1** now opens on the objective, not on a leaking basin: *the plate must be in the socket
  before eleven, because a plate that is not read does not publish, and a reading that does not publish
  is read as nothing at all.* Objective at word 4, the anomaly (`FIGURE 3,412 / INTERVAL —`) at word
  301, the turn to the counter at 606 and Faber's response at 739. All three are inside eight hundred
  words. The basin survives as a late paragraph, where the title lives.
- **Ch 3** now renders the cold crossing back. Three seconds of it, up through the wrist into a
  four-year-old pylon injury, visible from the outside in the man's shoulders and breath, attributed to
  a draught off the junction. The reader sees the cost before anybody names it. The term itself is
  still withheld: *cold minute* does not appear anywhere in Chapters 1–10.
- **Ch 8** is now a chapter whose middle is the scene. Setup runs to about 1,250 words, the scene to
  about 2,900, and the ledger reveal after that. The paragraph that defended the consent ordering to
  the reader is gone; the ordering is now dramatised, including Ines stopping mid-scene to say that
  the giving and the wanting are two different transactions and she will not let him confuse them.

**Halvard Rees.** He does not thank him. He refuses to, out loud, in front of Lin Hao, and warns him
against doing it a second time — which is the first turned line, made in nine seconds, by accident.
The old standing invitation to come back tomorrow is gone. The mechanic now costs him something he
cannot refuse.

**Arithmetic.** One calendar now governs the batch and every day-count on the page reconciles to it:
audit opens **Monday the nineteenth**; Ch 5 Wednesday the 8th is eleven days out; Ch 6/7 Friday the
10th is nine; Ch 10 Saturday the 11th is eight. The abstention rule is made Thursday the 2nd and broken
Thursday the 9th, so it **lasts seven days**, and all four downstream references now say seven.

**The plate chain.** 3,412 → 3,393 (the 19-grain draw) → 3,481 (up 88 in two abstinent days) →
3,568 (up 87 in the five days since). Total movement is 156 grains, which is the figure Ines quotes in
Chapters 9 and 10. The unsupported 256 is gone. Lin Wei's hall scene now says *his band*, not six
thousand grains, because the Board prints bands.

**Petition 4118.** The wall copy reads `I. DUARTE (RANK 2, AUDIT) — RE SCORE OF A DEPENDENT SIBLING,
D. DUARTE.` Ines filed it; Ding is the sibling. Lin Hao quotes the petitioner's name back to her in
Chapter 7, which is why Ch 7 has to say it aloud, and she now tells him plainly that he let her ask
three times first. Ding's drop is **300 grains** (1,100 to 800), not 620.

**The turned-line conflation.** Chapter 8 said nine turned. It now says: nine lines *felt* through the
contact, fourteen lines in total, and **two** turned. Ines reads off nine names she can identify, finds
that none of them is on the Thursday list, and then writes the tenth in pencil on a clean page. Two
turned out of fourteen is a man who has been in a building for nine days; nine out of fourteen is a
source, and a source has an address. The load-bearing failure condition survives.

**The Chapter 8 reveal.** The old version had Ines finding nothing, proving the name was absent, and
then producing it from a page she had not read. She now reads nine names, stops before the tenth, and
shows him the tenth is not on any list — because the Bureau cannot open a file for a person with no
reading to dispute and no instrument behind them. The name arrives where the hole in the record is.

**The repeated explanation.** *Nobody is punished, it is arithmetic* is now delivered twice. Ch 2 by the
counter clerk who is tired of saying it, and Ch 10 by Hester Quill — who escalates it into the actual
cost, a sister sleeping on benches twice. Ch 5's version is Tobias paraphrasing it badly for his own
enjoyment, and Ch 7's has Ines correct him inside two lines.

**The Ch 5 craft break.** The Bernt Halse passage flipped to present tense in a room Lin Hao was not in.
It is now one paragraph of narrated fact, and the non-feeder failure has moved off the bench corner
onto a person: he and Tobias take hold of the same requisition slip, nothing moves, nothing is given,
and he is grey at the temple before he reaches the second floor.

**Other.** The grey no longer appears in Ch 2. Ola's diary dates now run from the fourth of June rather
than colliding with a four-year-old petition. Nadia Ferrous has a shift pattern and a home, and Ola is
her sister rather than her flatmate. Chapter 9 now opens at ten past twelve with the lamp off and Ines
in her coat, instead of announcing that it is about to get cold. Chapter 9 no longer names the Church's
membership, which was spending Chapter 10's plant.

## Still deliberately on the edge

1. **Chapters 8 and 10 are long** — roughly 4,750 and 4,050 words. Ch 8 is the batch's only scene and
   carries the climax; Ch 10 carries five ordered reveals and a closing threat that the batch card
   specifies as one chapter. Both are within the "go longer for a revelation or a payoff" allowance
   but they are the two longest in the book so far, and the reviewer should judge whether Ch 10 earns
   it.
2. **Ola's diary and Nadia's mouth are the same hazard.** Both are honest, both are harmless in intent,
   and together they are triangulable in about ninety minutes. That is the threat the batch is built
   on and it should stay uncomfortable.
3. **The word *reservoir* is not on the page anywhere**, by design. It is named in Volume 2.
4. **Chapter 10's rota board** — `MON WED FRI — KITCHEN RUN — O. BRIGHT` — is the one planted clue in
   the batch that depends on the reader catching it. Worth confirming it is legible.
5. **Every escalation of *there is no form for this* is now spent** (Ch 5, 6, 7). There is none left
   before Volume 7, where it is meant to turn the ending. Do not spend it again in the next four batches.
   *There is no column for it* is spent once, in Ch 1. *Nobody asked*, *nobody thanked him* and *the
   record remained* are all still available, and Chapter 50 wants the middle one.

## Canon-change flag

None. Chapter 10's title follows the fixed title bank (*No One Keeps the Diary*), not the batch card's
*Not Back That Shift*. The title bank governed, the chapter was written under it, and the card heading in
`outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md` has since been corrected to match. The card's phrasing survives
only as the pull line for that chapter. Recorded in `state/open-threads.md`; no canon change.
