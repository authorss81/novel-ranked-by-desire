## 5. The arithmetic, which had already failed twice

**THE RULE, restated because it is the rule: a month is thirty days and has no thirty-first. The thirtieth is the last working day of the quarter. Subtract day numbers. Never calculate against a real calendar.**

### **5a. THE DERIVATION RUNNING FROM 433 TO 450 WAS REAL-CALENDAR, AND ONE FIGURE WAS WRONG**

**WHAT WAS ON THE PAGE.** Every chapter from 433 to 450 opens or near-opens by saying the requisition's age and deriving it aloud, in this form:

> *Eight from twenty leaves eight, with thirty-one and thirty and thirty-one and twenty-one makes one hundred and twenty-one.*

**EIGHT AND THIRTY-ONE ARE NOT NUMBERS IN THIS BOOK.** February has thirty days here and so does March and so does May, and the volume's own §5 says in terms: **no interval may be derived against a real calendar.** The formula above is `28 − 20`, then March 31, April 30, May 31 — a **real, non-leap-year calendar** — and it had been substituted for the correct `10 + 30 + 30 + 30` in Batch 0004's repair of 13 October.

**THE TWO METHODS AGREE ON SEVENTEEN OF EIGHTEEN FIGURES AND DISAGREE ON ONE, AND THE DISAGREEMENT IS THE PROOF.** `10 + 30 + 30 + 30 = 100` and `8 + 31 + 30 + 31 = 100`: identical. **So the totals could not catch it.** But the volume's own thirty-two other printed requisition ages — 63 at 413, 74 at 419, 76 at 420, 77 at 421, 79 at 422, 81 at 423, 85 at 425, 94 at 430 — are all **world-calendar**, and at Chapter 433 the two methods part company: **20 February to 29 May is 99 days in this world and 98 on a real calendar, and the chapter printed 98.**

**WHAT WAS DONE.** All eighteen derivations were put back on the volume's own calendar: **Ten from twenty leaves ten, with thirty and thirty and thirty and — .** **Seventeen of the eighteen printed totals are unchanged and one moves: 433 goes 98 → 99**, which is what the other thirty-two chapters in the volume already said. **The figures that travel forward are untouched: 115 at 442, 117, 119, 121, 123, 125, 127, 128, 130 at 443 to 450.** **130 at 450 is still 130.**
