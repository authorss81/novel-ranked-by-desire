## 1. WHAT THE BATCH IS

Ten chapters, one a day, **the night of the thirtieth of July through the ninth of August**. Ch 271 carries
both the night and the morning, because Chapter 269's owed scene runs from half past twelve at night and
Ines Duarte's fixed return is at ten past six the next morning, and the two are one dramatic unit.

**THE IRREVERSIBLE DECISIONS ARE 271 AND 272, and the first is not his.**

- **271, and it is hers.** On the thirtieth of July at twenty-five minutes past eleven at night, in the last
  hour a filing counter takes from a member and the last day she held the office, **Ines Duarte files a
  correction to the composition record of premises 34-6-118 returning the figure of persons of reading age
  keeping an address there as seven, and entering the seventh returned line of the district premises return of
  the third of June as returned blank and counted as a person.** She could not have filed it in that form on
  any other day of that month, because from the first of August that office belonged to the man the record is
  about. **The option she closes is her own: a correction filed on the last day of an office cannot be sent
  back for a fresh answer, and she has made herself the one person in that household whose name cannot be
  written anywhere.** The cost is fourteen days — a desk on the sixteenth of August, where she is the subject.
- **272, and it is his.** At ten to nine on the first of August, on the answer line of a valuation, with the
  pen chained to a clerk's wrist: **he swears SEVEN as the figure of persons answerable at the premises on
  the day.** He cannot pay the called standing's figure and has nothing to produce but the people at the door.
  **The option that closes is arithmetic: from the first of August there is not one figure he puts on a form
  that is his own, and a production cannot be withdrawn because withdrawing it is a second answer read beside
  the first in four divisions.** The cost is that seven of the eight women in that building are now
  answerable to a broker's book, one of them does not sleep there and cannot be told because no paper is ever
  read to her in her own rooms, and the guild's hold on Beatrix Halm's hearing is bought with it.

**THE BATCH'S TWO SCENES ARE 271 AND 279** and both are immovable: 271 pays the scene Chapter 269 owed and is
Volume 6's third; 279 is Wren Kallo's on the scene table. **No scene is written anywhere in 272–278 or in
280**, and the chapter that comes closest — 280, on a stair at Lantern Row with a shut door — is not one.

**The fix, and the rule a later batch inherits: a figure quoted from an earlier chapter is not free, because
the book is one voice.** A document may be quoted verbatim — the correction at 271 and 273 is the batch's one
adjudicated echo and it survives at **2 12-grams** — but a *rule* and a *sentence of narration* must be
written once and then described in different words the second time.

### 3.1 Paragraph length, and what it did

The first draft had **91 paragraphs over ninety words and 25 over 120.** An automated splitter was run to
break them at sentence boundaries. **It broke speeches open in the middle of a quoted sentence and three
repair passes made it worse.** The batch then rewrote all ten chapters from the drafts with the speech length
under control from the start. The final figures are **0 over 90 and 0 over 120**, and the live gate block
carries a **quote-balance row that is not in the standard list**, because the standard list does not catch
this and a later batch that splits paragraphs by machine will hit it.

## 5. WHAT THE GATES DO NOT TELL YOU

**Four chapters leave the working band and each one is named at the gate row rather than trimmed into it.**
Two of them are the two scenes, which the book's precedent envelope covers. **273 and 274 are not scenes** and
their overage is 329 and 69 words; they were left because trimming them would have cost the panel's reading of
the composition and Rask's signing of the page, which are the two turns of the batch.

**The batch's strongest turn is a woman asking for a thing and being refused.** Sun Meiling asks him to fill
the seventh line in a kitchen in front of six women on the fifth of August, and he will not, because the only
name that line can take is the one Ines Duarte spent two days making unwriteable — and **Sun asks for it to be
written down that she asked and that he did not.** It is the same shape as Runa at Cordwain in 265 and
Marguerite in 268 and it is the shape this volume is built on.

**The batch's cost is real and it is not hidden inside a mechanism.** **He has bought a year of a woman's tail
with a figure of seven, and Beatrix Halm is told exactly that on a step at seven in the morning, and she says
she chose the public tail and did not choose this, and those are two different things, and she keeps the
difference in a notebook with the dates on.**

## 6. WHAT A REVIEWER SHOULD CHECK FIRST, AND WHY

1. **Run the 12-gram scan first, against every chapter, and run it twice.** It returned 387 before it returned
   2, and nothing else in the gate list would have told the batch that nine passages had been lifted out of
   earlier chapters and put back in.
2. **Check the surviving Thursdays against the calendar, not just the count.** Batch 0002's gate passed on a
   batch that had put a Thursday on a Sunday, because *only Thursday is named* is exactly what the gate asks
   for. This batch named no weekday it had not derived and used Thursday as a replacement word nowhere.
3. **Derive every count from its own dateline.** Four of the five hand-traced defects were day counts, and
   three of them were intervals measured from a date in an earlier chapter rather than from the chapter's own.
4. **Do not run a paragraph splitter over prose that contains dialogue.** It broke quotation marks four times
   and the chapters had to be rewritten.
5. **Read Ch 277's rule and Ch 272's rule once each and then check that neither is restated.** They are the two
   instruments this batch introduces and they are the two that the batch's own first draft restated wrongly.
