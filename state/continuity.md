## 2 · THE FOUR PRACTICES OF THIS VOLUME, ALL STANDING, ALL RELIABLE, NONE OF THEM IN INK

**These are the volume's standing canon now, and any later chapter may rely on all four without re-arguing them.**

1. **THE WINTER MORNINGS.** The book comes up at a quarter to ten, five days in seven, and goes down at half past five by his own hands, in the dark, four flights and about four hundred yards. It came up through the worst weeks of the winter of year eleven and it was not shut for them, and that was the volume's first permitted closure. **What it costs is his own hands, in the dark, for as long as he stands at that table.** The back board of that book has begun to hinge open along about four inches of the bottom edge, about a tick every four seconds, and the only proper repair is a binder four hundred yards away in a cart in daylight with somebody else's hands on it. **He decided on the second morning and lost the argument about four seconds later and did it anyway. A later chapter may not have that book bound and may not have it lost.**
2. **THE SLATE THAT STANDS.** A figure in small chalk in a lane off Cordwain, and a third figure in the same man's head that has no unit he can write after it and is not on the slate and will not be this year, and is out by more than half a quarter. The four-year clock stands and is not discharged. **At 937 the head of the office's standing sheet went onto that same slate in another man's small chalk, beside the sooner figure, which was not rubbed out and was not argued, and he refused to have the two figures separated because a second figure under the first one is two figures. A later chapter may not rub the sooner figure out, may not chalk the third one, and may not separate the two.**
3. **THE SECOND WRITTEN REFUSAL WITH NO THIRD PROMISED.** There are two entries in that book that are neither a question nor an answer — one entered by the clerk at 927 in her own words because the office said the reason out loud first, and one entered at 928 by a division officer entitled to ask one institution for a statement of what it is, in three sentences and no name. **There will not be a third, and the refusal was given against a man who wanted a stamp because his sister says he did not go. What it closes is refusing in writing on demand, ever, on any ground, including the ground that it would be kind. What it costs is that from 930 the only way this office says no to a person it is not standing in front of is by being standing in front of them, five days in seven, on four flights. 4. **THE NO-LIST RULE WITH NO COUNT IN IT.** This office keeps no roll of who comes through that door, no index of it, no count of it, no number, no total and no figure for a quarter, and it will not give one **for the reason that a number is better than a name, because that is not a reason.** It corrects no copy of the card in any form. **The price is named and was named before it was spent: about nine people a fortnight knocking at the wrong end of the hours for the rest of the year, and the two places in this city where the office is reached by people who cannot read it going on with nothing to show and nothing to check. A later chapter may not restore a list, a roll, an index or a count of comers without a second decision taken out loud in that room, with the door open.**

**AND THE ONE INK THIS OFFICE PUT IN THE WORLD IN A YEAR, WHICH IS THREE LINES ON A CARD.** The winter-worn card came down off three nails at about eleven on the thirtieth of December of year twelve and a second card with the same three lines and not one name went up on the same three nails at about a quarter past eleven, in his own hand, with nothing written on it and the holes about a quarter of an inch wider than they were in November. **The three lines are a street, a door at the head of four flights off a third court, and hours between a quarter to ten and half past five on working mornings, five days in seven, and at no other hour.** There is no transcript of that card anywhere in the world, no copy of it, and no list of anybody. **A later chapter may not withdraw the renewal, may not add a name, a fourth line, or an hour about the turn of the year, and may not have the office write a copy or a transcript of it.**

**AND THE THING THE RENEWAL BOUGHT, WHICH IS THE VOLUME'S ANSWER.** He replaced a number with his mouth. In November, on the last hour of the card's hours, in the dark, from his own second step, he reads the three lines out loud to the last four people of the day, and a man on a landing on the tenth of December said the whole of it back to him from memory and got every word of it. **Every rule that office has is about paper — a written answer, a copy, a transcript, a slip, a form, a wall with four words under a number on it — and not one of them covers a person, and there is no instrument in this city that covers a mouth.** A later chapter may not have the office write any of that down.
