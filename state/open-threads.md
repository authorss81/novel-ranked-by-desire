### What this batch spent that Batch 0001 was holding

1. **THE IRREVERSIBLE DECISION IS CH 163, AND IT IS SUN MEILING'S.** A young man with a satchel puts a
   notice of premises standing on the step of Ferrier Street, dated the twelfth and effective from the
   fifteenth, and offers a delivery stub for the name and hour of whoever takes it. **She signs it.**
   Four months of refusing to be written down end on a doorstep in about ninety seconds, and the thing
   it bought is stated on the page by the woman who paid it: *a household that cannot be found is not
   safe, it is merely unlocatable, and the paper does not have to find her, it only has to find the
   building.* **She also writes, in the seventh space of the notebook, a second standing instruction of
   her own — a paper comes to her, she answers it, and she does not ask anybody else in the house what
   the answer is** — which is the household's first chain of command and it is held by the one woman
   who refused the first one.
2. **THE SECOND IRREVERSIBLE ACT IS CH 161, and it is his.** He promises Runa Sato, in front of five
   women and in writing, **that he will not tell her what was said in her hearing room**, and she names
   the question in advance so that he can decide in advance. He keeps it at 162 by lying to her face in
   a corridor, and he tells her that he has lied, and she says *right*. **He is the only person in
   Ostara who knows what was in that room and the only person in Ostara barred from saying it.**
3. **THE CHURCH OF THE QUIET NUMBER ARRIVED AT ITS SCHEDULED CHAPTER AND WAS EARNED.** It reaches him as
   **a number in the margin of a form about his own address** at Ch 166 — item eleven, eleven thousand
   and some hundred persons, holding no plate and no audit, membership attested, attestation not
   audited, no composition return ever filed — and it is named in full, by the woman it belongs to, at
   **Ch 169, and nowhere earlier.** No chapter from 161 to 168 names it, shows a member of it, or
   alludes to it, and the word *chaplain* first appears at 169.
4. **RUNA SATO'S HEARING HAPPENED AND DID NOT RESOLVE (162)**, in front of the reader, in two parts.
   She was in the room from ten until two with four people in it; she was **made to wait in the
   corridor** for the last twenty minutes because she would be able to object to the item that concerns
   another person; she was **not allowed to object**, and declining to confirm was entered; and she was
   **not allowed to be told**, and the third of those is still him.
   **What came out of it is the Bureau's own standing rule, printed at the head of the muster-room wall
   and quoted by Runa Sato to him on the eleventh about a different man:** a member who refuses to
   answer a count is entered as refusing, and a household with a member who has refused is a household
   with an unanswered count in it.
5. **THE THEORETICAL HINGE OF THE BATCH IS CH 165**: the only thing which defends a person against an
   audit is being the same woman all the way back. **And the only document in Ostara in which six women
   are demonstrably the same six women all the way back is the visitors' book, which is for sale for a
   shilling.** That is the engine and it is not resolved.
6. **THE ESCALATION IS CH 168.** Ossian Kerr, a band-four jobbing printer four doors up Ferrier Street
   for nineteen years, not an officer, entirely within his rights, buys the exhibit roll for a shilling
   and comes to the gate with six questions in pencil. **The standing instruction is used for the first
   time**, six answers in six hands, unconsulted — and a woman four doors up comes away knowing that the
   six answers are themselves the answer to the sixth question, and that there are eleven of him.
7. **NO SYSTEM PANEL IN TEN CHAPTERS, and one ledger rise, 117 to 118 at Ch 161**, in prose, with its
   cause named on the page (a man in a loading yard who moved a stone out of a path for four hundred
   people). Nine turned stays nine. **Nothing was spent on anybody in this batch**, and nothing was
   spent on himself, and the promise made in a hall in March stands and is not tested once.

### Drafting decisions that were not in the prompt, registered as canon proposals

1. **THE HEARING'S ITEM IS ABOUT HIM AND HE IS TAKEN IN FOR IT ALONE.** Runa Sato filed a written
   statement in advance and declined a count: how many movements of merit she made in the open in the
   Cobb Street hall on the twenty-ninth and did not write up. **He is asked it, gives three, and the
   officer enters that a number answered by a person other than the member cannot go on a record of the
   member's own conduct — so the count stands as refused and is entered against her and not against
   him.** The instrument's rule is not stated, derived, joked about, or used.
2. **RUNA SATO IS STRUCK OFF THE FIELD ROSTER (163).** Eight on the wall at ten in the morning on the
   twelfth, **seven** on the morning of the thirteenth, a rule through her line and a card under the
   rail reading STRENGTH RECONCILED, SEVENTH. **A member entered as refusing is still on the strength
   and still paid; it is the premises that cannot be returned to.** And the standing she entered in the
   fourth box of the composition return on the eighth is now a false line on a form.
3. **A PERSON MAY NOT BE PRESENT FOR AN ITEM CONCERNING ANOTHER PERSON**, because she would be able to
   object and an objection from the member reopens the proceeding. That is why she is in a corridor and
   why he is in a room, and it is why the promise of the eleventh had to be a promise about lying.
4. **AN ASSESSOR EXISTS AND IS NOT FIELD.** Once a fortnight, a different letterhead, no warrant and
   no reason, because a count that cannot be reconciled on paper has one lawful way left.
5. **THE EXHIBIT ROLL IS SET UP FRESH EACH MORNING AT THE COBB STREET COUNTER AND ITS STANDING IS
   PRINTED ON THE BACK** as a date and a line, from the twenty-ninth of March downward (164, 168).
   **Everything the household does is on it within a day.** Nadia Ferrous buys one copy on the
   fourteenth and learns that Sun Meiling's signature of the thirteenth was in type before she had
   finished her breakfast.
6. **FORM 4-C IS NAMED (165) AND ITS SECOND ISSUE ARRIVES ON THE SIXTEENTH (166)**, with a fifth
   question the Office already knows the answer to, and **a fifth permitted answer to the fourth
   question, drawn from the schedule of unmeasured associations** — which is how the blank at the foot
   of the box stops being a wall.
7. **SUN MEILING WROTE YES AND RUNA SATO WROTE YES** — no: **Sun wrote yes and Runa wrote the word that
   was true**, and both are true, and the form goes to a counter with two different answers to a
   question about the same afternoon, in two hands, unconsulted, which is the standing instruction
   working exactly as written. Ines Duarte: *that is not an answer, that is this house.*
8. **THE FORTY-ONE ARE ELEVEN HOUSEHOLDS (167)**, worked out on the back of a handbill by a signal
   operator who counts for a living. **Beatrix Halm takes the visitors' book out of the house without
   telling anybody and reads all a hundred and eleven entries aloud in a room off Ash Row with forty
   people in it**, to see which line moves a room. Seven of the eleven react. The thirty-eighth is a
   man who says *that is my sister.* **The cost is that the document has acquired a voice and the voice
   is hers**, and it cannot be unvoiced.
9. **OSSIAN KERR IS NOT THE MAN FROM BELLHOUSE.** Beatrix handled one of the eleven on the seventeenth
   and a **different one of the eleven** comes to the gate on the eighteenth, which is the escalation:
   the household prepared for one man and got a different one. He is four doors up and has been for
   nineteen years, which is worse.
10. **SUN MEILING ASKED AGNETE LINDQVIST'S NAME, AT THE DOOR, AT ELEVEN AT NIGHT ON THE SEVENTEENTH.**
    **This departs from `outline/volume-04.md` §4, which put the question in "a washroom on Ferrier
    Street."** It is registered here rather than silently adopted. The departure is deliberate and for a
    reason: a washroom implies Sun Meiling left the house in daylight and to a stranger, and she has
    spent the batch refusing to be the cheapest door in the city. **She goes out at eleven at night, on
    her own, with a piece of paper in her hand that has eleven thousand printed in the margin of it,
    and asks the plainest question available to her, which is what somebody who has refused a name for
    four months asks.** The washroom function is preserved in the staging of the scene itself, which is
    a corridor and a door and the other side of a door that has not been opened in eleven years.
11. **AGNETE LINDQVIST'S GIFT IS STATED AS A STANDING PROPERTY OF A ROOM, NOT A STATE.** It makes a room
    in which a person cannot be compelled to testify. It does not compel, does not sedate, does not dull
    an objection and does not make anybody want anything, and it is not used as a mechanism. Her own
    sentence about it is the cost: *there is no protection in here* — not for her and not for him.
12. **AGNETE LINDQVIST CANNOT GO BACK IN (170).** She opens the door onto the stair at about half past
    ten on the nineteenth, sleeps about nine hours, and on the morning of the twentieth gets to the
    fourth step and sits down on it and cannot get back into a building. **The door is left standing
    open behind her at the end of 169 and it is still open on the morning of the twentieth.**

### What the gate output does not tell you

**It did not see the eight interval errors in Gate A, of which the two that would have been caught by
any reader with a calendar are the most expensive kind this repository produces: a correct word attached
to a wrong date.** Ch 170's "four minutes to six" inside an afternoon scene, and Ch 170's "yesterday"
for the day before yesterday, were both clean on every mechanical gate in this file.

**It also did not see the three things a reviewer should attack first, and they are the ones I would
attack:**

1. **Whether Ch 165 gives away the volume's argument too cheaply.** *The only thing that defends a person
   against an audit is being the same woman all the way back* is stated in one scene by a woman who has
   never been more articulate in this book, and the chapter then points the reader at the visitors' book
   as the only document in the city that proves it. **That is a thesis delivered by the most reliable
   mouth in the household, which is exactly what a volume about being described should do and also
   exactly what a volume about being described should not do twice.** Chapter 195 is named *Nothing
   Said At All* and is Ines's, and it has to land without the argument having been made this plainly
   once already.
2. **Whether Ch 168 is a nuisance or an escalation.** The case for the escalation: it is the first thing
   in four months that the household cannot answer, cannot refuse, cannot price and cannot record, it
   costs them the standing instruction's first use, and it produces a person four doors up who now knows
   where six women sleep. **The case against: a private citizen with six questions in pencil is a very
   civilised device, and a reader who has spent nine chapters on forms may put it down as a change of
   register.** The defence is that he is right, that he says so, that he tells them about the next one,
   and that the household's reaction to him is dread rather than relief.
3. **Whether Ch 169's scene earns eleven years.** The staging is new against all ten entries in the grid
   — a corridor, a runner with a worn path, two doors, a window that does not open, a room of one chair
   — the rib is never mentioned by anybody, the name is used once and as a word inside a sentence, her
   last act is the door left standing open, and nothing is spent on anybody. **The risk is that the
   chapter's strength is its terms, and four terms are a great deal of front matter for a scene**; the
   defence is that she is a former clerk and a woman who cannot let a room take a testimony, and that
   the terms are how she is professionally able to want this.

**This record is not a review of the prose.** The review is a separate run and its file is
`reviews/volume-04/batch-0002.md`.

**`outline/volume-04.md` DID NOT EXIST when Volume 3 closed.** It has now been written, from
`outline/series.md`, `outline/ending.md`, the Volume 3 Batch 0005 block in `state/continuity.md` and
the Batch 0005 close record below. **Everything in it is a plan until a chapter puts it on the page.**
A disagreement between the outline and a figure in the Batch 0001 prompt is resolved in favour of the
outline and listed at the foot of this block.

**The title bank in §11 is the correction this repository owes for the Volume 3 review finding.** All ten
Volume 3 batch titles are scene summaries and `chapter-0144.md` *Nine Minutes Before* is a clock
restatement, and `AGENTS.md` bans that outright. They were mandated verbatim from
`outline/volume-03.md` §11, which is where the failure lives. **The fix is made where the bank is made
and Volume 3 is not retitled.** Volume 4's fifty titles were each put to one question — *would a reader
who has not read this chapter learn anything about it from the title?* — and no entry describes a
scene, and none is a clock, a date, a weather, an age or a quotation. **Ten entries are bare personal
names or a bare place name and carry a declared exception**, because forcing four words onto a name
produces a description, which is the defect being fixed; the manuscript's own precedent is `Ola Bright`,
`Lantern Row`, `Cordwain` and `Signal`.

### The spine of Volume 4, as planned

**Chapters 151–200. Fifty chapters. Five batches of ten consecutive days. Five scenes at 157, 169, 179,
189, 197.** Gaps 8, 12, 10, 10, 8. **No gap under eight and none over fourteen.**

**The engine, and it is traced to Chapter 122 rather than chosen.** Sabine Rooke named her price
before she asked for the ninth chair — her name on a sheet about him — and what she filed on the
twenty-second of March was a **composition return**: six women at an address in Ferrier Street, with
the space for the head of the household empty since the day she signed it. **The household he spent four
months refusing to build has been on paper since before this volume opened, and the one woman not on it
is the one who has refused to be written down all along.**

### The calendar, derived rather than assumed, and the three things it produces

**M+3's 1st is a Thursday and M+3 has thirty-one days** — both certified on the page at Ch 141, Ch 143,
Ch 144, Ch 145 and Ch 150. **Therefore M+3's 31st is a Saturday and M+4's 1st is a Sunday. M+4's
Thursdays are the 5th, 12th, 19th and 26th; M+5's 1st is a Tuesday and its Thursdays are the 3rd, 10th,
17th and 24th.**

**The derivation checks itself against a canon fact it did not produce:** Runa Sato's hearing is dated
in three files as **the twelfth of the fourth month, a Thursday because the reading is**, and the
twelfth of this derivation is a Thursday.

**M+4's length was undetermined and is now fixed at thirty days, and the anchor must be printed on the
page by Chapter 151.** Every branch office keeps the month's instrument days on a card. **The card reads
the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth, the twenty-sixth, and then the third of next month** — the
twenty-sixth plus seven can only be the third of May, which can only be true of a thirty-day April.
**M+5's length is NOT fixed and Batch 0004 must fix it with the same kind of printed anchor before it
uses any interval crossing 30 April.**

**The awkward fact, flagged and not smoothed: Ines Duarte's review, dated the morning of the twenty-second
of M+4, falls on a Sunday.** Nothing on the page explains it. **Volume 4 does not explain it either** —
no chapter dates the twenty-second, names its weekday, or shows her going in. Chapter 171 has her tell
him the date and Chapter 172 is the door and the waiting room. **What an office in Ostara does with a
review on a Sunday is a Volume 5 decision and is registered here rather than made.** The date itself is
canon and does not move.

### Proposed canon, adopted

1. **The composition return** exists, was filed on the twenty-second of March by Sabine Rooke, names
   six women at an address, and its head-of-household space is empty. **Found on the page at Ch 174.**
2. **Ivo Sarn is gone from the certification bench by the first of April**, with no notice published,
   and his name has been on a ceramic tile in the Reading Square since the twenty-second — nine days
   before Anneke Roos struck his paragraph out. **This is the cost of Anneke Roos's signature, named on
   the page in Ch 151.**
3. **The Exchange's monthly permission circular returns *no entitlement established*** for any account
   whose classification is not entered. A permission is a thing you may do and you cannot buy one when
   nobody can say what you may do. **Ch 155, on a repaint sheet by a rail.** This is the volume's
   mid-batch turn and it is why the volume's problem is legal rather than moral.
4. **The Exchange's sales floor is entered for the first time in this book, at Ch 196–197.**
5. **The plate does not move in Volume 4.** Not one grain, and no chapter may contain a figure from
   which one could be converted. **The band's resolution is a household and an indemnity, not a rank.**
6. **A standing household is completed by an indemnity**, and the head of a household answers in his
   own name for every member of it. **Ch 199.**
7. **The volume spends nothing.** No draw on a stranger, no draw on himself, no draw on anybody at all in
   Chapters 151 to 160. The ledger's rises are lines arriving, never lines being drawn from.
8. **No new character enters who is not already on the page** except **Agnete Lindqvist at 169**, and
   she enters immovably.

### Proposed canon, in and deliberately left out

**The Church of the Quiet Number does not appear in this volume before Chapter 169.** The Bureau's
change of mind about it is live in Volume 4 and **must arrive at him as a number in a return and not as
a name**, because that is the only lawful way it can arrive before the door opens. No chapter from 151
to 168 may name it, show a member of it, allude to it, or use *chaplain*, *congregation*, *null room* or
*quiet number* about anything at all.

### Batch 0001's own batch arc, registered before drafting

**Goal.** Find out what a public description of a household is good for, and get one woman's name onto a
piece of paper on purpose.

**Midpoint.** Ch 155, the Thursday, the repaint sheet and *no entitlement established*.

**Escalation.** 151–152 a man who cannot be found, and the machine that finds people; 153–154 what a
description is legally good for, and the leak's fourth name landing inside the Vestry; 155 the turn;
156–157 the enquiry goes out on a Friday and Tessa signs on the Saturday; 158–159 the household answers
without him, in four hands; 160 the climax.

**Batch climax and the irreversible decision, Ch 160.** He asks Ola Bright to copy one sentence, six
times, in six hands, and to hand him the page. It is a standing instruction — if a paper comes for this
house, answer it in writing, separately, and do not consult him — and it removes him from the chain of
command of the only structure he has ever wanted to be inside of. **Ola tells him before she copies it
what it has cost: the one unasked record in Ostara stops being unasked.** It cannot be handed back.

**Scene at 157. Tessa Okonkwo. Sixth-floor washroom at the Fourth Branch, eleven at night.** Adults,
awake, unrestrained, unmedicated, no impaired state, **no draw spent on anybody**, violence and sex in
separate registers. Ground, hand, name-exchange and aftermath all new against the grid.
