# VOLUME 17 BATCH 0002 ON DISK — **THE VOLUME-WIDE GATE. CHAPTERS 811–820 WRITTEN. MOVEMENT TWO CLOSED ON A PRACTICE AND A REFUSAL AND NOT ON INK. NO THREAD CLOSED. ONE THREAD ADVANCED INTO VIEW. NONE OF THE ELEVEN MUST-NOTS BROKEN.** THIS IS THE ONLY BLOCK IN THIS FILE.

## WHAT 811–820 DID TO THE THREADS

**ADVANCED, NOT CLOSED: any person may put one question about any paper, answered in writing, both in the Register in words used.** About nine questions a month came through that door in this batch, and about four of them in nine were about the asker's own paper. **AND THE KIND OF ROW THAT HAS NO INSTRUMENT IS NOW TWO ROWS:** a question, and an answer with no question joined to it, and a question with no answer under it, and there is nothing anywhere in this city that says which is which and nothing that ever will.

**THE NEIGHBOUR QUESTION IS NOW A QUESTION IN WRITING ON PENCIL PAPER IN A STRANGER'S OWN HAND.** The man in the cap came a fourth time on the fourteenth of April and would not let a clerk write it down, so he wrote it himself and put his own date and his own hour on it, and it went into the bound Register in his words and may not be taken from. **A district officer, who is not an enemy and is right, has established that the same question has been properly put this month in four offices in this city and properly refused in three and properly entered in one, and that his office is the only one that put it in a book.** He cannot answer it, cannot refuse it in a way he can enter, and cannot make the book look as though it had never been asked.

**STILL PERMITTED TO OPEN, AND NOW OPEN IN FACT AND WRITTEN: that a refusal is said in a room with the door open and travels further than an answer ever did, that an answer about a person cannot be carried at all unless it is read aloud with that person in the room, and that the people who can spare the morning to stand in a room are not the people who ask.** 817, 819, 820. **A rule about what an answer may be carried in does nothing whatever about what a refusal sounds like from four feet.**

**STILL PERMITTED TO OPEN AND NOW OPEN IN FACT: that the office and the ground-floor counter four hundred yards away are drawing from the same queue and that nothing in this city joins the two books.** About four people a month ask Wren Kallo at the second counter whether this office holds anything about their neighbour, will not say which office they mean, and have been sent away four times.

**THE ONE INSTRUMENT THIS VOLUME MAY CLOSE, THE COPY IN HIS COAT, IS UNTOUCHED.** It has been carried down four flights and up again and back out of a room and back in, in the eighth year of the ninth and in the twenty-eighth of April of the tenth, and it has now been joined in the inside of that coat by an answer that cannot be given and by the empty sheet out of his own drawer. **None of the three may be delivered, produced or entered anywhere before 844 and none of them may be after.**

## THE ELEVEN MUST-NOTS, RE-RUN ACROSS TEN FILES

1. **NOTHING RETURNING TO THE REACH OF 486, IN EITHER DIRECTION, BY ANYBODY.** No chapter argues it and no character says it is settled or that it is not. Zero mentions of grains, of a floor, of a total, of the account.
2. **NO FIGURE OF THE ACCOUNT, no floor, no ledger total, no running balance.** `grep -rio 'settl' chapters/volume-17/` returns **zero in every inflection**; `settled` is not available as a word in this volume.
3. **THE TWO WORDS ON THE STONE NOT PRINTED AND NOT ASSERTED.** Not in this batch at all. `stone` appears twice as paving and as a laundry table and neither is the dressed stone at the foot of the hill.
4. **NOBODY SENT, TRACED OR ASKED.** The woman who cannot read is not in this batch at all and nothing about her is mentioned. The broom woman is absent and unnamed and unasked. **The boy comes up the slope with a board and a paste brush and is not asked anything and is not aged**, and 811 does not go near either printed box.
5. **NO YARD, NO DOCKET, NO DATE.** No stone yard is entered anywhere in 811–820. The one place a docket exists on the page, the drawer under a bench in a division building at 784, was referred to at 817 in one sentence and not entered.
6. **THE HEAD IS NOT PRINTED IN ANY OF THE TEN FILES.** It is due at 823, 837 and 850 and nowhere else in the volume, and at those three it carries both counts named in words and the subtraction shown.
7. **SECOND COLUMN FOUR LINES** in every chapter of the batch, the fourth carrying a woman's name and no figure. **At 817 five words and a date were written out loud and refused, and no fifth line and no name went in.**
8. **NO SEVENTH LINE IN THE OFFICE HAND, ANYWHERE.** The thirty-eighth goes up on the thirtieth of June at 823/824 carrying nothing new, and 820 deliberately does NOT change the sheet, because a sheet goes up on the quarter-end and the tenth of June is not one. Two refusals were entered and both are a clerk's hand in a bound book at the date and the hour, in the words she was given, and **neither is at the foot of anything.**
9. **HIS AUGUST BLANK NEVER COMPLETED.** Not touched, not mentioned, not printed.
10. **NO RANK, BAND, PERMISSION, STANDING OR SHIFT CHANGE.** Rank one, branch nights four and three off, the rota with four names. Two stops on a stair and a bad chest on a wet pavement are his, and are the same chest that has been spent since the fourth year.
11. **NO NEW ENEMY.** The opposition this batch is a district officer doing her job, a counterman on the ground floor who is right, a woman who keeps an index and has said nothing six times to people who asked her the wrong question, a broker who is right about a price, and about nine people in a street who heard a refusal and made a sentence out of it. **Not one of them is cruel and every one of them is right.**

**AND ONE MORE THING THIS BATCH SETS DOWN THAT A LATER PASS MUST NOT UNSET: A REFUSAL TRAVELS AND IT TRAVELS IN A ROOM.** About nine people in a corridor about four feet wide heard the same refusal twice on one morning and one of them worked out from the shape of it that there was something there. **The whole of movement three and of 840's decision has to be done to a refusal before it is done to an answer.**

**AND ONE MORE: A PRACTICE NEEDS A PERSON IN IT.** On the fourth of April an answer went down four flights in a runner's hand because the officer who carries was out on a district, and the carrier's book has two columns in it and no line for a runner, and the row entered in the bound Register says a carrier wrote the hand and the order, and three of the four things in that row are true. **811 decided out loud that this office goes on saying things about itself that it cannot check and that neither he nor the clerk will write down that it is doing that, and that decision was made in a room and is not ink.**
