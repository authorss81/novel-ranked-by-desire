# VOLUME 17 BATCH 0003 ON DISK — **THE VOLUME-WIDE GATE. CHAPTERS 821–830 WRITTEN. MOVEMENT THREE CLOSED ON A REFUSAL AND NOT ON INK. NO THREAD CLOSED. THREE THREADS ADVANCED INTO VIEW AND TWO NEW ONES OPENED. NONE OF THE ELEVEN MUST-NOTS BROKEN.** THIS IS THE ONLY BLOCK IN THIS FILE.

## WHAT 821–830 DID TO THE THREADS

**ADVANCED, NOT CLOSED: any person may put one question about any paper, answered in writing, both in the Register in words used.** About nine questions a month came through that door in this batch and **about four rows in every nine are about a person**, and that figure is printed identically at 821 and 823 and is not merged with any other four. **AND THE KIND OF ROW THAT HAS NO INSTRUMENT IS NOW THREE KINDS:** a question, an answer with no question joined to it, a question with no answer under it, a request, and a row carrying a wrong sentence above a right one nine minutes apart. **Nothing anywhere in this city says which is which and nothing ever will.**

**STILL PERMITTED TO OPEN, AND NOW OPEN IN FACT AND WRITTEN: that a person can be read two days off the top of nine sheets from a doorway at nine feet, that this office cannot stop it, and that the person it was done to has lost the certainty that her door is a wall and cannot get it back.** 821.

**STILL PERMITTED TO OPEN AND NOW OPEN IN FACT: that an answer read aloud once in that room with the door open goes out into about four feet of corridor, that a correction goes under and never instead, and that the office has now refused to read a fresh answer a second time — so the wrong sentence stays on top and there is no instrument anywhere to take it out.** 825, 830.

**STILL PERMITTED TO OPEN AND NOW OPEN IN FACT: that the office's traffic and the month clock's cost are two populations of the same size that have never once overlapped, and that the only person who walks both distances is the man who refused to keep a tally of the faces between them.** 826.

**NEW AND NOW OWING, AND NOTHING IN THIS VOLUME CAN PAY EITHER: Brack has said he will go on giving the four-year sentence whether it is true or not, and a woman who mends umbrellas has asked for a day in four years — come down that street and tell me whether the bottom two inches came back — and she has not asked anybody for anything in that room in nineteen years.** 826, 829.

**NEW AND NOW OWING: A STRANGER IS COMING BACK TO THE RAIL ON THE THIRTIETH OF SEPTEMBER TO READ THE FOOT OF THE PAGE WITH HIS OWN EYES AND COMPARE IT AGAINST FOUR WORDS IN HIS OWN HAND, AND HE HAS PROMISED TO TELL NEITHER THE CLERK NOR THE OFFICE WHETHER HE WAS RIGHT.** 822. **That is 837.**

**THE ONE INSTRUMENT THIS VOLUME MAY CLOSE, THE COPY IN HIS COAT, IS UNTOUCHED.** It may not be delivered, produced or entered anywhere in 831–850 and it is not to be touched before 844. The inside of that coat on the left holds four things and a later pass that counts three is counting it as it stood at 814. **At 828 he added a fifth thing to it that is not a thing at all: six short lines on the back of his right hand, in his own hand, with the hour against each — a copy on skin, which cannot be produced in a room, cannot be taken off without taking the man with it, and comes off in about a week.**

## THE ELEVEN MUST-NOTS, RE-RUN ACROSS TEN FILES

1. **NOTHING RETURNING TO THE REACH OF 486, IN EITHER DIRECTION, BY ANYBODY.** No chapter argues it and no character says it is settled or that it is not. Zero mentions of grains, of a floor, of a total, of the account.
2. **NO FIGURE OF THE ACCOUNT, no floor, no ledger total, no running balance.** `grep -rio 'settl' chapters/volume-17/` returns **zero in every inflection**; `settled` is not available as a word in this volume and was not used.
3. **THE TWO WORDS ON THE STONE NOT PRINTED AND NOT ASSERTED.** `stone` in this batch is paving at 824, a print run at 827, and the dressed stone at the foot of the hill at 827, where he stands nine feet back from it and does not go nearer. **The words are not among the five hands in the four inches and are not going to be, and 827 says that in as many words and asserts nothing else about them.**
4. **NOBODY SENT, TRACED OR ASKED.** The woman who cannot read is not in this batch at all and nothing about her is mentioned; her sheet is named once, face down under a batten, unmoved. The broom woman is absent and unnamed and unasked. **The boy comes up the slope with a board and a paste brush, is not asked anything and is not aged**, and no chapter in this batch goes near either printed box. The form's ordinal is not printed anywhere in 811–850 and the hundredth belongs to 838.
5. **NO YARD, AND NO DOCKET OF LIN HAO'S, AND NO DATE OFF ONE — AND THE ROW THAT PREVIOUSLY CARRIED THIS WAS FALSE ON THE PAGE.** No stone yard is entered anywhere in 821–830 and Lin Hao's docket has not left his coat. **THE PREVIOUS TEXT OF THIS ROW CLAIMED THE DOCKET IS NAMED ONCE AT 830 AND THAT THE DATE ON IT IS NEVER PRINTED AND NEVER READ ALOUD. BOTH CLAIMS WERE WRONG, AND THE REPAIR RECONCILES THEM AGAINST THE CHAPTER INSTEAD OF AGAINST ITSELF.** The docket at 830 is a **second docket and it belongs to the man it is about, who is a member of the public and not an officer of anything**: it is named twice, once in his mouth as the reason he can give a day this office cannot, and once as it goes back inside his coat where it has been for nine years. **He does read a date off it aloud in that room — the eighteenth of the last month of the ninth year, and he says it is stamped for the eighteenth — and that date is entered under the four lines in a bound Register in a clerk's hand.** What the volume forbids is untouched: **the docket read out in 712 is the yard's own delivery docket, its date was read aloud once in that chapter and was not written down by anybody, and it is in no form in 821–850.** Two different papers, two different men, and the day the office cannot get is a day off a private docket that it has no standing to ask for and no instrument to copy.
6. **THE HEAD IS PRINTED IN ONE FILE.** `grep -c 'eight hundred and fifty-two' chapters/volume-17/chapter-082*.md` returns 823 and nothing else. It is due at 837 and 850 and nowhere else in the volume, and at those two it carries both counts named in words and the subtraction shown: at 837 `3,479 − 1,499 = 1,980 = 22 × 90`, twenty-three sheets and twenty-two quarters; at 850 `3,569 − 1,499 = 2,070 = 23 × 90`, twenty-four sheets and twenty-three quarters.
7. **SECOND COLUMN FOUR LINES** in every chapter of the batch. At 828 a woman offers to carry a sheet for it and is refused on a landing, in the words that those two printed words are a works department's routing line and the woman in the fourth line is paid once for it. **No fifth line and no name went in.**
8. **NO SEVENTH LINE IN THE OFFICE HAND, ANYWHERE.** The thirty-eighth went up on the thirtieth of June at 823 at four, carrying no new line, and 821 and 822 do not touch the rail at all, and **the rail does not move at 820 either**, which batch 0002 had to repair. A practice was entered at 824 and a refusal at 830 and both are a clerk's hand in a bound Register at the date and the hour, **and neither is at the foot of anything.**
9. **HIS AUGUST BLANK NEVER COMPLETED.** Named once at 827 as a space with nothing in it that no hand has gone near, and not touched.
10. **NO RANK, BAND, PERMISSION, STANDING OR SHIFT CHANGE.** Rank one, branch nights four and three off, the rota with four names, a white plate with a name on it and no figure cut into it anywhere. **The chapter at 828 is the one where that is the whole of his power: five refusals on a landing, none of them a decision, and a refusal being the only thing in that building a man with no standing is allowed to give.**
11. **NO NEW ENEMY.** The opposition this batch is a woman who keeps an index and has lost a certainty, a clerk who cannot read her own book back, a woman who mends umbrellas and charges nothing, a man with a slate who is right, a woman at a counter entitled to one question a day, a woman asking for a sheet of paper on a landing, and a man who brought a paper through a door in the autumn and is owed a day. **Not one of them is cruel and every one of them is right.**

## AND ONE MORE THING THIS BATCH SETS DOWN THAT A LATER PASS MUST NOT UNSET

**A CORRECTION GOES UNDER AND NEVER INSTEAD, AND IT IS NOW THE LAW OF TWO ROOMS.** At 814 and at 823 and at 830, in a filing floor and in a bound Register, the same refusal to move a day or take a line out is given three times by three people who cannot see each other. **The wrong sentence stays above the right one in that book for ever, and there is no instrument anywhere in this city that will let anybody take the top one out, and the person who decided it said out loud in that room that he decided it, because in about four years somebody is going to want it undone.**
