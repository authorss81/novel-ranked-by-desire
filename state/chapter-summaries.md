**941 · 28 Oct · 4,227 · The Door Used By Everybody.** A woman comes up those four flights at ten past ten with a man a pace behind her who has never been up that stair and has, and both of those were visible inside about four seconds. The way up cost her something and she did not hide it. She asks about her husband, who was there in the spring and would not come. **He is in the room. That is the only thing about the morning that went right, and it is also the whole of the problem: the practice from the eighteenth of August gives an answer about a person only with that person in the room, and it says nothing whatever about the person having asked.** He says the thing the office has said all year — we send nobody to a house, we keep no list, neither can be written down — and then says that somebody has done it for them, at a cost of two landings and forty minutes and her own morning, **and it worked.** Then he asks the man. **HE DOES NOT ANSWER HER QUESTION. HE ASKS THE MAN IN THE ORDINARY VOICE WHETHER HE WANTS THE ANSWER, THE CLERK'S PEN AN INCH OFF THE PAGE, AND THE MAN SAYS NO AND THEN SAYS WHY: twice to that office, once at two in the morning in the eighth month to a shut door because a piece of paper sent him, never angry, embarrassed, which is worse.** The clerk enters the worst available sentence and says so out loud before she writes it. **Ending: the only thing the no-list rule produced this morning was a doorway in which to refuse, and about four people had stopped on the second landing to hear a man use it.**

**942 · 5 Nov · 4,234 · The Dark Comes Early. CROSSING.** Thirty less twenty-eight is two, and two with five taken out of the next month is seven. Five mornings, a white edge on every tread, about nine people a morning coming up more slowly than in the spring. **THE BACK BOARD OF THAT BOOK HAS BEGUN TO HINGE OPEN**, with a tick about every four seconds and a line of daylight between the two halves of the board, and the only proper repair is a binder, which is four hundred yards in a cart in daylight with somebody else's hands on it. He says the word binder on the second morning and does not do it. **THE LIGHT GOES OFF THAT STREET AT ABOUT TEN PAST FOUR AND THE CARD RUNS TO HALF PAST FIVE**, so about four people a day are standing outside a door they cannot read. He takes hours off nothing, adds nothing, and puts no lamp in that passage. **INSTEAD HE READS THE THREE LINES OUT LOUD FROM HIS OWN SECOND STEP TO THE LAST FOUR PEOPLE OF THE DAY, FROM MEMORY AND NOT OFF THE CARD**, because a man reading three lines off a card at his own door is an official thing at a door and that office has no official thing at a door. **THE PRICE, SAID WITH THE DOOR OPEN: a man who says his own address in the dark at his own door is a man anybody can find in the dark, and in August he handed that job to a man who is paid nothing so that it would not be his.** The clerk names what he has replaced a number with. Ending: he stands at the bottom of a yard with a ticking book under his arm and a question for a woman at the top of a walled road, and he has had it since the third of September and has not found a day.

**943 · 12 Nov · 4,241 · The Intake Desk. **944 · 19 Nov · 4,248 · Not On The Roll.** The same hall, the same working afternoon, and she has been at that counter twenty years and has never once been asked about herself by any of about nine hundred people a week. **945 · 26 Nov · 4,255 · In Her Head Only. **946 · 3 Dec · 4,262 · Soft At The Corners. CROSSING.** Thirty less twenty-six is four, and four with three taken out of the next month is seven. **947 · 10 Dec · 4,269 · The Worn Card Read Back.** A man with a piece of paper that is half an hour different at one end comes up at twenty past eleven after standing at the bottom of that stair for a quarter of an hour, and asks him to read the three lines. **He had told himself in the corridor that he would refuse. **948 · 17 Dec · 4,276 · The Board Bare With Paste.** Half past three, an hour earlier than September, because the light goes off that kerb at about four in December. **THE RAIL IS BARE.** Under it, along the top batten, a strip of board the length of a forearm where the wood was covered in paste and the paper is not, tacky to hard, grey at the edges, lifting at one corner. THE REASON IS NOT A RULE. **949 · 24 Dec · 4,283 · What Is Still Owed.** The last working day of the year that is not the thirtieth, about four hours of useful light, about nine people up those four flights, one row with a date and an hour and nothing under the hour that has been there since the twenty-sixth of August. **The last asking of the year comes at about twenty past four and it is whether the door is there after the turn, and there are about nine minutes of the card's hours left.** He answers in three parts: the card does not say anything about a turn of the year and he is not putting a fourth line on it about one; he is not going to promise a thing he has not decided; and a man who has promised says four words at a quarter to ten and a man who has not says four words at about twenty past four. **THEN THE MAN TELLS HIM, HAVING WALKED UP FOUR FLIGHTS TO TELL HIM AND NOT TO ASK: a woman on his ground floor has been telling about nine people since about the ninth that the office is closed over the turn, off a man who read it at a counter on the second.** He does not ask to see the paper and says why. He goes out onto the landing at twenty to five to look at a piece of stiff grey card about the length of a forearm with three lines on it and not one name, and finds a street that is sound and two soft corners and three straight nails in holes that are wider than they were in November. **Ending: it would still be there on the thirtieth, and on the morning after that if not one person did anything about it.**

**950 · 30 Dec · 4,289 · Not One Name. **954 · 11 Feb · 4,330 · The Coin In His Hand.** A carters' yard off Cordwain, four carts under a tarpaulin, two women at a tailboard. **The man with the coin is not a fraud, has not lied to anybody, names his price before he names the door, walks the stair himself once more before he takes anybody, has never been up those four flights to that door in his life, and has walked it with a crate.** **THE THING BEING SOLD IS THE WALKING AND NOT THE ADDRESS.** The address is on a door in the open and has been for seven weeks and the man has never touched it and has never had it in his hands. **Four flights of air has always been the arrangement and he has spent nine years thinking of it as a wall, and it is not a wall, it is a walk, and a walk is a thing a person does.** He says the two things before she has finished her story: he sends nobody, and he did not send anybody in August. She came up four flights at her own cost to ask whether paying a man for the walk was a normal thing to do, **and the answer she came for is that nobody will ever be charged for being told where the door is — and he works out inside a flight that this is aimed at the wrong thing, because the man is not selling the telling.** He says he is not going to find the man and is not going to have anybody sent. **The other woman's last words to his back are that it is not much, and she would rather he heard the smallness of it from her.**

**955 · 18 Feb · 4,337 · The Ones Who Cannot Pay.** **Sanna Ruhl comes up four flights at ten past eleven with nothing in her hands and costs herself the morning, and asks him to say the figure out loud so she can check it against a lane, and gets it right first time because the man said it out loud in front of somebody who was not interested.** The amount is not much. **The people a price like that hurts are not a category; they are a few particular people, and he knows them, and he says so and stops there, and he says the reason he is not going to say how he knows them: the moment there is a way of saying that out loud there is a place to write it down.** **Some of them have already stopped coming and not one of them left a word with anybody, because there was no word to leave.** She refuses to be thanked and will not have him give her anything for a morning she spent. **He says with the door open that this office will never charge anybody for being told where that door is, and he knows before he has said it that it will do nothing at all, and he works out at about ten past twelve standing on the second landing that the man is not charging for the telling and that a man's legs cannot be stopped from being sold. AND HE WAS GLAD FOR ABOUT FOUR SECONDS, AND IT WAS NOT ABOUT ANYBODY IN THE PASSAGE: IT WAS ABOUT BEING THE MAN WHO SAID IT.** She names the gladness and he does not deny it. **The carrier with the bad back comes in at about half past one and says Brack's lane has it, wrong by about four times and in the direction that makes it expensive, and that he has priced two women the same and they have never met.**

**956 · 25 Feb · 4,344 · Brack Prices A Mouth.** Brack has had it about a week and has not written it anywhere, and will not for about four more days. **His figure prices a man rather than a service and is about four times what a woman in a yard said on the eleventh, and he has had a week to work out why and has got it exactly wrong, and the reason is that he has priced what a person will pay to have four flights taken off them instead of the four flights themselves.** **The lane's actual sentence is not that a man will take you up four flights but that the man in the white plate knows about it and has not stopped him, and that is worse, and it is true.** The slate carries the sooner figure on the left, unrubbed, and the head of the office's standing sheet in four-inch letters on the right, and a third figure in Brack's head with no unit after it that is not on the slate and will not be this year. **He does not correct Brack's figure and does not use it, and says so in the lane, because a figure he does not own in a lane is a figure that belongs to the lane.** **He says the four-year sentence himself, in the lane, and does not argue it.** He asks the one thing Brack can give and gets three names said out loud and not written down and not said twice, and **two more that Brack will not say, because the first three do it in the open and the last two do not, and putting them in a lane would make a price for two women who have never asked for one.** **The sooner figure is not rubbed out, the third figure is still unchalked and still owed, and the head is not moved and not confirmed.**

**957 · 2 Mar · 4,351 · The Second Time Round. CROSSING.** Thirty less twenty-five is five, and five with two taken out of the next month is seven. **The woman who was walked up four flights in October comes up four flights for the second time, with her coat buttoned, and says before she is asked that she has not come about a question of her own.** Somebody two floors below her has told her she can pay a man in a street to take her next time. **She has come to tell him that, at her own cost and in her own morning, because she does not want to be a person who uses a thing like that in silence.** What she actually came up with is that she went away in October having been given a doorway in which to refuse an answer about her husband, and has been saying that a rule did a thing for her ever since, **and now there is a price on the stairs and she does not know whether a rule did a thing for her or a set of stairs did a thing for her because somebody was paid to help her with them.** **And then the part she is ashamed of: she was glad, on a landing, at about eleven at night, that she could pay for it.** He answers her, not the market, in about nine seconds in the ordinary voice. **NOTHING GOES IN THAT BOOK, because she brought no question with a date and an hour against it and she refuses to manufacture the two so that he can write something down and feel better about it.** The carrier with the bad back hears from the turn that he has had a conversation that was going nowhere and has never once asked her about it. **The last thing she says on the way down is that the price has not made anybody worse than they were — it has only made the thing visible.**

**958 · 9 Mar · 4,358 · Paid Nothing, Said Anyway.** He goes to the counter under the window and stands four feet off for about eleven minutes and lets the counterman do his day. **Then the counterman tells him to come back at twenty past twelve, and says he will not say it while he is working, and will not be able to say it if he is asked about it afterwards.** The quarterly sheet is face up on that rail and the caption under the figure says what it is and what it is not, **and what happens in that passage every morning is that a person reads a count of pieces of paper and goes off believing it is a count of something else, and the counterman has never once been able to correct it, because that office does not correct copies of itself and he is a man in a passage.** **The sentence: every stranger who comes to that counter finds that door because of him, not because of the card, and neither the card nor the man in the street is him — but neither of those things would have happened if a man behind that counter had said no to a stranger in about the second year. A THING GIVEN AWAY EVERY DAY AT THE SAME COUNTER FOR NINE YEARS IS NOT A THING ANYBODY ELSE CAN BE THE FIRST TO SELL. THE MAN IN THE STREET IS THE SECOND PERSON IN THIS CITY TO HAND IT OVER, AND THE FIRST ONE HAS A COUNTER AND A RAIL AND NO NAME ON ANYTHING.** He calls it a grievance and says he has had nine years to turn it into an argument. **Lin Hao does not thank him, does not offer to pay him and does not ask him to stop, and says why: all three are things a person does to a man in order to stop something, and he has stood in front of that counter for nine years and has never done any of them.** Ending: the counterman says he will not get another chance to say it, and to come back in a week and not at about twelve.

**959 · 16 Mar · 4,365 · Two Women In A Lane.** Brack says the two names he would not say on the twenty-fifth: **Sanna Ruhl, who works a floor at the end of a market, and Ilva Tern, who stands at a stop where the last service has stopped calling.** They have never met, and about four miles and a shift separate them. **The sentence going round the lane about both of them is that they are doing it for him, and Brack has let it stand because you do not put a person on a wall about a thing they have not said to you.** What the lane has instead is that the address went round for nothing, that a man will take anybody up four flights for a small amount, that the man in the plate has heard about it and has not lifted a hand, and that there is nobody at the bottom of the stairs — which is true until tomorrow. He finds the second one first and asks her to come up four flights at about three. **Both women come up, and stand four feet apart in that room with the book on the table and nobody introduces them.** He says the same thing to both: **you are not doing it for me, you are doing it for the person standing in front of you, and it is said for me because I gave it away first, and in a city the first gift is the address of the second one.** **HE DOES NOT THANK THEM AND SAYS SO, because thanking, paying and asking are the three things you do to a person in order to stop something, and he is not doing any of them.** And then they compare: **Sanna Ruhl has been saying the third court for about four months and it is the second court, and four people have gone to the wrong courtyard and one got there by accident.** "That is the whole of what a mouth is." Wenna Raut says out loud that neither woman is going to be on anything. Ending: he walks both of them down and stands at the bottom of that stair in the dark and does **not** say the three lines out loud, because neither of them asked and both had already been up those stairs that day.

**960 · 23 Mar · 4,372 · The Bottom Of His Own Stair. THE MOVEMENT'S IRREVERSIBLE DECISION, A PRACTICE AND NOT INK.** He asks the clerk to count six things, out loud, in order, with the door open. **She does not agree with the count of six: five are refusals and the sixth is an absence, and you cannot refuse a thing that is not there, and the moment you name its price with the door open you have turned it into a rule, and a rule goes on a wall four hundred yards off.** What is left when the paper is gone is where he is. **A MAN AT THE BOTTOM OF FOUR FLIGHTS IS A MAN NOBODY CAN BE TAKEN TO, because anybody who wants to go there walks past him first; being shown the way is the whole of what is being sold, and a man who is there is not a man who can be shown. HE IS THE ARRIVING. AND NOBODY CAN BE CHARGED FOR REACHING HIM, BECAUSE THE REACHING HAS ALREADY HAPPENED.** The worse half, said rather than left for a lane: **a man at the bottom of his own stair is a man standing in the only public room this office has ever had, and he has spent nine years hiding from being described, and from tomorrow morning he is going to be standing where a stranger can look at him for as long as they like with no paper in between.** **THE PRICE, SAID BEFORE IT IS DONE ONCE: about four hundred yards of distance gone; a door nobody can knock on without walking past a man; every carrier's back visible for as long as this goes on; about four people a day who will learn his face before they learn anything else; and it comes out of the three days off, because he works four nights in seven at the branch and is not moving that shift and is not asking for anything.** **FROM TOMORROW, THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THIS MONTH, HE STANDS AT THE FOOT OF THAT STAIR FROM A QUARTER TO TEN TO HALF PAST FIVE ON WORKING MORNINGS, FIVE DAYS IN SEVEN.** He says it is not a practice, because a practice has to be written on the practice sheet by a clerk, and it is not a rule, because it does not concern anybody but him. **THE THING HE HAS ALSO DECIDED AND HAS NOT SAID: HE WILL NOT BE THE ONLY ONE, AND THAT DECISION IS IN NO ROOM AND IN NO BOOK AND ON NO PIECE OF PAPER.** Ending: the book down at half past five by his own hands, not upstairs overnight, the light gone off that street about an hour and a quarter before, and him at the bottom of his own stair with his hands empty saying the three lines out loud once into an empty stair from memory, because nobody is there to hear it.
