## Volume 2 — The Reservoir

### Ch 51 — The Held Band
Morning after the Review. The plate office socket prints a **third line** it has never printed in twenty-nine years: standing 8,138, band six, floor 8,141, **carried on a Review ruling, number 112**. Faber reads it aloud to a queue of thirty. The room learns that the band sits on a decision and not on the figure, and that any person with a plate can now read both sentences. Relief curdles into exposure — the 11,862 gap is now public property. He recognises ruling 112 as the number a clerk wrote in red beside the thirty-first register line on the Review day, in front of him. **Ledger rises 91 → 97 with cause stated** (his name in the largest type in the city; strangers stand nearer than the queue requires). Ends on a danger: a runner in a grey coat with no badge leaves a buff square at the counter — attend, bring the plate, bring the ruling — **four boxes marked and a fifth that nobody could write**. Three days' notice. A missed attendance marks a lapse against the band.

### Ch 52 — Ink In The Margin
The Reading clerk who owns his query can no longer help him: the query closed on a finding and a finding does not sit with the office that asked the question. At the Wing's brass counter the notice is **unstamped**, which the counter clerk says is worse than stamped. In the corridor above, **Anneke Roos**, a junior clerk, is transcribing rulings onto the observation register and has been two days on the thirty-first line, which carries a ruling number in red ink with nowhere to go: twenty-nine columns, all about accounts with a figure, an interval and a state. She sends the sheet upstairs; the mark will come back as enter, refer, hold or query, and for a new shape it is **hold**, which does not go away. He learns the printed public sheet is not the book. She tells him in a stairwell — and admits she is not allowed to — that a **Veil-Research consult line** on the docket has carried the initials **S.M.** against a three-year-old memo about a transfer that carried no name, and that the initials are the only thing on the answer.

### Ch 53 — The Thirty-First Line
Lantern Row, evening. Ines prices it: every office closes a file by **describing** the person in it; an open file is not safe, it is a place people keep coming to, with a new question every eight days. She cannot tell him what an audit does with a line it has never had to read. She states plainly that she has **not filed the himself line and cannot decide whether to**, and that she has drawn a four-sided sheet — the line, the arithmetic, and the one question — and shown it to nobody. She takes shared custody by putting it where he can find it. Then the Vestry cold store: **Tessa's rack is eighteen, nineteen before nightfall**, the stippling is pale blue above the joint and four inches has begun on the right forearm, and three charts are on the rack in a row — Nell Ardery, Garrick Neve, the yard checker — turned in four seconds. Tessa refuses thanks before he offers it and asks for the monthly number said aloud. **Second planting of Sun by name**: a woman from Veil-Research, years ago, asking after a man who came in shaking at 2,640 and went out shaking, date the second of May, no face. Tessa will not be thanked and says so first. Ends on his decision to attend the Wing **in person**, which closes the option of distant compliance.

### Ch 54 — The Inventory Summons
The irreversible decision of the batch. **Silas Drant**, a Wing attendance clerk of nineteen years, lays out the four shapes the office has for a man who is not where his plate says, and he fits none: the band was not struck from the figure, it is not carried by merit, the reading did not end in a lapse or in an interval, and he is not a held account. The machine makes the sound of a key that will not turn. Drant asks, openly, for the **names behind the observation entries** — eighty-one rows, nine years, and one empty row with his date already on it. Lin Hao refuses: **no**, flat and small, and the refusal is minuted, read back, signed, and dated in a room. Drant's three points: the refusal is an event, not a work item; a man who says nothing keeps a drawer shut for years; and by refusing he has given the office the only reason it has to keep him. The counter stamps the attendance twice — the second date **six days on** — and names the third sub-level room with no window, the paper prepared and unsigned. Ends on a cost incurred: he is now owned by a calendar.

### Ch 55 — The Question She Kept
Veil-Research side room, first court, the iron stair. Sun Meiling does not answer the memo: it was asked three years ago about a transfer with no destination column, and her four-line answer is in her own drawer and has not changed in three years. She shows him two sheets of **sets** — loops grouped in threes and fives from two different buildings — and says the Review is an event about a person that was copied onto an account line because it had to go somewhere. Then she asks a **wrong question on purpose**, a plausible and moving false account of his figure, to see whether he corrects her usefully or honestly. He corrects her badly and at cost, and names what spending on himself did. She stops him on the word *transfer*: what he described was not an accident collecting in a man, and a shape in three-year-old data has the same geometry. **First scene, immovable.** Four true statements before anything starts, both of them hers first; adults, awake, unrestrained, unmedicated; the tender arm respected; violence and sex in separate registers. Her terms before anything happens: **not sworn, not housed, not filed**, with three reasons — inside a house that keeps no registers; a roll makes her the most reachable person in the house; and she wants to be the one on the page who cannot be reached by reading it. He is wanted for what he carries rather than audited for it, for the first time since the Review. Ends on an emotional consequence with pull: she keeps one page back and will not say it until he has stood still long enough for a mass to go flat — **a condition, not a date**, because a man who aims at a date does not stand still at all.

### Ch 56 — A Name Before Her
A normal shift, and nothing happens, which is the alarming part. Nine corridors, the figure moving, no new faces, no turns. A transcript of the ruling is taped inside the washroom door at eye level with Ola's question on it: **what does a finding number do on a register line** — and Ola names the answer herself, which is the join: the ruling number is on his plate and on a Wing register line, and that is the only thing anybody needs to come and ask. The third floor is quiet by agreement. **Colm Deveraux's door opens two inches for the first time since the 9th of the season.** Through the gap, on a bad shoulder: a woman in a dark coat with no paper came on the second day of the week and asked him one question at the counter of his own floor — *whether anybody had asked him for names* — said *that was very useful* when he said no, and then got a second answer out of him about arithmetic that he has held for four months because of a stair at eleven o'clock. Nadia refuses in advance to be spent to fix his calendar and puts on the record that she chose, unasked. Ends on the consequence: **his refusal in the Exchange protected nobody**, because a frightened man gives the office answers for nothing, and the list is being assembled out of other people's mouths.

### Ch 57 — Cutler's Row After
Afternoon at the yards. **Nell Ardery** on the landing of number 14 asks him plainly to make the cold stop, and he refuses and gives the reason without dignity: four in five days, and a second one into a person who has had one is a reportable episode. **Garrick Neve** — who lives at number 22, one of the thirty-one requisitioned dwellings, because of him — has worked out to the minute what happened on the Review day, and asks whether to write it up, and decides in public that a rumour is worse than a fact. The **checker at the yard gate** has never given anybody an address; Lin Hao tells him to write his own episode up anyway, which is the first time anyone has ever told him to put something on his own record. **Halvard Rees**, the control who never turned, watches him spend nothing on the entire row and refuses to call it the right decision, because the cost of it is sitting on three people and not on him. Physical risk and cost: a loaded barrow comes down the line of the last six doors on the hoist wire and he takes it on his forearm, the second time in the arc, and the grey comes about a second and a half behind it. Ends on a danger: the checker's docket has a **buff Wing attendance slip** under the week's gate tallies, unfolded, rewritten, his name on it, no date and no box marked — and the man at the gate works out the whole of it and finishes his own chart that night.

### Ch 58 — The Refusal At The Desk
Second attendance, a different room, a Field officer present. Drant produces the **wall map**: a printed district map with a newsprint rectangle over the Reading square, thirty-one pencilled marks on the Ferrier arc and one in the middle of the square, and three dots ringed in a second colour. Ruta Vesely, the analyst, had his plate, his band, his band line and the Review date and nothing else. Ines's chapter-49 sentence is paid on the page: the dot is that room. The request is put a second time in front of a Field witness and Lin Hao **dictates his own minute**, as he did on the 23rd: the faces are not instruments and an office that wants a name is describing him and he will not be described by an instrument; the three on the map are his cost and not his evidence; the himself line exists, is unfiled, and will not be offered. Runa Sato answers only for her own file, in writing, and refuses to answer for him. Drant mentions a thin dated sheet in a drawer, unsigned, and does not turn it round. The refusal is minuted and the schedule lengthens to **every nine days, three dates written**. Ends on a reversal: the Office's ally is not a form but a calendar, and in five years the only thing that has ever moved one of these is the account going quiet.

### Ch 59 — Three Colds At Once
Vestry cold store after hours; Ines in the corridor, an auditor may stand in a corridor. **Tessa refuses thanks before he offers it** — she did this, it is her inventory, thanking her is thanking her for her own invoice — and asks for the monthly number said aloud, the number and not the names. He says **twenty-two**; the rack is at twenty-two, twenty-three before nightfall, with a gap above it that she describes as where the next eight go. Right forearm begun, four inches in three weeks, and she is not short of forearm, she is short of room: four walls, three and a half, and the half is a chart rack. Then the drawer: **two hundred and sixty-one bodies over eleven years that present better than their own histories say they should**, under a third traceable to anybody. And the medicine: **a settled mass reads as too well** — a fed body gets better and stays better, which is not a mistake a machine can make; a quarter of the district improving faster than medicine can do it is not a set of charts, it is a line. She gives a date without hedging: **the twenty-fifth of next month, six weeks from the Review**, and says that is when she would put her hand out, and that the instrument has been able to read it since four days before. Ends on the revelation with cost: standing still is not the absence of danger, it is the Office's ally, and it needs nothing from him.

### Ch 60 — The Standing Inquiry
Thursday box at the Exchange with the queue out of the doors and turning at the corner. **Garrick Neve** puts the weekly question, and then spends four seconds saying in public that he was one of the three, that he wrote his own episode up unexplained, that it cost him, and that a decision made quietly in a corridor is not a decision but a piece of administration. Reading: 8,138, three under a floor of 8,141, three grains is the whole margin. Drant attends to observe and does not interfere, logs the discharge as an attendance kept, and gives him three dates in front of him — **the third, the twelfth and the twenty-first of next month** — and tells him he does not have to come on the first two and does have to come on the third. The square is entered for the first time in six weeks: the black six, four figures under the name, the fifth still the largest number in Ostara, a woman and a child standing in front of it. The visibility produces **no conversation at all**. **T2 touched and left shut**: the query clerk steadies a corner of the week's docket that was lifting, with her thumb, for a moment longer than the hand-over needed, and says nothing. **B5 opened and not pulled**: Ines says her sister's name once, in the queue, for the first time in her working life outside two offices — Ding put her own name on an intake sheet for a chest, because Ines told her in April not to see anybody else about it — and Lin Hao asks not one question. The Reading Office has asked her formally for the source of the unfiled line and she has answered with a question. **Ledger rises 97 → 103 with cause stated.** Ends on an earned hook: the fifth written attendance is four days inside the date Tessa gave him, and Sun Meiling's page is still unsaid.


### Ch 61 — The Ledger Quayle Keeps
The 28th. **The branch corridor board gains a second line, typed**: his Thursdays, the inventory room, and *attendant*. The same first notice that was unstamped in Ch 51 comes back **stamped** — the interval has gone from nine days to **seven**, and it carries three ruled lines the Office could not put in a box: **days attended, persons spoken with, addresses**. The runner says plainly that the third line is the join and that she does not need to explain it. **Anneke Roos**, met again at the register desk, counts the Wing's books and admits there is a **fourth**, out of a drawer since before his first attendance, with a page for every entry the register could not take, five fixed headings and a ruled space where a sixth should be. Its page for him has carried twenty days of dates since the eighth. Her gift to him: **she does not read it, she keeps it**, which is the only difference she has found in eleven days. **Ola Bright** has taped nothing and posted nothing and says so first: a typed line gets copied by people who cannot type and read out by people who cannot help it. Change of situation: the Office has stopped asking him to describe himself and started asking him to describe where he has been, and the place that gets answered is a corridor he has to walk through five days a week to get his wages.

### Ch 62 — The Shape In Data
The 3rd, the first weekly attendance, the bargain. **Silas Drant** lays out a card of two columns: fifty-one of his figures since the start of the season (8,140, 8,139, 8,138, 8,138, 8,138) against every fourth ordinary plate at the same counter, which goes up two hundred, down ninety, up four hundred, up eleven. **A standing that does not feed is evidence.** He offers the strip: the branch plate-office strip, taken weekly on a Thursday, on his shift, in his own corridor, **not a copy**, forwarded to the Wing, which discharges all three ruled lines — faces, persons and addresses — and holds the interval at seven days. Drant reads it back and adds the price aloud: a held band carries a tolerance of three grains, he is at 8,138 against a floor of 8,141, and **from today the figure is printed in public with a bell on the machine.** He signs with a pen he was handed, four seconds before he realises it is the first thing he has ever given that office. Cost on the page: two days a fortnight is the cheapest thing on the table and he took it because he was tired. Ends offstage: **Alma Quayle** reads a flat strip a week for four weeks and then a month, and writes the sixth heading herself, in her own hand, on the twentieth, and it is two words long.

### Ch 63 — What Was Sent
The 5th, the fortnight she asked for. **The wall is bare** — two hundred sheets off the wall and into a drawer nine feet away, because she has been looking at pictures of him for three years and a picture of a shape is the safest thing a woman with her trouble can put on a wall. She gives the sentence whole, in instalments he is not permitted: **what is in him was sent; a sent thing has a sender; the file it went into is One, One is the bottom of the Wing's list, there is no Zero, and the sender chose the lowest address that would hold it.** The consult that came up to Veil-Research three years ago was headed with that number. He understands four bells separately: it was never his, somebody gave away 214,000 grains on purpose, and **his brother was right in a public hall three years ago** — so what he has been carrying is not a question about a figure but a receipt. She names the head of the Office and does not soften it. **Second scene, immovable, and a different direction from Ch 55:** in September she refused to audit him; here she asks to *read* him, out loud, with her hands on him, for an hour, and he wants it. Four true things, hers first and longer, then his, including the true one — *I do not know what I am doing* — said on arrival. Adults, awake, unrestrained, unmedicated, no draw spent, no impaired state, violence and sex in separate registers; she narrates him aloud throughout and it is for her, not for him. Aftermath, in her two findings: **he is a shape with no place in it**, which means a map cannot hold him and a dot on a printed district map will never be him — and that a man with no address can be neither found nor cleared. She re-pins two hundred sheets and says she is going to stop asking about the data and start asking about him.

### Ch 64 — The Address Book
Lantern Row, the 6th. **Ines reads him the four-sided sheet** and will not let him read it, because he would correct the arithmetic. Side one is the line reading himself. Side two is the arithmetic and a heading, *why it is there and what it cost*, with no names. **Side three is new**, written on the third after the strip, and it is *places I have watched you stand in* — eleven of them with times — and it is two-thirds useless and one-third lethal, because the register keeps addresses and no faces and his ledger keeps faces and no addresses and the third ruled line on the buff square is the join. She gives him no addresses because she has one and it is not hers. Side four now carries two questions, and the second is hers: *what does an Office do with a man it cannot put an address to* — and the answer she arrives at is that he is the only man in Ostara who cannot be found, and an account that cannot be found cannot be claimed, and that is the whole reason it landed on him. **The decision:** she hands the sheet in on the seventh rather than filing it, on her own hand, so that it becomes the only true document in the city about a man with no address and cannot be described away by the next person with the file; and she puts it in his pocket so that the minute will read *attendee volunteered receipt of document*. Under the twelve lines there is a twelfth, empty, with one word printed over it: **RECIPIENT**. [One hundred and nine lines. Eight turned.] Ends on what she cannot unask: on the seventh a woman is going to say a true sentence into a room and an office is going to be able to write down that she said it, and for the first time in three years there will be a piece of paper in Ostara that describes none of the five things paper describes here. It describes a man with no address, and **a heading is the only thing about a document that cannot be unasked.** Last beat, across the city: Sun Meiling re-pinning two hundred sheets in the order she always puts them up in, having stopped asking about the data.

### Ch 65 — The Ungiven Word
The Ferrier line, the 9th. **Beatrix has taken him off her list** — off it since the thirteenth, and he has never asked why, which she has been waiting four weeks for. The price of the Review is not a quarrel: if he is on a column she tracks what a minute costs him, and if he is off it he is a passenger, and a passenger is somebody she is glad to see and somebody she could refuse, and she tells him she would, on a bad night, and that it went through her like a needle when she said it. She has lost her map of him and kept her map of the line. **The ungiven word:** she uses one about four hundred times since June and has never used it about him, because a word is how a person commits to an answer in this city before they have the evidence, and there are two answers and she does not have the right one. In exchange she gives a date — **the ninth of September**, hers since June, never told to anybody, not a favour — and takes it back by telling him it is not after anything of his. **The threat:** four turns last week on her own line, a woman with a notebook and a booked seat, asking operators for their counts and whether they can feel it when it goes, calling it a survey and not an audit; at the Yard end she asked a signalman for the attendant at the branch office and got him in eleven seconds, because Beatrix spent four months asking eleven signalmen to put a light on for him. Seen from the car: the woman is at the yard gate and the checker is answering her.

### Ch 66 — What September Still Holds
The 10th, a Thursday, in two halves. **The Exchange box, and the first consequence of Ch 62's signature:** the figure has been flat for a fifth week and a man in the queue says the word *flat* out loud, and the wrong answer assembles itself in the largest room in the district — a standing that does not rise is a standing with nothing going into it — and **Garrick Neve decides not to correct it**, which Lin Hao is grateful for and feels sick about. The strip is on a spike in his own corridor with a bell on the machine: he knew what he was buying and did not work out what he was selling. **The household above the chop-house.** **Marguerite's three answers** for the strip — invisible, meaningful, valuable — and then her refusal to use any of them: a thing she has touched is a thing she has a standing to call in, and she is not going to have a standing in him before there is a document; and the real reason, which is that four months and two houses and eight women have not got him, and she would rather be the woman who never knew than the woman who made him knowable. **Nadia came up four flights** because she cannot take the lift anywhere and would not be a thing about herself, and tells the room that the woman with the notebook sat in her car and that she gave her his name, on purpose, having worked it out first, because she is not frightened of a notebook and he is — nine hundred people already know, it is the only thing she is in any record about, and strangers talk. The last good news of the batch, and the batch's best plain sentence: the ledger cannot be held shut with his hands; it happened in front of strangers. Marguerite, from the stairs: his porters are being asked too, and a date he has washed off twice is the only asset he has that is not in his own hands. Nadia makes the room's rule about September and will take the car down and not come back up for a month if he says the word.

### Ch 67 — The Wall Map
The 12th, fourth attendance, and **Ruta Vesely is met for the first time**. She has eleven minutes of office time and no authority, and asks for the box at the bottom of the map — *place of event confirmed by subject* — to be left empty. Her deduction, done off his plate and his band and the date of a room: **the thirty-one marks along the Ferrier arc are not a pattern of him, they are a pattern of where he walks**, and the dot is a spend, and there has been exactly one spend since the start of the season, so the map has one event on it and an office cannot keep a finding open on one event. If the dot is a person the map has a place and the Office has him; if it is a spend the file closes. **She does not believe the map and has written that down in the third colour.** He gives her the true thing rather than the useful thing: the room was the room, it will happen again only if he spends again, and the map will be wrong either way — **and he refuses to correct it, because a corrected map would be a description of where he goes, and he would rather have a wrong map.** The cost is named and it falls on other people: an unconfirmed box means the Office attends that room forever, and nine clerks set it up and take it down, and one of them has already been asked about a Thursday. The chapter's turned line, in his own handwriting and over her initials: *Place of event confirmed by subject. Subject confirms the room and declines to confirm any recurrence.* Drant says in advance that the Office has read an empty box as a refusal for nine years and this time it will be wrong in the correct direction. Asked whether he will spend again, he answers: **I do not know**, and it goes up as *unknown*, which is the only word the standing schedule has to keep answering.

### Ch 68 — The Cold Store Count
The 16th, the cold store, two degrees, lamp on a cord, couch nobody uses. **Twenty-six**, said aloud, and he is told off for wrapping a number in a sentence. **The ladder to date: 17 (Ch 50) → 18 (Ch 53) → 22 (Ch 59) → 26.** **The drawer has gone down**, 261 to **258**, because she went back through the bottom hundred where her own handwriting is worst and found three ordinary people who got better; and the rate of the rest has not moved in a month, and the reason is that the only man in this city doing it has not done it once. **The Office has reached the clinic**: an anonymised request form at the ward for *the number of patients presenting in a manner inconsistent with recorded history*, no names, perfectly within the rules — arrived by the chain of Ch 62's flat strip, then Ruta's finding, then a line, then an address. Her answer will be **258, cause: not established, not one of them** — the first thing she has ever put in writing — **and one case: her own.** One page, no symptoms, no history, and a column she has had to invent, and the reason in her own words: *the treating physician's own record is consistent with the same finding, without cause.* She is the only body in the city with a settled mass and a clean history, and if she goes up as a control then two hundred and fifty-eight of her patients stop being a line and start being a coincidence with a doctor in it. She will send it on the twentieth and has no idea who will write back.

### Ch 69 — The Fourth Box
The 17th, and **there are two of him**: the seven-day standing on one sheet and Drant's nine-day schedule on the other, and *a standing supersedes a schedule, a schedule does not supersede a standing*, so the third, the twelfth and the twenty-first Drant wrote in a column on the eighteenth of last month were never cancelled. **Three dates after this one** — the twenty-first on the schedule, the twenty-fourth and the thirty-first on the standing, four days apart then three then seven — and Drant tells him the thirty-first existed before he knew it, so he cannot later say nobody told him. **The second kind of attendance:** the Office has never in nine years attended anybody off an analyst's sheet, and now it has, and **three buff squares are on the table with Nell Ardery, Garrick Neve and the yard checker on them, dated the nineteenth**, and the fourth box on each is empty. **The irreversible decision:** the acknowledgement reads that the three episodes *were caused at his hand* and are his cost and not evidence against the persons in whom they occurred. Drant refuses to argue and states the price: **it is what his brother did** — a thing said out loud in a room is an instrument, and an institution handed an instrument has to do something with it — and the first document in the building saying a man did a thing to three people on purpose is now in his handwriting and cannot be unsaid. Unsigned, three people are asked what they felt by a man with a pen, and a woman who has had four in five days gives a bad answer, and the bad answer is the only version of that afternoon in the city. He signs. **Runa's warning:** he has made himself a thing that must be decided again, and the last person in Ostara to describe him is now a clerk with a rubber stamp. Ends on the file widening in both directions: a map with three names goes up on the twentieth, and the woman in the dark coat with no paper at all is being asked the same question by the same office.

### Ch 70 — The Man Who Filed
The 21st, the schedule's fifth date. **The signature created a referable thing** — an instrument with no box cannot be closed on this floor — and two things left the office in an envelope on the twentieth. Everything that leaves the floor is registered in a thin cloth-bound book on the attendance desk, and Drant turns it round because he is entitled to show a man where his own document went. The outgoing line carries the receiving house, **Veil-Research**, and a name printed on the form by the house so that the reply need not be looked for: **PERRIN SOWE, RESPONSIBLE.** He says the name before he has decided to; Drant then closes the door and tells him what it is — **a man who says a name out loud in a room, in four seconds, unasked, has given the office an instrument, and he is the reason.** He files nothing and tells nobody and enters a note that he was not asked. **Perrin Sowe is on the page as a consult line and a respondent's name and is in no room.** Up the iron stair, **Sun Meiling says it first**, because he came in with the face of a man being told something: Sowe has an office on the third floor with a window on the yard, has been there nine years, and has come down her corridor **once in three years**, and she took that for a man being busy. **What went back three years ago was not her four lines.** Her initials are on the transmission because a junior drafts and a responsible officer answers, and the four lines underneath them are his, and she has not read them and will not. She also reminds him that she has had a branch strip in her hand since the fourth — the corridor beat where a filing clerk on her own floor took it off a colleague, looked at it, gave it back and said nothing, and none of the four other people in that corridor looked up is **Ch 63, where she first tells it**, and it is referred back to here and not retold. **And then the door that does not open:** she offers him one true question, any question, and the only one he wants is whether that man knows where his brother is, and the price is a brother's name said out loud in a room with a woman in it, so he asks her instead about the rota, and gives her a day of the week. She keeps it, does not spend it, and tells him that not asking is a habit and a habit is not a fault and is also not a shield.

## Volume 1 — Rank Four

### Ch 1 — The Second Basin
Lin Hao's Wednesday plate comes back **3,412 grains with no interval** — a figure with no rate behind it, so no band, so no publication on Thursday. He takes it to the counter clerk, Faber, instead of walking out, and Faber calls it *unsettled*, wraps the plate in oilcloth, and puts him on the Thursday review list, which is how he ends up in a forty-name queue that goes up to the forty-first office. Behind the last counter he finally reads a door he has walked past for four years: **CLASSIFICATION WING — INTAKE AND SUB-LEVEL ACCESS.** Continuity: the counter shuts at eleven and an unpublished plate reads as nothing at all; six washrooms on the second floor and no window anywhere on it; his two-year blank in the record predates this posting. Open: what is on a Thursday list and who reads it.

### Ch 2 — No Interval
He reads the public band table and understands for the first time that **six is the first rank with a verb in it** — sealed filings open — so the want stops being a boy's arithmetic about a door and becomes a number that tells him what to do next. At the Correction Office petition rail he finds Petition 4118: Ding D. Duarte, rank two, auditor, refused, *no instrument of record*, act not scored under any available heading. A messenger gives him the floor phrase — *taken for a reading* — and a counter clerk, whom he deliberately lets see him ask, tells him they keep you until the arithmetic closes and to take a book. At the end of the corridor a rank-two auditor in grey is leaning against the wall where she has plainly been for some time, and she says his name. Continuity: Faber is settled and untouchable; **eleven unsettled readings a year citywide, nine audited by Ines** — the one figure in the batch, quoted identically in Ch 1, 7 and 8. Open: how a woman forty-one offices up has his name.

### Ch 3 — Nineteen Grains

chained it twice and told nobody.

### Ch 35 — A Clean Report
A Wing clerk brings the quarterly re-read **eleven days early**, the 18th, and the re-read is deferred to
the 29th by the provisional band with a receipt. Runa's Cordwain report goes in at two and comes back
stamped and **entirely accurate and not about him**; in the margin she records that the premises were
executed against because an address on the observed arc required a subject, and that this is a defect in
the method. A supervisor countersigned it **noted**. She tells him the margin is her, buying room. The
order is served on the Fourth Branch at twenty past six for the employer attestation, and the attestation
box is signed by **Tobias Renn**, who is off the register and was found because he knows where the 3rd is.
He wants it written that it is not a favour, in eight seconds, with a courier's receipt downstairs with his
name in it.

### Ch 36 — Requisitioned Rooms
**Scene.** The execution happens at ten past. Eleven warders, eleven minutes, a district clerk reading a
number that a tenant says is not the address. The back stair goes at twenty past and Runa Sato is under
it; she carries weight and the stair did not move. He had his forearm in to the wrist and had Halvard
Rees by the collar; the warders' tally has him out of the passage at six with no time back in. In the
flat at number 6 she tells him what the falsification costs her and writes it anyway — **failure
unforecast, officer alone in the passage, her hand twice on the form**, false in four places including him.
The scene is adults, awake, unrestrained, unmedicated, one arm each, and four true statements first; she
puts her weight on him and says so, and the chapter says the weight is the only part of herself she has
handed to anybody, and that the injury constrains them without causing anything. The whole day is at
**number 6** — she is moved up one floor at four o'clock when the warders want the ground floor back, and
the report is written there at nine. He decides, alone on a landing at seven in the morning, that there is
not another side: he writes a four-page statement putting his own name under the stair, the forced door,
the book, and every dwelling that comes off that row.

### Ch 37 — The Provisional Band
He writes and files the statement at twenty to six on the 19th. At the Exchange counter Garrick Neve puts
the **first of the two question families** — *where does the movement come from* — and he answers in front
of forty people: *a man who cleans washrooms is standing in a place where strangers hold doors for each
other four times a day. I did not do any of it. I stood in it.* The Board prints **PROVISIONAL SIX.
STRUCK ON A REQUISITION OF PROPERTY. CORDWAIN. 19TH.** with floor 8,141 and tolerance three. **He was not
six because he earned six** — the machine found a rank-six act and attached it to the only account in
Ostara with a declared reading on it. Rank six carries warrants, requisition of property and **compelled
testimony**, and the correct use of it would be against him. He tries to petition Lin Wei's file and
cannot: four thousand one hundred and twelve folios, and three grains.
[Provisional band struck. Six, pending quarterly re-read. Floor 8,141, tolerance three grains.]

### Ch 38 — Five Cold Minutes
He goes and finds all five turned lines in one night, and finds Halvard Rees first because he is the
control — the row emptied at ten past six and he is sitting on a wall in twenty degrees, and the climb was
done forty times and **not one of them came off him**. **Nadia** will not be warned: *do not spend me, I am
the one thing in this quarter that goes up and I am not going to be turned into a room.* **Ola** has left
twenty-two new entries and one underlined line — *the Vestry, week beginning the 18th. Nobody has asked me
to do this* — and he has made a clerk. **Teodor** will not come out of the counting house. **Tessa** is in
the cold store with the stippling above her left elbow and pale blue where it crossed, six episodes since
the third, none of them hers, a **twelfth clipboard** dated today, and one request: once a month, say the
number out loud to her. She will not thank him and says so first. **Emrys** has 441 and can only go down
and has told nobody. The cold minute goes out across an open door to **Ines**, and it is the third since
June, which is the line at which the Bureau stops believing coincidence. Her month on the rota name is up.
She puts it on the table: **Emrys Vaz, yard rota, six in the morning**, and she will write it tonight if he
says the word, and she will not say it first.

### Ch 39 — What the Board Does Not Say
Ines counts what the register is not. Nine years, about eleven unsettled readings a year, nine of the
eleven to her — **a hundred against thirty-one**, so the register is a catch and not a census, and it
records what this city *catches*: the ones entered are the ones somebody looked at, and the 31st is his and
is dated the 3rd, the day he stood up. In Runa's cupboard: the Field and the Wing do not share systems, so
the register was never withheld from anyone. Runa wants the declaration **written down and dated** with the
five names on it — a cheap weapon, and she cannot write the clean version herself. Ines blocks it and turns
on Lin Hao, and there is the four seconds where the negotiation belongs to him. He refuses Runa's document
and dictates his own: five names struck by declaration and not by evidence, **Emrys Vaz is not settled, his
plate reads 441, I knew it on the day and I said nothing.** It goes into the front of Runa's file. It puts
five names back on the branch board and the division will ask for the audit, and they will be right to.
Ines's price is not a speech — she carries a name up a corridor because a drawer is not a place to keep a
person.

### Ch 40 — The Winter Queue
The branch board has five names on it again with a gap where the reason should be, and **Tobias Renn** is
the third one down. The Exchange publishes the **Winter Review candidates: rank six**, twenty-two names,
ordered by published standing, and he is the last, and the word **PROVISIONAL** on his line is the only
reason he is on the sheet. Two numbers, and they do not mean the same thing: **three grains is how close
he is to losing it; 11,859 is how far he is from having it** (band six opens at 20,000, published standing
8,141, a witnessed deed scores 400, so twenty-nine deeds in six weeks, and he has nineteen people and no
twentieth). The sheet's small print, up since the 24th: **a provisional band is not carried into review.**
Runa: the document he filed on the 23rd is why a Reading Office query is open on his standing, and a query
wants an instrument he has not got. The clerk who entered his reading on the 3rd says she cannot invent him
one. Ends on the corridor board: **EMRYS VAZ, four hundred and forty-one, the next reading the 29th.**
[Winter Review queue published. Provisional band is not carried into review. Band six opens at 20,000. Published standing 8,141.]

### Ch 41 — Zero
26th. Published queue last of twenty-two, provisional the only reason he is on it. Yard gate shows no twentieth person; Vestry shows the five turned are the only named hands. Zero to spend. Ends on the Board repainting Thursday with the only instrument he holds being his own declaration.

### Ch 42 — What a Sealed File Asks
**Thursday the 22nd, four days behind Ch 41.** Box, first standing. Weekly question from the index: what
instrument. Answer: requisition of the 19th and declaration of the 23rd, no other. Third question new: what
a sealed file asks — rank held, deposit, form with folio count. Neve: the cheaper price is standing here,
the dearer one is the date at the top of the band line, seven days. Query owned by the clerk of the 3rd,
who cannot invent one; Runa makes him swear to stand the box every Thursday until the Review. Ends wanting
one true paper with another hand on it.

### Ch 43 — The Longest Walk
28th. Walks Branch to Cordwain and back looking for a twentieth face. Crossing full of the forty-one. Nell
Ardery confirms no new face on the arc; Halvard confirms not one of forty came off him. List written:
forty-one lines, five turned. Ends with grief for the trap: the only people he can relieve are the people
relief would mark.

### Ch 44 — An Empty Account
29th, Thursday. Box second standing, then re-read: 8,140, one down, one inside tolerance, band holds,
two grains of margin left. Query separate and still open; the Review will ask for an instrument in the
sixth week. Beatrix: account empty in the one respect that matters. Ines: paper discharged, rota name said,
word ungiven, May clipboard still unasked. Nadia: do not spend me. Ends with three stamped slips, none an
instrument.

### Ch 45 — Nine Seats
First Thursday after the re-read. Box, third standing: 8,139, two down, band holds. Marguerite terms
return: same line, one in four for five years, no road, plus **a date a week ahead**, a room in Bellhouse,
and nine seats with the house in the public seats. Runa: not an instrument. Ines: an evening is not an
instrument. He goes for the worse reason — leverage, not money.

### Ch 46 — The Best Rooms in Ostara
Scene. Bellhouse top rooms. Terms read aloud. No road. Evening asked for herself, answered yes before
anything starts. Scene on the page, unhurried, mutual, his tender arm respected. Afterwards he refuses the
line again for appetite rather than principle. She keeps the terms down, signed or unsigned, and will sit
the Review.

### Ch 47 — The Review
Thursday in the sixth week. Box fourth standing first: **8,138, three down, three inside tolerance**, band
holds through the day on the edge of it. Review room nine chairs, twenty-two called nine by nine; four
grains down lapses a man who held five for six years, which is the proof of the three-grain tolerance.
Query puts him in the room — Runa says so plainly: *without the query you would not be in it at all.*
Panel: provisional not carried, standing is a five. Ines puts the May clipboard on the table in the
corridor ("I have the date and the figure and I have not asked") and **neither of them touches it**.
Open-question slot offered — one question put on the floor, public, no notice. He takes it.

### Ch 48 — One Grain of Itself
Takes the slot. Question: what is the figure made of. **He spends the account on himself, once, first time
in the book**, and the answer is true: doors, trays, basket 400, door 200, aggregate 3,529, shifts wherever
he stands — *I did none of it, I stood in it.* The machine reads the figure against the **band line**
instead of the floor. Panel: **a candidate is seated on published standing unless the band was struck on an
instrument** — the requisition of the 19th is on the table, and a true answer under the open-question rule is
a finding, and a finding is an instrument. **Rank six, held, not provisional, seated on an instrument of that
room. No grain was scored and the plate still reads 8,138.** Cost, all four on the page: a line reading
himself, three new turned (Nell Ardery, Garrick Neve, yard checker), the cab promise to Beatrix broken and
unmendable, and the option of being invisible closed forever. **Ninety-one lines, eight turned.**
[Ledger. Ninety-one lines. Eight turned. One line reads himself.]
Ola reads the finding aloud to twenty people from the landing rail. Tessa charts all three new colds herself
and sits looking at her own arms. Ines stands in the back row with her face in her hands.

### Ch 49 — Four Thousand One Hundred and Twelve
Rank six held; the corridor board's five names gone **by instrument instead of by declaration**. Finding
carried to the Reading Office window. **Query closed — a witnessed answer in a room is an instrument.**
Deposits the form with the folio count (4,112) as the deposit for a sealed file. Index promises a trolley
by ten. Beatrix names the broken cab promise without heat and says the price of it is his to carry, not
hers to forgive in a speech. Nadia says do not spend her and rings the bell up one floor. **Ines: "I am not
filing it. I cannot decide whether to, which is not the same as deciding not to."** The rota word remains
ungiven by decision. Ends wanting one folio by ten.

### Ch 50 — Held Account One
Side room ten o'clock. Trolley, guard, seals checked twice, docket opened folio by folio, copy made at the
side press and logged in the docket book with the date and his band number. **4,112 folios, 4,111 sealed,
one open at the back by Wing order, never called.** Open folio: transfer receipt in Lin Wei's hand,
214,000 grains on the eleventh three years ago into an account the index does not contain, signed Lin Wei;
under it in Wing print: the file of the receiver is Held Account One. **There is no Held Account Zero** —
the clerk asked the index that morning and got the answer the same day. He reads both lines three times
standing up with his hands flat on either side of the paper because if he touched it he would crease it.
Copy slip pocketed with four counterfoils. The clerk who entered his reading on the 3rd closed her query
on a finding rather than a lapse.
Ines in the corridor, **an auditor may stand in a corridor and no further**, the whole hour, folder closed.
She puts it in one sentence: *he said the number was wrong in public and what he did was give it away where
no ledger could follow it. And you are the address.* Then the fact that no Bureau has a shape for: *I do not
know what a Bureau does with a line it has never had to read. Nobody does.*
Final image: he walks the long road home, forty-one minutes, and cleans the last row of basins with the
filed petition drying beside the soap, **turning it face down halfway through because a washroom counter is
no place to leave a brother's hand uncovered.** Runa closes the Field file. Marguerite writes: the terms
stay down. The Board is nine streets away with his name on a tile for the first time since he was a child
and he does not go and look at it. **Three people felt something cold that night and none of them knew
why** — Nell Ardery, Garrick Neve, the checker. Ola copies the finding under the re-read; Beatrix stands in
front of the rail without speaking; Tessa at seventeen boards, eighteen before nightfall; Nadia holds her
car at the terminus past midnight with nobody telling her to.
**Nobody has thanked him.**
