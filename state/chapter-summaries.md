### Volume 8, Batch 0001 — Chapters 351–360. THE FIRST TO THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF JANUARY. **WRITTEN.**

**THE BATCH'S JOB:** to take a man who cannot be requisitioned and make him the holder of the account, in his
own hand, in front of the eight women whose public posts the declaration carries — and, underneath it, to have
a woman who has decided to speak choose to say it to somebody and leave him unable to ask. **THE IRREVERSIBLE
DECISION IS 356 AND 357 TOGETHER AND IT CANNOT BE UNDONE.**

1. **351 — *The Line Called Twice* (2,167 words). The first of January, ten to nine, the counting room two
   floors under the Exchange.** *Called* goes into the chained real book a second time — word, rule, second
   date, pen on a chain round a wrist, time entered at twenty to nine. **A valuation nobody stands at is not a
   valuation, which is the only reason a man with no standing may be in the room at all, and he is in breach of
   a written instruction from the keeper while he is there.** **Hanneke Ruis tears a slip off a plain pad with
   a ruler, writes on it in a hand deliberately not her own, does not file it, says it once and will not say it
   twice: a line called once waits at a valuation until it is paid or produced, and a line called twice may be
   carried forward by the room that holds the book without a fresh sheet being drafted at all.** No line at
   that address has ever been called twice. **She refuses to be thanked, because a keeper who has been thanked
   by a man is a keeper who has been useful to a man and there is a man with a folder in four years.** **He has
   been in that room a hundred and four days.** He is safer than he has ever been and finds out that the reason
   is a clerk's convenience. **Nothing can be paid at seven and nothing can be produced off that door, and it
   is called on the first of February and the first of March until one of those two things happens.**
2. **352 — *The Week She Kept To Herself* (2,240). The third of January, the evening, the kitchen.** Wren Kallo
   asked for a week off the table and managed four. **She has chosen and will not say whom; he comes within a
   second of asking — *what question*, by reflex — and does not, and eight women watch him not ask, and nobody
   thanks him for it.** **She gives the shape and withholds the woman:** *they asked me a question I could only
   answer by putting a name on it, and I did not put a name on it.* She is angry at him for being both the
   reason she is frightened and the reason she cannot say so; Isolde Marchetti says that out loud at the table
   and adds that both facts are true at once and that he is going to say *it is* to everything for an hour.
   **She says she has begun to believe she told the wrong people**, because the office cannot reach the thing
   the shape is about, which is a woman on a list, and can only reach the building she is in. **The one
   sentence he is given about the office: a division that does not sit in the Wing, keeps no list of addresses,
   and was sent a strip about it in October.** He thinks, once, alone, in one line and no further, about what
   a division in this city would want an author for.
3. **353 — *The Plate Held By A Formula* (1,734). The fifth of January, a quarter to five, the north wall.**
   8,145 on the nose against a floor of 8,145, and **the wall silent for five days, which is the first week.**
   Beatrix Halm has nothing under the time for the first time since the card. **The arithmetic is hers:
   attendants score in aggregate to the whole staff and the split has never been published, and a man on the
   floor of his band on the strength of a formula he has never seen is the most anonymous figure available to
   him. IT IS NOT WHAT PUT HIM ON THE FLOOR — eight witness hands on the twenty-ninth are, and the chapter says
   so out loud.** **He does not want it: it is the first invisibility in eight volumes he did not build.** He
   cannot tell the kitchen, and Beatrix gives him why — one of the eight would be pleased for him and seven
   would hear *I have been frightened for four months and there is now a reason*, and this house has never once
   done that for anybody. **The hook is Beatrix's: it is the first thing in seven years he built that he cannot
   take back.**
4. **354 — *Six Of The Eight Have A Post* (2,245). The eighth of January, the middle of the morning, the Field
   counter at Cordwain.** Runa Sato will not come out to him and comes out to the counter, because that is what
   the counter is for. **Six of the eight hold a post a division can serve a paper at — Field ·
   counter-forensics, which holds the description and therefore serves anything laid against those premises · the
   Vestry · the guild · the Church · Veil-Research — five of the six inside the machine a requisition would
   travel through, and the sixth answering a departmental consult about a transfer that carried no name.**
   **He worked the count out on the twenty-eighth of October and told nobody for seventy days, which is ten
   weeks, in a kitchen with six of the people he has just read out.** She tells him why he did not: the answer
   was the count and the count is the size of the box. Her standing order is stated once, flatly, and **is not
   converted into a debt and is never invoited.** **The hook: a seventh is a tram office and not a division,
   and the eighth is Isolde Marchetti, and her posting is not in any division in this city at all, because what
   a woman at the Bellhouse has is a room that agrees to what she says in it — and nothing can be served on it,
   no description can carry it, and no clerk could write down what she does on an ordinary night.**
5. **355 — *The Thing He Is Going To Write* (1,768). The tenth of January, late, the kitchen.** **He opens by
   telling them he counted them at a counter and said nothing for ten weeks, and that they let him get away with
   it, and that this is why the evening got as far as it did.** He says the sentence he could not get out in the
   right order on the twenty-sixth of December: **the tick is against the held-by line and it is the only tick
   on the whole of the four parts, and the only person alive who can put anything into that box is the man it
   is about.** Then the one that makes it a decision: **a requisition cannot be laid at all without that box
   filled; a filled box is the only instrument in this business that does not care whether five people want
   anything; he has just built that room a door and he is the man who has to walk through it.**
   **Eight women will not help him, for the reason they did not help him on the twenty-sixth of November: any
   hand that helped would be a hand that had to be on it.** Isolde says why in the least noble way available and
   is right; **Nadia Ferrous says the true thing about an empty box — that it does not carry anything on its own —
   once, and is right, and cuts the bread.** Runa gives the arithmetic; Petra says what a witness line costs
   whoever signs; Beatrix writes the time and nothing under it; Agnete says she wanted to help and will not.
   **The hook: Sun Meiling's second question, *who signs it*. *Me.* *I know, and I am not asking about the
   paper.* And the chapter stops.**
6. **356 — *The Fifth Sheet Of Plain Paper* (1,733). The twelfth of January, from eight in the morning.** One
   sheet, bought alone two days before, forty minutes, his own hand: the head, *this is a declaration of
   holding*, the account and its figure, that it has not been paid and cannot be produced, his name and the
   date. **The first four described him; this one declares him the holding. And there is no reason clause,
   because a clerk reads a reason clause in four seconds and a whole page in a year.** Nobody helped him and
   nobody argued. **Two of the eight women cry and the chapter does not say which two and does not soften it
   and he does not know afterwards which one went to the window and has never asked.** He draws the line the
   chapter is about: **the price was the twenty-ninth of December and is gone; this is the bill; the bill has
   his name on it and the price had eight other women's.** **At the foot of the sheet, where the first four had
   eight words, he writes NOTHING — *a declaration is not an attestation and there is nowhere on it for a man to
   say he will answer for it* — because writing them would have made it an attestation, eight people would have
   signed under it, and nothing would have happened since the twenty-ninth.**
7. **357 — *Eight Hands Under A Fifth Page* (1,890). The fourteenth of January, the kitchen and then the room.**
   Read round his own table at half past nine, one thing back each — *it is short* · *that is a figure I have
   seen on nine pieces of paper and never once beside a name* · Agnete's hand flat on the paper for a second ·
   *there is nothing on that page I could use* · eight times written down by Beatrix · and from Wren Kallo,
   who tells him exactly what counter-forensics is going to do with it and why. Laid in at five minutes to
   eleven. **The chair does not repeat the twenty-ninth of December's sentence and names what is on the table:
   the room has held nine addresses and about four hundred figures and this morning it is holding a man.**
   **The gap in the witness line is where it was, two fingers wide, and he says out loud that it has never been
   his eight — it is a member of the room, that member has been empty since the first of November, and a line
   in a book with a gap in it is a thing a clerk copies out every quarter for four years.** **Sun Meiling
   prices the instrument in one sentence and refuses to soften it or explain it afterwards, because explained
   afterwards it sounds like a warning and it is a bill. **Her figures are EIGHT — the eight of them who signed
   the line under his page that morning, who will all be asked about it in four years — and she names herself
   as the one of the eight that nothing in the room can cover, because she cannot be sworn, and she says she
   signed at four minutes to eleven without being asked and without discussing it.** THE COST: **the declaration goes to the desk of anybody at
   that address who holds a post and there is no discretion in it anywhere and nobody has ever been asked to
   invent any.** Beatrix on the top step: the plate has not moved all week, the wall has not said anything, and
   in about five days a copy goes out at four to eight addresses in this building and six in divisions, one of
   them a wing off a court he could not find again with a map, where the woman who is line one on his page
   works.
8. **358 — *The Room With The Wall Of Sheets* (3,998; scene body **2,929 words, counted AND probed**). The
   sixteenth of January, about eight in the evening.** Off a third court, up the iron stair on the outside of the
   lamps, **eight doors shut and no light behind any of them**, and a side room with two hundred sheets on a
   wall that was bare for three days in the autumn of the year before last and has been covered ever since.
   **Three terms and not four, written on the back of a departmental consult docket, and the third is not a
   term** — nothing is spent and there is nothing in the building he can move · her gift is not used tonight on
   a record or a person or him · and the third, which is not a term. **A kettle and two cups.**
   **She names the price before anything else happens in the room**, because she has spent three years arranging
   to be the one who says things: the copy was served at her own desk at about nine on the fifteenth, and a
   thing served on you at your own desk is a thing you cannot say you did not receive, **and she is the only
   one of the eight who cannot be sworn, and therefore the only one who cannot be covered by anything.**
   **THE HINGE: she pulls the drawer out from under the bench for the third time in front of him, opens it for
   the first time in three years, and reads her OWN four lines out loud — a departmental draft about a transfer
   that carried no name, three years old, folded in three, grey at the folds, her initials in the margin against
   the fourth — and then refuses to say one word about what is in them, in a summary or in an order.** She read
   them because a copy of his page came to her desk at nine that morning with her name on the first line of it,

    the fact that the answer went back through her house three years ago signed by a man on the third floor who

    none: Lin Wei is LIN HAO'S brother and was her research partner, and any later batch that puts a brother in
    her mouth is a canon error and the line comes out.** **The scene is written out: no
   compulsion, no sedation, no exhaustion, no restraint, no psychic contact, no trance, no seal, her gift

**347 — The Council Minute In Four Hands.** The twenty-sixth. **The minute goes out on the ordinary stream at four in the afternoon, attested in four hands:** the chair, Alma Quayle as a witness and not as a seal, the clerk, and the woman who withdrew her seal. **It is the first document in this book with his name in it that he did not write and cannot take back**, and he is served with it at the gate the same evening and reads it at the top of the sixty-one with the light off. **Four hands is worth more than a song, and a song is on every street in six of them.** Runa Sato gives him the last arithmetic of the quarter: a requisition lies in the quarter it is laid, a void sheet is not a sheet in the next one, and after the twenty-third there are three people left who would seal a fresh one — **so a filled box may not be enough, which is the reason to fill it anyway.** At the table he says he intends to write it down and **cannot get the sentence out in the right order, and eight women watch a man fail at an ordinary sentence and none of them helps him**, because helping him would be the same as writing it for him.

**348 — The Thing He Signed Instead. THE BATCH'S IRREVERSIBLE DECISION.** The twenty-ninth. **He writes four sheets of plain paper at his own kitchen table from about eight in the morning, in his own hand, with eight women watching, and nobody helps him because a hand that helped him write it would be a hand that had to be on it.** The address and the date he took it and the fact that he asked for a description against the head rather than against the door; the account; the composition; **the eight in the order of the stair with the room against each line**, which takes about four minutes and gets no word from the table. **Sun Meiling makes him write a reason clause, because a clerk will read a reason clause in four seconds and a whole page in a year, and he writes eight words at the foot of the fourth sheet: *I am the man at the top of this page and I will answer for it.*** **Isolde Marchetti says it is not her reason and she is not asking for it, and that this morning is the first morning anybody in this room has given anything back to the person who made a reason out of a tune.** At five minutes to eleven he lays four sheets on a table under a window in front of the room and signs at the foot of the last one in front of nine people. **Eight people sign a witness line. There are nine seats and there is a gap in the line, two fingers wide, where the fourth seat is.** The chair names what has happened: *this is a person.* **Sun Meiling names the price: eight people carry his name in a book of their own for four years and one of them has said nothing about it to anybody.** At four in the afternoon **a copy goes out to eight addresses, all inside one building on Ferrier Street, and one of them is the second floor he is standing under, and he is barred from asking her one question about it for the rest of his life.**

**349 — The Card Comes Down At Six.** The thirtieth, early morning. He is in the square at half past four with Beatrix Halm. **The quarterly card goes up at six, on different paper, in a different hand, from a boy with a paste brush and a list of numbers.** **The floor of the quarter is 8,145, up one, and it does not move again until the thirtieth of March. And against premises 34-6-118 the card prints 8,145 — on the nose, which is the floor.** The plate read 8,131 since the twenty-ninth of November and did not move once in December, because a plate is a figure for a week and moves when somebody else writes a deed down. **Eight hands carried him fourteen points in one day to the line of it and not one grain past.** **The ranks wall says no word about him.** Beatrix: *not because you are liked; a man standing on the floor is a man in band, and a wall in this city does not put two words on a man who is in band. It goes back up the day you go under.* **Fourteen weeks and six days off that wall and five weeks and three days back on it, and this city has spent the whole of its memory on the five weeks.** **He does not go to Lantern Row at twenty past six with the good news, because she told him on the thirtieth of November what she will not be.**

**350 — The Thing She Cannot Keep. THE SCENE. Wren Kallo. VOLUME SEVEN'S LAST CHAPTER.** The thirtieth at night, her two rooms on the second floor of premises 34-6-118. **Five things on a sheet in a clerk's hand with the pen capped across it:** nothing is spent; she is not impaired and will not be made so, and there is no drink in the room; **her gift is not used, not once, not on a record and not on a person and not on him**; there is nothing in that room holding either of them, not the gift, not the term of the ninth of July, not a document; **and five, which is that she is not there because he is in trouble, and he has to say it out loud.** Scene body **1,626 words, written out, unhurried once it begins, no compulsion, no sedation, no psychic contact, no seal, no violence, her gift used on nobody.** She undresses first and in order and gets a button wrong on purpose being generous about it; she makes him describe her body twice; then the hour. **Afterwards she tells him what she cannot keep: two documents about the same eight women at the same door made inside a month by the same pair of hands**, in one of which she is a Bureau officer certifying a composition at a distance and in the other the fourth line of a list of persons at her own address, and the second was made to fill a hole the first one left open. **There is not another person alive who can see that shape and there is not another person alive who is forbidden to hear about it, and he may not ask and she will say she does not know what he is talking about.** **He does not ask.** She says she is going to have to tell somebody, has not chosen anybody, and has had since a quarter past six that morning and a card on a wall. He gives her one sentence that is any use at all. **Then she goes down the sixty-one without a sound and out of a door at twenty to two in the morning of the last night of the year, and the wall behind her says nothing at all about anybody, and she does not know yet who she is going to tell.**
