## Volume 1 — Rank Four

### Ch 1 — The Second Basin
Lin Hao's Wednesday plate comes back **3,412 grains with no interval** — a figure with no rate behind it, so no band, so no publication on Thursday. He takes it to the counter clerk, Faber, instead of walking out, and Faber calls it *unsettled*, wraps the plate in oilcloth, and puts him on the Thursday review list, which is how he ends up in a forty-name queue that goes up to the forty-first office. Behind the last counter he finally reads a door he has walked past for four years: **CLASSIFICATION WING — INTAKE AND SUB-LEVEL ACCESS.** Continuity: the counter shuts at eleven and an unpublished plate reads as nothing at all; six washrooms on the second floor and no window anywhere on it; his two-year blank in the record predates this posting. Open: what is on a Thursday list and who reads it.

### Ch 2 — No Interval
He reads the public band table and understands for the first time that **six is the first rank with a verb in it** — sealed filings open — so the want stops being a boy's arithmetic about a door and becomes a number that tells him what to do next. At the Correction Office petition rail he finds Petition 4118: Ding D. Duarte, rank two, auditor, refused, *no instrument of record*, act not scored under any available heading. A messenger gives him the floor phrase — *taken for a reading* — and a counter clerk, whom he deliberately lets see him ask, tells him they keep you until the arithmetic closes and to take a book. At the end of the corridor a rank-two auditor in grey is leaning against the wall where she has plainly been for some time, and she says his name. Continuity: Faber is settled and untouchable; **eleven unsettled readings a year citywide, nine audited by Ines** — the one figure in the batch, quoted identically in Ch 1, 7 and 8. Open: how a woman forty-one offices up has his name.

### Ch 3 — Nineteen Grains
On the Ferrier tram his hand is on a stranger's wrist before he decides to put it there. Something cold and heavy runs out of him into **Halvard Rees**, a wire-splicer from the yards, and a four-year-old pylon injury goes quiet. **In the same instant the cold crosses back** — three seconds of it, up through the wrist and into the old place, visible from the outside in the man's shoulders and breath — and Halvard attributes it to a draught off the junction, as any sane man would. In the concourse under the Reading his wrapped plate reads 3,393 — nineteen grains lower — and the notation arrives.
[Ledger. Three lines.]
Continuity: the draw is relief, not strength; Halvard is finished, not well. **He does not thank him** — he refuses to, out loud, in front of Lin Hao, and warns him against doing it twice. That refusal is the first turned line, made in nine seconds, by accident. Two of the three lines belong to people he has never met. Open: what the other two are.

### Ch 4 — Cold Minutes
Two days of touching nobody cost him the ordinary ability to stand in a corridor with people in it. The Thursday review is nineteen minutes and nine questions with a tired auditor; nothing is wrong with him that is anybody's business, and of the forty names on the list nine go down. On the landing outside, two auditors discuss a woman in a Vestry lift who has been logging cold spells for four months — **the third one is different, because a pattern gets an address.** On the Friday his figure has risen by 69 while he spent nothing: accrual is proximity, not contact. Exhausted and sleepless he takes the Vestry lift and the operator **Nadia Ferrous** catches his wrist as the car lurches; a cold minute goes into her hand and she laughs, and says *that's the third one this week*.
[Ledger. Nine lines. One turned.]
He makes a rule he admits is cowardice, and it lasts seven days. Continuity: **the grey at his left temple starts here**, after two days of not sleeping, and stays. It does not exist before this chapter.

### Ch 5 — The Junior's Return
**Tobias Renn**, junior filing clerk, rank two, four years in the filing room, tells him at length and with pleasure that the draw-down opens Monday the nineteenth, that ninety-one names get re-read and nine go down, that the *files* are what matter, and — reading from the card — that Lin Hao's second line reads **COMMON ORIGIN. SOURCE UNPLACED.** Tobias is a non-feeder: Lin Hao takes hold of the same requisition slip at the same moment, nothing moves, nothing is given, and he is grey at the temple before he reaches the second floor. Supply room three is closed pending audit, the key is with Colm Deveraux, and **Emrys Vaz**, seventeen, carries stock up from the yard at six. His want, for the first time, has a face on it that is not his own. Continuity: **Tobias's file goes in Chapter 23 on his own arithmetic.** Bernt Halse on the fourth floor gets one paragraph here and has no idea what is coming.

### Ch 6 — Supply Room Three
Withdrawn from the rota, Colm Deveraux tells him there is no form that opens a store in a draw-down week and refuses to be moved. **At ten past ten on the Thursday night** Lin Hao puts a hand on his chest to shove a door — and the wrong thing goes in: not relief but **account**. Colm's Exchange figure jumps a thousand and one grains, his rate does not move, and he goes up a back stair and locks his office and will not see him again. **Forty minutes later**, at eleven, the Vestry supper run comes up; he takes four trays off the rail and **Ola Bright** catches his arm on the step, and a cold minute goes out of him, and she shakes her hand out and laughs about it before she has let go. Stock moves at six. Four sheets of penciled diary are found under a saucer on the landing rail — eleven entries from the fourth of June, one word, *again* underlined twice.
[Ledger. Fourteen lines. Two turned.]

### Ch 7 — Lantern Row
He goes to Lantern Row because he cannot think. **Ines Duarte**, rank two, thirty-four, two rooms she cannot afford, four hundred files, will not take her coat off. His file is four pages and a bone-coloured card: four years of employment, a mop, a two-year gap where he does not appear at all, and at the bottom of the gap a cross-reference — **LIN WEI. R.6. SEE ALSO — READING OFFICE TRANSFERS, CLASS.** The want moves from nine to six. The bigger fact is the one she cannot stop thinking about: in three weeks not one instrument has touched that file, and somebody has gone to the trouble of keeping it clean for years. She asks *who helped you*. He does not answer; he tells her what Petition 4118 says about her sister instead. She lets him, and does not leave. Continuity: he has one felt want on the page — the ninety-one — and it is what drives him into the room.

### Ch 8 — Afterward, Ines
He tells her nineteen grains. She assembles the whole thing in under a minute and gives him the rule: rank six petitions a sealed filing. She tells him about Ding and the long stair and the 300 grains she has been carrying for four years. He says *I've got some of that* and tells her the cost before she decides. She takes her coat off and hangs it where anyone coming in will see it, and says **yes, do it, now, before I think about it.** The relief goes into her; she shakes from the heels up and says her sister's name into a pillow.

Then the scene, written at length — explicit, unhurried, and entirely hers, including an instruction she gives him *mid-scene* that the giving and the wanting are two different transactions and she is not going to let him confuse them. Afterwards she says the sentence he has believed without checking for three years: you have never once in your life been the thing that happened to somebody. Then, with her head on his chest, she feels the ledger — nine of the fourteen — reads off nine names she can identify, finds that not one of the nine is on the Thursday list, and writes a tenth in pencil on a clean page. The name is **Ola Bright**: not on his file, not on hers, not in any list in the city.

### Ch 9 — A Cold Minute, Recorded
The four pages go on the table and Ines reads them in the order the cold happened. The argument: he will go alone, she will not let him, and she is right — she is the only person who can find where a person with no reading sleeps. The count is fourteen; he has deliberately touched three; **eleven lines belong to people he has never touched.** At four in the morning she says *somebody has to* has just been proved twice — once by her sister and once by a woman on a bench with a pencil — and refuses to be sent back to her room. They come down into the Vestry arcade at ten to five into a shelter hall of thirty people who have been waiting since six the previous evening, and on the end of the front bench is a woman in a woollen hat with a school exercise book open on her knees.

### Ch 10 — No One Keeps the Diary
Ola Bright is delighted, not frightened, and hands over forty-one pages. Nadia has been telling the whole arcade, including a man from the Church of the Quiet Number. Eleven episodes, never four in a week. Ines explains that the diary is the only record the cold leaves and that the last tenth of the distance to interesting is a room with no window. **Bernt Halse**, 51, fourth-floor stock clerk, went down at ten past ten on the Thursday **in the morning** — the same clock time, twelve hours earlier, as Lin Hao putting a hand on Colm Deveraux, which is the sentence he cannot stop trying to join up — and has not come up; his sister **Ditta** has been on the Vestry benches since six on Friday, twice sleeping there. **Hester Quill** at the Classification Wing's public counter explains, kindly, that nobody is taken for a reading for anything they did, that the person stays until the book closes, and that her job is to make sure the name was on a form. He asks Tessa Okonkwo whether a body can be found in the records that does not match the body in front of her; she says yes, she has a box, and she does not count them. On the clinic rota board: `MON WED FRI — KITCHEN RUN — O. BRIGHT`. He does not see it. Across the road, a rank-five clerk in grey has stood on the step of the Church of the Quiet Number every morning for three years filing the same petition — *count the ones nobody counted* — and asks Ines, the fourth auditor in three years, to stand on the step and read it.

### Ch 11 — Ola Bright
He does not take the notebook. He sits on the arcade wall and asks to read all forty-one pages properly, leaving the book with her. He tells her one true thing — the third one is different — and she writes it down. Beatrix Halm's name is in the book eleven times, four spelled Bright. Intelligence source secured without frightening her.

### Ch 12 — The Ferrier Line
He buys a public ticket for the first time in four years and rides the line end to end twice. The 980 shortfall is passengers, not staff: a tram of strangers helping each other is the densest unrecorded good in the city. He feels hunger to be counted. One mis-spelling is a date matching Beatrix's arcade-car rotation to the yards.

### Ch 13 — Nine Hundred and Eighty
Tobias explains rank five is 8,000 a week and only filed deeds count; Lin Hao is at 4,012. He stands alone in the witness box and learns a man may attest his own hands. Two new tram lines move the count. Ledger sixteen lines, three turned.

### Ch 14 — Two Witnesses
Nadia will attest loudly for nothing, which is why he cannot use her; Ines cannot because an auditor attesting a drawing account ends her seal; Ola has no plate and cannot be recorded. He copies Beatrix's depot roster. End of shift, eleven at night, the yards. An address, not yet an ally.

### Ch 15 — The Interchange at Four
He pulls a woman with a basket out of the path of the nineteen in front of two hundred witnesses and is scored 400 grains, public and permanent, moving to 4,412. Three feeders feel it at once — Beatrix Halm in the cab, Nadia Ferrous in the arcade car, Halvard Rees at the Cordwain bench — each named with room, job and line. Ledger sixteen lines, three turned.

### Ch 16 — What a Band Costs
The Exchange shows four hundred buys a hearing this week instead of March. Four more identical deeds would close the distance, each costing three cold minutes. Emrys Vaz's rota line is deleted on the twenty-second. He takes a day off, the first in the Branch in a draw-down. Tobias has worked out the interchange pattern.

### Ch 17 — The Cab at the End of the Shift
Beatrix opens the scene because she wants a use for what she feels. Explicit scene in the cab of the nineteen and on the tram roof in rain. Price agreed before starting: he spends them on people. She gives him four years of turned lines, clusters along the Ferrier arc, and one name from the back of the book already on a staff register.

### Ch 18 — Signal
Her effects and his addresses laid together make a geography: unrecorded good runs in one arc between the yards and the free clinics, four times a day. He rides the long way deliberately to feed it; Ines catches the commuting pattern in four days. Ledger nineteen lines, three turned. Tuesday plate shows two hundred grains and not enough, audit in five days.

### Ch 19 — Field Order
The interchange attestation forces a re-read; his four-year office aggregate held pending a witnessed deed applies at once, plus 3,529, crossing 8,000 mid-week to 8,141. White tile, rank five, held accounts, right to be asked before requisitioned. Same plate carries a deputy request for Field observation. Ledger nineteen lines, three turned. Rhee wants him with forty minutes.

### Ch 20 — A Woman With a File
Rhee, forty-six and rank five, nine hundred and eighty short per head, offers employment as stabilisation, not punishment. Lin Hao neither takes nor refuses; he asks for the audit deferred and Rhee grants nine days, buying the Branch a fortnight and spending his only clock. In the corridor Runa Sato, Field, stands with a sealed order and a file, and looked at him first. Somebody with a seal chose him.
