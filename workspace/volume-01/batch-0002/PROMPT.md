# Batch 0002 — Volume One, chapters 11–20

## Job

Ten finished chapters, in ascending order, into `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0011.md` through
`chapter-0020.md`. Chapter files, not summaries, not cards, not outlines.

This batch does three things and stops. Lin Hao learns that he can be **seen on purpose** and score
for it — a rank four becomes a rank five in a public room in front of two hundred witnesses. He
finds the woman who has been keeping the district's cold minutes properly, **Beatrix Halm**, and
takes them off her. And a woman with a file arrives from the Field and does not leave.

It does **not** change his rank again, does **not** give him a Channel, does **not** touch the
reservoir's meaning, and does **not** answer anything about his brother.

## Hard gates for this batch

**Chapter 15, the Ferrier interchange.** He pulls a woman out of a tram's path in front of two
hundred witnesses and is scored four hundred grains for it. Rank five. The score is public and
permanent and it is the first time in the book anybody has written his name down for something he
did on purpose. Show the arithmetic: four hundred grains is real distance, and it came out of the
count of people who felt it at the same moment.

**Chapter 17, the scene.** Beatrix Halm, in the cab at the end of her shift, and on the roof of a tram.
Explicit, written as the scene, for the reader who came for it. Adults, awake, unmedicated,
unrestrained, neither impaired. Her gift is not usable here: she feels the city's current and knows
where something is wrong, and she has been deaf in one ear since the Veil so a busy evening takes
her head off. The register shift — she is not like Ines, and the chapter must not read as a rerun.
She wants a use for what she feels, not a man. He gives her one. Nine chapters since Chapter 8;
fifteen is the hard ceiling.

**Chapter 20, the Field order.** Runa Sato arrives with a mandate and a file and a face he has not
seen before, and the chapter ends on the fact that somebody with a seal chose him.

## Craft for this batch

- Paragraphs of two to four sentences. Break anything over roughly ninety words. Never over 120.
- Sixty to seventy per cent plainly readable prose, twenty to thirty documentary, never three
  documentary paragraphs in a row without somebody standing in the room.
- Long accumulating sentences are the voice. Break them at every revelation. Short plain sentences
  when something is understood.
- One System message per chapter at most, in square brackets on its own line, in the ledger
  notation. It reports in people, never in acts. It never tells him what to do. Chapters 11, 13, 15
  and 19 carry one on the cards; the rest do not need one.
- No bold, no italics, no section headings unless a chapter genuinely has two sections.
- Titles come exactly from the title bank in `outline/volume-01.md`:
  11 Ola Bright · 12 The Ferrier Line · 13 Nine Hundred and Eighty · 14 Two Witnesses · 15 The
  Interchange at Four · 16 What a Band Costs · 17 The Cab at the End of the Shift · 18 Signal ·
  19 Field Order · 20 A Woman With a File.
- Every feeder who feels a cold minute gets a name, a room, a job, and one line of dialogue in the
  chapter they are spent on. Never a device. **Chapter 15 spends three at once** and all three of
  them get all four things.
- After any institutional passage, one plain sentence about what it means for one named person.
  Ditta Halse and Emrys Vaz are available; so is the stock clerk's rota.
- **Ration, carried from Batch 0001.** *There is no column for it* is spent (Ch 1) — none until
  Volume 7. *There is no form for this* is spent in all three registers (Ch 5, 6, 7) — **none remain
  before Volume 7, where it turns the ending. Do not use either again in this batch.**
  *Nobody asked*, *nobody thanked him*, *the record remained* are all still unspent; spend at most
  one and prefer the specific.
- **No feeder thanks him.** Halvard Rees sets the pattern in Chapter 3 — he refuses to, out loud, and
  warns him against doing it twice. Anyone who thanks him breaks the mechanic.
- **Never speak the word *reservoir*.** Named in Volume 2. The Bureau's word is *unsettled*.
- Keep every paragraph under roughly 120 words. Chapters 8 and 10 of Batch 0001 already ran long.

## Voice of the two new women — they must not be the same woman

**Ola Bright** is comic relief and she is not a joke. She is bored, she is generous, she is
twenty-six, and she is the only person who has written any of this down. Her register is fast, funny
and completely without malice, and the danger she is in is the danger of being *interesting*.

**Beatrix Halm** is the opposite of Ines in every way. Ines is a document that walks; Beatrix is a
signal operator with a headache that is actually forty thousand people and a body that has been
treated as a condition for four years. She is direct, she is tired, she is angry at her own division,
and she opens the scene because she wants something out of it, not because she has been overcome.

## Before Chapter 20 ends

He has one felt emotion and one concrete want. He wants rank six, he wants the audit off ninety-one
people, and he wants the count of turned lines under a number he has never told anyone. Pick the one
that is true for the chapter.

## Do not

- Do not write prose outside Chapters 11–20.
- Do not do weekday arithmetic. Batch 0001 left four mutually incompatible weekday labels in the prose
  (Saturday the 11th, Monday the 19th, Thursday the fifth, the fourth of June a Tuesday). The dates
  are canonical and the weekday names are not. **Count in intervals** — eleven days, nine days, eight
  days, two days, forty minutes — and speak a weekday only where the world turns on it: Thursday
  (repaint, readings published) and Monday the 19th (the draw-down opens). Anything else should read
  *the day before*, *that night*, *the Friday*. Read the calendar warning in `state/continuity.md`
  before you place a date.
- Do not name a faction or office `bible/terminology.md` does not define.
- Do not have anybody coerced, sedated, restrained, or psychically touched as the mechanism for a
  scene. Beatrix's deafness is a *cost*, not an impairment used to produce anything.
- Do not let him become strong. He is a man with a supply, four hours of sleep, a grey temple, and a
  score that only moves when somebody writes it down.
- Do not declare the series finished or write a completion marker.
