# Prompt — Volume 2 Close (audit, then record)

**Volume 2 is closed in prose. Chapters 51–100 exist, all fifty of them. This is the close phase, not a
batch phase. It does not write chapters.** Read `PHASE_SYSTEM.md` ("The volume close") and `AGENTS.md`
("If the volume is complete, create exactly one volume-close prompt") before starting.

Do not rewrite Volume 2. An audit that repairs the whole volume is not an audit. Prose repairs go into
`reviews/volume-02/volume-close.md` as findings with a reason, and are applied only where a reader is
actively mis-served. **One exception is named in Part three and it is not optional.**

## Why this phase exists, and what it inherits

Four Volume 2 batches have been written. **Batch 0005 closed the volume in prose and did not write a
review of itself**, which is correct and is the fourth consecutive batch not to. Every other batch was
reviewed by a separate run and repaired. **Chapters 91–100 have now been reviewed by a run that did not
write them, and repaired: sixteen arithmetic and meta-language defects and four craft findings, all
twenty fixed on the page.** The full working is in `state/continuity.md` at the top. **This phase should
assume the repair is correct, not that it was complete.**

**And it should assume the same about the pipeline's record of that repair, because the pipeline's record
of everything is bad.** Nine batches in this repository have certified their own prose and every one of
those certifications was wrong about the page it described. Twelve defects once reached a file that
certified its batch clean while every gate that file ran was a count. Three separate false certifications
have been about ages. **Two have been about repairs recorded in a state file and never carried into the
chapter, and Batch 0005 produced a third: it recorded the Ch 100 *Thursday the 20th* defect as *all
corrected* when it had cut the weekday name and left the sentence standing.** Run every gate against the
chapter, not against the note about the chapter.

### Two open defects that this phase should deal with before anything else

**D1 — the six-week count in Chapters 57 and 60.** Unreviewed and unrepaired. Chapters 51–60 have not been
reviewed by anybody.

**D2 — the length of last month is contradictory on the page, and Batch 0005 found it while deriving its
spine.** `state/current.md` and `state/continuity.md` say **thirty days**, from Chapter 77's *"Seventeen
days to the end of that month."* Thirteen plus seventeen is the thirtieth, and 30 − 23 + 7 is **fourteen**,
which is the figure Chapter 87 prints and which the Batch 0004 repair verified. **But Chapters 70, 80 and
83 all carry a thirty-first**: Ch 70 (the 21st) names *"the twenty-fourth and the thirty-first"* as standing
Thursdays ahead of him; Ch 80 **is** the thirty-first; Ch 83 has a clerk say the return was due on the
thirty-first and *"the thirty-first is four days ago"* on the 3rd, which is only true of a thirty-first that
existed. **With a thirty-one-day month Ch 77 is off by one and Ch 87's day-count is off by one** — and the
number Ch 87 carried *before* the Batch 0004 review corrected it was fifteen, which is the thirty-one-day
answer. **Batch 0005 crossed no month boundary and wrote around it; that is a mitigation, not a repair.**

**Settle it against the page and then repair the page, in whichever direction the evidence points, and
record which direction and why in the close record.** Every derived date in Volumes 1 and 2 depends on it.
This is the first thing this phase should take.

## Part three — the one prose repair this phase must make

**Batch 0005 registered D2 and wrote around it. This phase must settle it, and the settlement may require
a prose repair to reviewed chapters.** This is the one place where the audit does touch the page, and it is
required rather than optional, because **every derived date in Volume 2 rests on it and a reader will
eventually notice two chapters disagreeing about how many days are in a month.**

Do it this way: establish from the page which length is right. **Ch 70, Ch 80 and Ch 83 are three separate
reviewed chapters carrying a thirty-first, and Ch 83's *the thirty-first is four days ago* on the 3rd only
works in a thirty-one-day month.** If the page wins, repair Ch 77's sentence and Ch 87's day-count, and then
sweep every date and interval in Chapters 71–100 that was derived from the thirty-day reading. **Sweep
Chapters 51–70 as well**, because the Batch 0002 and 0003 repairs moved day-counts in those chapters. If the
state files win, the three chapters are the error and they say so reviewed and repaired, which the audit
must then weigh rather than assume.

**Record the sweep in `reviews/volume-02/volume-close.md` with the command and the output, chapter by
chapter, and register the decision in `state/open-threads.md`.** Do not leave two incompatible calendars in
a finished volume.

**One note from the Batch 0005 review repair.** Ch 93 said *I have thirteen days* to the third of next
month, which was wrong either way, and the repair **removed the arithmetic instead of getting it right**:
it is now *I have until the third of next month*. **That was deliberate — a deadline survives either
calendar and a count does not — but it is a mitigation of the same kind Batch 0005 originally made, and
when D2 is settled it should be checked to make sure the sweep has not reintroduced a derived count there.**
The same applies to Ch 100's *about a month* / *about two months* against Tessa's nine weeks at Ch 100 and
Ch 97: **nine weeks is the fixed figure and the other two must agree with it.**
