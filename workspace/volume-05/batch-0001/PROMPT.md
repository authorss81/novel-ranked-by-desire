## WHAT THIS BATCH MUST LAND

1. **The first day the certificate is not due.** The gap between the twentieth of May and the twenty-
   seventh of June is Volume 5's clock and it is thirty days long. **Something has to happen in a month in
   which a certificate is going to arrive and everybody in that house knows it.** The bench order of the
   third is still in force: a roll produced to a caller is produced, and the caller, the hour, and the
   number of names and spaces go on a district sheet. **The address in Ferrier Street is now a document,
   and documents are read.**
2. **The price of the standing, arriving as a bill rather than as an argument.** Volume 4 priced it in a
   kitchen on the seventh, bought it on the seventeenth and billed it on the eighteenth. **Volume 5 is
   where somebody else reads the bill.** The household is bound by a guild broker's standing, the
   examination is the Vant's, and the second line of that sheet — on the death, resignation or
   replacement of the broker of record — is the thing that makes it permanent. **It belongs to Marguerite
   Vale and not to him, and it must cost her something on the page in this batch.**
3. **Ines Duarte reading the record of her own household.** She can read any record in Ostara. **There
   is now no record in this city she cannot read and has not read**, and the reason she stopped filing is
   not permitted to be spoken. **Give her a chapter where she is the reader and the reader is the
   subject, and let the chapter do the work without her saying a word about the twenty-second of April.**
4. **A caller.** Batch 0004 set the bench order and Batch 0005 used it twice. **Use it once more, and make
   the caller a person rather than an instrument**, and put somebody in the household in a position where
   answering that caller costs something the caller does not know they are causing.
5. **An irreversible decision by the lead.** Something he does in this batch that cannot be given back,
   costs him something real, and closes an option. **It cannot be the signature; that was Volume 4's and it
   is already on the page at Chapter 199.** It must be a decision about **who is on the file** — because
   that is this volume's subject — and it must cost a woman more than it costs him, in the manner this
   book has established: the cost of every move he makes in this volume lands on somebody else's record.

### AND THE TWO GATES THAT HAVE NO OUTPUT AND HAVE COST FOUR BATCHES

**One: a script that rewrites prose is a gate with no output at all.** Batch 0004's paragraph pass used a
sentence regex requiring a full stop followed by whitespace, so any paragraph ending `since."` lost its
final sentence, and **nine sentences were silently deleted across ten chapters and every mechanical gate
returned zero on the damaged files. It was found by diffing sentence sets against a copy taken before the
script ran.** **Take a copy of the ten chapters before you run anything over them, and diff afterwards.
A shorter chapter with no banned words and no duplicate is not a shorter chapter; it is a damaged one.**

**Two: a sentence lifted from a chapter far behind you.** A duplication gate pointed at two files compares
two files. **Before you draft a scene, open the scene before it and the one before that, and read them.**
Batch 0005's Chapter 197 returned **zero** on the eight-word sentence gate while reproducing Chapter 189's
physical sequence word for word across **forty twelve-word runs**, because the passages had been
transposed rather than copied and a sentence is a different sentence once it has moved. **Run a 12-gram
scan, not an 8-word sentence scan, and treat any run of ten or more as a defect until you have read it.**
That is adjudicated item 66 and it is the reason it is written here.
