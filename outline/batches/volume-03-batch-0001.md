# Batch 0001 — Volume 3, Chapters 101–110

**Escalation step 1, The Notice.** Eleven Chapters of the volume's opening. **This batch turns the private
inquiry into a public question, puts Yusuf Adeyemi on the page, takes Lin Hao off the Board, starts
Marguerite Vale's scene, assembles the exhibit roll, and closes the option of a private description
permanently.**

**Batch goal.** Get the matter out of the Wing's hands and into a hall, on a date the Bureau set and he
cannot move, without anybody in the household losing anything that cannot be replaced — **and pay for it
by making himself a party to six women's record in a document he did not have the standing to touch.**

**Starting condition.** The 29th of last month is the last page of Volume 2 and Volume 3 opens after it.
Plate **8,138**, floor **8,141**, tolerance **three**, 11,862 under-band. Band six seated on the Review
instrument, **suspended pending description**. Ledger **112 lines, eight turned, one of them reading
himself**. File 4,112, one folio open. Standing and fourth floor **discharged**. Classification paper
dated the 19th, unsigned, gone back up three floors twice. Ruled sixth space empty. Household **seven
women, six notebooks, his signature refused**. The Wing knows his building, his floor and his household.

**Starting relationships.** Ines can describe him and has refused to, and her review has not been told to
her. Runa's hearing is pending and is not this volume's hearing. Tessa's word *mass* is in a file in her
own hand. Sun is at the top of a list and will not let the sentence about it be written down. Marguerite's
terms are down, unsigned and out of force. Beatrix's price is unpaid and September is hers. Nadia's lift
log has forty-one names on it that were never hers to give away.

**Mid-batch turn (Ch 105).** The first Board repaint of the volume, and **his name is not on the wall.**
The strip stopped forwarding when the standing was discharged and the Board has nothing for him, and a
man who spent four months being publicly mismeasured discovers what it feels like to be publicly
*unmeasured*, and the thing in him is not relief. **From 105 he knows that the only thing that would put
him back on that wall in front of a hundred and forty thousand people is exactly the thing he has spent
two volumes refusing.**

**Batch climax (Ch 108–110).** The exhibit roll is served, it contains the household, and he signs an
acknowledgement with a written objection attached that excludes the one item on the roll that is not his.

**Irreversible decision (Ch 109).** He signs, he objects, and **the objection names the household
notebook in a document that goes to a registrar** — which puts six women's record into a Bureau file *by
being excluded from one*, and makes him a party to something he refused to sign at Chapter 94. **It cannot
be withdrawn, and the option of standing outside his own file is closed.**

## Required character changes

- **Lin Hao** — from a man who has refused, on the record, to be described, to a man who has **signed a
  document in his own hand that puts six other people's records in front of a public hearing.** He has not
  yet described himself. He has done the harder preliminary thing and he has done it for other people.
- **Yusuf Adeyemi** — on the page from 103, and established immediately as **not an enemy**: he is
  straight with him, tells him what is coming, and says he is hated and intends to go on. He is not
  suspicious, not amused, not interested in the reservoir, and not the least bit impressed.
- **Marguerite Vale** — starts at 106 by asking to be made dangerous on his behalf. **Her terms stay down
  and unsigned and do not come back into force.**
- **Tove Marlin** — appears as the woman who will read the roll aloud exactly as written, which is the
  same promise she made in a room on the nineteenth of last month when she read Runa Sato's household line
  back to her.
- **Ines Duarte** — her second refusal is *not* demanded and *not* resolved. She reads the notice correctly
  and tells him what the question underneath it is.
- **Drant** — still on the fourth floor, still opening his office, **still not sent the strip**, and now
  the man whose late month-end return is the document that puts a thirty-first on the page.

**Nobody leaves. Nobody dies. No new partner. Wren Kallo does not appear in this batch** — she is named
for the first time in Chapter 116 and her scene is Chapter 118 and both are immovable.

## Required world reveals

1. **What a Correction Office notice is and what it actually asks.** Not *are you guilty*. *May a member's
   account remain undescribed.* — a fair question with the answer already legally settled at no.
2. **That a notice cannot be withdrawn by the person it names**, and that publication on the district board
   is what makes it irreversible. The Office does not need his agreement to make him public.
3. **What an exhibit roll is.** The Office builds the record it will read in the hall, and the roll is
   made of documents rather than people, and every document in it belongs to somebody in his household.
4. **That the Office does not ask the household. It asks about the household.** Which is why the household
   is exposed and the seven women are not on the roll at all.
5. **What it feels like to be taken off the Board**, in a city where the Board is the only window.

## Continuity and register rules for the writer (binding)

**Prose.**
- Chapter markdown stays plain: title as a first-level heading, plain paragraphs, section headings,
  **bold and italic sparingly and never as a substitute for a strong sentence**. **No blockquotes, no code
  blocks, no tables, no horizontal rules.**
- **A system message is square brackets, on its own line, plain language, never bold, never italic, never
  a heading, never batched with anything.** At most one per chapter and, in this batch, expect none.
- Paragraphs mostly two to four sentences. **Break any paragraph over roughly ninety words at a natural
  beat. Nothing over roughly one hundred and twenty.**
- Keep the mix at roughly 60–70% plainly readable prose, 20–30% documentary register, 5–10% experimental.
  **Never more than three consecutive paragraphs in the documentary register**, and one plain sentence
  about what it means for one named person after any passage of machinery.
- **Chapter endings vary** across the ten: question, danger, decision, reversal, cost, discovery, hook.
  **Check what the page actually ends on** — test whether the final paragraph *contains* the string, not
  whether it *begins* with it, the way the Volume 2 audit had to for Chapters 42–49.
- **Meta-language gate.** A grep returns hits that are all in-world (*a plate settled into the reader*, *a
  register*, *this book* as a physical object). Those are legitimate. **A character who knows the book's
  shape is not.** Volume 2 shipped four such violations through a gate that passed.

**World rules.**
- **Planning-register words never appear in prose:** *reservoir*, *quiet account*, *Concil*, *Ninth Seal*,
  *Channel*. Verified zero across Chapters 1–100. Routing is not named, learned, used or implied.
- **Spoken weekdays: Thursday only.** And the gate that greps for weekday *names* is **structurally
  blind** — it returns a clean zero on a page where the right name is attached to the wrong date, which is
  how a Thursday got into a plate hold that does not contain one and survived two audits.
- **No age is stated in narration or in dialogue, for any person, named or walk-on.** Ages live in
  `bible/characters.md` and are never contradicted. Three false certifications about ages have happened in
  this repository.
- **The household is seven women and six of them keep the notebook.** Sun Meiling does not. Do not write
  six.
- **Rationed constructions.** `He wanted, concretely` — spent. `For <person>, his <verb> meant` — restricted
  to Ch 41, 44 and 48 only. `nobody thanked <object>` — **retired outright, never with any object.**
  `nobody asked` — the *note* is spent, the *motif* is available. `there is no form for this` — at most
  three across the book and each must escalate; **Ch 101–110 may not use it at all**, because the volume's
  engine is a form that *does* exist and requires a reason. `there is no column for it` — spent at Ch 1 and
  again at Ch 52, **do not use it again before Volume 7**. `the record remained` — vary or cut.
- **Nothing is classified. No folio opens. The account scores no grain. The word *Channel* is not spoken.**

**Characters who must not appear in this batch:** Wren Kallo (named first at 116), Yusuf Adeyemi's
superiors, Alma Quayle, Anouk Ferreira, Perrin Sowe, Cassius Rhee, Saskia Verhoeven as a character, Sun's
reviewer, the Concil of the Nine by name.

## Chapter 101 — The Notice On The Board

**POV and location.** Lin Hao, six floors of washrooms at the Fourth Branch and the register desk, the
**first day of the new month**.

**Immediate goal.** Get through a shift in a building he has no standing in: no fourth floor, no strip, no
Thursdays, and ninety-one people who used to watch him and now do not.

**Resistance.** The branch is not hostile and it is worse than hostile. It is *convenient*. Porters hold
doors. Nobody stops him. He is a man who is not on anybody's list, which is what he spent four months
engineering, and it turns out that is also what it feels like.

**Major turn.** Two papers arrive on the same morning and neither of them is addressed to him by name.
**The first is Drant's month-end return, dated the thirty-first of last month, a day late, because the desk
that was supposed to send it on the thirty-first has been discharged since the eighteenth.** That single
printed date is the anchor the volume's calendar rests on and it must be on the page in the chapter's own
first half. **The second is a notice of a hearing**, served on him personally, and it is not a summons and
it does not accuse him of anything.

**Emotional or practical consequence.** He reads the first paper without interest and the second paper four
times. The relief he has been living on — that he is illegible — turns out to have a bill attached to it
that has just been issued.

**Required continuity facts.** Plate 8,138, floor 8,141, tolerance three, 11,862 under-band, band six
suspended on the Review instrument. Ledger 112, eight turned, one reading himself. File 4,112, one folio.
**He is still employed.** The discharge in Chapter 98 was the *standing and the fourth floor*, not the job,
and `bible/power-system.md` rule one is that the account accrues to a person nobody audits for the deed —
which requires him to keep being the person nobody audits. Standing discharged at ten past eleven on the
18th of last month. Ninth strip printed on the 28th and forwarded nowhere. Classification paper dated the
19th, unsigned, returned twice.

**Ending hook or completed beat.** The notice asks one question in a single numbered paragraph, and it is
not a question about what he did. **It is a question about whether an account may stay the way it is.**
Ends on a new danger: the date is on it, and the date is in about nine weeks.

**Word target.** 1,900–2,600.

## Chapter 102 — A Reason To Be Measured

**POV and location.** Ines Duarte, two rooms on Lantern Row, the **second**, cold, her table against the
wall.

**Immediate goal.** Find out what a Correction Office notice is, in the words of the only person in Ostara
who reads every record that passes her table.

**Resistance.** She can read anything and cannot read her own position. **Her review has not been
announced to her.** She has three private offers she has not taken, a finding number on a document she has
refused to produce, and a refusal of the fourteenth of last month sitting on a desk with nothing coming
back from it. She reads Lin Hao's notice in about ninety seconds and then spends the rest of the chapter
being read by it.

**Major turn.** She tells him what the question underneath the question is: the office has gone looking for
a procedure and has come back with **may a member's account remain undescribed** — and the answer is no,
not because the Office is wicked but because an undescribed account cannot be administered by anybody. She
also tells him the thing nobody else will: **a notice is not withdrawn by the person it names**, and it is
published on the district board tomorrow, and after that the only question left is what he says in public.

**Emotional or practical consequence.** He stops being able to imagine a quiet ending. Not because she
frightens him — because she is **kind and completely unhelpful**, and tells him the truth and then refuses
to help, which is the most familiar thing in Ostara and the least bearable.

**Required continuity facts.** **The second refusal is NOT demanded in this chapter and her review is NOT
resolved** (V3-4; this is the hinge of Volume 5). **He is not a tank, he is a door is not restated**
(B103). Ines has never filed the himself line and files nothing here. Tova Marlin is a Wing clerk who
reads things back exactly as written and apologises for the room. **Saskia Verhoeven is not mentioned.**
The rota word is ungiven. The May clipboard is on her table and neither of them touches it.

**Ending hook or completed beat.** The notice goes on the district board on the third and there is nothing
he can do about it, and ines says the sentence that ends the chapter: *everything that happens next
happens in front of people.* Ends on a consequence.

**Word target.** 2,000–2,700.

## Chapter 103 — The Man From Cobb Street

**POV and location.** The Cobb Street hall, above the Exchange, the **third**. Yusuf Adeyemi, alone with a
clerk he does not need.

**Immediate goal.** Go and look at the man, and decide what he is.

**Resistance.** **Adeyemi is not performing.** He does not want to know what the account is. He does not
suspect anything, is not amused, and is not interested in the question Lin Hao has been carrying for two
years. He has a list of cases with hearing dates on it and Lin Hao's is the eleventh. He is a man with a
procedure and he has run it for eleven years and he says, without being asked, that he is hated for it and
that he intends to go on.

**Major turn.** Adeyemi describes, in about six sentences and with no sympathy in it at all, what actually
happens in his hall: **it is a hearing and not a trial**, the member may speak, the Office must put its
case in full, the room holds a hundred and forty seated and will hold rather more standing, and **the member
may bring one witness and the Office may bring an instrument.**

**And then, as a courtesy, and in the same six sentences, he says the thing that is going to try to kill
this man: the counter-forensics instrument has never once worked in an empty room, and the Office expects
a full hall, and a hearing is public, and he has no power to keep anyone out of it and would not if he
had.** He offers it as information and he offers it honestly and he does not appear to know what it is.

**Emotional or practical consequence.** The threat acquires a face and the face is not the enemy, and
Lin Hao cannot find the purchase he has spent a hundred chapters relying on — the one where whoever is
coming is wrong. **The felt emotion is vertigo, not fear.** He is given the trap by the one man in Ostara
who is telling him the truth, and he knows it is a trap, and he walks into the building anyway.

**Required continuity facts.** Adeyemi is 44 per `bible/characters.md` — **and no age is stated in the
prose.** He is honest about his profession and willing to be hated for it and he becomes something stranger
than an enemy by Volume 7; **nothing in this chapter may make him the villain.** Alma Quayle is not
mentioned in person. The Concil of the Nine is not named. The classification paper is not discussed as
anything but a paper. **He is not a new final enemy and introduces no organisation.**

**Ending hook or completed beat.** Adeyemi gives him the date aloud and it is the one already printed on the
notice, and then asks him a courtesy question — whether he intends to contest — and Lin Hao's answer is
the first sentence of the volume's middle. Ends on a decision.

**Word target.** 2,200–2,700. **Named reason for the upper half:** Adeyemi's first page has to establish a
man who is not an enemy entirely in dialogue without turning into a lecture.

## Chapter 104 — The Form Requires A Reason

**POV and location.** The Vestry clinic corridor and the reading window, the **third, later the same day**.

**Immediate goal.** Find out what the Office will want from him, before he has to answer it.

**Resistance.** The Office does not ask the member first. It asks everybody else. **A request goes to the
clinic for the eleven-year summary line — the same one, asked on the third of last month, answered
*summary not produced, given by the holder in person*** — and it goes back unanswered a second time, and
Tessa has to choose between a lie from a clinician and a lapse against a service, and she chooses the
lapse and pays for it.

**Major turn.** He gets the form the Office wants him to complete, and it is not a description. **It asks
him to state, in his own words and in one paragraph, why his account should be permitted to remain
undescribed.** He reads it twice and understands that **every possible answer is a description** — a
reason for leaving an account alone is a statement of what the account does — and that the form has been
written by somebody who knows that and has written it anyway.

**Emotional or practical consequence.** The trap is legible, cheap and correct. **Tessa's lapse is the
chapter's cost and it is hers** — a service that has now failed to reply twice, which turns a clinic into
an office that is being asked properly and not answering. He is told about it by somebody else, later, and
is not in the room.

**Required continuity facts.** The word *mass* is in a Wing file in her handwriting from the eighteenth and
is still hurting. The rack went from twenty-seven to twenty-eight on the twenty-eighth and is still hers.
The stippling is eleven years old and does not move. **No draw is spent.** Tessa is the only body in the
city with a mass and a clean history and 258 charts of people who would go in with her. **The form's
existence is new paper, not a change to any existing form.**

**Ending hook or completed beat.** He writes nothing on the form. Enters on the form that a reason will be
given in the hall on the day, in person, and not on paper. Ends on a decision that closes an option.

**Word target.** 2,000–2,700.

## Chapter 105 — What The Hall Is For

**POV and location.** The Reading Square and the Board, **the fourth — the first Thursday of Volume 3** —
and the walk back.

**Immediate goal.** Walk past the wall he has walked past for four months and see what is on it.

**Resistance.** The square is doing what it does every Thursday at ten. The tiles come down and go up. It
is not an event.

**Major turn — MID-BATCH TURN.** **His name is not on the Board.** The strip stopped forwarding when the
standing was discharged on the eighteenth of last month; the Board repaints every Thursday morning whether
or not it has anything new to say; and for the first time since March there is no tile with his figure on
it and no word under it. Four months of a number he could not carry being published in a place a hundred
and forty thousand people walk past every week, and it has stopped, **because he refused a closure.**

**Emotional or practical consequence.** **The felt emotion is not relief and the chapter must not let it
be.** He stands in a square looking at a wall that has erased him and understands, on his own feet, that
the only thing in Ostara that would put him back on that wall is for the Correction Office to measure him
in public — which is the thing he has refused twice. **A man who has been publicly mismeasured for four
months discovers what public unmeasurement costs, and the cost is that nobody has to care about him.**

**Required continuity facts.** Plate 8,138 for nine consecutive Thursdays and it has not moved. Band six
suspended pending description. **No chapter in this volume may describe the suspension as a loss.** He is

## Chapter 109 — What He Signs Instead

**POV and location.** Ferrier Street, the room above the chop-house, the **eighth**. Six women and the
notebook on the table.

**Immediate goal.** Decide, in front of the six of them, whether he acknowledges the roll or refuses it.

**Resistance.** The argument is real and it is about him. Nadia wants him to refuse outright, because a
refusal is a sentence and a sentence can be fought. Ola has copied six lines a day since the twenty-first
and knows exactly what a document is. Runa is not in the room and her minute is on the table and nobody
speaks for her. **Sun Meiling is not in the room and does not keep the notebook and has said why.** The
six will not let him sign their book and they will argue about whether he is allowed to object on their
behalf.

**Major turn — THE IRREVERSIBLE DECISION.** He signs the acknowledgement of the exhibit roll, with a
written objection attached, and the objection is that **one item on the roll be excluded from being read
aloud in the hall** — and the item is **the household notebook**, which is not his, which he has never
been allowed to sign, and which six women keep without him.

**The cost, stated exactly:**

1. **A document he has no standing to object on behalf of another person's record goes to a registrar**,
   and the objection names the notebook in order to exclude it, **which puts six women's record into a
   Bureau file by being excluded from one.**
2. **He has made himself a party to a household register** and he cannot undo it, and the six women have
   to decide whether they were protected or merely counted.
3. **The option of standing outside his own file is closed.** Every roll from here forward is a roll he has
   acknowledged.

**Emotional or practical consequence.** **The worst part is not the Office. The worst part is that it
worked, and that six women let him do it.** One of them says the sentence that ends the chapter, and it is
not gratitude.

**Required continuity facts.** **The six women who keep the notebook are six. Sun Meiling does not and is
not in the room.** Ola Bright copies the six lines into the pad. **His signature is refused on the
notebook and is still refused at the end of the volume.** Nadia's notebook was opened on the fourteenth for
the sixteenth to the twentieth and one line per woman per day from the twenty-first. **No age is stated
for anybody.** **The Rota word, the May clipboard and Marguerite's terms are not touched.** **Nobody leaves
the room and nobody leaves the household.**

**Ending hook or completed beat.** Tove Marlin reads the objection back **exactly as written**, word for
word, as she promised, and does not soften it and apologises for the room. Ends on a cost and a reversal:
the objection succeeded, and succeeding is what it cost.

**Word target.** 2,300–2,900. **Named reason for the over-length:** the irreversible decision needs the
six women's argument in it, in their own words, before he signs.

## Proposed canon, registered BEFORE Chapters 101–110 are drafted

**Batch 0005 was the first batch in this repository to register its proposed canon before drafting, and it
is the standard. Nothing below is canon. It is a proposal. The prose is the canon; a card is a card. If the
drafting rejects one of these, record the rejection in `state/open-threads.md` rather than quietly dropping
it — Batch 0004 registered its own late and the repair pass had to find the drift.**

### The calendar

1. **M+1 is thirty-one days.** Undetermined on the page; fixed here so that Volume 3 has a calendar at all.
   **Chapter 101 must print it**, by a document dated the thirty-first of last month delivered late because
   the desk was discharged. Do not let a writer subtract it.
2. **M+2 is thirty-one days, its 1st a Monday, Thursdays the 4th, 11th, 18th and 25th.**
3. **M+3 is thirty-one days, its 1st a Thursday, Thursdays the 1st, 8th, 15th, 22nd and 29th, and the
   Reading is the 29th.** **The chain is derived, not assumed: M's 1st is a Tuesday (Ch 70, 79, 80, 81, 83,
   87), M has thirty-one days, so M's 31st is a Thursday and M+1's 1st is a Friday, which puts M+1's
   Thursdays at 7, 14, 21 and 28 (Ch 87, Ch 94, Ch 100) and Ch 100 — the 29th of M+1 — on a Friday.**

   Volume 2's printed dates, so the Reading could not be a Thursday on the sixty-third day in any calendar
   this book admits. The nearest Thursday on or before it is the 29th of M+3 at sixty-two days**, which is
   one day inside "about nine weeks" and inside "about two months". **This was solved and not asserted, and
   no later writer may "correct" it to the sixtieth day or the sixty-fourth.**

10. **A hearing notice cannot be withdrawn by the person it names**, and publication on the district board

## What this batch must not be

- **Not a procedure loop.** Nine of the ten chapters must not be *problem → document → clerk → refusal →
  document*. If a chapter can be lifted out and dropped into another chapter without changing anything, it
  is a failed chapter and it is the failure mode `AGENTS.md` names.
- **Not ten refusals.** He refused once in Volume 2 and the refusal cost him a calendar. In this batch he
  **signs**. A man who refuses everything is as flat as a man who agrees to everything, and the volume's
  change has to be visible in the shape of the ten endings.
- **Not ten chapters of the same register.** Alternate pressure: work, relationship, institution, threat,
  cost. **Emotional temperature must vary** — 101 is flat and cold, 103 is vertigo, 105 is the hollow
  middle, 106 is pleasure and warmth, 108 is dread, 109 is anger, 110 is a worse-than-nothing bargain.
- **Not Adeyemi as a villain.** Every line he speaks in 103 must survive a re-read in Volume 7, where he
  becomes something stranger than an enemy and ends the book as Registrar of Unrecorded Merit.
- **Not a summary of the scene.** Chapter 106 is the product.
