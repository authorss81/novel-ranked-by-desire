# Volume 7, Batch 0005 — Chapter cards, 341–350

**Movement 5 of `outline/volume-07.md` §6: The Room Where Nine People Sit.** The first to the thirtieth of
December. Ten chapters, thirty days. **The scene is 350, it is Wren Kallo's, and it is Volume Seven's last
chapter.**

**THE STAGE REACHED IS *WANTED* AND NOT *REQUISITIONED*.** `outline/series.md` and `outline/volume-06.md` §5
both forbid the fourth stage in this volume. **A requisition tabled and refused is not a requisition
received.** Volume 7 must close leaving a man who **can be requisitioned and has not been**, and the nine
minutes at 346 are the proof that the machine works and that this time it did not close.

**Ferreira's Volume 7 offer is the small one. `outline/ending.md` fixes the great one for Chapter 486 and no
part of it may be pre-spent here.** She does not make the speech that wins in the last volume and she is not

## The batch in the form the phase system asks for

**Batch number:** 0005. **Chapter range:** 341–350. **Batch goal.** Take the requisition off the household
and put it on himself, in his own name, in his own hand, on a page at the top of which there is a name.

**Starting condition.** **Eight documents about one address and, from Chapter 348, nine, and the ninth has
his name at the top of the first page.** The classification is written against the head. The account is
callable. **The thirtieth of December is the last working day of the quarter and the card comes down at six.**

**Starting relationships.** The eight, described, on a list, with Wren Kallo on it under a clerk's number and
her own posting. **Ines Duarte in her own rooms with a broken term behind her.** **Nobody has been sworn
and nobody will be.**

**Batch midpoint (345).** **He learns what the nine need in order to act, which is a named subject and a
reason, and he has both and gives them neither until he has given them something better.**

**Batch climax (348).** **He signs.** A description of the address, the account, the composition and the
eight, in his own hand, with his name at the top of the first page, lodged with the Concil as the head of
the premises. **The option closed: he can never be the address again in a document.**

**Required character changes.** **Anouk Ferreira meets him and is right.** Wren Kallo closes the volume by
doing the one thing her gift and her cost make unavoidable, which is telling somebody.

**Required power changes.** **The band on the card on the thirtieth is whatever the page has made it, and
the card prints it in a shade the square has not had.** No grain is spent. **No requisition is received.**

**Required world reveals.** **What nine rank-nines need on a page in order to move, and how long they have
when they have it** · **what a majority seal position is and how close it was** · **what happens to the
security of an account that is formally offered and formally refused.**

**Open questions for Volume 8.** *A seal exists and it is his brother's and the file that would say so opens
at 372.* · *An account offered and refused is still an account, and the refusal is on four hands.* ·
*Wren Kallo has told somebody, and Volume 8 is the volume that finds out who.*

## Cards

**341 The Room Where Nine People Sit** — the first of December. The monthly sitting, seen from a corridor,
**and the volume's first sight of the bench.**

**342 A Chair And A Majority** — the sixth. The arithmetic of nine, established by a woman who is not a
villain and who does not need to be one: **nine seats, one absent, one recused, and a requisition that needs
five.**

**343 The Reason They Could Not Use** — the tenth. **They can use the reason. They have had it since the
twenty-second of October.** What they have never had is a subject with a name.

**344 The Woman Who Held A City Still** — the fourteenth. **Anouk Ferreira, 63, on the page, and she is
right about why nine countable people were able to hold a city still for twenty years.** No chapter announces
her age.

**345 The Offer She Did Not Make** — the seventeenth. **THE MIDPOINT.** The smaller offer: bring it in, let
it be classified once, properly, and it will be administered instead of taken. He refuses it, and **what he
refuses it with is an instrument and not an argument.**

**346 Nine Minutes And No Majority** — the twenty-third. **The requisition is tabled and fails.** Five of
nine, and the page is not what they need, and somebody has left the room.

**347 The Council Minute In Four Hands** — the twenty-sixth. **A minute in four hands, which is the shape of
every important fact in this book since Chapter 296**, and the difference is that this one has his name on it.

**348 The Thing He Signed Instead** — the twenty-ninth. **THE BATCH'S IRREVERSIBLE DECISION.** A page with his
name at the top, in his own hand, describing the address and the account and the eight, lodged with the
bench. **Six volumes of documents with no name at the top of a page end here, and he did it on purpose, and
it is the arc of the book moving one step.**

**349 The Card Comes Down At Six** — the thirtieth, early morning. The quarterly card, a floor of 8,145, the
figure he stands at, and the word in the shade the square has not had.

**350 The Thing She Cannot Keep. THE SCENE. Wren Kallo. Volume Seven's last chapter.** — the thirtieth, at
night. **He is the most legible object in Ostara and she is the only person in that building who cannot sit
still with that, and she goes and tells somebody, and the volume ends on her walking out of a door.**
Scene rules as in Batch 0002.
