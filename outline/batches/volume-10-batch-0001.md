## 451 — THE ANSWER AND THE CHAIR. 30 June, about two in the afternoon.

**The sitting.** He stands up when the sheet is called and says the two sentences: the holding is his, and the ground is his own description of his own premises, made over it on the morning of the twenty-seventh of October at about eleven by a clerk called Imrie on four words he said in a kitchen. **And the standing answer, which is the whole of what he has, in front of nine people: a lodged man cannot be administered against a composition he is not keeping.**

**What actually happens and is not optional:** the sheet is **CARRIED**, with five seals, one of them his, because a member does not refuse his own seal when the room sits. **The clause of the twenty-first goes under the minute at the making of the minute, after the last seal, and is read by everybody who opens that minute in four years and by nobody else ever.** Three slips in one drawer become one instrument at four o'clock. The clerk writes up her book before anybody leaves the building.

**What changes, and it is the change the whole volume stands on:** the sheet has been administered weekly since the first of April — `PC-47`, crossed on the thirtieth of March, already in the next quarter, cannot expire — **and what the thirtieth of June changes is that it is CARRIED. The administration runs down the chain to the division that holds its fourth part, and its fourth part is a description of premises 34-6-118. He cannot be administered against, because a lodged man is not keeping the composition. Eight people live under that roof and they can.** He has done this. The instrument he bought at a pencil's cost on the twenty-first is now the thing that reaches his own kitchen.

*May not:* lift a recusal; make an offer of any kind; be thanked for anything; be told that the four words are a small matter. **May not print a floor** — 8,147 and 8,131 were printed at 450 and belong to 450. **May not print the word under the fifth line or the word under the gap; neither is in this repository.**

## 453 — THE CONDITION ON THE TABLE. 3 July.

The offer, in Ferreira's own mouth and not in a letter: **a standing at the Terrace, a chair, a name that outlives him, the Ninth Seal used for the good of the city** — on the sole condition that the reservoir be classified once, cleanly, by **Alma Quayle.**

**The condition has a price and it is not money and he can pay it in public and only he can pay it.** A recusal is lifted by the subject and by nobody else. There is no sheet for lifting one. **If he lifts Alma Quayle's recusal, the woman who spent ninety days in a chair that counts for nothing goes back to being a seal, and the thing he bought in 441 — *that the standing is the consignment you lodged with a pen in a queue* — is false the moment the account has a classification on it.**

**And she is right, and she has calculated correctly.** This is a better offer than he will get anywhere else. It is what he came into this city wanting at fifteen. **The reader should be able to tell, by the end of this chapter, that she has not made a mistake.**

**What she does not say, and this is the trap and it is in her own mouth at some point in this chapter:** that a man draining his own account is not a threat. She says it later, at 486, and it is not news in this volume. **What may not happen: he accepts, he refuses, he names what the account is, or he says the word *unrecorded* about himself.**

*May not:* let anybody say he cannot lift the recusal — he can, and it is one sentence, and he does not say it. *May not* make Ferreira wrong. She is right and the volume depends on it.

## 459 — THE THING SPENT IN HIS CHEST. 15 July. **THE BATCH'S IRREVERSIBLE DECISION.**

**`outline/ending.md` fixes this and it may not be softened:** he spends so hard and so fast that **the reservoir pays out faster than a human frame can absorb it and something in his chest is spent, permanently.** He buys it with a cough that stays with him for the rest of the book. **He knows what it will cost. **WHAT IT DOES NOT CLOSE, AND MUST BE SAID IN THE CHAPTER:** the account. **He is not emptying it, he is emptying it faster than a man can, and the thing that stops him is his own chest and not a rule.** A reader should be able to tell at the end of this chapter that he has miscalculated and that the miscalculation is in him.

**He knows and he does it in front of witnesses.** Somebody is in the room who understands what is happening and is not able to stop it — **that is Ada, who has seen it happen to herself twice, and who is the only person present who understands the mechanism well enough to know what it is going to cost.**
