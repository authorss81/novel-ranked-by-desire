## Batch goal

**To put a man who has spent four years being unmeasured into a line in a lane behind nine other people, pay a grain to be counted, and find out that the only institution his act actually created is one nobody can check — and then, four months later, refuse the only tool that could take it down, on the ground that he put two words on a wall himself and cannot be the man who takes another man's words off a slate for standing on the wrong surface.**

**And underneath it: for a man who emptied the largest account in the city's history in public and spent four years finding out what that costs, to be handed a title he did not ask for, in an office with two chairs in it, and to discover that the sentence he bought with the whole of his standing has produced exactly one other thing — a phrase on a wall that a boy painted at six in the morning and nobody, including eleven thousand people who were right, has ever been able to read.**

## 501 — FOUR YEARS IS NOT A NUMBER. 1 February, year one.

**He has had eleven weeks and three days of it.** Three floors of washrooms, a stairwell, a bin room, a cistern on the second that somebody wrote about in a ledger on the first of January and that stopped running by the sixth. A woman on the second floor at about twenty past eleven with a cloth and a bucket who has been there every night since the eleventh of October and who has never once asked him a question. A woman at the counter from one until seven who is the night man and who has the page, and the page is the page.

**What happens and is not optional:** a letter comes to the branch on the paper the Correction Office has, and the letter says the office exists and that he is the Registrar of it. **It is not a request and there is nothing to refuse it into, and he did not ask for it and he does not remember asking for it, and the chapter does not let him say he is considering it.**

**And Beatrix Halm is under the ranks wall at half past four the same afternoon, and she says out loud, to nobody in particular, what has happened since the book ended: two words, a dash, two words, a wall a city cannot read, four years of it, and a man in a delivery coat telling everybody it is about him — and a slate, and nine districts.**

**What changes:** the volume's opening fact is a sentence and a sentence is not a number. **For four years he has been able to say he does not know what it was for, and a title has arrived that makes him responsible for a thing that still has no meaning and no instrument, and the only person who can describe what the last four years have been is a woman with a notebook who has been under that wall every morning of them.**

*May not:* have him refuse the title; have him thank anybody; print a figure of the account; name a day of the week; print a floor.

## 502 — THE MAN WITH THE SLATE. 9 February.

**He is named and he is described before he speaks, because he has been on the page four times without a name and the reader has been owed this since the eighth of October.** A delivery coat. Five stools. A line. **A grain a head off anybody who wants to stand in it.** Two words chalking in about four-inch letters at about a quarter past nine every morning. **Made up in three days, in October, and he has told that to everybody who stands in the lane and has never once said the Board took it off him.**

**And he is right about every fact of it.** Runa Sato told him on the eighth of October that they are not going to come, and told him he should not want it to be a competition, and he went on doing it every morning anyway, for four hundred mornings, and the four hundred is what this chapter is.

**What changes:** the volume has an opponent who cannot be defeated, checked, licensed or appealed, and who is not a criminal and does not profit where a person loses. **Beatrix Halm's sentence from the eighth of October is the volume's motto and it is on the page four years early: you cannot check a slate. You can check a wall.**

*May not:* make him a fraud; let him lie to anybody; let him be bought; give him a rank or a plate; make him want the Reservoir back.

## 503 — FIVE STOOLS AND A LANE. 18 February.

Beatrix on the district round. Runa Sato on hers, and the two of them do not agree, and the disagreement is the chapter. **Runa Sato's position, in her own words and because it is hers and nobody else has it: the four hundred behind him are not behind him, they are in a lane, they happen to be behind a man with a stool, he put himself in front of them about six hours ago and he has been there ever since, and in nine years she has never once seen this city make a line that a rumour did not make, and a rumour always goes away inside four days.**

**What changes:** the reader is given the correct frame for what a line is, from the only person in the book whose profession is knowing what a line is — **and it does not help, because the line has been there for four hundred mornings.** *What also changes:* it becomes clear that four years of doing nothing about it is a position and not an absence.

*May not:* have Runa Sato be proved right; have her be softened; give her a reason box; print the number of mornings as a figure that is not derived in the chapter.

## 504 — A GRAIN TO STAND IN A LINE. 28 February.

**He goes.** Not as a man in disguise and not as a man with a plan. He goes down the hill at about eight in the evening, pays a grain, and stands behind nine other people in a lane off Cordwain for about four hours, and at about half past midnight a man chalks a figure on a slate in four-inch letters and the figure is his.

**What happens and is not optional:** **the figure is not nothing.** Four hundred people in this district felt something on four particular days and the man in the delivery coat is the only person in Ostara who has been keeping count of it in public, and he counted them himself, and he is right. **Lin Hao does not correct the figure and does not take the grain back and does not say a word about who taught him to count.**

**What changes:** he is on a slate in Cordwain, in chalk, in public, and the reader is told plainly by the woman standing behind him — a stranger — that a man who stands in a line for four hours is agreeing with the line. **The last thing nobody can take from him, which is the part of him no institution knows about, has been spent on the cheapest possible errand, and it is not a sacrifice. It is four hours.**

*May not:* have him be recognised by the man; have him correct the figure; have him be beaten or robbed; make it noble; make anybody film it.

## 505 — THE AUDITOR AND THE CHALK. 6 March, year one. **THE SCENE.**

**The only instrument in Ostara that can check a slate is standing three floors above him in a corridor at about three in the morning, and she has been in this building since the first of October and has not said one word about being here.**

**Stage it from nothing.** The room's rule is an auditor's rule and it is hers: she reads in order, the date first, the hour with it, nothing skipped, and **the thing she is reading at three in the morning is a page with his name on it and a figure of merit on it in chalk that a stranger made up in three days.** She is reading it the way she reads everything. She cannot stop where she is.

**What changes:** she asks him a question she has never asked him in six volumes, **and it is about the tile.** Not the account, not the brother, not the correction she filed on the twenty-ninth of December and did not press. **What is on the tile, who put it there, and why has he stood under it about sixty times without asking.**

*May not:* discuss Perrin Sowe; forgive him; say anything said in a corridor; give her a fifth instance of the ink-under-the-reader's-nose construction; let her say a first name for a feeling; answer the question on the page. **THE QUESTION IS ASKED AND NOT ANSWERED AND THE CHAPTER IS ALLOWED TO END THERE.**

**Break the counted minute and do not name it.**

## 506 — A QUARTER OF A CORDWAIN MORNING. 15 March.

A town-hall counter, a question, and the discovery that the city has two instruments and no rule about which one wins. **The specific question, not the general one: can a slate be produced in answer to a question, and by whom, and what happens to the answer.** A clerk looks for the rule. There is not one. The clerk is not the villain and the clerk is also not able to help.

**What changes:** the thing Volume 10 could not do — put the unrecorded into the record — has been done by a private man with a piece of chalk, and the Bureau's answer to it is a woman at a counter who cannot find a rule, **and that is the first time in the volume that the Bureau's answer is visibly worse than the problem.**

*May not:* resolve the conflict; make the clerk a martyr; print the phrase about there being no form (it has been used three times in this manuscript and this is not one of them — name the specific rule that is missing instead).

## 507 — SIX WORDS IN HER OWN HAND. 30 March, year one. **QUARTER-END.**

The counting room, two floors under a sales floor. Fifth book, strap, lamp. **Hanneke Ruis writes the composition of a street into the fourth block of the quarter's return out of her own memory, in her own hand, under her own name, with nothing behind it, exactly as she has four times a year for thirty years — and a man from a lane off Cordwain comes to the counting room with a slate and asks her whether her figure is right.**

**She gives him the right answer**, which is that she does not know and has never known, and that she has been writing it down the same way for thirty years.

**What changes:** the oldest unrecorded instrument in the city and the newest one are in a room together with a lamp in it, **and neither of them can be checked, and they do not agree, and neither woman nor man in the room is lying.** This is the chapter that makes the volume's argument without a word of argument.

**Sun Meiling is at the far table with her folder and is not asked one question by anybody, which is the fourth time on the page.** *May not:* give Hanneke Ruis an age; make her sentimental; make her wrong; let anybody tell her she is the last honest record in the city.

## 508 — THE KEEPER AND THE QUARTER. 16 April.

The standing sheet of the quarter goes out at four in the afternoon with an address at the head of every line, and any person in this city may go to a public desk and read it on a working afternoon. **The office's first quarter is on it, and the office's first quarter is one entry, and the entry is a figure about one person in nine hundred of what went out on the first of October, and there is nothing on the page to check it against.**

**What changes:** the Register exists, it is public, it is small, and it is real, and **all four of those are true at once and none of them is a surprise to anybody who has read Chapter 499.** The reader is given the comparison and is not given the verdict.

*May not:* print the account figure; print a floor; say the return was an act of justice or of showing off; give the reader a percentage that is not derived in the chapter.

## 509 — CORDWAIN KEEPS ITS OWN FIGURES. 30 June, year one. **QUARTER-END.**

Three quarters in. Slates in nine districts and they do not agree with each other. **A bakery, two laundries and a rooming house off Cordwain, and a landlord off Lamplit Row who quoted the standing of a premises at that column to a tenant every quarter for nine years and wrote *as per the Board* for the first time in his life on the tenth of October and did not like the sound of it.**

**And two of the nine slates are right about a thing the Register has wrong**, because two people kept better count than an office with one entry in it.

**What changes:** the Register is not obviously better than the market, and **that is the first crack in the volume and it comes from outside, which is where it should come from.** The Bureau cannot notice a slate and has no instrument that could.

*May not:* make the Bureau notice; make the slates wrong; make the Register wrong; resolve which is better.

## 510 — THE FIRST FIGURE OFF A CHALK. 30 September, year one. **QUARTER-END. THE BATCH'S IRREVERSIBLE DECISION, AND IT IS HIS.**

**The office asks him to strike the slates down.** Yusuf Adeyemi can grant it and cannot refuse it and there is no disposition and no form and no instrument, and he asks him because on the tenth of October, four years ago, in a room that could carry nothing, in front of about ninety people, Lin Hao stood at a bench and defended the Bureau from the bottom of the ladder and said its whole job was to be **the thing you can ask.**

**And he refuses, in about one sentence, and the sentence is this: *he put two words on a wall himself, and he is not going to be the man who takes another man's two words off a piece of slate for standing on the wrong surface.***

**WHAT IT CLOSES:** the option of striking down a rival measure — for four years and, on the evidence of this batch, for ever. **The slates stay in nine districts and become the thing he is going to have to answer for the rest of this volume.**

**WHAT IT COSTS:** **he is the reason there is a second institution in Ostara.** He said the thing that made the first one his position and not its own merit, and the thing he said in that room was that the Board is the thing you can ask, and the answer to that is a thing you cannot ask, and he has just told ninety people and a Registrar that he is not going to do anything about the second one. **He cannot afterwards be a man who defended measurability and then watched a private chalk measure a city.**

*May not:* make it a speech; have anybody agree with him in the room; have Adeyemi argue him out of it; print a figure of the account; print a floor; name a day of the week.
