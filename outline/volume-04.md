# Volume Four — Six (chapters 151–200)

> In this volume, **Lin Hao must build a household that can hold rank six**, or **he stands at six alone and is taken apart for it**.

**This outline did not exist when Volume 3 closed.** It is written now, from `outline/series.md`,
`outline/ending.md`, the Volume 3 Batch 0005 block at the head of `state/continuity.md`, and the
Batch 0005 close record in `state/open-threads.md`. **Everything in it is a plan until a chapter puts
it on the page.** Where this outline disagrees with a figure given in the Batch 0001 prompt, **this
outline governs and the disagreement is flagged** in the proposed-canon block at the head of
`state/open-threads.md`, not silently adopted.

`outline/volume-03.md` is the model for shape and depth. Volume 3's title bank is the worked example
of what this volume's title bank must not be, and the reasoning is at the foot of §11.

## 1. Central pressure

**Traced to a source, not chosen.** The source is Chapter 122.

Sabine Rooke keeps the room the parties wait in while the Cobb Street hall is cleared. In Chapter 122
she asks Lin Hao for the one thing she has never asked for — the ninth chair, on the morning of the
sitting — **and names her price before she asks: her name on a sheet about him**, which she says out
loud makes the drawer of eleven years of her own paper a different question.

**What she filed on the twenty-second of March is a composition return.** A composition return is how
Ostara says *who lives at an address and under whose standing* — a filing, due at the turn of the
period, shelved when it arrives and read by nobody when it is late. Sabine Rooke filed it late, on the
day of the sitting, as the price of a chair. **It names six women at an address in Ferrier Street and
it has no head of household, because there is not one, and the space for one has been empty since the
day she signed it.**

**So the pressure is: the household he spent four months refusing to build already exists on paper,
it does not name him, and the one woman who is not on it is the woman who has spent four months
saying she would never be written down.**

Everything else in this volume is downstream of that.

**Why this is not Volume 1's pressure.** Volume 1 was a scale. A band, a threshold, a Winter Review,
a number that could be won or lost in a room on a Thursday, and a man who could win it by doing enough
good deeds in public. **Volume 4 has no number to win.** He is three grains under a floor and eleven
thousand eight hundred and sixty-two grains under a band he is already carrying on paper, and there is
no deed on earth that closes that gap, because the gap is not made of deeds. He has proved this across
three volumes and it has never once been the problem in front of him.

**Why this is not Volume 2's pressure.** Volume 2 was a room with no window and a form on it. **Volume 4
has no room and no form.** It has a drawing-board shelf on the third floor of the Reading where a late
return is put, and a drawer, and a piece of paper that says six names and a blank line, and that is
the whole of the machinery. **The danger in Volume 4 is not that somebody will put him in a room. It
is that nobody will, and the paper will simply keep existing without him.**

**And this is the first volume in which somebody else's file is the thing at stake.** In Volume 3 the
paper was about him: a description in his own hand, a finding, a roll. **From Chapter 151 the paper is
about six other people**, and every move he makes in this volume is a move he makes on somebody else's
record, and the cost of every one of them lands on a woman rather than on him.

### The sentence the Bureau has already said, and is right about

Sun Meiling said on the thirtieth of March, on a landing above a chop-house, that a household which
does not stop him is not a shield, it is an audience. **Volume 3 proved it.** Volume 4 answers it with
the other half of the same argument, and the half is not hers:

> **A household that cannot be found cannot be defended. It can only be lost, slowly, in instalments, by
> people who never have to say they took anything.**

That is the volume's engine, and it is `bible/world.md` — rank is public, bands are public, a name is
on a wall — arriving at a house on Ferrier Street.

## 2. Beginning state

Traced from the last page of Chapter 150 and from `state/continuity.md`'s Volume 3 Batch 0005 block.

**Position.** Plate **8,138**, floor **8,141**, tolerance **three**, **11,862 under band six**. The
plate has not moved a grain in fifty chapters and **does not move a grain in this volume either**.
Band six is seated on the requisition paper dated the nineteenth of last month and a finding dated the
twenty-ninth of March. **Not a rank. Not on the plate. The plate cannot carry it.**

**Ledger 115 lines. Nine turned, and the ninth is himself.** He felt a cold minute once, in a corridor
on the thirteenth of February, with a woman with a card, and three times in a hall on the twentyninth.
**Four is the number at which a person begins keeping track**, so he now feels one coming with about a
second and a half of warning, and **a turned line can be led to a source by a doctor, a tram operator,
an accountant or a relative**, and he has made himself the only person in Ostara who can be led to
himself. **It is never refunded anywhere for the rest of the book.**

**The promise.** He said in a hall, into a microphone, before the instrument was run, that he would not
move the account on himself again. **A promise with a number on it, and he keeps it for the rest of the
book. There is no draw spent on himself anywhere in Volume 4.**

**File 4,112, one folio open, no second.** No classification entered. 214,000 grains, Held Account One,
no Zero.

**The description in his own hand.** Four places. Its last line is that he is unable to say what moved,
that he will not attempt to say it again, and that he told the hearing officer in advance and not
after. **For the rest of the book the thing the Bureau holds about him is his own sentence.**

**The desk's four-line note** — that a member who states an intention in advance has had the
opportunity to prepare the room and the persons in it — is a lie, is in the file with a number on it,
and is not withdrawn by anybody.

**The classification paper** dated the nineteenth of last month went up three floors at four o'clock on
the twenty-ninth and came back down unsigned, exactly as it has done every working day since. **It is
not signed in Volume 4 either.**

**The four ribs on his left.** Three movements landed there in four hours in front of two hundred and
eleven people, and Ines Duarte made him say the location of it out loud before she would put a thumb on
it, and Sun Meiling never touched it and made him say the number three times. **Permanent.**

**The household.** **Seven women: Ines Duarte, Beatrix Halm, Tessa Okonkwo, Nadia Ferrous, Runa Sato,
Marguerite Vale, Sun Meiling.** **Six of them keep a notebook with six lines a day and his signature has
been refused three times.** Sun Meiling does not keep it and has closed her own refusal: she will not be
kept, will not be on a plate, will not be in a roll, will not be sworn to anything, and **will be in the
record by not being in it.**

**Wren Kallo is the eighth partner and is not one of the seven.** She is unsworn by the fixed rule in
`bible/characters.md` — a Bureau counter-forensics officer bound to him is a woman nobody in her own
division will speak to — and she is at the table sometimes and on no roll at any time. **No chapter may
enumerate the household without deciding which list it is on.**

**Ola Bright is not a partner.** She is Nadia's flatmate, she keeps the copybook — three hundred and
eleven sentences of her own copying — and since the sixteenth of March it has been under the table
where no hand in that house can reach it. She took the pen out on the thirty-first and wrote one line
in the seventh space, under the sixth line, and it is a sentence and not a signature.

**Nadia Ferrous's forty-one names** are in a transcript that is for sale over a counter in Cobb Street
for a shilling, and **there is not one line in any document in this city that says she chose it.**
Her service file goes to the Exchange counter every Friday.

**The leak** is four people deep and three have been told: Wren Kallo named a plate clerk, Cressida,
and a sister who asked how their mother's knee was. He chose that on purpose on the eighth and was
shown the bill on the twenty-ninth.

**He is still employed**, cleaning six floors of washrooms at the Fourth Branch. **Do not take the
job.** It is the reason the account accrues at all.

### What the sixth space now holds

Anneke Roos struck Sarn's paragraph through in her own hand on the twenty-ninth and entered his own
sentence under it, word for word from the transcript face, as *superseded in the room*, and signed it.
**It was the first thing she had signed in four months and she said out loud that it was going to cost
her something she had not been told about, and worked out herself what it was:** a clerk who has
entered a thing, struck it, and entered another over the top has a position on the content of it.

**Volume 4 pays that sentence. It is the first chapter.**

## 3. Major locations and factions

**New or newly load-bearing in this volume.**

- **The Reading's third floor, the drawing-board shelf.** Where late composition returns are put. Four
  shelves, and the third is where anything that missed its period goes. **A man who cleans six floors
  in a branch office a mile away is never going to know that this shelf exists, and that is the
  volume's first reversal.**
- **Sabine Rooke's room**, the parties' room on the third floor, the room the parties wait in while the
  hall is cleared. It is not a hearing room. **Eleven years of her own paper is a drawer she can no
  longer open without thinking about the chair.**
- **The Exchange sales floor and its counter.** Where merit is spent on permissions. One window with
  the shutters up. **Nobody in this book has ever been inside it.**
- **The Vestry, upper landing and the dispensary hatch.** Tessa Okonkwo's clinic, unfunded by the
  Bureau and tolerated by it.
- **A washroom on the sixth floor of the Fourth Branch.** His by labour. **The scene at 157 happens in
  the one room in Ostara he cannot stop anybody else from taking**, because rank six may requisition
  property and a washroom in a Bureau building is property.
- **The Field hostel at Cordwain**, and the muster room with the roster on the wall.
- **A signal cabin at the far end of the Ferrier line.** Beatrix Halm's, and the only one of the
  seven places in Ostara that belongs to one of them rather than to her job.

**Factions, and what each one wants in this volume.**

- **The Bureau of Merit.** Wants the composition return completed, because an incomplete return is a
  household it cannot serve papers on, and an unservable household is a household it cannot regulate.
  **It is not hiding the return. It shelved it.**
- **The Exchange.** Wants to sell a permission to somebody entitled to buy it, and **has discovered
  that a described account has no entitlement**, which is the fact the volume's midpoint chapter is
  built on.
- **The Vestry clinics.** Want to keep treating people without filing, and are about to be named in
  somebody else's file, which is the thing they have asked not to happen for twenty years.
- **The Vant.** Wants to know what the reservoir is worth, which is Volume 6, and in this volume wants
  to know whether a household can be bound to a guild broker, which it can.
- **The Church of the Quiet Number.** **Closed. See §13.** Eleven thousand members, no ranks, no audits,
  and the Bureau has stopped considering it a curiosity between Chapter 151 and Chapter 169. **No
  chapter before 169 may name it, show a member of it, or allude to it, and no chapter before 169 may
  use the word *congregation*, *chaplain*, *null room*, or *quiet number* about anything.**
- **The Concil of the Nine.** Referred to only as nine people. Not named.

## 4. Escalation sequence

Five named steps. **Each changes the situation rather than informing it, and each closes one of his
options.** No step is a chapter that could be lifted out and dropped into another chapter unchanged.

### 1. The Instrument — 151 to 160, the first to the tenth of April

On the first of April the fourth floor of the certification bench has no Ivo Sarn on it, the machine he
built is under a bench cloth, and his name has been on a ceramic tile in the Reading Square since the
twenty-second of March — **nine days before the clerk struck his paragraph out.** Anneke Roos is the
one who struck it and is the one who cannot say where he went, because nobody has told her.

From that: **the machine that found Sarn is the same machine that can find anybody, and it takes about
nine minutes, and it works on people who never knew they were in it.** The only people in Ostara it has
not found are seven women who have declined to be written down.

Tessa Okonkwo supplies the professional answer nobody else in the book can: **a description is
sufficient to serve a requisition of property on, and property in this city is anything a person is
using.** The Office will take the tables, the chairs, the notebook and then the woman who cannot prove
she owns them. The composition enquiry goes out on the sixth. **Tessa signs a chart on the seventh**,
which puts the Vestry clinic and her name and his name on one piece of paper for the first time in five
volumes, and she names what it costs her before she signs: the clinic is tolerated because it is
unfunded, and an unfunded thing that appears in a file stops being tolerated.

Then the household answers the enquiry **without him** — four replies in four hands, over a weekend,
because four of them decided to be findable without asking him — and Ola Bright copies all four into
the book under the table and does not tell him. **He is not a party to his own household's answer.**

> **Option closed: the household as a place that cannot be reached.** It can be reached. It was reached
> on the sixth by a piece of paper he did not see go out, and it answered in four hands he did not know
> about, and the only thing that is now gone is the belief that seven women who cannot be found are
> safer than seven women who can.

**At 160 he does the thing none of them would do, and it is irreversible: he asks Ola Bright to copy
one sentence, six times, in six hands, and to hand him the page.** It is a standing instruction — if a
paper comes for this house, answer it in writing, all of you, separately, and do not consult me. It
removes him from the chain of command of the only structure he has ever wanted to be inside of. **And
Ola tells him, before she copies it, what it has cost: the moment a thing in that book is something he
asked for, the book stops being the one unasked record in Ostara.** It cannot be handed back. **Scene
at 157.**

### 2. The Return — 161 to 170, the eleventh to the twentieth of April

Runa Sato's hearing is on the twelfth, a Thursday, because the reading was, and **it does not resolve
and it is not this volume's resolution.** She is not allowed to be in the room, not allowed to object,
and not allowed to be told — and the third of those is still him. What comes out of it is a **standing
instruction of the Bureau's own**: a member who refuses to answer a count is entered as refusing, and
a household with a member who has refused is a household with an unanswered count in it.

Ines Duarte tells him, on the fifteenth, that her review is on the twenty-second and that the only
thing which defends a person against an audit is being the same woman all the way back. The Exchange
returns his composition form with a second issue. Beatrix Halm works out what the city's turned lines
say. And on the eighteenth a man who is not from the Bureau comes to Ferrier Street with a form, having
bought the transcript over a counter in Cobb Street **for a shilling**, and he is entirely within his
rights.

**On the nineteenth, Agnete Lindqvist walks out of a room she has not been outside in eleven years.**
She is the chaplain of a church Volume 3 refused to name and that this volume's chapters 151 to 168
may not name, show, or allude to. Her gift makes a room where nobody can be compelled to testify. She
is a former rank-four clerk. She comes out because a woman in a washroom on Ferrier Street asked her a
question in the plainest words available to her, and the answer is that she has been waiting three
years for somebody to ask.

> **Option closed: the private floor.** Six women who cannot be found are not protected; they are
> merely unlocatable, and the Exchange's second issue proves the Office can complete a return without
> them. **The only structure that can hold a name against a paper is a name on a paper.**

### 3. The Standing — 171 to 180, the twenty-first to the thirtieth of April

**Midpoint span, and the reversal lands here.**

On the twenty-second Ines Duarte goes into her review and **the chapter does not go in with her.** She
is four hours in a room on the fourth floor with four people, and the chapter is the waiting room on
the third floor at Cobb Street, which is the room the parties wait in while a hall is cleared, and a
man who has never once been on the wrong side of a hearing waits in it for four hours and is not told
anything. **It is the most frightening chapter in the volume and nothing at all happens in it.**

On the twenty-fourth he goes up a floor he has never cleaned, because a woman has told him there is a
sheet on the third shelf, and **the reversal is proved on paper in five minutes.**

> **THE REVERSAL, Ch 174.** The composition return exists. It was filed on the twenty-second of March,
> a fortnight and ten days before this volume opened, by Sabine Rooke, as the price of the ninth chair.
> It names six women at an address. **Its space for the head of the household has been empty since the
> day it was signed.** He could have found it in nine minutes in the building he works in, at any hour
> of any of the four months since, and he did not, because he has never had a reason to go up a floor
> and because he has spent four volumes being the least findable person in Ostara and has confused that
> with being safe.

**And the second half of the reversal, which is the one that costs:** the return is a filing about six
people with **no head**, and the Office cannot complete it without one, and **the seventh woman is the
one who has spent four months refusing to be written down.** Bringing the household into force means
completing a return that must be complete, and completing it means either putting Sun Meiling on a
page against her own closed refusal, or running a standing household that is one person short forever.

> **Option closed: a household that is only ever an audience.** It has to become a thing with a name
> at the top of it, and the name at the top of it is going to be his, and his is the only name in Ostara
> currently written into four documents describing himself as something he cannot explain.

### 4. The Five Names — 181 to 190, the first to the tenth of May

Five women and a space, and a return that has been re-issued because an incomplete return is a return
the Office cannot act on.

**One of the six asks to be taken off it.** That is the first time in this book that a person has asked
to become unfindable on purpose, and it is the first person who understands what Lin Hao has been doing
for four volumes.

The Board repaints on the third. Wren Kallo's leak either deepens or stops, and **it stops**, because
she has found a way to be a person who holds a thing and does not have it: she takes the fourth name
herself and does not tell a single other person, which is the first secret she has ever kept and it is
killing her, and she says so, cheerfully, and it is not funny.

**At the batch midpoint the fifth name goes on**, at a change of shift, in front of nine people, in a
woman's own hand, on a service file that has gone to an Exchange counter every Friday since the tenth of
March — **and Nadia Ferrous says out loud that she is doing it for a reason that has nothing to do with
him and that she would like the room to understand that.**

Then Sun Meiling is asked, for the only time in the book, to be on something, and gives the answer she
has already given, in a form that cannot be argued with. Tessa's chart reaches the Wing and the Vestry
is put on notice in a sentence with no name on it, which is the Office's politeness and is worse than
having a name.

> **Option closed: leaving them off and keeping the protection.** Six names on a roll bind him to six
> liabilities and buy six people who can be asked before they are touched. One name off the roll keeps
> Sun Meiling's refusal and makes the household permanently incomplete, which the Exchange's own rule
> will not permit after a date.

**Scene at 189, Beatrix Halm**, in a signal cabin at the far end of the Ferrier line, where she is asked
to stop writing minutes down.

### 5. Six — 191 to 200, the eleventh to the twentieth of May

Six names and one space, a sixty-day certification rule, and the last two women.

A standing household takes sixty days from a complete composition return, which means **it cannot be
finished in the volume** — but it can be *begun*, and beginning it is the irreversible act, and it is
Chapter 199, and it is a signature, and it is an **indemnity**: the head of a household answers in his
own name for every member of it. **Every liability that belonged to six women separately becomes one
liability that belongs to him.** That is what standing costs, and it is itemised in §7.

The fourth rib is examined in a clinic in April's last week and found to be the fourth rib and not
anything else, which is the one thing the body will agree to.

Ines Duarte's review has happened and is not resolved and she will not discuss it, and she is still the
same woman, and that is the sentence Chapter 195 is named for.

**At 197 the scene is with Marguerite Vale**, in the Exchange sales floor after it closes, where a
permission is bought — a hearing moved up, a door, an hour of a machine — by the one person in Ostara
who has entitlement and intends to use it, **and the price is that a household bound by the highest rank
in it is bound by a guild broker, which is the first thing in this volume the Vant can put a hand on.**

> **Options closed: the man alone, and the man who stays unlisted.** From Chapter 199 he is the easiest
> person in Ostara to find. He chose it, in a sentence he wrote himself, and the cost is that from that
> day a paper can reach him by writing to him first.

**Scene at 197.**

## 5. Midpoint reversal

**At 174, proved on paper, in five minutes, by a man going up a floor he has never cleaned.**

The composition return exists. It was filed on the twenty-second of March as the price of a ninth
chair; it names six women at an address; and the space for the head of the household is empty. Every
protection the household has believed in for four months — that it could not be found, that it was not
a document, that its record was six lines a day in a book under a table — is a description of a thing
that has been filed since before this volume opened.

**Why it is proved on paper.** Nothing in it is proved by anybody looking at Lin Hao. It is proved by a
shelf on the third floor, a late return, six names and a ruled space, and by the arithmetic of a
calendar: the filing is dated the twenty-second of March and the hall was the twenty-ninth.

**What it changes.** Before 174 the threat is *they may be found*. After 174 it is **they have been
found, on the sixth of April, by a piece of paper, and they answered it in four hands, and the man who
they answered it to is the man the return does not name.** The threat has not softened. **It has changed
species, from an exposure into an incompleteness**, which is worse for a household, because an exposure
can be refused and an incompleteness can only be completed.

### The rule for what this may not be restated as

Volume 2 stated its reversal once, proved it in Chapter 75, and spent thirty-five chapters being
attacked by offices that could not use it. Volume 3 spent four uses rationing it and it still leaked
into jokes. **The rule, binding on every chapter in this volume:**

1. **It is stated at 174 and at no more than three places in the volume, and never after 180.** After
   180 the fact is simply operating and nobody has to say it again.
2. **It is never restated as *he is the address*, and never in any form of B103.** He is not a tank, he
   is not a door, the account is not an address, and the phrase does not appear.
3. **It is never used by any character who has not read the paper.** Anneke Roos, Marguerite Vale and
   Wren Kallo know it. Ines Duarte and Tessa Okonkwo do not until they are told, and Tessa has to be
   told in her own words in a clinic and reacts as a clinician. Runa Sato, Nadia Ferrous, Beatrix Halm
   and Sun Meiling do not know it in this volume at all.
4. **It is never restated as a victory.** It is a loss with a use, and the chapter that says so says it
   in the first person and does not resolve it.
5. **It is never used as a joke, and no character who has read it may say anything funny about it.**

## 6. Climax and resolution

### Climax — Chapter 199, the nineteenth of May, and Chapter 200

**199 is the signature.** He signs as the head of a household, in a room where the whole thing is a
form, in front of a clerk who records what is in front of the clerk, and he signs an **indemnity**, and
the indemnity says in one sentence what the whole volume has been about: *the head of a household
answers in his own name for every member of it.*

**200 is the first day of it.** The roll goes to the counter on the Monday. Six names and one space
that stays empty. Sun Meiling is not on the roll and is not going to be, and the reason she is not going
to be is now a fact about a legal document and not only a preference, and that is the volume's last
emotional movement and it costs her something to hold.

### Resolution, with the costs itemised

**The volume's promise is met.** He builds a household that can hold rank six. **It cannot hold rank six
for long** — the Exchange's rule is that a standing household is bound by the highest rank in it, and
the highest rank in it is not his, and the highest rank in it is a guild broker's — **and he knows that
on the day he signs and signs anyway**, because the alternative is six women with no standing behind
them at all.

**What is gained.**

- A name at the top of six women's liabilities, in his own hand, on a roll that an office has to read.
- A warrant. A requisition answered in his name before it is answered in theirs.
- **The first legal standing of his life.** Four volumes at 8,138 grains and three of margin, and the
  first thing he has ever had that is not a number.
- The right to be asked before a member of his household is requisitioned. **Which is rank five's
  privilege extended by the bond, and it is the thing seven women have needed since Chapter 1.**

**What is paid, permanently.**

1. **He becomes findable on purpose.** Every form, notice and paper in Ostara now has a name at the top
   of it that leads to a man in Ferrier Street. He has been the least findable person in the city for
   four volumes and has made himself the most findable man in it in one signature.
2. **Six liabilities become his.** Every one of the six has a cost outstanding. Runa's written
   disobedience and a hearing that did not resolve. Tessa's *mass* in a Wing file in her own hand, and a
   chart. Nadia's forty-one names and a service file. Beatrix's broken price, unpaid, uncollected and
   unforgiven, and a year of her handwriting on a map that she will have to say is gone. Ines's two
   refusals and a review she will not discuss. Marguerite's terms, down, unsigned and out of force,
   which a standing household cannot bring back into force and which she has just bound herself to
   anyway.
3. **He is the head of a household bound by somebody else's standing.** A rank-two binder, and the
   binder is a woman with a guild in her life. **The Vant now has a thing it did not have.**
4. **Sun Meiling is one person short forever**, and the return stays incomplete, and the household's
   legal existence rests on an argument that the Exchange has already refused once.
5. **The sentence in the copybook under the table** — Ola Bright's one line, written on the thirty-first
   of March, the only line in that book that is not a woman's day — is now a document, because
   everything he asked for after the tenth of April was copied into the same book, and the one unasked
   record in Ostara has stopped being unasked. **He did that, and it was the price of the first name.**
6. **The four ribs on his left are a permanent instrument.** Tessa's chart in Chapter 194 records them
   as the fourth rib and records nothing else, and the record says a body he cannot explain.
7. **The plate does not move.** 8,138, floor 8,141, tolerance three, 11,862 under band six, for fifty
   more chapters. **He ends the volume with a household, an indemnity, a warrant and three grains of
   margin, and no rank at all.**

**What is not resolved and must not be.**

Ines Duarte's review. Runa Sato's hearing. Beatrix Halm's ninth of September, and the broken price.
Sun Meiling's shape that says February. The two ninth chairs. The ninety rubbed-out sentences. Marta
Vey's name. The desk's four-line note. The classification paper dated the nineteenth. The leak.
**Agnete Lindqvist's eleven years indoors, of which Volume 4 opens the door on and does not close.**

### What the Bureau does, and what it is right about

The classification paper is not signed. The instrument stays withdrawn. The notice does not close. **The
Office sends exactly two things that can be quoted: a monthly permission circular with a ruled space for
an account's classification that returns the words *no entitlement established*, and a composition
enquiry answered in six hands.** Both are correct, both are instruments acting on Thursdays, and
between them they have produced a household.

## 7. Emotional resolution

**Tabled against the two closed volumes, and against Volume 3.**

| | Volume 1 | Volume 2 | Volume 3 | **Volume 4** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What he wanted | rank six by the Winter Review | to know what was in him before spending it | to survive being measured in public | **a household that can hold what he carries** |
| What he got | a band on paper and a lost standing | the mechanism and the price of it | a description in his own hand | **a roll with six names and a space he filled** |
| Who paid | the district he stabilised | his sleep, and six feeders | six women's days, in a transcript for a shilling | **six women's liabilities, and his own findability** |
| What he became | a man with a band | a man who knows | a man who is a line | **a man who is a name at the top of a page** |

**The sentence.** Volume 1's man was looked through. Volume 2's man was held. Volume 3's man was read
aloud in a hall. **Volume 4's man is a heading**, and he chose it, and the choosing is the whole of his
arc moving: the flaw is *he hoards*, and hoarding ends in a signature that cannot be taken back, and the
thing he hoards over is not merit — it is other people's exposure to his.

## 12. The calendar, derived

**The repository's standing lesson is that a count is not a reading and an inference is not an anchor.**
Month length was inferred from a sentence about a different month, four state files adopted it, and a
review then corrected two correct chapters to match its own inference. **Every link below is derived
from something already on the page and the derivation is printed.**

### What is certain, from the page

- **M+3's first of the month is a Thursday** and **M+3 has thirty-one days.** Both are fixed on the
  page and certified: Chapter 141 the fifteenth is a Thursday, Chapter 150 the thirty-first **is not a
  Thursday and the chapter opens by saying so**, and Chapter 144's fifth-to-the-last night and Chapter
  143's Thursday the twenty-second and Chapter 145's Thursday the twenty-ninth all agree.
- **Therefore M+3's thirty-first is a Saturday.** Twenty-ninth is a Thursday, thirtieth a Friday,
  thirty-first a Saturday.
- **Therefore M+4's first of the month is a Sunday**, being the day after.

**This is the derivation, and it checks itself against canon in two independent places.** M+4's Thursdays
are therefore **the fifth, the twelfth, the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth**. Runa Sato's hearing is

produce.

### What this volume fixes, and where the anchor is printed

**M+4's length is undetermined on the page and every prior state file that asserted a figure was wrong
— that is adjudicated item 50 and item 51, and both were expensive.** So:

> **This outline fixes M+4 at thirty days, and requires Chapter 151 to print the anchor rather than leave
> it to subtraction.**

**Chapter 151 must carry a printed card of the month's instrument days.** Every branch office keeps the
month's Board repaint days on a card by the time clock, because the Board repaints at nine on a Thursday
and the men who paint it have to know which days they are coming. **The card reads: the fifth, the
twelfth, the nineteenth, the twenty-sixth, and then the third of next month.** Twenty-sixth plus seven is
the thirty-third, which can only be the third of May, which can only be true if April had thirty days.
