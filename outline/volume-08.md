### 1b. THE OTHER DOOR, AND WHY IT IS NOT THE SAME ONE

**Wren Kallo is not the woman who told him. She is the woman who is going to tell somebody else**, and the
volume sentence's two halves are one door because of what her speaking does.

- **Chapter 350, last line:** she goes down sixty-one steps at twenty to two in the morning of the last night
  of the year, *and she does not know yet who she is going to tell.*
- **She may never be asked about it.** Chapter 316 and 350 and 342: *you may not put one question to me
  about that address and I will say I do not know what you are talking about.* **He has asked once, on the
  sixth of December, and received the standing answer, and said he would not ask again.**
- **What she has is a shape, and it is this one (350):** two documents about the same eight women at the
  same door, made inside a month by the same pair of hands, in one of which she is a Bureau officer
  certifying a composition at a distance and in the other she is the fourth line of a list of persons at
  her own address. *There is not another person alive in this city who can see that shape, and there is not
  another person alive in this city who is forbidden to hear about it either.*
- **And what she says she is going to do with it, if anybody asks her:** *tell them it costs nothing to be
  wrong about me and a great deal to be right.*

**SO THE TWO DOORS JOIN LIKE THIS, AND IT IS THE WHOLE OF THE VOLUME'S PRESSURE:** the description of
premises 34-6-118 was referred for classification on the nineteenth of October with **the reason being a song
made in public on the fifteenth which cannot be reproduced in writing and for which no author has been paid**
(302, 311). Every division that wants a reason on that file now wants an author for that song. **The only
person in Ostara who can supply one is the woman who sang it, and she is at premises 34-6-118 on a public
list with her room against it.** **So when Wren Kallo speaks — which she has decided to and has not chosen
whom — the shape she gives them ends up at the iron stair.**

**And he cannot find out whether it already has, because he is not permitted to ask, and she will lie to his
face in whatever room he asks it in.**

**THAT IS WHAT THE VOLUME SENTENCE MEANS AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME IN EIGHT VOLUMES THAT THE WOMAN AND THE
DOOR HAVE BEEN THE SAME MAN.** Every scene in 351–400 is between a woman and a man who cannot pay for what
she is doing. This one is the volume's engine and it is not a scene and it must never be written as one.

**IT IS ALSO THE ONLY THING IN THIS FILE THAT WAS NOT OBVIOUS, AND IT IS WHY THE PROMPT ASKED.** The cheap
answer is that the volume is about Wren Kallo's decision. **It is not.** Wren's decision is Volume 8's clock;
Sun Meiling is Volume 8's woman; and the thing the volume is about is a man buying a door by making himself
the holding, in front of eight women who will pay for it and one of whom is the reason.
