# Volume Two — The Reservoir (chapters 51–100)

> In this volume, **Lin Hao must learn what is in him before he spends it on himself again**, or **the Classification Wing classifies the account and empties him out at rank one**.

## Central pressure

The pressure comes from the Classification Wing's office under Alma Quayle, and specifically from a clerk's routine transcription: the thirty-first entry on the Wing register, dated the 3rd, now carries a Review finding number beside it in red ink.

That is a shape the Office has no procedure for. A rank-six petitioner stands 11,862 grains under his own band, seated on an instrument rather than on merit, carrying a ledger line no auditor has ever read and three turned feeders triangulable to one room. No form asks for that man. No column holds him. No re-read resolves him, because a re-read measures a figure and the figure is not what is wrong.

Why this is not Volume 1's pressure again: Volume 1 was an audit and a Review — a public machine asking *are you good enough*, with a published threshold, a count of deeds, and a band that could be won or lost in a room. Volume 2 is an inventory of nature — an Office asking *what are you, and who has the standing to say*, with no threshold, no deed count, and no procedure that ends in a rank. Volume 1 measured whether the plate could carry the band. Volume 2 asks whether the man can be described at all, and keeps open the terminal answer: classification, which empties a man out and returns him at rank one. Classification is held open the whole volume and is never entered; Batch 0001 must not classify him, and the volume earns its threat by showing what the Office does instead.

What the Office actually does, when a man presents a rank the plate cannot carry, is uncomfortable: it summons him to attend, inventories his attendances, maps his turned lines, asks him to surrender names, offers him a clean description in exchange for standing still, and lengthens the time he must stand still until the account settles enough to be read. It does not hunt him. It files him.

## Escalation sequence

Five named steps, each occupying a chapter range. Each step changes the situation; none re-runs Volume 1's audit.

**1. The Red Number (51–60).** The Office notices. A Wing clerk transcribing Review findings onto the register finds a finding number beside an unsettled entry and has no box to put it in. Summonses issue: attend, present the plate, answer what instrument seats the band. Lin Hao attends in person and refuses the one compliance that would end it early — surrendering names — which escalates a routine transcription into a standing inquiry. Sun Meiling is planted by name only. Himself line and three-room turn stay unfiled.

**2. The Inventory (61–73).** The Office with no procedure measures him anyway. Weekly attendances, plate presentations, cold inventories at the Vestry, a wall map with a dot on the Review room. Every instrument confirms the figure and misses the mass. Quayle's desk inherits the file. Sun Meiling's first scene (55, carried forward) and her sentence (63, the one that reconciles everything in Volume 8) reframe the inquiry without answering it: the shape in the data is not natural. The volume's erotic and household pressure advances without repeating a Volume 1 shape.

**3. The Address (74–85).** Midpoint span. The inquiry's own map forces the reversal: three turned in four seconds cannot point at a street and points at a room, and a room with nine chairs cannot be fed by accident. He understands the account is not his and not natural — it is an address, and something was sent to it. Tessa's and Runa's scenes carry the body cost and the Field-against-Wing friction. He stops trying to earn his way out of the gap and starts trying to learn what the gap is.

**4. The Holding (86–96).** The system reacts by holding still. The Office, unable to describe him, lengthens what it can control: attendance intervals, plate holds, a draft holding order prepared but not signed, the forty-day settling clock named as the Office's ally. Old threads pay toward the new question — Colm's locked office, Ding's stair, Bernt's absence, the wall map — without opening the May clipboard, the second folio, or the Church. The threat is now explicit and dated: stand still long enough and the account settles into something classifiable.

**5. The Keeping (97–100).** Climax span. Put under a clean, lawful demand to spend the account on himself once more — this time to prove it can be described — he refuses, at cost, having learned what is in him well enough not to spend it that way again. The held-account order stays unsigned. The volume resolves its promise practically (he keeps the account unclassified) and emotionally (he accepts that being seen is the price of keeping it). The new Volume 3 question opens: now that the Wing knows where he is, what does a public measurement do to everyone who fed him.

## Midpoint reversal

At 75–76, in the Turned Room span, the inquiry's analyst lays the three Review colds against the register's thirty-one and the dot lands on the Review square. The reversal is structural, not a surprise: **the account is not a supply he found but an address he was given.** Everything Volume 1 treated as accrual-by-proximity inverts — he did not gather nineteen and then fifty faces; faces were sent where he stood because he stands where no auditor looks. The himself line is then legible for the first time: the one spend that could never be relief, because there was nothing in him that came from himself. From here he no longer asks how to close 11,862 grains. He asks who sent 214,000, why the receiver's file is One with no Zero, and what forty days of standing still will make of him. The sentence at 63 planted this; the map at 75 proves it to the Office as well as to him.

## Climax and resolution

**Climax (97–99).** The Office puts a lawful, narrow demand with a deadline: attend the inventory room, present the plate, and repeat the Review spend on himself under controlled conditions so the Wing can describe it once and close the inquiry. Classification paperwork is prepared, dated, and left unsigned on the next desk — the terminal failure made visible. Ines's scene (97) forces the filing question to its edge: she can file the himself line and give the Office its description, or hold it and stand with him outside procedure. He refuses the repeat spend, in person, on the record, knowing refusal lengthens the inquiry and shortens his standing. Sun Meiling's standing, Tessa's body count, and Runa's closed file each bear part of the cost.

**Emotional resolution.** He accepts that keeping the account means being looked at on the Office's schedule — hoarding privacy is no longer available, and spending privacy on himself is no longer permitted. The flaw moves: from hiding what he has to learning what it is before he uses it.

**Practical resolution.** No classification. No rank lost, no rank earned; plate still reads within tolerance of its floor, band still seated on the Review instrument. The himself line stays unfiled but known to two auditors. The three Review turned stay turned. The inquiry stays open into Volume 3, which is correct: Volume 2 promised to survive learning, not to end the Office.

## The new question that motivates Volume 3

Volume 3 (*The Reading*) asks: **he must survive being measured in public and keep his ledger, or a public reading of him becomes a public reading of everyone who ever fed it.**

Volume 2 hands it this: the Wing now knows where he is and what shape he is — an address with ninety-one faces and a dot on one room — and its instruments still cannot describe him except by standing him still. The only instrument left that can hold him in the open is a public reading. If the inquiry becomes a Reading, the map becomes a list, and a list becomes a summons for every feeder. He kept the account from being classified by learning what it is. He has not yet learned how to be read without reading everyone else aloud.

## Planned power and relationship progression

**Power (Stage one, investigating, Ch 1–100).** No rank earned, no rank lost; no grain scored from the account at any point; the 11,862 gap never closes by a score. What is learned, each with a cost on the page: the himself line is permanent and unspendable twice; three turned in one room is triangulable and the Office triangulates it; the figure drifts where he stands and the faces stop when he leaves the corridor (rule carried forward, never re-explained); stillness settles — roughly forty days unspent and the mass flattens into something classifiable, which is why the Office's weapon is time; routing is glimpsed once (a figure that jumps with a body unchanged) and is not learned, not used, not named in prose — the Channel stays Volume 5. Ledger rises with stated cause; turned count rises and never falls. At most one system message per chapter, only where the count turns, never repeated.

**Relationships.** Sun Meiling enters as core-and-unsworn: planted by name before 55, started at 55, binding by 63, refusing the household's protection logic by 100 for the reason fixed in `bible/characters.md` (inside Veil-Research, the sworn are the requisitionable). Ines moves from *cannot decide whether to file* to *can describe and refuses on their schedule* without resolving the rota word or touching the May clipboard. Tessa's inventory worsens on the page (rack past eighteen, stippling past the joint) and becomes a standing debt he acknowledges monthly. Beatrix's broken price is carried without heat and without forgiveness; September stays hers. Nadia advances on her floor (T9) without being spent. Runa's closed observation becomes explicit protection at a written cost. Marguerite's house holds him unsigned throughout; terms never accepted, never signed, never in force. No new partner is started in this volume. No partner leaves. The four who will use the option to leave are untouched.
