# Volume Three — The Reading (chapters 101–150)

> In this volume, **Lin Hao must survive being measured in public and keep his ledger**, or **a public reading of him becomes a public reading of everyone who ever fed it**.

**Planned in one phase that wrote no prose.** Traced from Chapter 100, from the *Volume 2 closed* block
of `state/continuity.md`, and from `reviews/volume-02/volume-close.md`. Every proposed canon change this
outline relies on is registered in `state/open-threads.md` **before** the chapters that carry it are
written. A count is not a reading, and an inference is not an anchor; the calendar section at the foot of
this file is the one place in the repository where that has already bitten, and it is written so a writer
can subtract a date against it rather than trust a gate.

---

## 1. Central pressure

**Where it comes from.** From the fact that the Classification Wing cannot finish what it started.

The band is suspended pending description. The standing is discharged. The inquiry has no dates and the
dates are somebody else's. Volume 2 ended with the Office holding Lin Hao in the open by standing him
still, and by *standing him still hitting other people*, and with the one instrument the Wing has left
being the six-paragraph paper on Anneke Roos's desk that goes back up three floors unsigned every day at
four o'clock.

An office that cannot describe a thing privately will eventually describe it in public. That is not a
policy decision. It is what an instrument is for. **The Correction Office exists to take a thing that
cannot be settled in a room and put it in a hall with the doors open**, and the Correction Office is
outside the Wing, and the Wing's draft description is not signed, and the only way a man who refuses to
be described gets described anyway is if a room full of strangers is appointed to do it.

**So the pressure is Yusuf Adeyemi, and the pressure is his profession.**

He is not the villain and must never be written as one. `bible/characters.md` makes him honest about what
he does and completely willing to be hated for doing it. `outline/ending.md` makes him something stranger
than an enemy by Volume 7 and, at the end of the book, Registrar of Unrecorded Merit, which is a joke he
tells nobody the meaning of. **A Volume 3 that treats him as the antagonist is wrong on the page before it
is written, and the outline does not.** He is the first person in three volumes who tells Lin Hao the
truth about what is coming and is not doing it to hurt him, and Lin Hao's whole problem is that he cannot
tell whether a man being straight with him is the same as a man being kind to him.

**Why this is not Volume 1's pressure again.** Volume 1 was a scale. A public machine, a published
threshold, a count of deeds, a band that could be won or lost in a room on a Thursday. The instrument was
a number and the question was *are you good enough*. **Volume 3 has no threshold, no count, no band to win
and no victory available.** The instrument is a room and the question is not *are you good enough* but
**in front of whom**. A man can pass Volume 1 by doing enough in public. He cannot pass Volume 3, because
Volume 3 is not asking him to do anything.

**Why this is not Volume 2's pressure again.** Volume 2 was an office asking *what are you*, in rooms
with no window, on a schedule the office set, with the audience as the thing he was hiding from. Every
question in Volume 2 was asked privately and the privacy was the weapon. **Volume 3 asks the same question
in a hall with a hundred and forty people in it and a clerk writing down every word**, which is the
opposite of a weapon he can hide behind: it is a weapon that works on everybody in the room.

**And here is the engine, and it is Chapter 100's last line made into a mechanism.** Every instrument the
Office has for reading an account works by watching what comes out of it. What comes out of his is other
people. Therefore a public reading of him is a public reading of everyone who ever fed it. **The threat
is not that the Office is wicked. The threat is that its procedure is correct.**

## 2. Beginning state

Traced from Chapter 100 and from `state/continuity.md` *Volume 2 closed*.

**Position.** Plate **8,138** against a floor of **8,141**, tolerance three, **11,862 under his own
band**, and nothing the account did has ever been converted into a grain. Band **six still seated on the
Review instrument and not on his plate**, now **suspended pending description** — a worse shape, not a
loss, and the suspension is the hole Volume 3 is poured into. **Ledger 112 lines, eight turned, one of
them reading himself.** File **4,112, one folio open and no second**, 214,000 grains, Held Account One,
no Zero. Standing and fourth floor discharged at ten past eleven on the 18th; the ninth strip printed on
the 28th and was forwarded nowhere.

**The dates are somebody else's and they are not Thursdays.** That is the position Volume 2 handed over
and Volume 3 opens inside. A man who had five dates a month and a bell at half past has none, and the
absence is going to be the first thing the Office uses.

**He is still employed.** The discharged instrument in Chapter 98 was the **branch standing and the
fourth floor** — the attendance schedule and the floor where the machine runs. It was not his job.
`bible/power-system.md` rule one is that the account accrues to a person who cannot be audited for the
deed, and in practice to people who are not looked at: the attendant, the night porter. **If he loses the
washrooms the engine of the entire book stops.** Volume 3 therefore opens with him cleaning six floors of
washrooms at the Fourth Branch as usual, with no standing, no fourth floor, no strip and no Thursdays,
which is the loneliest job description anybody in Ostara holds.

**What he learned, and it is a correction and not a discovery.** A ledger entry is a pair — a person
relieved and a person it came from. His line has one name on it. It reads *to*, not *from*, from a side
of the entry with no name on it. **He is not a tank, he is a door.** Ines Duarte can write that
description in a fortnight and has refused to, on their schedule rather than forever, and will refuse a
second production too. **The line is unfiled and legible to two auditors and he has never seen it.**
That sentence is not restated in this volume. B103 is not to be resolved before the Brother's File.

**What it has already cost, and all of it is still being paid.** The word *mass* in a Wing file in Tessa
Okonkwo's handwriting. Runa Sato's household line read back to her as **available**, with a disciplinary
hearing in about two months. Sun Meiling at the top of a list in the best finder in the city, having
refused a direction to her own house in writing and refused to let the sentence about it be written down.
A classification paper dated the 19th, unsigned, returned at four on the 20th, twice. **A ruled sixth
space in Anneke Roos's fourth book that is empty on purpose and is going to get a line in it.**

**The household is a register.** Bellhouse runs a visitors' book off Marguerite's own mantel and she reads
it herself at the end of every week. Nadia's notebook was opened on the 14th for the six dark days and
**his signature on it was refused**. **The household is seven women and six of them keep it; Sun Meiling
does not, and that is the whole of her refusal in miniature.** Three women each hold one buff square and
are still not named. Nadia's lift log carried forty-one names off her own page into a document they had no
business being in, and the consequence is hers and is still hers. **The Wing now knows his building, his
floor, his household and the names of six of the seven.**

**The corridor.** Eight chairs against a wall of the second sub-level in two rows of four and one square
to a door four feet out. A plate on that door that says what a classification is and what happens to a
person who goes through one and the word rank and the word one, **read for the first time in five weeks,
from the top of a stair, with no handle on that side of it.** Inside, per Volume 1 and Volume 2, a cloth
on a table. **Volume 3's floor.**

**Volume 1's debts carried in.** He has a want — rank nine, the file — and it is stated in Volume 2 and
will be restated at the volume's close in the same words, costing something each time. He is hoarding.
Marguerite's terms are declared off the table and stay down and unsigned and **cannot come back into
force**. Beatrix's broken price is unpaid and September is hers. The May clipboard, the rota word, the
Church, the word *Channel*, the second folio, Ding's chest beyond two sentences, **Rhee**, and anything
anticipated after Volume 8 are all shut.

## 4. Escalation sequence

Five named steps. **Each changes the situation rather than informing it, and each closes one of his
options.** No step is a chapter that could be lifted out and dropped into another chapter unchanged.

**1. The Notice (101–110).** The Wing cannot close an inquiry with an unsigned paper, so the matter goes
to the only body in Ostara that can compel a public appearance, and **Yusuf Adeyemi enters the page**
doing his job and saying plainly that he does not enjoy it and will not stop. The notice is dated, served,
published on the district board and **cannot be withdrawn by the person it names**. It does not accuse
him of anything. It asks a single question in public: *may a member's account remain undescribed?* — which
is a fair question and the answer is no. Adeyemi puts a date on it, and the date is sixty-two days out, which is what Ch 100 already said.
Marguerite asks for the description to be made to matter (106); the Wing assembles the roll (108–110) and
the roll contains the household.

> **Option closed: the private description.** The moment a notice is served, the description will be made
> in public or not at all. Every quiet exit the Wing had — a room, a table, a paper that never gets signed —
> closes, permanently, in the first ten chapters.

**2. The Register (111–120).** The Office does the thing an office does: it builds the record that will be
read in the hall, and what it builds is a roll of everything that touches him. Six women in a Field
minute. A visitors' book with sixty-one entries becoming more. A notebook with six lines a day and a
refused signature. A lift log with forty-one names on it that were never his to give away. A buff square
held by a woman whose name finally reaches a page (113). **And the instrument**: the Certification desk is
asked to certify that the counter-forensics tool can do what the Wing says it can do, and the only officer
outside the standing roll who ever wrote one of them is asked to confirm she wrote it (115–117), and she
cannot keep it to herself.

> **Option closed: he is not associated with these people for free.** Every person named on the roll is now
> a person the city can ask about him, and the roll is public, and it cannot be unrolled.

**3. The Live Column (121–133).** Midpoint span. The exhibit roll is built, the hall is booked, and Adeyemi
rules that the Office's own instrument must be shown to be capable of producing what its proponent says it
produces. The Certification desk produces its own best work: a certified counter-forensics run of four
years back on a different member, in which **the output's counterparty column lists six names and the
subject's own file says twenty-one recorded movements, and the margin reads *instrument out of tolerance*,
and four offices copied it forward and nobody reconciled it** (125–127). From there the reversal lands:
**the instrument is a subject-movement instrument. It reads a counterparty only when the subject touches a
person while it is running.** It cannot read a backlog, because it never stored one.

> **Option closed: he cannot be measured without moving.** A public reading of him is not a snapshot of
> the ledger. It is one name per moment he touches somebody in the room. **The Office cannot fill the room
> — a hearing is public and anybody may stand at the rail — and it does not need to.** All it has to do is
> tell him, politely and correctly, that the instrument works best in a busy hall. Scene at 129 (Tessa).

**4. The Choice (134–145).** Sixty-two days and one arithmetic. **The only movement available in the room that
does not put a name in the column is himself** — the account cannot be compelled, so the reading will be
empty whatever the Office does, and an empty reading closes nothing and gives the Office a certified
document saying that nothing left. He works out what the only available move is, and then he has to tell
six women about it, and the argument at 131 is real and it is about him and not about the Office. He
begins, at 133, keeping **a list of his own acts** — the thing he has never kept, because his ledger
records faces and never deeds. The turned count does not move. He does not practise on anybody.

> **Option closed: the last private thing he has.** Announcing it in advance requires somebody else to hold
> the hours. The record stops being his and becomes shared, and sharing it is the exact thing he has never
> done. Scene at 141 (Ines).

**5. The Room (146–150).** Climax and resolution. **Thursday the 29th of the third month, sixty-two days after
Chapter 100's line.** The Cobb Street hall, two hundred and eleven people on the floor and at the rail. He
is compelled by his own suspension to submit to the instrument, which is the Office's answer to a man who
refused a description in a room in Volume 2. He submits, it reads nothing, and **he says so before it is
run so that nobody is ambushed, which is the flaw moving out loud.**

Then he moves it. **Three times, on himself, in the open, at a table, in front of the city.** It lands in
the fourth rib on his left that Ines Duarte corrected him about on the seventeenth of last month, and it is
one to four seconds each, three times, in a hall. The counterparty column stays empty for four hours.

**And he turns.** He has felt one cold minute before, on the thirteenth of last month, in a corridor, with
a woman with a card. Three more in a hall is four. **He is a turned line, and a turned line can be led to
a source by a doctor, a tram operator, an accountant, or a relative** — and he has just made himself the
only person in Ostara who can be led to himself.

> **Options closed: he is never measured in public again, and he never spends on himself again.** Both are
> stated by him, in the hall, in advance, and he has to keep the second one for the rest of the book.

## 5. Midpoint reversal

**At 125–127. Proved on somebody else's paper, in the Office's own file, by arithmetic.**

The Certification desk certifies instruments on the Correction Office's evidence. Adeyemi's standing rule is
that an instrument offered in evidence must be shown to be capable of producing what its proponent says it
produces. The proponent's man is Ivo Sarn, and to satisfy the rule the desk produces its own best work: a
certified counter-forensics run of four years earlier on an unrelated member, in which the printed output
carries **six named counterparties** and the subject's case file of the same date carries **twenty-one
recorded movements**. Fifteen are unaccounted for. The margin reads *instrument out of tolerance*. The
discrepancy is carried forward four times and never reconciled, because every body that touched it was
waiting for the next reading and not for the last one's arithmetic.

**The reversal.** *The counter-forensics instrument is a subject-movement instrument. It reads a
counterparty only while it is observing the subject and the subject touches a person.* Six counterparties
means six separate live readings on six separate occasions. Fifteen of the twenty-one were read on paper,
by an office, from an account of what the subject said. **The instrument cannot read a backlog, because it
never stored one.**

**Why it is proved on somebody else's paper.** It is proved on a stranger's run, in a stranger's file, four
years old, by two numbers that do not match. **Nothing in this reversal is proved by anybody looking at
Lin Hao**, and that is the point: what saves him is an old clerical failure about a man nobody in the room
has heard of.

**What it changes.** Before 125, a public reading is the end of his privacy and the beginning of a summons
— the whole hundred and twelve at once, in public. After 127, a public reading is **one name per moment
he touches somebody in the room**, which is survivable only if he touches nobody, and the only movement
left in the room is his own. **The threat has not softened. It has changed species, from an exposure into
a demand on his body, which is worse for a man who hoards.**

### The rule for what this may not be restated as

`reviews/volume-02/volume-close.md` §4: Volume 2 stated its reversal once, proved it in Ch 75 on the
Office's own paper, and then **spent thirty-five chapters being attacked by offices that could not use it.**
That is the model. Volume 3's reversal is stated once and **four times at most in the whole volume**, as
follows:

1. **It is proved once, at 127, in a room with a clerk in it.** No interior monologue re-derives it. No
   character who was not present explains it to him afterwards.
2. **It may not be restated as *he is safe*.** It is not a reprieve. It converts an exposure into a demand
   on his own body, and every chapter after 127 must carry a cost.
3. **It may not be restated as *he is not a tank, he is a door*** in any form. B103 is not to be resolved
   before the Brother's File, and this reversal is a different fact about a different instrument.
4. **It may not be restated as *the account is an address*.** That was Volume 2's reversal, proved at Ch
   75, and it is not to be repeated.
5. **It may not be restated after Chapter 133** in any chapter, in any register, including in a joke.
   Chapters 134–150 are about the price and the choice and the room, not about the arithmetic.
6. **It may not be used by anyone who did not read the file.** Adeyemi is not permitted to know it until
   143, and Wren Kallo is not permitted to *say* it until 118, and Sarn is never told it at all.
7. **A reader who missed it must be able to get it from the scene at 125–127 alone.** It is proved by two
   printed numbers on a printed page and not by an explanation.

### The one mechanic this volume is not allowed to get wrong

**`bible/power-system.md` rule three: it only moves on contact with the person it came from.** A stranger
in a hall did not feed him. **Putting a hand on a stranger moves nothing and costs him real things** — the
*failure* clause: grey at one temple, a grey night, a night of sleep like being woken on the hour, in
proportion to what he was trying to move, and it never improves.

**Therefore: he cannot give to the room. Every plan that has him relieve a stranger in the hall is
mechanically false and costs him instead of helping him.** The only legal movements in a public hall are
the hundred and twelve people who fed him, and **himself** — the himself line is the established exception,
it was spent once in a corridor on the thirteenth of last month with a woman with a card, and it is the
only line in the book that reads *to* him rather than *from* him. **The climax is legal because of the
exception, and the exception is a line he has never seen and has never understood, and Volume 3 must not
explain why it exists.** That is B103 and it stays shut.

**The reservoir cannot be compelled, sedated or compelled into movement.** The Office cannot make the
account move by procedure. It can only make him stand in a room and ask. **That is why the hall works and
why he has to volunteer.**

## 6. Climax and resolution

**The climax, 145–148. Thursday the 29th of the third month, the Cobb Street hall, two hundred and eleven
people on the floor and at the rail, which is inside the two hundred and thirty on the plate by the door.**

He is not brought. He is **compelled**: a suspended band cannot be compelled *of* a thing, but a member
under a proceeding can be compelled *to submit to measurement*, and Volume 2's refusal is the reason the
proceeding exists. He attends because not attending is a finding.

The Office's case is one paragraph and a certified instrument. The paragraph is Sarn's and went into the
ruled sixth space of Anneke Roos's fourth book at **Chapter 120**, into a space that was empty on purpose:
*a settled mass in the possession of a member, accumulating by the member's own condition, no source
identified, no measure obtainable.* **It is not true.** It is the belief four offices held for four months
and it is false in exactly the direction that matters, because it makes him the maker instead of the door,
and a man who makes his own mass is a man who can be regulated.

He submits to the instrument. **It reads nothing, and he says so in the room before it is run**, so that
nobody is standing at the rail without being told. The counterparty column stays empty. Adeyemi rules that
an empty reading closes nothing and asks him one question, which is not an order: *does it move at all in
this room.*

**He answers it truthfully, in public, in advance, and then he does the thing.**

**THE IRREVERSIBLE DECISION: he moves it on himself, three times, at a table, in front of two hundred and
eleven people, having told the room in advance that he is going to and having told them why he cannot move
it on anybody else.**

- It is legal because the himself line is the one established exception, spent once on the thirteenth of
  last month in a corridor with a woman with a card.
- It lands in **the fourth rib on his left**, the one Ines Duarte corrected him out loud about on the
  seventeenth of last month — the thing he had been guarding since the spring out of habit and not out of
  sensation. One to four seconds, three times.
- He has felt a cold minute **once** before, in that corridor. **Three more is four.**

**COST, and none of it is refunded in the last two chapters:**

1. **He turns.** Eight turned becomes **nine**, and the ninth is him. A turned line is a person who is
   keeping track of the cold minutes, and a turned line **can be led to a source by a doctor, a tram
   operator, an accountant, or a relative.** He has made himself, in public, on the record, in front of the
   Correction Office and two hundred and eleven witnesses, the only member in Ostara who can be led to
   himself.
2. **He has said out loud, in a hall, that he will never do it again.** That is a promise with a number on
   it and he has to keep it for the rest of the book.
3. **A description of him exists that he wrote**, in four books and a transcript, and it cannot be
   unsaid. For the rest of this book the thing the Bureau holds about him is his own sentence.
4. **The band comes off suspension and goes back onto the instrument it was always on** — a requisition
   paper and a finding, not merit. The six does not become a rank. The plate has not moved a grain in five
   months and is three grains above a floor that has not moved either.

**THE RESOLUTION, and it is a consequence and not a neutral action.**

- **The only true description of him in Ostara is a certified document saying that in four hours in a public
  hall, nothing left.** The counterparty column is empty and the emptiness is certified, dated, filed and
  signed by the certification desk, **and it is the only document about him that has ever been true.**
- **No classification.** The paper dated the 19th of last month is still unsigned and goes back up three
  floors at four o'clock. **The sixth space in Anneke Roos's fourth book now carries his own sentence
  underneath Sarn's, with Sarn's struck through in her hand**, entered as *superseded in the room* and
  signed with her own name, which is the first thing she has signed in four months and which she says out
  loud is going to cost her something she has not been told about yet.
- **No rank earned. No rank lost. Plate 8,138, floor 8,141, tolerance three, 11,862 under-band, the whole
  volume.** Not one grain of the account is converted and no chapter contains a figure that would allow it.
- **The inquiry closes on his own words**, which means the Wing loses the calendar it had been using, which
  means **for the first time since the eighteenth of last month nobody in Ostara sets his dates.**
- **He keeps the ledger. Every one of the hundred and twelve lines and every line added since.** No name
  reached the roll, the transcript, the column, or the Wing.
- **And he stops being the only person in Ostara who knows it.** Six women and one clerk hold it in pieces,
  by his decision and against his whole character, **because he announced four hours of himself in advance
  and somebody had to hold the hours.** Wren Kallo cannot keep a secret — that is fixed, that is her whole
  cost, that is what made her an excellent auditor and a poor friend — and by the last page of this volume
  **the secret is four people deep and she has told three of them.** He decided that on purpose, having
  calculated that a woman outside the standing roll who cannot keep a secret is a woman who will have to
  answer for it, and that he would rather she answered for it as a party than discovered it as a stranger.
- **The household notebook stays theirs.** He objected on their behalf in Chapter 109 and is still refused
  a signature on it at 150 — which is the last beat of the volume's central relationship and the correct
  one. He has given away the hours and he still cannot sign their book.

## 7. Emotional resolution

**Where the flaw is and where it has to be.**

| Volume | The move |
| --- | --- |
| **1** | hiding what he has, to learning what it is |
| **2** | learning what it is, to refusing to be described by anybody |
| **3** | **refusing to be described, to announcing four hours of himself in advance to two hundred and eleven people and then doing it, and giving away the only hours somebody else had to hold** |

The flaw is that he hoards. He hoarded his plate and his rank and his ledger and his household and his own
silence. **Volume 3 does not make him braver and it does not make him public. It makes him predictable** —
the man who has hidden one true thing for five volumes states it in a room, with a date, before it happens,
so that nobody at the rail is ambushed. That is the opposite of hoarding and it is not the opposite of
hiding, and he should not be comfortable with it.

**He spends on himself, for the second time in his life, in the worst possible place, at his own expense,
and he tells two hundred and eleven people it is going to happen before he does it.** He has never spent
on another person for a decision of his own since Chapter 48 and he spends on himself twice more.

**The felt shape of it: he leaves the Cobb Street hall nine turned lines deep instead of eight, with the
ninth being himself, a sentence of his own in four books, and lighter than he has ever been** — and there
is nothing in him like relief. What is in him instead is the specific vertigo of a man who has announced
himself to a city and found that the announcement did not help at all, because the thing it was supposed
to protect was not them. **Nobody in this volume tells him it was brave and nobody in this volume is wrong.**

## 8. The new question that motivates Volume 4

Volume 4 (*Six*) asks: **Lin Hao must build a household that can hold rank six, or he stands at six alone
and is taken apart for it.**

Volume 3 hands it exactly this.

The description now exists and it is his own, and a description in his own hand is a record, and a record
is worth exactly as much as the number of people willing to be inside it. **The only instrument in Ostara
that can hold a band of six is a standing household**, because the band binds to the plate and the plate
binds to the bond, and a man standing at six on his own plate with no bond behind it is a man who can be
requisitioned one room at a time.

**And Volume 3 has just demonstrated, in public, that he pays for everything out of himself.** He stood at
a table in a hall and did it three times and told nobody to stop him, because there was nobody in the room

## 12. The calendar, and the defect this volume inherits

**The repository's standing lesson is that a count is not a reading and an inference is not an anchor.**

inference**. It is written here so the next writer subtracts a date instead of trusting a gate.
