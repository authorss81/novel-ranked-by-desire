## 5. The fixed calendar and the fixed facts

- **July 1 is a Saturday. July 6, 13, 20 and 27 are Thursdays. July 10 a Monday, July 12 a Wednesday,
  August 1 a Tuesday.** Derived from Volume 5's own derivation out of the twenty-ninth of June. **No
  chapter in Volume 6 names any weekday but Thursday.**
- **The monthly crew repaints the side board on the first; the Thursday pass is a different crew in a
  different shade and does not touch it.** Two crews, two shades.
- **May has thirty days and no thirty-first.** The last working day of a month is the thirtieth.
- **Anchors for every count in this volume:** the finding filed **23 June**; the true report **6 July**;
  Petra off roll **4 June**; the sworn page **14 June**; the offer **3 July**, expiring **12 July**; the
  term agreed on Lantern Row **the night of 9 July**.
- **Batch 0001 printed the working for the one derived date it could not read off the page:** the day Ines
  can be put at a desk where she is the subject is **the twentieth of July** — the sixth, plus a fortnight,
  which is the ordinary answer to a question of that kind, and which is not the same clock as the
  fourteen-day term printed on the charge itself.

1. **The Creditors Come In First — 251–260. WRITTEN.** He calls in the debts out loud, one at a time, in
   his house, and every one of them is refused, given, or taken by somebody else. Runa takes a
   carbon; Sun asks to be counted; Tessa takes his plate number and nine years; Beatrix takes the price
   that died on the twelfth and is not angry about it; he tells the kitchen — **eight women, four on the
   roll and four on nothing** — that he keeps a ledger with nine turned lines in it and the ninth is
   himself. **Solveig Rask enters** and prices four debts and refuses the
   fifth and refuses the reservoir. **The broker takes her papers off the table on the fourteenth, and the
   kitchen table, which had held this house since the twenty-second of March, is bare from that day.**
   **He disputes his own sworn figure of the fourteenth of June in a room with a form number on
   it, and says the number is seven.** Isolde Marchetti sings under the ranks wall and is named.
2. **The Head May Give Up A Room — 261–270.** *Plan.* **261 is Isolde's scene and the household becomes public.**
   The offer's second half: what a broker is permitted to do with a head who has gone to the keeper of
   the real books. The instrument nobody has used — a head may give up a room — comes back, and so does
   Sun's re-issue, and the only hand that can certify it is a woman at a desk where she is the subject.
3. **The Examination Passes — 271–280.** *Plan.* **271: Ines comes back, on her own terms, having filed a
   correction she has not filed yet, and does not live under his roof again.** The Vant's annual sitting
   is named and dated. The tail on the fourth page. Rask's date.
4. **The Figure That Cannot Be Refused — 281–290.** *Plan.* **The reservoir gets a figure on a dated page
   in a book that reconciles**, and the batch that writes it is the batch that pays for it. 289.
5. **Terms — 291–300.** *Plan.* 299, and Volume 6's answer to `outline/series.md`'s sentence.

**Escalation, stated once so no batch has to re-derive it.** Volume 5 was about a woman deciding whether
she can be with a man she has to audit, and the cost was hers. **Volume 6 he stops investigating and
starts invoicing**, and the escalation runs the other way: the household's private record becomes the

who has started putting himself on paper.**
