# Series Outline — Ranked by Desire

## One-sentence premise

Twenty years after the Veil broke over Ostara and turned power into a weekly public score, a washroom
attendant who cleans toilets for the Bureau of Merit is told his merit reading came back unsettled — an
account with no number, fed by every unrecorded good deed in his reach — and he has to climb nine ranks
to open his brother's sealed file without ever letting the city find out what he is carrying.

---

## The protagonist

### External want

**Rank nine.** Not as ambition. Rank nine is the only door to Lin Wei's file, the file is sealed above
every other door in Ostara, and the file is the only answer to the question Lin Hao has been carrying
since Chapter 1: was his brother right, and what did it cost him to say so.

The want is restated at the end of every volume in the same words, because that is what a want does. Each
restatement must cost something, and by Volume 9 the words have changed meaning, because by Volume 9 he
wants nine in order to *close* the file and not in order to read it.

### Internal need

**To accept that being seen is not the same as being used.**

He has built an entire life on the distinction: be legible in the one respect that costs nothing, be
illegible in everything else. The reservoir rewards him for exactly this and so the flaw and the power are
the same object, which is the whole design. Every volume takes a piece of the privacy away, and at Volume
10 he has to stand in a public room and let an auditor read him correctly, in front of the Council, and
mean it.

### Central flaw

**He hoards.**

Every advantage that comes to him feels like theft from someone smaller, so he keeps it small, keeps it
quiet, refuses to compete openly, and refuses to let any single advantage grow large enough to protect
anybody. The hoard is why he is nearly taken in Volume 2. It is why he is helpless in Volume 4 with a
rank he cannot use. It is why Ines has to leave in Volume 5, because she cannot be with a man who will
not spend what he has while she is being watched. And its final form, in Volume 10, is that he decides
the reservoir belongs to *him* — that he has the standing to give it back — which is the same instinct
with more authority and a worse argument.

### Fear

That his brother said the right thing. That the number really was wrong, that Lin Wei saw exactly what it
was, and that Lin Hao is the walking receipt. Every time the ledger shows him a name he recognises, the
fear gets a little more specific.

### Final change

He stops hoarding. In Volume 10 he gives away the largest unrecoverable thing in the city while standing
in front of everyone, having already decided that he is not the person who owns it.

## The central mystery

**Lin Wei said the number was wrong, in public, and by morning he was gone. What did he do, and where is
he?**

Fixed answer, never changed, planned before Chapter 1:

Lin Wei did not smuggle, bribe, or steal. He found that a whole class of unrecorded deeds was settling
into accounts with no instruments behind them, traced it, and identified what it was. Then he used the
one power in Ostara that needs no rank and no witness: he **donated his entire accumulated merit —
214,000 grains — into an account that exists on no ledger.** The Bureau could not classify what he had
done, and an authority that cannot classify a thing must remove the thing. He has been in the
Classification Wing ever since as a held account. The account has been filling itself, from strangers,
ever since. Lin Hao is its address, because Lin Hao is the only person in the city low enough to be an
acceptable recipient for it.

**The mystery is delivered in four beats, and no fifth beat is permitted.**

| Volume | Beat | What the reader learns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Shape | The reservoir is not natural and not his. It is an address. Something was sent to it. |
| 7 | Mechanism | He learns the Channel and, with it, that he can route merit — and that a transfer of this kind requires a Seal, and his brother's Seal is the one that matches. |
| 8 | Proof | The file. The transfer receipt. A room in the Classification Wing with a man in it. |
| 10 | Meaning | The account was never Lin Wei's. It was everyone's who never got written down. Closing it is the only way to make Lin Wei's objection true. |

## The antagonist ladder

Each rung is worse than the last *and* more personal, and the opposition never stops being reasonable.

**1. The procedure — Cassius Rhee, deputy warden of the Fourth Branch (V1).**
A man trying to stabilise nine thousand people who are three hundred grains short, who has found a
portable merit source inside his own staff and would like to own it. He offers Lin Hao a job. That is the
fight in Volume 1: not a villain, a recruitment.

**2. The measurement — Alma Quayle, Classification Wing (V2, returns in V7).**
Wants to say what he is. Her son scored two and died in a queue. She is the first person who comes close
to classifying him and the reason Volume 2 closes with a *held account* rather than a defeat.

**3. The exposure — Yusuf Adeyemi, Correction Office (V3).**
Runs public hearings. Lin Hao's whole advantage is invisibility and Adeyemi's profession is forcing
invisible things into the open. He is honest, hated, and the only antagonist who becomes an ally.

**4. The cost of being loved — Ines Duarte's superiors, the Vestry, the Council bench (V4, V5).**
The opposition stops being an institution and becomes the household. Volume 5 is a woman deciding whether
she can be with a man she has to audit.

**5. The arithmetic — Solveig Rask, keeper of the Vant's real books (V6).**
The one person who has run the numbers on what the reservoir is worth and got it right, and who therefore
knows exactly what it costs to keep it legal.

**6. The rule — the Concil of the Nine, chaired by Anouk Ferreira (V7, V9, V10).**
They are not corrupt. They are nine people who have held a city still for twenty years because they were
countable, and they are right about why it worked, and Volume 7 is about learning that the reason it
worked was a thing they did not know about and cannot survive knowing.

**7. The file — Perrin Sowe, Veil-Research (V5, V8, V10).**
Lin Wei's colleague. The man who noticed the same thing and filed it instead of donating it. He is the
exact, unbearable version of Lin Hao's own flaw, and he is the final human problem of the book.

**8. The system itself (V10).** Not a person. A set of rules that has survived twenty years by being
correct about everything except the thing nobody measured, and which must be reopened, in public, by
someone it will classify.

## Power stages and their costs

The escalation is **investigating → manipulating → breaking → being reacted to → choosing what to lose.**
Full mechanics in `bible/power-system.md`.

**Stage one — investigating (Ch 1–100).** He learns it moves, learns it hurts, spends by accident and
then on purpose.
**Cost:** cold minutes. Two or three turned lines. A stranger on a tram who cannot get warm for a week.
**Irreversible decision (Ch 48):** at the Winter Review he takes the open-question slot and spends the
reservoir **on himself**, once, to answer a true question in front of nine thousand people. It puts him at
six. It closes the option of being invisible forever, and it puts a line in his own ledger that says
himself, which should never be there, and it turns three feeders at once because nothing he has ever done
was that size. **This is the "once" referenced by the Volume 2 goal.**

**Stage two — manipulating (Ch 101–200).** He converts relief into leverage, and leverage into rank, by
engineering the public attested deeds that score him.
**Cost:** every band he buys is paid for by somebody's cold minute, and he does the arithmetic himself.
He becomes a man who manufactures good deeds in order to be allowed to be good in private.
**Irreversible decision (Ch 200):** he builds a standing household, which under Ostaran law binds him to
the highest rank in it — meaning every woman who joins him raises his legal exposure as much as his
standing.

**Stage three — breaking (Ch 201–300).** He learns the Channel: he can route a draw through himself into
someone else so the Exchange credits them, which means he can manufacture merit out of unrecorded help
and move it where he likes.
**Cost:** whoever he routes through becomes a target. The first person he routes through is Ines Duarte,
and it is done to save her and it costs her a piece of her own memory. Ines leaves in Chapter 246.
**Irreversible decision (Ch 298):** he calls in every debt he owes, including one Marguerite never told
him was owed, and puts the Vant's whole exposure of him in front of the Concil in exchange for the
Brother's File being opened one folio early.

**Stage four — the system reacts (Ch 301–400).** The Bureau does not hunt him; it *reclassifies the
problem*, and the Council issues the standing requisition that finally reaches him. He learns the
reservoir has an address and the address has a name.
**Cost:** to keep one turned person alive he has to stop spending entirely for eleven days, and every band
he has built goes flat while he does it, in public, on a board he is standing in front of.
**Irreversible decision (Ch 372):** he opens his brother's file while the requisition is live, which
means there is no version of this where he walks away.

**Stage five — he chooses (Ch 401–500).** He reaches nine. He learns what the account is. He decides to
return every grain of unrecorded merit to the people it came from.
**Cost:** everything. Rank, standing, household immunity, the file's usefulness, and the private ledger he
has lived inside for five hundred chapters. He ends the book at rank one.

  Wren Kallo, Agnete Lindqvist, Petra Ilyin, Isolde Marchetti and Dagmar Oyelowo, and each of those is

- **Chapters 454, 465, 474 and 483 are Volume 10's four labelled scenes** in `outline/ending.md`. The

  the clinic's records between them. Separating them would break the batch plan and the title bank. They
  are the only two short gaps in the book.

A scene may be moved one or two chapters when a volume outline is written, but **never past the next
entry in this row**, and a movement that pushes a gap to sixteen is a failed budget and the batch is
fixed rather than accepted.

## The volume progression

Ten volumes. Fifty chapters each, 500 total. Each line is the volume's single-sentence goal in the
required form.

**Volume 1 — Rank Four (chapters 1–50).**
> In this volume, **Lin Hao must hold rank six through the Winter Review**, or **the reservoir is requisitioned by the Fourth Branch and he never gets it back**.

**Volume 2 — The Reservoir (chapters 51–100).**
> In this volume, **Lin Hao must learn what is in him before he spends it on himself again**, or **the Classification Wing classifies the account and empties him out at rank one**.

**Volume 3 — The Reading (chapters 101–150).**
> In this volume, **Lin Hao must survive being measured in public and keep his ledger**, or **a public reading of him becomes a public reading of everyone who ever fed it**.

**Volume 4 — Six (chapters 151–200).**
> In this volume, **Lin Hao must build a household that can hold rank six**, or **he stands at six alone and is taken apart for it**.

**Volume 5 — The Auditor's Price (chapters 201–250).**
> In this volume, **Lin Hao must keep Ines Duarte off his file while she starts wanting to be off her own**, or **the only person who can read him honestly is removed from his life**.

**Volume 6 — The Broker's Terms (chapters 251–300).**
> In this volume, **Lin Hao must call in every debt he owes before the creditors call in him**, or **the Vant's books put a price on the reservoir that he cannot refuse**.

**Volume 7 — Seven (chapters 301–350).**
> In this volume, **Lin Hao must survive classification**, or **the reservoir is requisitioned by the Concil of the Nine and stops being his**.

**Volume 8 — The Brother's File (chapters 351–400).**
> In this volume, **Lin Hao must find what his brother found and keep the woman who told him**, or **the only account he has ever had closes on him and he is a man with a number**.

**Volume 9 — Rank Nine (chapters 401–450).**
> In this volume, **Lin Hao must reach rank nine**, or **Lin Wei's file stays shut and the account stays open**.

**Volume 10 — Returned (chapters 451–500).**
> In this volume, **Lin Hao must decide who the reservoir belongs to and give it back while the Council watches**, or **Ostara keeps measuring the wrong thing for another twenty years**.

### Pressure rotation

Not ten volumes of the same crisis. Each volume changes one of: his identity, the meaning of the
mechanic, the reliability of an ally, the political balance, his moral position, the interpretation of the
mystery, the cost of victory, or the *kind* of problem he can solve.

- V1 local survival, V2 body and mechanism, V3 public and political, V4 household and training,
  V5 character conflict, V6 money and negotiation, V7 institutional, V8 mystery, V9 ascent and action,
  V10 moral and emotional.

## The final resolution

**Final external conflict:** the Council must be persuaded, in an open session, that the largest
unrecorded account in Ostara should be returned rather than classified — and the Council's entire
twenty-year survival rests on the opposite proposition. Ferreira will argue, sincerely, that Lin Wei
disappeared because he handed something to a stranger, and that Lin Hao is doing the same thing with more
power, and that the only honest answer to a person who gives away the city's unrecorded merit is to put
them somewhere quiet where they cannot do it again. She is not wrong.

**Final internal choice:** he gives it away *publicly*, on the Board, in the Reading Square, in front of
the city — knowing that the act destroys his rank, his standing, his household's protection, and the
only evidence he has of his brother — because the reservoir is not his to keep quietly. His whole
character is the man who takes what arrives and never gives it away.

**Final emotional answer:** that he was never carrying his brother's money. He was carrying an apology
that two men could not deliver and that the whole city had been paying into without knowing. And that
the women who stayed did not stay because he was powerful, and were still there after he was not.

**Planned ending state of the world:** Ostara does not abolish the Board. It cannot — the numbers in it
are the only thing holding nine thousand Cordwain families upright, and Lin Hao knows that better than
anyone by Volume 10. What changes is one thing, permanently and publicly: the Board gains a second line,
which from the week after the Council session reads, for every citizen, **unrecorded — carried by** — and
which almost nobody on earth knows what to do with. Eleven thousand people in the Vestry go home and know
exactly. Lin Hao goes back to a branch office on a night shift at rank one. Nobody thanks him.

## Why this is not another fleet novel

Three things are deliberate and must not be diluted:

**The antagonist is the metric.** The Bureau is not hiding a conspiracy. It is measuring the measurable,
publishing it, and being right about it for twenty years while the unmeasured half of the city's kindness
falls into one man's chest. Every fight in the book is with a rule that works.

**The power cannot take.** It gives back only what was given to it. That single restriction means no
woman is ever compelled, sedated, resisted, or overridden, in ten volumes and fifty-one scenes — and it
means every woman in the household wants him on her own terms, fast, and is glad about it. The erotic
engine and the ethical engine are the same object.

**The reveal is the cost.** The answer to the central mystery is not information Lin Hao is relieved to
receive. It is the reason he has to empty an account he has spent five volumes building, in front of
watchers, at the end. The ending is not a reward for the climb. The climb is what makes the ending
possible and it is what the ending throws away.
