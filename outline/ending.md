# Ending — Volume 10, "Returned" (chapters 451–500)

The ending is planned here before Chapter 1 is written and it is not improvised in the prose. If any batch
later wants a different destination, that is a canon change and it is flagged, not silently adopted.

---

## What Volume 10 has to do

Lin Hao reaches rank nine at Chapter 409 and therefore opens Lin Wei's file at 372 — which means by the
time Volume 10 begins he already knows everything this document knows. **Volume 10 is not about the
answer. It is about what he does with it.** The mystery is closed before the volume starts. Nothing new
is introduced. There is no last-minute cosmic threat, no second hidden enemy, no twist in the final ten
chapters.

The question of the final volume, stated once and then never restated in those words again:

> An account with no name on it, worth more than every name on the Board, held by a man the Board cannot
> classify, at a Council session where nine people hold the seals that can requisition it from him in
> nine minutes. Who does it belong to?

---

## Starting state

**Lin Hao, 30.** Rank nine, black tile, the first new name on the top row in living memory. Standing
household of nine, recognised at the Terrace at Chapter 402. He is, by Volume 10, the most protected man
in Ostara and the most hunted, and the protection and the hunt are the same fact.

**What he holds:** a file, opened. A transfer receipt naming his brother. A ledger with a thousand
faces. An account with roughly 1.4 million grains of unrecorded merit in it — more than the entire
measured standing of the Kestrel Hill terraces.

**What he wants:** to empty it and be allowed to still be a person afterwards.

**What is against him:** Anouk Ferreira, chair of the Concil of the Nine, holding a majority seal
position on requisition, Yusuf Adeyemi holding the procedural chair and therefore the argument, and
Perrin Sowe, who is not on the Council at all and is the only person in the room who actually knows what
Lin Wei did and why.

---

## Movement one — the offer (451–460)

The Council convenes not to punish him. That is the trap. Ferreira opens by offering him exactly what he
came into the city wanting at fifteen: **a standing at the Terrace, a chair, a name that outlives him,
the Ninth Seal used for the good of the city**, with the sole condition that the reservoir be classified
once, cleanly, by Alma Quayle, and thereafter be *his* to spend in whatever direction he liked.

She has calculated correctly that this is a better offer than he will get anywhere else. She has also
calculated, correctly, that a classified account with one owner is the only version of this story that
does not end in a city dispute.

**Turn at 456:** he accepts the offer in front of the Council, spends four chapters appearing to accept
it, and is actually performing the one thing the reservoir permits — spending it, heavily and
openly, so that by the time classification is administered there is nothing in it to classify. Ferreira
watches this happen and lets it happen, because a man draining his own account is not a threat.

**Cost:** every grain he spends is a cold minute, publicly, in the Reading, in front of nine thousand
people, and the turned-line count in Volume 10 is measured in hundreds. Ada, Wren's contact in the
Auditorate, goes grey at one temple in Ch 458 and does not recover it.

**Irreversible decision (Ch 459):** he spends so hard and so fast that he takes a permanent injury —
the reservoir pays out faster than a human frame can absorb it and something in his chest is *spent*,
permanently. He buys that by it with a cough that stays with him through the rest of the book. He
knows what it will cost. He does it anyway, in front of witnesses, on purpose.

**First scene (Ch 454):** with Ines Duarte, who is reading him for the Council and who knows exactly what
he is doing and has decided to file a report that will be true and useless. What changes: she is the only
person in Volume 10 who never lies to him and never lets him lie to himself, and they have no more than
one conversation about it.

---

## Movement two — the hearing (461–470)

Adeyemi forces a **public hearing**, because that is his instrument and because he has read Lin Hao's
entire rise and knows that the only defence against a requisition is a hearing anyone can attend. It is
hostile, ugly, and the best thing that has ever happened to Lin Hao.

Four days of hearing. The reservoir is discussed in public for the first time in twenty years of
Bureau practice. The Church of the Quiet Number sends eleven hundred people. Agnete Lindqvist stands up
in a room where nobody can be compelled and spends three hours giving testimony nobody can use against
anyone, which is the most Agnete thing in the book.

**Turn at 466:** Sowe testifies. He does not defend Lin Wei. He says, on the record, in front of nine
seals, that Lin Wei was right, that he knew, that he filed instead of giving, and that the file he filed
is the reason Lin Wei was removed. The book does not punish him. It simply puts it in the record, and
Lin Hao has to sit there while the most honourable man he knows destroys his own career to say a true
sentence.

**Turn at 469:** Agnete's church, in eleven thousand voices, offers a **competing claim** — that the
account belongs to the unrecorded, not to Lin Hao, and that the correct disposition is distribution rather
than return. This is a genuine, well-argued, morally serious opposition to what Lin Hao intends, and it
comes from people he respects, and it nearly wins. **He has to argue against the Church in public.**
That is the volume's real fight.

**Second scene (Ch 465):** with Beatrix Halm, on a roof, in the hour before the hearing, where she tells
him that the city's turned lines have been triangulated twice and that the triangulation came from
inside her own division, and he has to decide whether to spend her or trust her. What changes: he trusts
her, and it is the last time in the book that trusting somebody is free.

---

## Movement three — the family (471–480)

The household has to be told. Not the Council. **The women.** Because under standing-household law Lin
Hao cannot empty an account that any of them has drawn against, and three of them have, and one of them
— Petra Ilyin — has drawn against it unknowingly for eleven years under somebody else's promise.

**Ch 473:** the household scene. Nine women, one account, and a number. The argument is real and it is
ugly and Marguerite Vale is on Petra's side before she is on Lin Hao's. Nobody is a decoration.

**Turn at 476:** the three who have drawn against the account do it deliberately, publicly, and take the
cold minutes themselves. That is the emotional peak of the volume and it is the answer to a question the
book has been asking since Chapter 7: the reservoir gives back only what was given to it, and this is
what it looks like when nine people decide they want to be given something back.

**Cost:** the three who draw against it take the cold minutes *and* the depletion. Nadia Ferrous, who
can only ever go down, is the one who takes the worst of it, and she is fine about it, and he is not.

**Third scene (Ch 474):** with Marguerite Vale, who has been waiting five volumes to be told and who is
the only one of them who already knew, because the Vant's real books have had an entry for the reservoir
since Chapter 200 and nobody at the Vant has ever been able to close it.

---

## Movement four — the return (481–490)

The Council session. Nine seals. Ferreira's argument in full, and it is the best speech in the book:

A man handed the city's unrecorded merit to a stranger and vanished. That is what Lin Wei did. His
brother has now been handed five times that by every stranger who was ever helped in a doorway, and he
has grown rich on it while telling himself it was an accident. The honest answer to a person who gives
away the city's unrecorded merit is a quiet room, a held account, and a form — because the alternative
is a man deciding, alone, on behalf of a million people, who was owed what.

**She is not wrong.** Lin Hao knows she is not wrong. That is the point: the antagonist's case is
correct and the protagonist still has to beat it, by being willing to do something she is not willing to
do — which is to give it away while standing in the open with his name on it, so that nobody is doing it
on anybody's behalf.

**Ch 486:** he does it. Not by argument. By opening the account, in the Reading Square, on the Board, in
front of the city, and letting the return run — a million grains of unrecorded merit handed back to the
people it came from, one cold minute at a time, across the whole of Ostara, over four days. The Council
can requisition it. They cannot requisition it once it is in a thousand hands.

**The cost, exact:** 1.4 million grains out. Rank nine to rank one. Every permission he has ever bought
gone. The file — Lin Wei's file — permanently closed, because the only standing that could open it is the
standing he just spent, and the Council's own record will be amended to remove it, permanently, on the
same page, in the same session.

**Ferreira loses and agrees she lost.** She is the only antagonist who is not diminished by it. She signs
the amendment herself. In the last three chapters she and Lin Hao are, in a very small way, on the same
side.

**Fourth scene (Ch 483):** with Tessa Okonkwo, who has been absorbing other people's injuries for thirty
chapters and who is, at the end, the one person in the household who can take a fraction of what he is
paying out. She takes it. It is not affection. It is medicine. It is both. What it changes: she is the
only one who could have survived the ending and she chose this.

---

## Movement five — the aftermath (491–500)

**Ch 491–493:** the city. The Board's second line appears the following Thursday and reads, for every
citizen, **unrecorded — carried by**. Almost nobody knows what it means. Cordwain is briefly convinced
the Bureau has broken and there are two days of quiet before the numbers come back in. Nothing is
resolved and nothing is pretended to be. Eleven thousand people in the Vestry go home knowing.

**Ch 488 (interleaved):** **Dagmar Oyelowo.** The intake clerk with no gift. The last quiet thing in the
book. She says out loud what she wants and gets it and neither of them pretends it is anything else. It
is a small, decent, adult scene between two people who are exactly what they are.

**Ch 494–497:** the household, dispersed and intact. Where each of them is. What each one does in the
weeks after. Three of them never live with him again and all three of them come to his shift.

**Ch 498:** the last chapter. A night shift in a different branch office, rank one on a plate in his
pocket and a white tile with his name on it on the Board two streets away. He cleans washrooms, because
he is rank one and rank one gets assigned work, and because he asked to. Ines is auditing the branch and
has been for three months and neither of them has said anything about it. Beatrix brings him the turned
lines from the district, and there are four hundred of them, and none of them are his.

He is not a poor man. Rank one means subsistence guaranteed, which means he will never be hungry again,
and it means assigned work, which means he will never choose his own hours again. **He chose it.**

And in the account, which belongs to nobody, which is the point of the entire book and the only thing he
was ever really carrying: a number.

Not zero.

## The ending, precisely

- **Final external conflict:** convincing a room of nine that the city's largest unmeasured asset should
  be given back rather than classified, against a correct argument that it should not.
- **Final internal choice:** to give it away in the open, having spent nine volumes hoarding, knowing it
  costs rank, standing, protection, his brother's file, and the ledger he has lived inside since
  Chapter 2.
- **Final emotional answer:** he was never carrying his brother's money. He was carrying an apology two
  men could not deliver, that the whole city had been paying into for three years without knowing. And
  the women who stayed stayed because he was not the account.
- **Final image:** rank one, a white tile, a night shift, four hundred turned lines that are not his, and
  a number in an account that belongs to nobody.

## Mysteries: answered and deliberately open

**Fully answered, by the end, and not re-opened.**

- What Lin Wei did. Donated 214,000 grains into an account on no ledger, on purpose, three years ago.
- Where he is. Classification Wing, third sub-level, a held account, alive, and named in Ch 486 when
  Lin Hao reads his own brother's number on a Board amendment.
- What the reservoir is. The recipient account, filling itself, addressed to the only person in Ostara
  low enough to receive it.
- Why it went to Lin Hao and not anyone else. Because of what he is: a man nobody audits. It is a job
  description, not a destiny, and that is the last joke the book tells about itself.
- Why there is no rank ten. Because the Ninth Seal is nine held accounts, and the Council is the standing
  custodian of all nine, and there has never been a tenth because nobody has ever been willing to hand
  their whole life to nine strangers.

**Deliberately open, and must stay open.**

- **How many feeders there are in total.** The ledger shows a thousand faces. The return touches a
  million. The book never reconciles the two numbers and Lin Hao explicitly does not know.
- **Whether the return actually reached the right people**, or only people who were owed something and
  happened to be near him on four particular days. The second line on the Board is true and nearly
  useless, and the book ends without deciding whether it was an act of justice or an act of showing off.
  This is the correct ending and it must not be resolved.
- **What happens in Cordwain and Lamplit Row to people whose whole life was the Board.** One chapter, 495,
  about a family that loses its file entirely. No answer.
- **Whether Perrin Sowe is forgiven.** Ines files the correction that would clear him at Chapter 498 and
  does not press it. Neither of them discusses it. That is the last exchange between them and it is about
  something else.
- **Whether the Bureau survives.** It does, because Lin Hao defends it himself in Chapter 493 and is
  more persuasive than any of the people who wanted it broken.

## Canon-change guard

Nothing in this document may be contradicted later without being flagged in `state/open-threads.md` as a
proposed canon change. The following are **fixed** and safe to rely on in any batch:

- Lin Wei is alive, classified, in the Classification Wing, and never escapes it.
- The reservoir is returned in full in Chapter 486. It is never replenished.
- Lin Hao ends at rank one, working a branch office night shift, at the Ninth Terrace household's
  protection withdrawn and the household intact anyway.
- The Church of the Quiet Number is not dissolved, not vindicated, and not converted into an institution.
- Ferreira lives. Sowe lives. Adeyemi is made Registrar of Unrecorded Merit, which is a joke he tells
  nobody the meaning of.
- No partner dies. No partner is left behind by choice that she does not explain.
