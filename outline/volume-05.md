# Volume Five — The Auditor's Price (chapters 201–250)

> In this volume, **Lin Hao must keep Ines Duarte off his file while she starts wanting to be off her
> own**, or **the only person who can read him honestly is removed from his life**.

**This file was reconstructed during the Batch 0001 review repair, eleven chapters into the volume, and
that has to be said plainly because it changes how much weight anything in it carries.** Volumes One
through Four each had an owned outline written before their first chapter. This one did not; the Volume 4
close audit should have written it and did not, and for ten chapters the two Volume 5 batch prompts have
substituted the Batch 0001 head block in `state/current.md` and the close record at the head of
`state/open-threads.md`. Those two files remain the authoritative record for what has actually been
written.

**So read this file in two registers, and do not blur them.**

- **§1 to §5 are written fact.** They are derived from `outline/series.md`, `outline/ending.md`,
  `bible/characters.md`, `outline/volume-04.md` §8, and Chapters 201–210 as they stand on the page.
  Nothing in §1 to §5 may be contradicted by a later batch.
- **§6 to §11 are a plan.** They are the nearest honest reading of a volume whose middle third has not
  been written yet, they are revisable by the batch that owns each stretch of it, and **a batch writer
  who finds a better answer on the page takes the better answer and rewrites this file in the same
  commit.** The one thing a later batch may not do is quietly contradict §1 to §5.

## 1. The sentence, and where it came from

`outline/volume-04.md` §8 closed Volume 4 with a question and not an answer:

> **What does a woman do who is the only person in Ostara who can read the record correctly, when the
> record is now her own household's?**

**Batch 0001 answers it in Chapter 205, twenty-three pages before the chapter that will have to live with
the answer.** She reads the household's file out loud on her own floor, in date order, and finds two
things: her own division raised her premises and did not tell her, and her own line on the certification
schedule carries no instrument. **She does not say why. Nothing in Volume 5 before Chapter 246 may make
her say why**, and `bible/characters.md` does not record a reason.

The volume's real shape, therefore, is not a mystery about Ines. **It is a woman deciding whether to be
a fact about herself on purpose.** Batch 0001's last line is her asking to be put on a sworn list on his
word rather than on an instrument — the weakest standing available to a person in this city, voidable,
and gone the day his plate goes — because for four years the one thing she owned was that she was in
nobody's book, and she worked out on the twenty-third that being outside a book is not the same as being
safe in a room.

## 6. THE MOVEMENTS AS THEY NOW STAND, 201 to 250

**Movements 1 and 2 are WRITTEN. Movement 1 is Chapters 201–210. Movement 2 is Chapters 211–220 and
this batch rewrote this section against the page, because a plan written eleven chapters into a volume
is an act of invention and a plan written twenty chapters into one is an act of cowardice.** What
follows is the nearest honest reading of the volume's remaining thirty chapters. A later batch that
finds a better answer takes the better answer and rewrites this file in the same commit.

1. **The Auditor's Price — 201–210. WRITTEN.** He certifies six and it costs Sun Meiling her only
   standing; she takes it back a night earlier, in pencil, on her own row. Ines asks to be carried.
2. **The Enforcer — 211–220. WRITTEN.** Petra Ilyin comes to collect from Marguerite and stays. **The
   movement's subject turned out not to be the certificate at all: it is that a household acquires
   members inside obligations it cannot see.** Eleven years before Batch 0002, Marguerite secured a
   thirty-year account against a woman instead of against money, and the woman could not leave a room,
   and neither of them ever asked the other a question. **What it spent:** the lead's plate, pledged in
   a chained book with no right of withdrawal; **what it bought:** a debt for Volume 6, a standing a
   stranger can call on the twenty-seventh, and a household that is now on a public board in ink.
   **The batch's own line, which is the volume's line:** *a man who for five volumes kept himself small
   and unspent puts the one number he has ever had into a chained book because six women he certified a
   fortnight ago had become an asset with his name at the top of it.*
3. **The Certificate — 221–230. NOT WRITTEN.** The twenty-seventh of June is in Batch 0004 and this is
   where the batch has to decide what it is for. **Tessa Okonkwo said both sentences out loud on purpose
   in Ch 207: a certificate is a document, and a safe house is the absence of documents, and the moment
   it exists somebody is entitled to ask what is at that address.** **Batch 0003 runs the eleventh to the
   twentieth of June and cannot reach the twenty-sixth or the twenty-seventh, so the certificate is
   Batch 0004's, and that is now on the record rather than left to be discovered.** What 221–230 owns
   instead is everything the certificate will be *audited against*: **the audit division's notice of
   production and its extract, which are now the only complete version of the household's record and
   which nobody in that division has acted on yet**; **Runa Sato's copy on a district sheet, which
   Ch 206 located and has never been seen**; **the twelfth docket, dated the first of July, entered on
   the first of June by a hand inside the Vant that has not been identified**; and **the first valuation,
   which is the first of July and is not a thing this volume can put off.** Ch 228 is immovable and is
   the scene inside this stretch, and **it must not be a scene about the certificate arriving.** A
   certificate is a piece of paper and the whole of this volume's argument is that paper is what makes
   a house visible. **Ch 228 is a scene in the third movement about a person, in the woman's own
   professional discipline, staged on ground, hand, name-exchange and aftermath that none of the
   twenty-two earlier scenes uses.**
4. **The Price — 231–242. NOT WRITTEN.** **Ines is removed from her own division's protection because she
   is a member of the record**, and the mechanism is already on the page: an officer does not certify a
   household she is a member of, and the only instrument for removing a person from a certified schedule
   is for an officer with a conflict, and the schedule does not go up on the twenty-sixth and the
   certificate does not issue on the twenty-seventh. **This is the volume's midpoint reversal and it must
   land on her, not on him.** She says on the page at Ch 219 that she is going to ask, and she does not
   ask, and the asking is somebody else's act. Ch 241 is the scene.
5. **The Auditor Walks Out — 243–250. NOT WRITTEN.** **Ch 246 is fixed by `outline/series.md`: INES
   DUARTE LEAVES. Not a death, not a betrayal. She files a report that is true and walks out of a flat
   she is still in.** That is not a departure from the volume's sentence; it is its answer. He could not
   keep her off his file and she did not want to be kept off her own. **The volume closes on the thing
   she left behind: a report that is true, in a division that now has to answer for having produced it.**

**Escalation, stated once so no batch has to re-derive it.** Early he investigates the record. **At
Ch 217 he stops investigating and starts spending** — the first irreversible act of his own in five
volumes, and it costs him the only thing he has. Mid-volume he manipulates it: the head's sworn answer
outranks a queue's statement because a man's name is at the top of one, and a roll that agrees with a
sworn answer is a copy, and a copy can be produced out of a file. Late he breaks it: at Ch 246 the
opposition is not an institution but a woman walking out, which is what `outline/series.md` line 104
already promises. **And the volume's personal sacrifice is Ines's, made deliberately, in public, in her
own hand.**

## 8. PLAN — what Volume 5 hands to Volume 6

`outline/series.md` fixes the next volume's sentence: **Lin Hao must call in every debt he owes before
the creditors call in him**, or **the Vant's books put a price on the reservoir that he cannot refuse.**

**So Volume 5 must leave debts standing and must not discharge them. There are now four, and the fourth
was added by Batch 0002.** The examination passed to Solveig Rask in four years and is still open-ended.
The open-ended security with no named holder is in a public return with his name in a broker's dictation.
**A thirty-year account entered against the broker of record in the year the Veil broke, carried
nineteen years on money and eleven on a woman, which is live and secured as of the seventh of June by a
plate at 8,138 that cannot be withdrawn before the first of July** — and which nobody in that household
has yet asked out loud what it is for. **And after 246 the one woman who can price any of it correctly is
no longer in the room and has left a document behind that says exactly what it is. Volume 6 is a volume
about being invoiced. Nothing in Volume 5 may make the invoices stop.**
