## 1. The sentence, and where it came from

`outline/volume-04.md` §8 closed Volume 4 with a question and not an answer:

> **What does a woman do who is the only person in Ostara who can read the record correctly, when the
> record is now her own household's?**

**Batch 0001 answers it in Chapter 205, twenty-three pages before the chapter that will have to live with
the answer.** She reads the household's file out loud on her own floor, in date order, and finds two
things: her own division raised her premises and did not tell her, and her own line on the certification
schedule carries no instrument. **She does not say why. Nothing in Volume 5 before Chapter 246 may make
her say why**, and `bible/characters.md` does not record a reason.

The volume's real shape, therefore, is not a mystery about Ines. **It is a woman deciding whether to be
a fact about herself on purpose.** Batch 0001's last line is her asking to be put on a sworn list on his
word rather than on an instrument — the weakest standing available to a person in this city, voidable,
and gone the day his plate goes — because for four years the one thing she owned was that she was in
nobody's book, and she worked out on the twenty-third that being outside a book is not the same as being
safe in a room.

## 6. PLAN — five movements, 201 to 250

**Proposed, not fixed. Each movement must open its own pressure, turn at its midpoint and pay its own
promise; the volume is not a chunk of a long narrative.**

1. **The Auditor's Price — 201–210. WRITTEN.** He certifies six and it costs Sun Meiling her only
   standing; she takes it back a night earlier, in pencil, on her own row. Ines asks to be carried.
2. **The Enforcer — 211–220.** **Petra Ilyin enters at 214 and the movement is about what a promise
   costs the person who made it when they cannot remember making it.** CAME TO COLLECT FROM MARGUERITE
   AND STAYED, per the bible. The household's problem in this stretch is not the certificate; it is that
   a sworn promise binds her body and the household has just discovered it has members inside obligations
   it cannot see.
3. **The Certificate — 221–230.** The twenty-seventh of June arrives. **A certificate is a document, and a
   safe house is the absence of documents**, and Tessa said both sentences out loud on purpose in Ch 207.
   This movement pays that: the clinic becomes visible the day the instrument exists, the audit division's
   four words are acted on by somebody, and Runa Sato's copy on a district sheet is located or is not.
   **Ch 228 is the scene and it is inside the arrival.**
4. **The Price — 231–242.** **Ines is removed from her own division's protection because she is a member
   of the record.** This is the volume's midpoint reversal and it must land on her, not on him. Ch 241 is
   the scene.
5. **The Auditor Walks Out — 243–250.** **Ch 246 is fixed by `outline/series.md`: INES DUARTE LEAVES. Not
   a death, not a betrayal. She files a report that is true and walks out of a flat she is still in.**
   That is not a departure from the volume's sentence; it is its answer. He could not keep her off his
   file and she did not want to be kept off her own. The volume closes on the thing she left behind: a
   report that is true, in a division that now has to answer for having produced it.

**Escalation, stated once so no batch has to re-derive it.** Early he investigates the record. Mid-volume
he manipulates it — the head's sworn answer outranks a queue's statement because a man's name is at the
top of one, and that is a fact he can use. Late he breaks it: at Ch 246 the opposition is not an
institution but a woman walking out, which is what `outline/series.md` line 104 already promises. **And
the volume's personal sacrifice is Ines's, made deliberately, in public, in her own hand.**
