# NOVEL_SPEC — Ranked by Desire

## Identity

- **Title:** Ranked by Desire
- **Slug:** novel-ranked-by-desire
- **Author:** Lin Hao
- **Genre:** Modern magical-fantasy city, superpower society, explicit erotic fantasy
- **Market note:** not for Inkstone, which prohibits explicit content. Built for readers who want
  explicit superpowered-city erotica and are impatient about getting to it.
- **Target length:** 500 chapters across 10 volumes. The planned ending is fixed in `outline/ending.md`.
- **Protagonist:** male, adult

## The promise to the reader

This is an explicit erotic superpower novel. The reader bought it for the sex and for the fantasy of
being wanted by the most powerful people in the city. Deliver both, often, without stalling.

- Write the actual scenes. Explicit, arousing, physical, unhurried once they start.
- **Scene budget: one explicit scene every 8 to 15 chapters.** The reviewer checks this.
- Never let more than fifteen chapters pass without a scene once the first partner is established.
- A scene changes something. Her standing, his standing, who knows, what a rival thinks she can take.
- The protagonist is **wanted**. Women in this city are competing for him by volume three and openly
  by volume five. Saying yes is fast, and it is enjoyable for them. That is the fantasy.
- Real romance between the scenes: attraction, jealousy, negotiation, tenderness, and the specific
  awkwardness of a man being fought over by people who would have ignored him last month.

## Premise

Twenty years ago the Veil broke over Ostara and the gifted came out of it. Power is public now. Every
registered citizen is scored by the Bureau of Merit weekly, and the score is published on a board
where anyone can read it.

Lin Hao is rank four. He is a washroom attendant at a Bureau branch office, which means he is scanned
every morning on his way to work and is worth less than the equipment he cleans.

His brother scored too well, tried to say so in public, and disappeared.

Then Lin Hao is asked to submit a merit reading, and what comes back is not a number. It is a
**reservoir** — merit that accrued to him without being recorded, fed by every person he ever helped
and nobody wrote down. The Bureau cannot classify it. He cannot spend it unseen.

The city that only counts what it can measure has produced something it cannot count, and it is
attached to a man who cleans toilets. Every woman with a rank worth having wants to know where that
reservoir comes from. He is not hiding well.

## The protagonist

**Lin Hao**, 25. Reads people well because his whole job is being looked through.

- **Want:** rank nine, which opens his brother's file.
- **Flaw:** he refuses to compete openly. Every advantage feels like theft from someone smaller, so he
  hoards it, which is its own waste.
- **The arc:** he climbs on anonymous merit until he is powerful enough to matter and visible enough
  to be hunted, then has to spend everything he built while people watch.

## The household

**Estimated size: 10 to 14 partners across the book.** A floor, not a ceiling. Add partners the
story earns; do not repeat the same beats on a new woman.

Every partner is an adult. The core roster:

1. **Ines Duarte** — rank-two auditor who can read any record in the city, and the first to suspect
   the reservoir is not natural.
2. **Runa Sato** — brute-force field operative who hates that he outranks her on paper.
3. **Marguerite Vale** — guild broker who trades in favours, and keeps investing after return stops
   making sense.
4. **Tessa Okonkwo** — medical specialist who keeps the Bureau's gifted alive.
5. **Sun Meiling** — his brother's former research partner, waiting three years for someone to ask.

Escalation is fast. A partner wants him, and the scene happens soon after — not after a volume of
withheld tension. Tension belongs inside the scene, not in place of it.

## Progression

- **Ranks:** one through nine. Nine opens any file in Ostara.
- **Reading:** weekly, public, on a board anyone can read.
- **Reservoir:** merit given quietly settles in him instead of scoring. Unspendable unseen.
- **Cost:** every draw is felt by the people who fed it, briefly, and some of them notice.
- **Escalation:** as he climbs, the Bureau tries to classify him, and being classified means a room
  with nobody in it and a form being filled out.
- **Weakness:** a reservoir never drawn on stabilises into something ordinary. He has to keep spending
  it, which means keeping earning it, which means keeping helping people who will never know.

## Structure

Ten volumes, about fifty chapters each. Every volume carries its share of the scene budget.

1. **Rank Four** — he must reach rank six, or his brother's file stays shut.
2. **The Reservoir** — he must survive learning what he is, before he has spent it once.
3. **The Reading** — he must survive being measured publicly.
4. **Six** — he must build a household, or nobody stands between him and the audit.
5. **The Auditor's Price** — he must keep Ines off his file while she starts wanting to be off her own.
6. **The Broker's Terms** — he must call in every debt he owes, before the creditors call in him.
7. **Seven** — he must survive classification, or the reservoir is requisitioned.
8. **The Brother's File** — he must find what his brother found.
9. **Rank Nine** — he must reach nine, or the file stays shut.
10. **Returned** — he must decide who the reservoir belongs to.

## Ending

He reaches rank nine and opens the file. His brother did not disappear; he donated his merit to an
account that exists on no ledger, which is what the reservoir is. Lin Hao has been carrying it.

He spends it returning every unrecorded merit to the people it came from, which drops him to rank one
and closes the file permanently.

Last chapter: a night shift in a different branch office, rank one on the public board, a household
that stayed, and a reservoir with a number in it that belongs to nobody.

## Content rules

- Every sexual character is an adult, fixed here and never contradicted in the prose.
- Women are willing participants. They want this. Do not make reluctance the emotional core of a scene
  and do not build the book's appeal on a woman being overridden.
- Write scenes explicitly. Implication and fade are not the target; the actual scene is.
- Keep violence and sex in separate registers. Do not turn a fight into a scene.
- Nothing in a chapter announces ages or genre. That record lives in `state/synopsis.md` and here.
