# Themes

Five themes. Each one gets a rule for how it shows up on the page and a rule for what would violate it,
because a theme that can be stated in one line and never dramatised is a slogan.

---

## 1. What a person is worth is not what a person is worth

**In a scene:** a woman with more rank than Lin Hao has points chooses him, and the reader watches her
make that calculation and watch it fail to matter.

**Violates it when:** the book treats rank as a scoreboard. It is not a scoreboard. It is a set of
permissions, a rent index, a queue order, and a public humiliation device, and every woman in the
household has spent her life being it. None of them has ever been valued by it.

**The sentence that keeps it honest:** a score is what the city can say out loud about a person, and
every woman in this book has a much better number that nobody has ever asked her for.

---

## 2. The cost lands on somebody who did not agree to it

**In a scene:** he spends. A stranger, on a tram, at a quarter past five, gets one cold minute in a
place that was warm. That stranger is a person, is written as a person, and forty chapters later is
either the reason something works or the reason something terrible happens.

**Violates it when:** the cold minutes become an abstraction, or a scoreboard, or a toll. They are never
a toll. Lin Hao's whole moral life is that he is doing arithmetic on strangers and has decided that
arithmetic is not a defence.

**Consequence rule for the writer:** at least one feeder is named, given a room, an occupation, and a
line of dialogue in the same chapter they are spent on. Never in passing. Never as a device.

---

## 3. Being wanted and being seen are different things

The city wants to see him. The household wants him. He is mistaken for a long time about which is which,
and being loved by people whose standing depends on not looking too closely is the actual difficulty of his
life.

**In a scene:** Ines, rank two, the only person who can read the city's records, has to decide every
week whether to report the man she is sleeping with. It costs her nothing to be tender and it costs her
everything to be honest, and the book never resolves that by making the Bureau villainous.

**Violates it when:** the women become rewards for his progress. They are not. Each one has her own
standing, her own agenda, her own account to settle, and the option to leave, and at least three of them
use that option during the book and come back on their own terms.

**The awkwardness the book keeps:** a man being fought over by women who would not have looked at him last
month, and the reader gets to enjoy that *and* to watch him be sick about it.

---

## 4. The institution is incomplete, not evil, and that is worse

The Bureau is not hiding anything. The Bureau is measuring what it can measure and publishing it, and
every single person inside it believes this is the neutral thing to do with power, and most of them are
right, and the number of people it hurt while being right is the point of the book.

**In a scene:** a kind clerk improves a plate by hand because the true number is indefensible, and it
saves a family, and she is not a villain and neither is anyone above her, and the consequence arrives
four volumes later anyway.

**Violates it when:** a Bureau official explains the system as a smokescreen. They do not. Alma Quayle
believes in measurement. Yusuf Adeyemi believes in correction. Anouk Ferreira believes the city survives
because nine people are countable. Ference they are all partly right and it does not help anybody.

---

## 5. A gift that can only be returned is not a gift

The reservoir gives back only what was given to it. That limitation is the moral centre of the novel and
it is not a limitation the plot ever gets around: in nine volumes he never takes anything from anyone. He
returns. Which means every woman who wants him is a woman who has to decide she wants to be given
something back, and that is a different thing from being wanted and it is the book.

**In a scene:** he is in bed with a rank-six woman who could have him requisitioned by morning and is not
going to, because in the last hour he put his hand on her wrist and gave her back the eleven days of sleep
she has been owed since her mother's reading.

**Violates it when:** the power is used to seduce, compel, soften an objection, or win a negotiation.
Not once, in nine volumes. If a scene would read differently if he had used the reservoir to make her want
it, the scene is wrong.

**Ending answer:** the last volume asks who the account belongs to, and the answer is that an account
belonging to nobody is the only kind worth giving back, and the man who keeps it is the last person in
Ostara who has any use for it.

---

## Genre register note

This is an explicit erotic novel that also believes in bureaucracy, and the second fact must not eat the
first. The machinery gets 20 to 30 per cent of the prose. The rest is bodies, rooms, weather, and what
people say when they have decided something. Every four or five paragraphs of institutional or technical
explanation, the reader gets one plain sentence about what it means for one specific person — usually a
woman who is standing in the room while the numbers are discussed and is not being talked about.
