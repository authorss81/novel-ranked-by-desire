# World

## Ostara

A working city of about two million on a river delta, built in brick and tram wire, that stopped being
a normal city twenty years ago. Ostara is not beautiful and it is not poor. It is administered, and the
administration is the weather.

### The break

Twenty years ago a fault under the Saltworks opened and something came out of it that the Bureau's
predecessors called **the Veil** — not a fog, not a gas, a *thin place*. Nineteen days of it. In
those nineteen days roughly one person in four changed: some woke able to hold a door open with their
mind, some woke able to stop a falling man's ribs from breaking, some woke able to lie so smoothly that
nobody could tell. About four percent of the population, and no two of them the same.

The Veil has not closed. The Saltworks is fenced. Two fissures in the north wall breathe on a weather
schedule nobody has ever fully explained. The Bureau's position, unchanged for twenty years and never
once seriously argued in public, is that the gifted are a **population** and populations must be
administered, because the alternative is that individual people get to decide what they can do.

### What the break did to ordinary life

Everything else followed from one decision. In the fourth month after the Veil, the provisional
council asked how many powerful people there were, and nobody could say, because counting them required
asking them and most of them had no reason to answer. So the council built an instrument that did not
require consent: **a weekly reading**. Every registered citizen, scanned, scored, published. Within a
year the argument was over. The reading was not going away, and everyone who was on the board could
see who was not.

### The city that counts itself

Rank is public. The number behind it is private — you see your band on the Board in the Reading Square
and your exact score on your own plate. That asymmetry is deliberate and it is the single most
important fact about Ostaran social life. You cannot compare exact scores, so you compare bands, so the
city compares bands obsessively, so a band is the only currency that matters.

Bands are displayed on a board of ceramic tiles at the head of the Reading, repainted every Thursday
morning. Rank one is a white tile, rank nine is black. There are nine ranks and the top tile has never
held a name in living memory.

### Districts

**The Reading** — the civic core. The Board, the Bureau's central house, the Exchange, the courts, the
trams that run past all of it at all hours. Broad, marble, cold, loud with numbers.

**Cordwain** — industry. The works, the tram yards, the freight, the loading bays. Ranks three to five
and shift work. Most of the city's rank-five stabilisations happen here, and most of them happen
because somebody's record was adjusted for a supervisor's convenience.

**The Slough** — legally the Fourth Quarter, and nobody has called it that in twenty years. Low-rank
housing, the free clinics, the long staircases. Ranks one and two. Lin Hao lives in a room above a
chop-house on Ferrier Street.

**Kestrel Hill** — rank five to seven housing, terraced, expensive, smug. Lin Hao will end up here
briefly and hate it.

**The Ninth Terrace** — the nine current rank-nines and their households, walled, above the tram line,
with a private station. This is where the power actually lives and where Lin Hao will eventually be
invited, resentfully, to dinner.

**Bellhouse** — the entertainment district. The opera, the fighting houses, the supper clubs, the
courtesans' quarter where the best information in Ostara has always been for sale. Where the money is
traded, the Board is discussed, and rank is worshiped by people who cannot reach it.

**The Vestry** — the charitable quarter. Free clinics, shelters, kitchens, and the **Church of the
Quiet Number**, which holds the only organised theology in Ostara that says unrecorded help is the only
help that counts. It has eleven thousand members. The Bureau considers it a curiosity and a
recruitment risk, and Volume 4 opens when it stops considering it a curiosity.

**Lamplit Row** — where the unattributable live: people with no address of record, which is the only
remaining way to be unfindable. Half of it is a genuine shelter and half of it is a criminal economy
built entirely on the absence of paperwork.

**The Saltworks** — the Veil's scar on the north edge. Fenced, patrolled, and settled on its outer
side by people the Bureau calls **the Unscored**: people who came out of the Veil and never registered,
living inside a cordon they cannot legally leave.

### Institutions

**The Bureau of Merit** — the body that reads, scores, publishes, classifies, audits, requisitions, and
licenses. It has four arms that matter to this story: the **Reading Office**, which produces weekly
scores and owns the Board; the **Classification Wing**, which decides what a person *is*; the
**Exchange**, which sells merit back as rights; and the **Field**, which does the requisition work when
the paperwork says someone may be taken.

**The Exchange** — where merit is spent. Not on objects. On *permissions*: a berth on the ferry, an
hour of a scarce machine, a signature, a door, a hearing moved up. Lin Hao's reservoir is worth an
enormous number of permissions to anyone who can convert it, which is the entire economy the mid-book
runs on.

**The Correction Office** — public hearings for people who believe their score was wrong. It is the
Bureau's most hated arm and its most honest. It is also the mechanism by which the city discovers
things, because a hearing forces a record into the open where it can be looked at.

**The Vestry clinics** — where the gifted go when the gifted break. Tessa Okonkwo runs the largest.
Unfunded by the Bureau and tolerated by it, because a body that cannot be repaired stops producing merit
and stops producing merit means someone in the Vestry has to, personally, forever.

**The Vant** — the merchant guild that trades in favours and holds the only ledger that matters outside
the Bureau's walls. Marguerite Vale is its senior broker. Her rival, Solveig Rask, keeps the real books.

**The Concil of the Nine** — the nine rank-nines, who meet monthly, who issue correction orders that
override local readings, and who can requisition merit from any citizen in Ostara by majority seal.
Nine people. Between them they decide what the whole city is worth.

**The Church of the Quiet Number** — the one institution in Ostara whose theory of merit is correct and
whose practice is therefore illegal. They hold no ranks of their own, they are not audited, and their
congregation is the largest single unmeasured group of people in the city.

### Factions and what each one wants

- **The Bureau of Merit** — to remain the only institution that can say what a person is worth, because
  the alternative is a city where powerful people are unlisted. They are not lying. They are early.
- **The Concil of the Nine** — to keep the Board. Not for power, for insurance: as long as people believe
  rank matters, rank-nines are the most important people in the city, and they intend to keep being.
- **The Vant** — to own the gap between what a thing is worth and what the Board says it is worth. The
  reservoir is the largest such gap in Ostara.
- **The Vestry and the Church of the Quiet Number** — to be permitted to help people without filing.
  They have asked for twenty years.
- **The Correction Office** — to make the record correct, and to be hated honestly while doing it.
- **The Unscored** — to be permitted to exist inside the cordon without being treated as a permanent
  emergency.
- **The Board** — not a faction, but the thing every faction wants to control, and in Volume 3 the thing
  that controls a man.

### Technology

Electric, industrial, and rationed. Trams on overhead wire, lifts in the Kestrel Hill terraces, pneumatic
tube for records inside a building and none between buildings, mechanical adding machines in the Reading
Office, plate readers that press a metal disc against a socket and give back a number. Typewriters,
carbon paper, carbon-copy triplicate for anything that matters. **Radios do not work near an active
fissure.** Every broadcast that Ostara has ever received came from more than forty miles out, which means
the city has had no reliable way to hear itself think for twenty years, and whoever controls the wire
controls what a person knows is happening in their own city.

### Ordinary life, which is the point

People wait. People queue at branch offices to have their plate stamped because a stamped plate is proof
of having been seen. Rent is indexed to rank, so a promotion is a rent reduction and a demotion is an
eviction notice. Children are taught to report kind deeds to a form at school. Good marriages are
arranged downward and resent upward. People genuinely try to do good things where they can be seen
doing them, and the ones who do good things where nobody can see are — statistically, and this is the
whole problem — the ones nobody in the city knows exist.

### The household

When a rank-nine household forms in Ostara it is not a scandal. It is a *structure*, and it is
legally recognised: a **standing household** shares quarters, shares one plate, and is bound by the
highest rank in it for legal purposes. Nobody in the Council is embarrassed about it. It is how powerful
families in Ostara have always stayed powerful — by making sure the score survives the people in it.
Lin Hao's household, when it exists, will be the first one in the city where the lowest-ranked member is
the reason for the rest, and the entire political structure of the mid-book is built around making sure
that does not survive the century.

### The criminal economy

Rank is a fence, not a wall. What is traded illegally in Ostara:

- **Draws.** Merit moved between accounts without a seal. Nearly always small. Nearly always done by
  someone who thinks they are stealing from the Bureau rather than from a neighbour.
- **Plate work.** Altered plates. Forged scores. A good plate forger in Lamplit Row can move a man
  four ranks and charge him a year's rent.
- **Quiet accounts.** Merit held where the Board cannot see it. Expensive. The reservoir is the largest
  quiet account in the city's history, and it is not on the market.
- **Witnesses.** A witness who will not retract. This is what most of the guild business actually is,
  and it is why Marguerite Vale is feared.
- **Turned lines.** People who can feel merit being spent out of them. Untrained, undiagnosed, and by
  Volume 2 the most valuable and most dangerous informants in Ostara, because they can feel a spend the
  way a dog hears a whistle.

### What the city believes about itself

That it is fair, that the Board is honest, that if you have done enough good in the open you have been
paid, and that anyone who complains about the score is complaining because they did not do enough. This
is not propaganda. Most people in Ostara have never met anyone whose score was wrong by more than a
band, and they have absolutely met people whose score was right by less than one.
