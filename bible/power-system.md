# Power System

## The one-sentence version

Merit is what a person leaves behind when they change another person's condition; the Bureau measures
the part of it that can be witnessed; Lin Hao carries the part that cannot, and he can only spend it by
touching the person it came from.

---

## Part one: how merit settles

Merit is not morality and it is not a reward. It is a **quantity that accumulates in a person when
their act changes somebody else's condition in a way that can afterwards be argued about.** That last
clause is the whole world. If it cannot be argued about afterwards, it still accumulates — it just
does not accumulate *visibly*.

**Settling** is the term the Bureau uses. They measure it, plate it, and publish it weekly. Nobody in
Ostara knows the unmeasured fraction exists until Lin Hao does.

### The five sources, by how much of it the Bureau can see

**1. Attested deed.** A witness, a filing, a stamp, a school form. Fully scored. This is the great
majority of what an ordinary Ostaran's score is made of, and it is why helping people where nobody is
looking is *specifically* unscoreable rather than merely unlucky.

**2. Instrumented deed.** No witness, but a machine recorded something. Half or quarter scored, depending
on which office's machine it was. A tram driver who brakes for a child at an unregistered crossing gets
an instrumented entry and a query. A nurse who stays an hour past her shift in a clinic that files by
headcount gets the clinic's aggregate, not hers.

**3. Office deed.** Done inside a Bureau function. Scored in aggregate to the whole staff and split by a
formula that has never been published. This is the only reason Lin Hao scores at all. Branch attendants
score at all because the Bureau is obliged to be able to say it scores them.

**4. Quiet deed.** Help given where no witness and no instrument could see it. Not scored. Not disputed
either — it simply does not exist as far as the Board is concerned. Most people in Ostara commit several
a week without knowing there is a category for it.

**5. Unattributed settling.** Quiet merit that has no person attached to it — because the person who
earned it declined to be told they earned it, or because the person it went to died first. This is the
parent of the reservoir, and it is the rarest and largest thing in the story.

### The unit and the bands

Merit is published in **grains**. A weekly plate returns a number of grains. Ranks are bands, and bands
are what the Board shows.

| Rank | Weekly grains | What it buys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 100 | Subsistence guarantee. Assigned work you do not choose. |
| 2 | 400 | Choice of district. Free clinic access. School places without the merit form. |
| 3 | 1,200 | Own tenancy. Own a plate — no longer scanned at someone else's gate. |
| 4 | 3,000 | Travel permit. Ordinary court standing. Lin Hao starts here. |
| 5 | 8,000 | Held accounts. Private office. The right to be asked before requisitioned. |
| 6 | 20,000 | **Sealed filings open.** Warrants. Requisition of property. Compelled testimony. |
| 7 | 55,000 | **Requisition of merit from a person.** The sealed archive. |
| 8 | 140,000 | Correction orders that override a local reading. The Council bench. |
| 9 | 400,000 | **The Ninth Seal.** Opens any file in Ostara. |

Rank nine is the ceiling and has been for twenty years. The top tile on the Board is black and has never
held a name in living memory. In Volume 9 the reason becomes clear and it is not a bureaucratic cap.

### The Exchange

Merit is spent on **permissions**. Objects are bought with money, which is itself earned merit and
converted. A permission is a thing you may do: take the ferry, hold the machine for an hour, have your
petition heard this week instead of in March, be asked before you are searched. This is why rank feels
like *time* rather than like wealth. A rank-six person is not rich. A rank-six person does not wait.

## Part two: the reservoir

The reservoir is not an ability. It is an **account with no number**, and it behaves like one.

### The six rules

**1. It accrues to a person who cannot be audited for the deed.**
No filed record. No witness who will still speak. No instrument. In practice it accrues to people who
are not *looked at*: the attendant, the night porter, the woman who empties the bins on the fourth floor
at two in the morning, the man who carries the injured to the tram stop because carrying him is quicker
than waiting for help. Lin Hao is the most looked-through person in his own building. That is the whole
reason the account is his, and it is not an accident or a destiny — it is a **job description**.

**2. It has mass and no score.**
The plate returns a stable figure with no interval on it. A score is a rate, and a rank is a band on a
rate, and this has neither. The counter clerk's word for it is **unsettled**, and when Lin Hao hears it
he does not understand what has happened to him yet.

**3. It only moves on contact with the person it came from, and only as relief.**
Not strength. Not healing. **Relief** — the pain that was owed, the exhaustion that had three days left
to run, the grief nobody saw him carry, the debt that was about to come due on Thursday. He puts his
hand on a person's hand and something they are owed arrives early and settled. It does not make them
stronger. It makes them **finished**, the way a person is finished at the end of a thing they have
been surviving.

**4. It is felt.**
The person it came from feels the spend as one to four seconds of cold, in the hand, or in the chest, or
in a specific old injury. Most people shrug it off and think they were cold. A person who has felt it
three or four times starts **keeping track**. A person keeping track is a **turned** line. Turned people
can be led to him by a doctor, a tram operator, an accountant, or a relative, and they do not know they
are being led until they are in a room with him.

**5. It must keep moving.**
An unspent reservoir **settles**. In roughly forty days an unsettled mass flattens into something the
Bureau's instruments can read, and readable means scoreable, and scoreable means classifiable, and
classifiable means a room with nobody in it. So he cannot simply bank it. He has to keep earning it,
which means he has to keep being the person who helps where nobody is looking, which means he can never
stop being a washroom attendant with no standing and no history and no reason for anyone to protect him.

**6. It keeps the ledger.**
He knows the faces. He knows the streets and the hour they were on them. It never tells him the deed and
never tells him whether a person was worth it. He is doing arithmetic on strangers, and the arithmetic
is the moral weight of the entire book.

### The notation

The reservoir's own accounting arrives in the ledger notation. It is a System, it is diegetic, and it is
**incomplete by nature** — it reports in people, never in acts, and it is written in plain language with
no numbers the reader has to hold.

    [Ledger. Nineteen lines. One turned.]

    [Draw. The lift hand. It will cost her a cold minute.]

    [Open. Three.]

    [The plate will not give an interval. Come back Thursday.]

The rules for it: rare, one per chapter at most, never bold, never italic, never a heading, always in
square brackets on its own line. It may state a rule, a warning, a cost, or a count. It never tells him
what to do.

### Failure, and what failure costs

**Spending wrong.** If he puts his hand on someone who never fed him, nothing moves. He loses real
things: grey at one temple, a grey night, a night of sleep like being woken on the hour. The cost is
proportional to how much he was trying to spend, and it comes out of the body rather than out of a
score. He can do this as often as he wants and it never improves.

**Turning.** The only true failure. Nine turned lines in one season is a legible pattern; a good analyst
can triangulate a turned line to a source, and once that is done the source has an address. Lin Hao's
entire strategy for five volumes is **keep the count of turned lines under a number he has never told
anyone**, and the number goes up anyway, because he cannot stop earning either.

**Settling.** Letting it go still for forty days. Then the Bureau has a reading that is too stable to be
alive, and a file opens with his name on it and nobody else's, and from that day he is being handled.

**Classification.** The terminal failure of the early game. A classified account is a *held account*:
a legal category, a form, a room in the Classification Wing with no window and a door that is not locked
from inside. Everyone classified in Volume 2 came back. None of them came back with anything, and none
of them came back with a rank.

### The hard limits — what the reservoir cannot do

- **It cannot raise his rank.** Not one grain of it is scoreable. This is the engine of the entire plot:
  he can become extremely powerful and completely illegible, and the only way up the Board is to commit
  attested deeds in public, one at a time, by people who will write them down and stand in front of
  witnesses. Every rank he gains costs him the only thing he had.
- **It cannot be taken by force.** It does not respond to seizure, sedation, restraint, or pain. It does
  not respond at all to anyone who is not the person who earned it or the person it is aimed at. This is
  why he cannot be compelled with it and why no scene in this book may use compulsion, sedation, or
  psychic contact as a mechanism for intimacy.
- **It cannot be traded.** It cannot be sold, lent, deposited, or split. Marguerite Vale can launder
  *around* it in Volume 4 but never take a grain of it, and the attempt costs her something she would
  rather not have spent.
- **It does not grow faster than he earns.** There is no sitting in a room and pulling. The rate is
  exactly the rate at which unrecorded good happens in his vicinity and is given to him, which is why his
  job, his district, and his hour-by-hour life are a power stat.
- **It cannot be demonstrated on demand.** He cannot show it to somebody to prove it works. Every proof
  he ever gives is at somebody's expense.

### The growth arc, in plain terms

| Stage | Chapters | What changes | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attractant | 1–100 | Learns it moves, learns it hurts people, spends by accident and then on purpose | Cold minutes. Two or three turned lines. A man in a tram who cannot get warm for a week. |
| Instrument | 101–200 | Learns to convert relief into leverage, and leverage into rank, by engineering the public good deeds that score him | Every grain of rank he buys costs somebody a cold minute. The arithmetic becomes explicit and ugly. |
| Channel | 201–300 | Learns he can *route* merit — send his own draw back through someone else so the board credits them | The person he routes it through becomes a target, and the first woman he routes it through is Ines Duarte. |
| Breach | 301–400 | Learns the reservoir has an address and that the address has a name on it, and fights the classification | He must stop spending entirely for eleven days to keep a turned person alive, and the rank he has built goes flat while he does it. |
| Return | 401–500 | Learns the account is his brother's, and decides to close it | Everything he has. Rank, standing, household immunity, and the private ledger he has lived inside for five volumes. |

### The one rule the writer must never break

**The reservoir gives back only what was given to it.** It takes nothing. It cannot be used to sedate,
to compel, to incapacitate, to dull an objection, or to make anyone want anything. It can relieve. It
can warm. It can finish. What it cannot do is make a woman want him. Every woman in this book decides
that on her own, quickly, and is glad about it, and the power he actually has over people is that he is
the only man in Ostara who can hand back what they are owed — which is not the same thing as power over
them, and the book never pretends it is.
