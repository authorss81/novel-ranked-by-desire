# Characters

## Rules for this file

- **Every partner's age is fixed here and is never contradicted in the prose.** No chapter announces an
  age, and no chapter implies one that is not on this list.
- Every sexual character is an adult. An impaired state — sedation, exhaustion, toxin, restraint,
  psychic contact, trance, seal — is **never** the mechanism that produces a scene.
- A partner is not a collectable. Each woman here wants something she could get somewhere else, has her
  own standing and her own agenda, and is able to leave. She does not stay because she cannot leave.
  She stays because he is worth it.
- Attraction turns into a scene fast. Tension belongs inside the scene, not in place of it.
- Scenes are planned by chapter number in the volume outlines. Do not improvise a new partner's entry
  volume without flagging it in `state/open-threads.md`.

## Lin Hao — the lead

**Twenty-five. Rank four, and rank four is worth about what a good week at a cooperative costs.**

A washroom attendant at the Fourth Branch office of the Bureau of Merit. He has worked there four
years. He is competent at it, quiet, and early. He reads people very well because his entire working life
is being looked through: read at the gate, nodded at by clerks, stepped around by anyone holding a
reading card. He knows what a person looks like when they are about to be unkind and he has developed an
instinct for leaving rooms that has never once been wrong.

**Want: rank nine.** Not power, not safety, not revenge. He wants the file. Lin Wei scored six in a
single reading three years ago, stood in a public hall and said the number was wrong, and by morning he
was gone. The file is sealed above rank nine's floor. Rank nine opens any file in Ostara. That is the
whole want, stated as a number because in this city a number is a want.

**Need: to accept that being seen is not the same as being used.**
He wants to be looked at only on his own terms. His entire strategy is to be exactly legible in the one
respect that costs nothing and completely illegible in every other. He cannot do that for nine volumes.
The final volume requires him to stand in a room full of people and let them read him correctly.

**Flaw: he hoards.** Every advantage he gets feels like theft from someone smaller, so he takes it
quietly, keeps it small, and refuses to compete openly — which means he never lets anything grow large
enough to protect anyone, including himself. His hoarding is the reason he is nearly taken in Volume 2
and the reason he is helpless in Volume 4. The flaw at its final form, in Volume 10, is that he decides
the reservoir belongs to him to give away, which is the same instinct with more authority.

**Fear: that his brother said the right thing.** That the number was wrong, that Lin Wei saw what it
really was, and that Lin Hao is the proof, and that everything after Chapter 1 is a man carrying around
his brother's objection in his chest.

**What he is good at:** cleaning, listening, reading a room, being underestimated, and returning
something to a person at the exact moment it is needed. He is not a fighter and never becomes one; the
field scenes are won by other people putting themselves in front of him and by him deciding, afterward,
that he owes them for it.

**His ordinary life, on the page:** wakes at five, walks the tram line to the Fourth Branch, cleans six
floors of washrooms while the office fills, eats from the staff kettle, walks home past the Board on
Thursday, sleeps badly. He owns one plate, one knife, one chair, and a photograph of two men on a tram
step that he does not look at often.

## Lin Wei — the brother

**Thirty-one. Went out of public record three years ago at twenty-eight.**

A gifted registrar at the Reading Office, which is to say he was the person who physically wrote the
numbers onto the Board. He was precise, quiet, funny, and — the thing that killed him — unable to leave a
discrepancy alone. He found that a class of quiet deeds was settling into accounts that had no
instruments behind them, traced it through three months of plates, and worked out what it was. He said so
in a public hall, in front of a hundred and forty people, with his score on the board behind him.

**What actually happened** (fixed, revealed Volume 8, never contradicted): he did not smuggle anything.
He used the only power in the city that requires no rank and no witness — he **donated** his entire
accumulated merit, all 214,000 grains of it, in one movement, into an account that exists on no ledger.
He was not destroyed by the Bureau. He was destroyed by the fact that the Bureau could not classify what
he had done, and an authority that cannot classify a thing must remove the thing. He has been inside the
system ever since as a **held account** — one of the people in a room with no window, at a level of the
Classification Wing nobody outside has visited.

The account he opened has been filling itself from unrecorded good deeds ever since. It has an address.
The address is Lin Hao, because Lin Hao is the only person in Ostara low enough to be an acceptable
recipient for it.

## The household

Twelve partners. Five are core; seven more join between Volume 1 and Volume 8. Entry volumes and scene
chapters are fixed here so nobody re-invents them, and **the twelve are numbered in the order they enter**
— that is, in the order of their first scenes — so a writer who needs to know who is on the page by
Volume 4 can count down the list instead of guessing.

**Core and sworn are two different lists and only one name is on both.** The **core five** are the women
whose arcs run the length of the book and who are named as the core roster in `NOVEL_SPEC.md`: **Ines
Duarte, Tessa Okonkwo, Runa Sato, Marguerite Vale, Sun Meiling.** Beatrix Halm enters earlier than three
of them and is not core; that is a scheduling fact and not a ranking, and no batch may promote or demote
anybody on this list.

**The standing household is nine of the twelve.** Nine women are sworn in on one plate at the Ninth
Terrace in Chapter 402 and the household is bound by the highest rank in it. **The law binds the plate and
the bond. Shared quarters are how most households do it and they are what a reader will assume, but
quarters are not the test** — a sworn member may hold her own plate and her own rooms, and her name stays
on the roll, and the roll is the thing the Bureau reads and the thing requisition runs against. The three
who are not sworn are fixed, and no batch invents a fourth:

- **Wren Kallo** cannot be sworn in without destroying the posting that makes her useful. A Bureau
  counter-forensics officer bound to a rank-nine man is a woman nobody in her own division will speak to
  by Volume 6, and she chooses that over the protection.
- **Sun Meiling** cannot be sworn in while she is inside Veil-Research, and by Volume 8 the sworn-in women
  are the ones who can be requisitioned. She is the one who needs protecting, and the household protects
  people by being a household. She is core and unsworn, and both of those are deliberate.
- **Dagmar Oyelowo** has run the Ninth Terrace intake desk for twenty years, and the desk is a public
  post. A name on a household roll is read at every counter a member of it ever stands in front of, and
  hers is the counter; the oath would make her legible where she works and would not reach her there.

All three come to his shift in the last chapters anyway. **Nobody in this book stays because she cannot
leave.**

### 1. Ines Duarte — rank two, auditor. Age 34. Enters Volume 1. Scene: Chapter 8.

Reads any record in Ostara and knows it when it has been touched. First to suspect the reservoir is not
natural, and the only person in the first volume who treats Lin Hao as a person before she treats him as
a file.

**Gift:** she holds a record in her head and knows when it has been altered. **Cost:** every record she
holds etches, and her own memory goes to pay for the retention. She is losing her twenties behind other
people's paperwork and she knows the arithmetic and does it anyway.

**What she wants:** to be the last auditor who can read a record from the actual paper. She knows within
four volumes that the reservoir is her own case, and she keeps working it.

**First scene (Ch 8):** at her rooms on Lantern Row, the night after he comes to them. The reservoir is in
the room and it is relief — what she is owed after four months of holding a file that tells her when her
own sister was scored down for being kind. He gives it back. She decides. **What it changes:** she can feel
his ledger through the contact, and she says a name from it aloud, and neither of them knows whose it is.
From Chapter 8 she is his partner and his professional exposure, and she cannot report him, and that is
the whole tension she carries into Volume 5, which is titled after her.

**Arc:** becomes the woman who can read the unrecorded case, because in Volume 10 she is the only
auditor alive who has ever held a record that was never written. Ends in Volume 10 having got her
twenties back and no idea what year it is.

**She does not live in the household and she is still sworn into it.** She walks out of his flat in
Chapter 246 and comes back in 271 on her own terms, and `outline/series.md` is explicit that from then on
she is not under his roof. She keeps her own rooms on Lantern Row and her own plate for the rest of the
book, and at the Terrace in Chapter 402 she signs for the household anyway, in person, and does not
argue about it. Because the bond and the roll are the law and quarters are not, this is legal, it is
deliberate, and it is the strongest thing either of them says out loud. **No later batch may put her back
under his roof without flagging it in `state/open-threads.md` first.**

### 2. Beatrix Halm — tram operator. Age 27. Enters Volume 1. Scene: Chapter 17.

Signal operator on the Ferrier line, which means she can feel forty thousand people moving. Introduced as
the person who felt a cold minute and started writing them down.

**Gift:** she feels the city's current and knows where something is wrong before anyone does. **Cost:**
she has been deaf in one ear since the Veil and the city's noise lives in the other one, so on a busy
evening she cannot hear herself think.

**What she wants:** not a man. A use for what she feels. She has spent four years being told her
headaches are a condition and she is furious about it, and Lin Hao is the first person who uses the
information instead of dismissing it. She moves in on that, fast, and the rest follows.

**First scene (Ch 17):** in the cab at the end of a shift, and on the roof of a tram. **What it changes:**
she gives him the city's turned lines for free, on the condition that he spends them on people, not on
himself. He has an intelligence source nobody can intercept.

### 3. Tessa Okonkwo — medical specialist. Age 31. Enters Volume 1. Scene: Chapter 26.

Keeps the Bureau's gifted alive at the Vestry clinic and is the reason nobody in the Council has ever had
to think about what happens when a rank-six body fails.

**Gift:** she can hold a body past the point it should stop. **Cost:** she takes on the damage. She
carries, in her own frame, a working inventory of every injury she has held, and she is running out of
room in it.

**What she wants:** to be allowed to treat someone without writing the file. She is the first person who
understands the reservoir as medicine rather than as a threat, and in Volume 2 she is the reason Lin Hao
stops being afraid of it.

**First scene (Ch 26):** the clinic's cold store, after hours, because she will not do this in her own
ward and says so. **What it changes:** she stabilises a turned person by paying the cold minutes herself,
out of her own inventory, and Lin Hao has to be told that the cost of a turned line is not only his.

### 4. Nadia Ferrous — lift operator. Age 23. Enters Volume 1. Scene: Chapter 29. **Adult, always
written as one.**

Youngest of the household, loudest, and completely unambiguous about what she wants from the first page
she appears on. She does not need the reservoir. She felt a cold minute in a warm lift and has told
everybody about it.

**Gift:** she arrives before anyone else. **Cost:** distance only folds downward for her. She can go down
fast and up never, and she has never once in her life been able to leave.

**What she wants:** him, specifically, and out loud. She is not a mystery and she is not a test.

**First scene (Ch 29):** in a stopped lift between the Vestry and the clinic, and neither of them gets
stuck by accident. **What it changes:** she becomes the household's conscience and its comic relief and,
in Volume 7, the person who physically carries the answer up a floor she cannot otherwise climb.

### 5. Runa Sato — field operative. Age 29. Enters Volume 1. Scene: Chapter 36.

Brute-force division, rank six, sent to watch him because the Bureau does not send auditors, it sends
her. Hates that he outranks her on paper and much more that he does not have a rank at all.

**Gift:** she carries weight — mass, and later stored merit. **Cost:** her body absorbs what she carries.
She is heavier than she looks by a stone and a half and she is aging at a rate she has stopped
discussing.

**What she wants:** to be measured by what she did, not what her file says, and she is entirely honest
about the fact that this is vanity.

**First scene (Ch 36):** after the requisition at Cordwain, in a requisitioned flat, with both of them
injured and neither of them impaired by anything the book should not use. **What it changes:** she
declines to write the report that would end him. From Chapter 36 the Bureau's most dangerous field
operative is lying in her own files, and she knows exactly how expensive that is.

### 6. Marguerite Vale — guild broker. Age 41. Enters Volume 1. Scene: Chapter 46.

The Vant's senior broker. Trades in favours, keeps the real books, and keeps investing after the return
stops making sense.

**Gift:** a debt she touches binds both parties physically and in writing. **Cost:** every debt she takes
she eventually has to carry herself, and at forty-one she carries more of the city's obligations than
any living person.

**What she wants:** a position that does not depend on the Vant. She is thirty years inside a guild that
will replace her the moment Solveig Rask decides to, and she is looking for the one asset in Ostara that
cannot be seized by a court.

**First scene (Ch 46):** in her own rooms, which are the best rooms he will ever stand in, and she is
plainly enjoying it. **What it changes:** she offers to launder the reservoir into a legal account, and
he says no, and she keeps investing anyway. That refusal is the most attractive thing anyone has done for
him in five hundred chapters and it is the beginning of Volume 6.

### 7. Sun Meiling — research wing. Age 33. Enters Volume 2. Scene: Chapter 63.

Lin Wei's former research partner, who waited three years for someone to ask her the right question. She
is the one who will say the sentence in Volume 8 that reconciles everything, and she is one of the core
five and is still not on the roll at Chapter 402.

**Gift:** she sees the same shape in different data. **Cost:** she lost the ability to see a single thing
clearly; she sees everything as a set, which is why nobody, including her, can tell her when she is wrong.

### 8. Wren Kallo — Bureau counter-forensics. Age 38. Enters Volume 3. Scene: Chapter 118.

Built the tool that would classify Lin Hao, and has to decide whether to keep her hand in the machine.
Gifts: she sees the shape of an edit in a record. **Cost:** she cannot keep a secret — which made her an
excellent auditor and a poor friend, and which means by the time she joins the household the secret in
question is already four people deep and she has told three of them.

### 9. Agnete Lindqvist — chaplain of the Church of the Quiet Number. Age 45. Enters Volume 4.
Scene: Chapter 169.

Formerly a rank-four clerk. Her gift makes a room where nobody can be compelled to testify. **Cost:** she
has not been outside one in eleven years, and Volume 4 is partly about getting her out of it.

### 10. Petra Ilyin — guild enforcer. Age 35. Enters Volume 5. Scene: Chapter 214.

Came to collect from Marguerite and stayed. **Gift:** a sworn promise binds her body. **Cost:** she has
been under a promise for eleven years and cannot remember who made it.

### 11. Isolde Marchetti — singer at the Bellhouse. Age 39. Enters Volume 6. Scene: Chapter 261.

Used as a public instrument against Lin Hao before she becomes his. **Gift:** a room that hears her agrees
to what she sings — local, exhausting, and impossible to turn off. **Cost:** she is never in a room
without it happening.

### 12. Dagmar Oyelowo — intake desk, the Ninth Terrace. Age 50. Enters Volume 8. Scene: Chapter 388.

Ran the Terrace intake for twenty years and never scored once. **Gift:** none at all. She is the only
partner in the book with no ability, she tells Lin Hao the truth about his brother in Volume 8, and she
is wanted and says so and it is tender and it is about being wanted at the bottom of the Board by a man
who is at the top of it.

**Chapter 943 is her second scene and it is the last scene in the book, five hundred and fifty-five
chapters after 388, and it is the volume's close.** Axis: the winter light, carried by that hall's own
rule that nothing on a wall is read after the light has gone off it, because reading by a carried lamp
puts the shadow of whoever is holding it on the words — and nobody names it. **She initiates, is enjoyed
and says so plainly, and `PC-44` is honoured: no gift is manufactured and she is put on no roll.** What
she gives him is the volume's debt: a door with three lines on it is the only address in this city a
person can find without producing anything, and she has spent twenty years refusing to be findable on
purpose and does not want it back. What she will not do is let any of it be called a hardship. **What
she asks for is the one thing he has given away about nine times a fortnight all year and has never
given to her: she wants him to say his own three lines out loud, in daylight, in a public hall, with
about nine people in it — and not one of them has a pen.** She is at the bottom of Volume 19 telling him
that the only archive in this city is her memory, that it has never been checked against anything, and
that she has been wrong about about four of it in twenty years. She is wanted, and she says so, and she
still has not been thanked.

## The opposition

### Cassius Rhee — deputy warden, Fourth Branch. Age 46.

The Volume 1 antagonist, and he is not a monster. He runs the worst branch in the district, he is
forty-six and rank five and losing ground to men half his age with better files, and he has a district of
nine thousand people who are three hundred grains short every week. He wants a **portable merit source**
inside his own branch so he can stabilise them. A reservoir, classified and attached to a man on his own
staff, is exactly that. He offers Lin Hao a job.

### Alma Quayle — head of classification. Age 57.

Volume 2 and Volume 7. Her son scored two and died in a queue. She is not after Lin Hao's freedom; she is
after being the person who can finally say what he is, and she has spent thirty years arranging to be
that person.

### Yusuf Adeyemi — Correction Office. Age 44.

Volume 3. Runs public hearings. Lin Hao's advantage is invisibility and Adeyemi's entire profession is
forcing invisible things into the open, and he is honest about it and completely willing to be hated for
it. He becomes something stranger than an enemy by Volume 7.

### Hanneke Ruis — keeper of the counting room, two floors under the Exchange sales floor. **No age is fixed for her and none may be given on the page.**

Volume 7. **She took the keys on the seventeenth of September** and is the second woman to hold them inside four years. She had the address of premises 34-6-118 and a figure of seven in a real book on her second day and had read both by her third, and she has not met the man whose address it is until Chapter 302.

**What she wants:** to be the keeper of that room. She says so in 302 in about nine sentences and she says it as arithmetic and not as modesty. **She will not put her name under a figure about a household**, because the afternoon she does she stops being the keeper of the room, and she has been there seventeen days and has a strap on the fifth book.

**Her agency, which is the reason she is on this page and not in it:** she tells him in 302 that his sworn answer is not wrong and is one day old, that an answer which has been overtaken does not correct itself, and that three instruments with his handwriting in them carrying one figure of persons from one door in two months is a habit. She will not be his instrument about any of it and she says so to his face. **She answers questions from him in writing and will not see him again downstairs while there are two figures for one door**, which is a rule of the floor and not a decision. **In Chapter 309 her note is the last thing that tells him the price of the correction, and she wrote *do not send me a third question in writing this week.***

### Solveig Rask — keeper of the Vant's real books. Age 42.

Volume 6. Marguerite's rival, and the only person in the guild who has ever run the arithmetic on what
the reservoir is actually worth, and got it right.

### Anouk Ferreira — chair of the Concil of the Nine. Age 63.

Volumes 7, 9, 10. The final opponent, and she is not the villain — she is a woman who has held the city
still for twenty years and believes the only reason Ostara is not a graveyard is that nine people were
countable. She is right about that and she is going to lose anyway.

### Perrin Sowe — Veil-Research wing. Age 55.

Volumes 5 and 8, and the last volume. Lin Wei's colleague, the man who noticed what Lin Wei noticed and
chose the Bureau. He is not a monster either. He is the specific, unbearable version of Lin Hao's own
flaw: he looked at an unrecorded good and filed it.

### Tobias Renn — junior filing clerk, Fourth Branch. Age 24.

Volume 1's small antagonist. Rank two, resentful, and the first person who profits from Lin Hao being
taken. He is the reason a supply room closes in Chapter 6.

### Ola Bright — lift attendant's flatmate. Age 26.

Not an antagonist. She is the person who kept the diary of cold minutes, she does it because she is bored,
and she is cheerfully, catastrophically unhelpful, and Lin Hao has to befriend her before he can get her
to stop writing.
