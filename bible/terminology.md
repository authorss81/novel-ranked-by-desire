# Terminology

Every word below is fixed. Use the column word, not a paraphrase of it. The right-hand rule exists
because these terms are load-bearing and the reader will notice when they are used as filler.

---

## The measure

**Grain** — the unit merit is counted in. One week's score is given in grains; ranks are bands over
grain totals.

**Reading** — the weekly scan and score. Also the act, also the score itself. You *submit* a reading,
you *hold* a reading, the *Reading Office* produces them.

**Plate** — the personal metal disc that carries a reading. You press it to a socket in a branch office
and the machine gives back a number. Lin Hao owns exactly one and has never lost it.

**Band** — one of nine rank ranges. The Board shows bands. Your plate shows grains. This asymmetry is the
most important social fact in Ostara.

**Rank** — one to nine. Nine opens any file in Ostara. There is no ten, and the reason arrives in
Volume 9.

**The Board** — the wall of ceramic tiles at the head of the Reading, repainted every Thursday morning.
White for rank one, black for rank nine. The top tile has never held a name in living memory.

**Score** — a number. **Unsettled** — the Bureau's word for a figure that has no interval and no band and
therefore cannot be turned into a rank. This is the word Lin Hao first hears about what he is.

---

## The law

**The Bureau of Merit** — reads, scores, publishes, classifies, audits, requisitions, licenses. Four arms
that matter: the **Reading Office**, the **Classification Wing**, the **Exchange**, the **Field**.

**The Exchange** — where merit is spent. It sells *permissions*, not objects: a berth, an hour of a
machine, a hearing moved up, a door opened.

**Warrant** — the authority to compel testimony or requisition property. Rank six.

**Seal** — the mark that lets a citizen move merit between accounts. Without a seal a movement is
theft. Lin Wei had one use of his life.

**Held account** — the legal category for a citizen whose merit has been removed from their control and
placed in Bureau custody. It is a form. It is a room with no window. It is where classified people go,
and everyone in this book who has been "taken for a reading" has come back from one.

**Classification** — the decision about what a person *is*, as opposed to what they scored. Classified
means measured, catalogued, and no longer private.

**The Correction Office** — public hearings for people who think their score was wrong.

**The Concil of the Nine** — the nine rank-nines, who meet monthly and can requisition merit from any
citizen by majority seal.

**Requisition** — the Bureau taking a person, or a place, or a thing, on paper. A requisition for a person
is a *reading visit* that does not end the same day.

---

## The reservoir

**Reservoir** — an unsettled account. Merit that arrived without a witness and cannot be filed. Named in
Volume 2; called *unsettled* by the Bureau from Chapter 2, which is why Lin Hao hears the word before he
has a name for the thing.

**Settling** — what merit does when it arrives anywhere at all. Named by the Bureau, and the whole plot
lives in the gap between settling and *scoring*.

**The ledger** — his private record of who fed it. Faces, streets, hours. Never deeds.

**Cold minute** — what a feeder feels when he spends. One to four seconds, in the hand or the chest or an
old injury. Also a person who has felt several and started to think about it.

**Turned** — a person who is keeping track of the cold minutes. Turned people can be led to him. Nine
turned lines in one season is a legible pattern and that is the only true failure condition in the first
three hundred chapters.

**The draw** — a spend. Also the act of touching a person's hand and returning what they are owed.
Relief, not strength. He cannot take anything from anyone, ever.

**The Channel** — routing a draw back through himself into someone else so the Exchange credits them. He
does it once by accident in Volume 1 and cannot explain it afterwards, and it is not a mechanic he can use
again until he learns the Channel in Volume 5. It works. It also makes that person a target, and the first
person he routes deliberately through is Ines Duarte, and that is the hinge of Volume 5.

**Quiet account** — merit held where the Board cannot see it. Sold illegally. The reservoir is the largest
one in Ostara and it is not for sale.

## People and places

**Ostara** — the city. **The Reading** — the civic square and the central house. **The Slough** — the
Fourth Quarter, low-rank housing. **Cordwain** — industry and the tram yards. **Kestrel Hill** — rank five
to seven terraces. **The Ninth Terrace** — walled, above the tram line, where the Nine live. **Bellhouse**
— entertainment, supper clubs, the best information. **The Vestry** — the charitable quarter and the
free clinics. **Lamplit Row** — the unattributable, and the paperwork-free criminal economy.
**The Saltworks** — the Veil's scar, fenced, with the Unscored settled inside the cordon.

**The Veil** — not fog. A thin place. It opened under the Saltworks twenty years ago and stayed open, and
one person in four came out of it changed.

**The Vant** — the merchant guild. Its senior broker keeps the real books. **The Church of the Quiet
Number** — eleven thousand members, no ranks, no audits, and the only organised claim in Ostara that
unrecorded help is the only help that counts. They are correct and it is illegal.

**The Unscored** — people who came out of the Veil and never registered, living inside the cordon they may
not legally leave.

## Usage rules

**Rank, band, grain, plate, reading.** These four do the work. Do not introduce a fifth unit of
measurement. Do not say "points", "score units", "merit points", "levels", or "XP". The city uses grains
and bands because it is an administration and not a game.

**Ledger notation.** The reservoir's accounting is a System message: square brackets, on its own line,
plain language, at most one per chapter.

    [Ledger. Nineteen lines. One turned.]

    [Draw. The lift hand. It will cost her a cold minute.]

    [Open. Three.]

It reports in people, never in acts. It never tells him what to do. It is incomplete by design, and the
first time it lies to him it should be genuinely ambiguous rather than conveniently mysterious.

**On the reservoir's pronouns.** It is not a god, a spirit, a system of record with a will, or a
parent. It is an account. Write it flatly. When it seems to want something, that is a man reading his
own want into a column.

---

## Constructions to ration

Each of these is effective once or twice and dead by the tenth. Do not use them as a substitute for a
scene that should be dramatised.

**"Nobody asked."** Use at most once in the book. It is a knife and it dulls fast.

**"Nobody thanked him."** Same. If a feeder thanks him, the whole mechanic is broken — the point is that
they *cannot*, and the moment one of them says it out loud it should land as a shock and the author
should notice the shock.

**"There is no form for this."** Use at most three times, and each use must escalate: first it is
bureaucratic frustration, then it is a wall, then in Volume 7 it is the thing the whole ending turns on.
After the third, use the *specific* form that is missing instead of the general observation.

**"There is no column for it."** Same, and harder: the reservoir having no column is a central image, so
it may appear in the first hundred chapters and then not again until Volume 7.

**"The record remained."** Vary or cut. Prefer naming what the record says, or what the clerk's face did.

**"He understood the rule perfectly."** And then one plain sentence about what it means for one person.
Every time. The machinery paragraph goes on as long as it needs to, and then somebody's mother gets hurt.

**Numbers.** A number appears only when it changes something in the story. Rank thresholds are the
exception — those exist so a reader can feel distance. Beyond that, if a number does not move a decision,
delete it.
