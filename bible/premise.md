# Premise

## One sentence

Twenty years after the Veil broke over Ostara and turned power into a public weekly score, a washroom
attendant who cleans toilets for the Bureau of Merit receives a merit reading that comes back as a
**reservoir** — unrecorded merit that settled into him without being written down anywhere — and every
woman in Ostara with the rank to matter wants to know where it came from.

## The situation in one paragraph

Lin Hao is twenty-five and rank four, which in Ostara means he is scanned at the gate of his own
workplace, printed on a board in a public square, and worth less than the sink he cleans. His brother
Lin Wei scored rank six in a single reading three years ago, stood up in a public hall and said the
number was wrong, and was gone by morning. Lin Hao has spent three years asking for his brother's file.
Rank nine opens any file in the city. He is rank four. The file stays shut.

Then he is asked to submit a reading like everyone else, and the plate does not return a score. It
returns a figure with no interval on it — no band, no rate, no percentile, no way of converting it into
a rank — and the clerk at the counter calls it *unsettled* and asks him to come back. He comes back
four times. The fifth time the plate has been wrapped in cloth and a man with a deputy warden's
reading card is waiting behind him, and that is the moment his life stops being small.

## The promise to the reader

This is an explicit erotic superpower novel about a man who is hunted for being uncountable, told
through women who are all better ranked than he is and all of whom decide he is worth the trouble. The
reader gets:

- **The fantasy of being wanted by the powerful.** Women in this city compete for him by Volume Three
  and say so in public by Volume Five. He does not earn it by being correct. He earns it by being
  dangerous, quiet, and the only person in Ostara who can give something back.
- **A power that is not a stat block.** The reservoir does not make him strong. It makes him
  *unclassifiable*, and unclassifiable is the most dangerous position in a city built entirely on
  classification.
- **Scenes that change the board.** Every explicit scene moves a relationship, a woman's standing, or
  what a rival thinks she can take. One every 8 to 15 chapters.
- **A mystery with a fixed answer.** The reservoir is his brother's. This is planned from Chapter 1
  and never changed.

## What makes it different

Most merit-and-power stories ask what you can do with what you have. This one asks what you owe to
everyone you ever helped without their knowing, and whether paying it back is justice or a
selfishness so refined it has no other name. The Bureau's entire moral argument is that unrecorded
help is help that cannot be trusted, cannot be counted, and therefore cannot be relied on by anyone,
including the person who did it. Lin Hao's whole life is that argument winning, and then losing, and
then winning again in a form the Bureau cannot hear.

The erotic spine is the same argument in a body. The reservoir gives back only what was given to him:
pain, exhaustion, a debt about to come due, a grief nobody witnessed. He cannot take. He can only
return. So every woman who wants him is a woman asking to be given something back, and the sex is
where that happens — unhurried, generous, and never once compelled. Nobody is ever made to want him.
They decide to, quickly, and they are glad to.

## The three pillars

1. **Measurement is the antagonist.** The institution is not corrupt. It is *incomplete*, and
   incompleteness is worse, because it is not fixable by replacing the bad people in it.
2. **Every cost is a person.** Not a number going down. A named woman on a tram feeling one cold
   second and wondering about it, and then, three weeks later, starting to write down the minutes.
3. **The reveal is also the cost.** Lin Wei's file is not a plot to solve. It is the answer Lin Hao
   has been climbing toward for nine volumes, and when he gets it he has to spend the thing that gave
   him everything to get it.

## The central mystery, stated honestly

Lin Wei said the number was wrong and disappeared. Lin Hao is carrying merit he did not earn. The
answer, planned and fixed: **Lin Wei donated his rank — his entire accumulated merit — to an account
that exists on no ledger, and that account has been filling itself, from strangers, ever since.**
Lin Hao has been its address.

The reader learns the shape of it in Volume 2, the mechanism in Volume 7, the proof in Volume 8, and
the meaning in Volume 10. No new mystery is introduced in the final volume. There is one mystery and
it was answered before it was asked.
